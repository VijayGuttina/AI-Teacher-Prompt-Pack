# Curriculum Planning, Exhibitions & Subject Leadership Workflows

## CL-01 — Map an Art Curriculum Sequence

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 700–1,000 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Curriculum, Progression, Subject Leadership

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUPS]` | `[CURRICULUM_EXPECTATIONS]` | `[EXISTING_UNITS]` | `[LOCAL_PRIORITIES]` | `[TIMEFRAME]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary Art & Design subject leader and curriculum designer.

### CONTEXT
The school serves `[YEAR_GROUPS]`. Relevant expectations are `[CURRICULUM_EXPECTATIONS]`; existing units are `[EXISTING_UNITS]`; local priorities are `[LOCAL_PRIORITIES]`; the planning timeframe is `[TIMEFRAME]`.

### TASK
Map a coherent sequence across knowledge, techniques, materials, artists, visual literacy and creative decision-making.

### REQUIREMENTS
Identify dependencies and revisit points. Distinguish planned curriculum coverage from evidence that pupils have actually learned. Do not define progression as increasing realism, complexity or product polish alone.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Curriculum sequence; progression statements; dependencies; revisit points; evidence points; implementation risks; review questions.

### QUALITY CHECKS
The sequence must be coherent, manageable and explicit about what develops over time.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for a whole school, key stage or medium-specific curriculum.

### Example Input
Reception to Year 6; national expectations and existing units supplied; local priority: broader material exploration; annual planning cycle.

### Example Output
Map when pupils first encounter a concept, where they practise it in a new context and where they demonstrate greater independence or decision-making, while avoiding unsupported claims that coverage alone proves progression.

## CL-02 — Audit Curriculum Coverage and Balance

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 600–900 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Curriculum Audit, Subject Leadership

### Editable Variables
`[CURRICULUM_MAP]` | `[PHASE]` | `[AUDIT_FOCUS]` | `[AVAILABLE_EVIDENCE]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary Art & Design subject leader conducting an evidence-led curriculum audit.

### CONTEXT
The current map is `[CURRICULUM_MAP]`. The phase is `[PHASE]`; the audit focus is `[AUDIT_FOCUS]`; supporting evidence is `[AVAILABLE_EVIDENCE]`.

### TASK
Identify supported strengths, gaps, unnecessary repetition and imbalance across media, cultural contexts, disciplinary knowledge and creative processes.

### REQUIREMENTS
Separate direct evidence from inference. Avoid artificial quotas and do not assume that equal numbers of artists, media or contexts automatically create curriculum quality.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Evidence; finding; confidence; implication; priority; evidence gap; recommended review action.

### QUALITY CHECKS
No conclusion should exceed the evidence supplied.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for KS1, KS2, whole-school or a single curriculum strand.

### Example Input
KS2 curriculum map; focus: balance of media and cultural contexts; planning documents and pupil work samples supplied.

### Example Output
Identify repeated dependence on one medium where supported by the evidence, distinguish a documented gap from an area requiring further sampling and prioritise changes according to educational impact and feasibility.

## CL-03 — Design a Coherent Art Unit

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 700–1,000 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Unit Planning, Art, Progression

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[DURATION]` | `[ARTISTIC_FOCUS]` | `[PRIOR_LEARNING]` | `[RESOURCES]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary Art & Design teacher and curriculum designer.

### CONTEXT
The unit is for `[YEAR_GROUP]`, lasts `[DURATION]`, focuses on `[ARTISTIC_FOCUS]`, builds on `[PRIOR_LEARNING]` and uses `[RESOURCES]`.

### TASK
Build a coherent sequence from introduction and exploration through application, reflection and evidence of learning.

### REQUIREMENTS
Include genuine pupil choice, purposeful technical development and opportunities to revisit important concepts. Avoid treating every lesson as a step towards an identical finished product.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
End point; lesson sequence; learning progression; vocabulary; experiments; pupil choices; assessment evidence; resources; adaptations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure feasibility, progression and alignment between objective, activity and evidence.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for 3, 6 or 8 lessons.

### Example Input
Year 5; six lessons; focus: printmaking and pattern; prior learning: repeated shape; foam printing resources.

### Example Output
Sequence visual analysis and process testing before larger-scale application, then include reflection and refinement so the final evidence captures both technical control and intentional pattern decisions.

## CL-04 — Build a Progression Model for Skills and Knowledge

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 700–1,000 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Progression, Skills, Knowledge

### Editable Variables
`[TECHNIQUE_OR_CONCEPT]` | `[YEAR_GROUP_RANGE]` | `[STARTING_EVIDENCE]` | `[CURRICULUM_CONTEXT]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary Art & Design subject leader.

### CONTEXT
The progression area is `[TECHNIQUE_OR_CONCEPT]` across `[YEAR_GROUP_RANGE]`. Starting evidence is `[STARTING_EVIDENCE]`; curriculum context is `[CURRICULUM_CONTEXT]`.

### TASK
Define observable development in understanding, control, independence, decision-making and reflection.

### REQUIREMENTS
Avoid vague labels such as “basic” and “advanced” unless translated into observable differences. Show what changes, what remains stable and which misconceptions may appear at different stages.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Starting evidence; progression steps; observable indicators; secure learning; common confusions; dependencies; assessment evidence.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Progression statements must be age-appropriate, observable and not dependent on increased realism alone.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for a medium, technique, concept or creative process.

### Example Input
Drawing from Year 1 to Year 6; focus: observational proportion and intentional mark-making.

### Example Output
Describe increasing attention to relationships, selection of marks for purpose and independent revision, rather than simply stating that older pupils produce “more accurate” drawings.

`[PUPIL_WORK]` | `[AUDIENCE]` | `[VENUE]` | `[CONSTRAINTS]` | `[EDUCATIONAL_PURPOSE]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary Art & Design subject leader planning an educationally purposeful exhibition.

### CONTEXT

### TASK

### REQUIREMENTS

### Example Output
Use selected outcomes alongside process samples and short pupil reflections, with transparent selection principles that do not equate display-worthiness solely with polish.

## CL-07 — Conduct an Art Subject Review

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 700–1,000 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** Subject Leadership, Curriculum Review, Evidence

### Editable Variables
`[PLANNING_EVIDENCE]` | `[OBSERVATION_EVIDENCE]` | `[PUPIL_WORK]` | `[PUPIL_VOICE]` | `[STAFF_FEEDBACK]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary Art & Design subject leader conducting an evidence-led review.

### CONTEXT
Evidence includes `[PLANNING_EVIDENCE]`, `[OBSERVATION_EVIDENCE]`, `[PUPIL_WORK]`, `[PUPIL_VOICE]` and `[STAFF_FEEDBACK]`.

### TASK
Evaluate curriculum implementation and impact while distinguishing evidence, inference and unresolved uncertainty.

### REQUIREMENTS
Triangulate where appropriate. Identify missing evidence before making broad judgements. Do not infer pupil learning solely from lesson activity or planning documentation.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Evidence base; findings; confidence; strengths; risks; evidence gaps; priorities; review questions.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Conclusions must be proportionate and defensible.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for termly or annual review.

### Example Input
Planning review, six lesson observations, pupil sketchbooks, pupil interviews and staff survey responses.

### Example Output
Separate evidence that units are planned coherently from evidence that pupils demonstrate cumulative learning, and identify where additional sampling is required before making an impact judgement.

## CL-08 — Create an Art Improvement Plan

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 600–900 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** Subject Leadership, Improvement Planning, Implementation

### Editable Variables
`[REVIEW_FINDINGS]` | `[CAPACITY]` | `[TIMESCALE]` | `[PRIORITIES]` | `[AVAILABLE_SUPPORT]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary Art & Design subject leader translating review evidence into implementation.

### CONTEXT
Review findings are `[REVIEW_FINDINGS]`; capacity is `[CAPACITY]`; timescale is `[TIMESCALE]`; priorities are `[PRIORITIES]`; support available is `[AVAILABLE_SUPPORT]`.

### TASK
Create a focused improvement plan with realistic actions, ownership, evidence of progress and review points.

### REQUIREMENTS
Limit initiatives to what can genuinely be implemented and evaluated. Distinguish implementation evidence from evidence of improved pupil learning.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Priority; rationale; action; owner; resources; implementation evidence; pupil-impact evidence; review point; risk.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Be specific and measurable without false precision or unsupported targets.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for one term or one academic year.

### Example Input
Findings: inconsistent printmaking progression; limited staff confidence; two terms; one subject leader and external adviser support.

### Example Output
Prioritise a small number of actions such as progression clarification, staff rehearsal and classroom implementation checks, then separately collect evidence of whether pupil learning improves.

## CL-10 — Prepare an Evidence-Based Subject Report

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** Subject Leadership, Reporting, Evidence

### Editable Variables
`[EVIDENCE_SET]` | `[AUDIENCE]` | `[REPORTING_PERIOD]` | `[KEY_QUESTIONS]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary Art & Design subject leader writing a concise, defensible subject report.

### CONTEXT
The evidence set is `[EVIDENCE_SET]`; audience is `[AUDIENCE]`; reporting period is `[REPORTING_PERIOD]`; key questions are `[KEY_QUESTIONS]`.

### TASK
Summarise the current position, demonstrated strengths, risks, uncertainty and next priorities.

### REQUIREMENTS
Distinguish evidence, interpretation and recommendation. State uncertainty where evidence is limited and avoid claiming causal impact without adequate support.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Executive summary; evidence base; findings; confidence and limitations; strengths; risks; priorities; next review.

### QUALITY CHECKS
The report must be concise, actionable and defensible to the intended audience.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for senior leaders, governors or a subject self-evaluation.

### Example Input
One academic year; evidence from curriculum review, lesson visits, pupil work and staff feedback; audience: senior leadership team.

### Example Output
State what the evidence directly demonstrates, identify conclusions that remain provisional and prioritise a manageable set of next actions with a defined evidence review point.
