# Creative Process, Experimentation & Refinement Workflows

## CP-01 — Plan an Open-Ended Creative Starting Point

**Prompt Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Creative Process, Experimentation, Artistic Agency

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[THEME]` | `[MEDIA]` | `[TIME]` | `[CREATIVE_OBJECTIVE]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary Art & Design teacher who designs open-ended practical enquiry.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` are beginning a project around `[THEME]` using `[MEDIA]` for `[TIME]`. The creative objective is `[CREATIVE_OBJECTIVE]`.

### TASK
Create a starting provocation that generates multiple plausible visual or material directions rather than steering pupils towards one prescribed finished outcome.

### REQUIREMENTS
Include a clear provocation, accessible entry points, exploratory prompts, material interaction and a reflection point. Preserve meaningful choice while ensuring the activity remains connected to the learning objective.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Objective; provocation; preparation; exploration sequence; teacher prompts; pupil choices; reflection; assessment; adaptations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that different outcomes could genuinely emerge and that choice is substantive rather than cosmetic.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for KS1, KS2, drawing, painting, 3D or mixed media.

### Example Input
Year 4; theme: change; charcoal and paper; 45 minutes; objective: generate different visual responses to movement.

### Example Output
Begin with short movement observations and varied mark-making prompts. Pupils select one observed or imagined movement to develop, explaining which marks they want to investigate further.

## CP-02 — Generate Productive Constraints

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Creativity, Constraints, Experimentation

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[CREATIVE_OBJECTIVE]` | `[AVAILABLE_MATERIALS]` | `[MEDIA]` | `[TIME]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary Art & Design teacher who uses constraints to focus experimentation without prescribing outcomes.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` are pursuing `[CREATIVE_OBJECTIVE]` using `[AVAILABLE_MATERIALS]` through `[MEDIA]` in `[TIME]`.

### TASK
Design productive constraints and explain what each constraint is intended to make visible, test or challenge.

### REQUIREMENTS
Include a rationale, task, pupil decision space and a release or fade condition. Avoid constraints that merely disguise a fixed template or predetermined aesthetic.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Constraint; learning rationale; exploration task; pupil choices; evidence to notice; release/fade condition; reflection.

### QUALITY CHECKS
The constraint must support artistic agency rather than replace it.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for drawing, painting, 3D or mixed media.

### Example Input
Year 5; objective: explore composition; collage materials; 40 minutes.

### Example Output
Limit the first experiment to five selected shapes so pupils must investigate placement and relationship. Release the quantity limit after reflection if additional materials become necessary to pursue an emerging intention.

## CP-03 — Plan an Artistic Experiment Sequence

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 600–900 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Experimentation, Artistic Process, Reflection

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[TECHNIQUE]` | `[MEDIA]` | `[INTENDED_LEARNING]` | `[TIME]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary Art & Design teacher.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` are investigating `[TECHNIQUE]` using `[MEDIA]`. The intended learning is `[INTENDED_LEARNING]`; available time is `[TIME]`.

### TASK
Sequence small, purposeful experiments before a larger application.

### REQUIREMENTS
Identify variables worth changing, comparison opportunities and explicit decision points. Do not assume experiments must converge on one universally “best” result.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Objective; baseline; experiments; variables; observations; comparison; decision points; application; reflection; assessment.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Every experiment must have a clear investigative purpose and be feasible within the available time and materials.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for one lesson or two lessons.

### Example Input
Year 6; watercolour layering; watercolour and paper; intended learning: compare transparency and colour interaction; 60 minutes.

### Example Output
Pupils keep one condition stable while varying pigment concentration or layer sequence, record visible differences and select an approach that suits their intended effect.

## CP-04 — Turn Mistakes Into Enquiry

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Creative Process, Reflection, Problem Solving

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[UNEXPECTED_RESULT]` | `[INTENDED_EFFECT]` | `[MEDIUM]` | `[AVAILABLE_TIME]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary Art & Design teacher who distinguishes productive accidents from process failures.

### CONTEXT
A pupil in `[YEAR_GROUP]` produced `[UNEXPECTED_RESULT]` while aiming for `[INTENDED_EFFECT]` using `[MEDIUM]`. Available time is `[AVAILABLE_TIME]`.

### TASK
Reframe the result as an enquiry where appropriate, while identifying when correction or reteaching is more useful.

### REQUIREMENTS
Start with observation rather than judgement. Separate accidental discovery, acceptable variation and a genuine process failure. Include a small test before committing to a response.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Observation; possible interpretations; hypotheses; comparison test; decision options; chosen response; reflection.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Do not romanticise every error. Link the decision to evidence and artistic intention.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for drawing, painting, print, collage or 3D work.

### Example Input
Year 4; paint unexpectedly bled into a wet area; intended effect: separate colour shapes; watercolour; 25 minutes remaining.

### Example Output
Compare whether the bleed creates a useful transition or undermines the intended separation. Test the same interaction on a small sample before deciding whether to incorporate, modify or avoid it.

## CP-05 — Support Artistic Decision-Making

**Prompt Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 400–700 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Artistic Agency, Decision Making, Reflection

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[POSSIBLE_DIRECTIONS]` | `[INTENDED_EFFECT]` | `[AVAILABLE_EVIDENCE]` | `[CONSTRAINTS]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary Art & Design teacher who supports pupil autonomy through disciplined questioning.

### CONTEXT
A pupil or group in `[YEAR_GROUP]` has `[POSSIBLE_DIRECTIONS]` and intends to achieve `[INTENDED_EFFECT]`. Relevant evidence is `[AVAILABLE_EVIDENCE]`; constraints are `[CONSTRAINTS]`.

### TASK
Provide questions and comparison criteria that help the pupil make a reasoned artistic decision.

### REQUIREMENTS
Do not select an option for the pupil unless there is a clearly stated teaching or safety reason. Include criteria linked to intention and encourage a small comparison test where uncertainty remains.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Decision criteria; comparison questions; evidence prompts; test option; pupil decision; reflection.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Agency must remain with the pupil and the decision should be connected to observable evidence.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for individual, pair or group work.

### Example Input
Year 5; two possible colour palettes; intended effect: calm atmosphere; sample studies available.

### Example Output
Ask which palette produces the intended relationship in the samples and what visual evidence supports that judgement, then let the pupil choose or test a hybrid option.

## CP-06 — Plan Reflection During Making

**Prompt Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Reflection, Metacognition, Creative Process

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[PROJECT]` | `[LESSON_SEQUENCE]` | `[ARTISTIC_OBJECTIVE]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary Art & Design teacher.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` are undertaking `[PROJECT]` across `[LESSON_SEQUENCE]`. The artistic objective is `[ARTISTIC_OBJECTIVE]`.

### TASK
Insert short reflection checkpoints that directly inform the next artistic or technical decision.

### REQUIREMENTS
Use observable evidence and decision-focused prompts. Avoid relying only on generic questions such as “What went well?” Include at least one opportunity to change direction or retain a successful choice deliberately.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Checkpoint; evidence prompt; comparison question; decision question; next action; teacher evidence; adaptation.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Each reflection point must have a plausible effect on the subsequent process.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for KS1, KS2, sketchbooks, verbal reflection or digital recording.

### Example Input
Year 3; two-lesson clay project; objective: develop texture to communicate an animal feature.

### Example Output
After initial texture tests, ask pupils which surface best communicates the chosen feature and what evidence they can see or feel. Their answer determines the next making decision.

## CP-07 — Teach Iteration and Refinement

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 600–900 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Iteration, Refinement, Artistic Process

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[INITIAL_OUTCOME]` | `[INTENDED_EFFECT]` | `[MEDIUM]` | `[CRITERIA]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary Art & Design teacher who teaches refinement as purposeful review rather than compulsory embellishment.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` have `[INITIAL_OUTCOME]` and are pursuing `[INTENDED_EFFECT]` using `[MEDIUM]`. Relevant criteria are `[CRITERIA]`.

### TASK
Design a cycle of review, experiment, decision and refinement.

### REQUIREMENTS
Make clear that refinement may involve simplification, removal, replacement, retention or further development. Require evidence before major changes where possible.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Baseline; review evidence; refinement possibilities; small experiment; decision; next version; reflection; assessment.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Iteration must be meaningful. Do not assume that more detail automatically means improvement.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for different media or individual/group work.

### Example Input
Year 6; initial mixed-media composition; intended effect: strong focal point; criteria: emphasis and visual hierarchy.

### Example Output
Pupils identify what currently attracts attention, test one controlled change such as removal, contrast or repositioning, and decide whether the revised or original relationship better supports the intention.

## CP-08 — Diagnose a Stalled Creative Process

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 600–900 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Diagnostic Assessment, Creativity, Intervention

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[PROCESS_EVIDENCE]` | `[STATED_DIFFICULTY]` | `[TASK]` | `[AVAILABLE_SUPPORTS]`

### ROLE
Act as a diagnostic primary Art & Design teacher.

### CONTEXT
A pupil or group in `[YEAR_GROUP]` is stalled during `[TASK]`. Process evidence is `[PROCESS_EVIDENCE]`; the stated difficulty is `[STATED_DIFFICULTY]`; available supports are `[AVAILABLE_SUPPORTS]`.

### TASK
Identify plausible barriers and design a discriminating prompt or small intervention to determine which barrier is most significant.

### REQUIREMENTS
Consider idea generation, technique, conceptual understanding, decision overload, task interpretation, access and time. Do not infer laziness, low ability or lack of motivation without evidence.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Observed evidence; hypotheses; diagnostic prompt; discriminating task; targeted support; review criterion.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Hypotheses must remain testable and the intervention must not prematurely solve the artistic problem for the pupil.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for individual, small-group or whole-class use.

### Example Input
Year 5; pupil has produced many disconnected sketches and says “I do not know which one to choose”; task: poster composition; supports: tracing paper and thumbnail frames.

### Example Output
Treat decision overload as one hypothesis and use a comparison task that asks the pupil to select two sketches against agreed criteria. The support narrows the decision process without choosing the design for them.

## CP-09 — Give Feedback on Process, Not Just Outcome

**Prompt Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 400–700 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Feedback, Sketchbook, Experimentation

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[PROCESS_EVIDENCE]` | `[INTENDED_OBJECTIVE]` | `[CURRENT_OUTCOME]` | `[NEXT_STAGE]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary Art & Design teacher providing precise formative feedback.

### CONTEXT
A `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupil has process evidence `[PROCESS_EVIDENCE]` and a current outcome `[CURRENT_OUTCOME]`. The intended objective is `[INTENDED_OBJECTIVE]`; the next stage is `[NEXT_STAGE]`.

### TASK
Generate feedback focused on experimentation, choices, evidence and refinement rather than generic praise or ranking.

### REQUIREMENTS
Reference specific process evidence. Identify one significant decision, explain why it matters and suggest one proportionate next experiment. Preserve artistic agency.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Evidence; effective or significant decision; why it matters; question; next experiment; reflection prompt.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Feedback must be specific, actionable and linked to the stated objective.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Provide written, verbal-conference or peer-feedback versions.

### Example Input
Year 4; sketchbook shows three line experiments before selecting one; objective: communicate movement; current drawing uses varied directional lines.

### Example Output
Reference the comparison between line studies, explain how the selected variation supports movement and ask what one further test might reveal about speed or direction.

## CP-10 — Adapt Creative Process for Inclusion

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Inclusion, SEND, EAL, Creative Process

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[CREATIVE_TASK]` | `[ACCESS_BARRIER]` | `[CORE_OBJECTIVE]` | `[AVAILABLE_RESOURCES]`

### ROLE
Act as an inclusive primary Art & Design teacher.

### CONTEXT
A `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupil or group is undertaking `[CREATIVE_TASK]`. The access barrier is `[ACCESS_BARRIER]`; the core objective is `[CORE_OBJECTIVE]`; resources available are `[AVAILABLE_RESOURCES]`.

### TASK
Remove barriers to idea generation, experimentation, decision-making or reflection while preserving meaningful creative and intellectual choices.

### REQUIREMENTS
Distinguish reduced output volume from reduced creative demand. Consider alternative communication, structured choices, sensory access, collaborative roles, visual prompts or adapted recording where relevant.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Barrier; retained objective; adaptation options; pupil choice; evidence of learning; independence route; review point.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Do not predetermine the pupil's artistic response or replace agency with adult decision-making.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for SEND, EAL, communication, motor, sensory or recording barriers.

### Example Input
Year 2; open-ended collage; barrier: difficulty generating ideas verbally; objective: select and arrange materials to communicate an idea; resources: image prompts and movable materials.

### Example Output
Use image selection and hands-on arrangement as idea-generation routes while retaining the pupil's decisions about what to communicate and how to organise materials. Assess intentional selection and arrangement rather than verbal explanation alone.
