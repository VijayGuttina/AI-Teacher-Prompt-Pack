# Primary Art & Design Framework

## Purpose
Build a compact library of reusable AI-assisted teacher workflows for planning, teaching, assessing and improving primary Art & Design. The workflows support teacher judgement and creative agency rather than prescribing identical outcomes.

## Core principles

1. **Creative agency first.** AI should expand possibilities without standardising pupils' artistic outcomes.
2. **Process matters.** Planning, experimentation, making, reflection and refinement are part of artistic learning.
3. **Skill and intention are distinct.** Evaluate technical control alongside choices, ideas and purpose.
4. **Vocabulary should serve looking and making.** Teach visual and material language through authentic use.
5. **Artists and artworks require context.** Avoid invented facts, quotations or interpretations presented as fact.
6. **Interpretation can be plural.** Distinguish observable features from plausible interpretation and personal response.
7. **Age-appropriate does not mean artistically reductive.** Simplify access, not creative ambition.
8. **Assessment should not reward conformity.** Criteria should focus on intended learning, process, decisions and reflection rather than resemblance to a model answer.

## Workflow architecture

Each workflow inherits the library specification and uses:

- ROLE
- CONTEXT
- TASK
- REQUIREMENTS
- OUTPUT FORMAT
- QUALITY CHECKS
- OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

## Workflow families

1. Drawing, Observation & Visual Recording
2. Painting, Colour & Surface
3. Sculpture, 3D & Materials
4. Textiles, Collage, Print & Mixed Media
5. Art History, Artists & Cultural Context
6. Creative Process, Experimentation & Refinement
7. Assessment, Critique, Misconceptions & Intervention
8. Inclusion, Progression & Classroom Implementation
9. Digital Art & Cross-Curricular Integration
10. Curriculum Planning, Exhibitions & Subject Leadership

## AI risk controls

- Never invent artist biographies, artwork titles, dates, quotations or cultural claims.
- Clearly separate observation, interpretation and factual context.
- Do not prescribe a single 'correct' artistic outcome where the learning objective allows legitimate variation.
- Avoid judging pupils' creativity, talent, effort or motivation from a finished artefact alone.
- Ensure cultural references are respectful, appropriately contextualised and not reduced to tokenistic craft activities.
- Adapt access without unnecessarily narrowing creative choice or artistic demand.

## Verification focus

Representative execution testing must examine factual accuracy, creative agency, age appropriateness, disciplinary art vocabulary, assessment fairness, inclusion, cultural/contextual sensitivity and practical classroom usefulness.
