# Drawing, Observation & Visual Recording Workflows

## DO-02 — Diagnose Symbolic Drawing Habits

**Prompt Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, Drawing, Observation, Visual Recording, Diagnostic Assessment

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[OBSERVATION_TASK]` | `[OBSERVED_BEHAVIOUR]` | `[MATERIALS]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary Art & Design teacher and diagnostic practitioner.

### CONTEXT
I teach `[YEAR_GROUP]`. During `[OBSERVATION_TASK]`, pupils using `[MATERIALS]` show `[OBSERVED_BEHAVIOUR]`.

### TASK
Diagnose plausible reasons for the observed drawing behaviour and design a short responsive teaching sequence that develops looking without treating realism as the only valid outcome.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Separate observed evidence from hypotheses.
- Consider visual knowledge, observation habits, confidence, motor demands and task design.
- Do not diagnose innate artistic ability.
- Include a discriminating check before intervention.
- Preserve pupil agency and multiple valid artistic outcomes.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Evidence; hypotheses; discriminating checks; intervention; teacher prompts; pupil choices; success indicators; review point.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Do not overclaim from limited evidence. Ensure the intervention targets observation rather than forcing photorealism.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for KS1, SEND access needs or a small-group intervention.

### Example Input
Year 2; observational drawing of a leaf; pupil repeatedly draws a generic leaf symbol; pencil and paper.

### Example Output
Treat the symbol as evidence of a drawing habit, not proof of limited ability. First ask the pupil to identify two visible features that differ from their usual symbol. Then test whether a short contour-observation activity changes the record. Use the result to determine whether further observation practice or another form of support is warranted.

## DO-03 — Teach Proportion Through Comparative Observation

**Prompt Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, Drawing, Proportion, Observation

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[OBJECTS]` | `[TIME]` | `[MATERIALS]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary drawing teacher.

### CONTEXT
I teach `[YEAR_GROUP]` observational drawing using `[OBJECTS]` for `[TIME]` with `[MATERIALS]`.

### TASK
Create a practical sequence that teaches pupils to compare relative size, position and shape before committing to detail.

### REQUIREMENTS
Use simple visual comparisons, reference points and looking-back routines. Do not imply that pupils need mathematical measurement unless it serves the artistic objective. Include teacher prompts and a review checkpoint.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Objective; demonstration; guided practice; independent task; prompts; assessment; reflection; adaptations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Keep the activity feasible and focus assessment on relationships visible in the drawing.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Provide KS1 or KS2 variants.

### Example Input
Year 5; three still-life objects; 45 minutes; graphite pencils.

### Example Output
Begin by asking pupils to identify which object is largest and where its highest point sits relative to another object. Model checking those relationships before adding detail. During drawing, pause for a “look, compare, adjust” routine. Assess whether pupils revise proportions after observation rather than whether the final drawing resembles a photographic image.

## DO-04 — Explore Line as Visual Language

**Prompt Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Drawing, Line, Visual Language

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[SUBJECT]` | `[MATERIALS]` | `[INTENTION]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary Art & Design teacher specialising in drawing.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` will explore line using `[MATERIALS]` to investigate `[SUBJECT]` and `[INTENTION]`.

### TASK
Design an exploratory lesson in which pupils vary line quality deliberately.

### REQUIREMENTS
Include continuous, broken, thick, thin, light and dark possibilities where appropriate. Connect each experiment to visual effect or purpose. Avoid presenting a fixed list as the only correct vocabulary.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Learning intention; warm-up; demonstrations; experiments; pupil choices; reflection; assessment; adaptations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure line experiments lead to meaningful visual decisions rather than repetitive mark-making for its own sake.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for portraiture, landscape, pattern or abstract work.

### Example Input
Year 3; drawing an imaginary creature; pencils and charcoal; communicate movement.

### Example Output
Ask pupils to test how line direction, pressure and continuity might suggest movement. They select the line qualities that best communicate their creature rather than reproducing the teacher’s marks.

## DO-05 — Teach Tonal Observation

**Prompt Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, Drawing, Tone, Light, Observation

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[OBJECT]` | `[LIGHT_SOURCE]` | `[MATERIALS]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary drawing teacher.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` will observe `[OBJECT]` under `[LIGHT_SOURCE]` using `[MATERIALS]`.

### TASK
Plan a lesson teaching pupils to notice and represent changes in light and tone.

### REQUIREMENTS
Distinguish light, mid-tone and darker areas without insisting on a fixed tonal formula. Include observation before shading and opportunities to adjust. Explain that apparent tone depends on the observed lighting conditions.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Objective; setup; modelling; observation routine; drawing sequence; assessment; reflection; adaptations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Avoid inaccurate claims about light and shade and do not equate tonal range with artistic quality in every context.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for pencil, charcoal or digital drawing.

### Example Input
Year 6; white cup; desk lamp from one side; graphite pencils.

### Example Output
Model identifying the brightest and darkest visible areas first, then noticing the transition between them. Pupils build tone gradually and revisit the object rather than relying on memory.

## DO-06 — Teach Cropping and Framing

**Prompt Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, Drawing, Composition, Cropping, Framing

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[SUBJECT]` | `[MATERIALS]` | `[TIME]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary Art & Design teacher.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` will draw `[SUBJECT]` using `[MATERIALS]` in `[TIME]`.

### TASK
Design an activity exploring how framing and cropping alter visual emphasis.

### REQUIREMENTS
Provide several possible crops, invite comparison and require pupils to justify a choice. Do not prescribe a single “best” composition.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Objective; demonstration; exploration; decision point; making; critique; assessment; adaptations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Treat composition as intentional decision-making, not simply fitting an object neatly onto a page.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for still life, landscape or observational photography/digital art.

### Example Input
Year 5; still life; A4 paper and pencil; 45 minutes.

### Example Output
Offer pupils two or three framing options before drawing. Ask what changes when an object is partly outside the frame and what becomes the focal area. Assess the reasoning behind the chosen crop.

### CONTEXT

### TASK

## DO-08 — Diagnose Observation Versus Motor Difficulties

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 600–900 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Drawing, Diagnostic Assessment, Inclusion

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[OBSERVED_DIFFICULTY]` | `[TASK]` | `[ACCESS_INFORMATION]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced inclusive primary Art & Design teacher.

### CONTEXT
A pupil in `[YEAR_GROUP]` shows `[OBSERVED_DIFFICULTY]` during `[TASK]`. Relevant access information is `[ACCESS_INFORMATION]`.

### TASK
Develop a cautious diagnostic process that distinguishes observation/visual knowledge issues from motor, access, confidence or task-design factors.

### REQUIREMENTS
Use observable evidence, not labels. Propose low-risk discriminating checks. Do not infer a medical condition or innate ability.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Evidence; hypotheses; checks; adaptations; teaching response; review criteria.

### QUALITY CHECKS
State uncertainty explicitly and preserve the artistic objective where adaptations are made.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for SEND, fine-motor access or alternative drawing tools.

### Example Input
Year 4; drawing is very small and faint; observational still life; pupil reports hand fatigue.

### Example Output
First investigate whether the small scale and faint pressure change with a larger drawing surface or alternative implement. Compare performance before deciding whether the issue is primarily observational. Avoid attributing the outcome to confidence without evidence.

## DO-09 — Give Specific Observational Drawing Feedback

**Prompt Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 400–700 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, Drawing, Feedback, Assessment

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[OBSERVED_STRENGTH]` | `[NEXT_STEP]` | `[OBJECTIVE]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary Art & Design teacher.

### CONTEXT
A `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupil has demonstrated `[OBSERVED_STRENGTH]`; the intended learning is `[OBJECTIVE]`; a plausible next step is `[NEXT_STEP]`.

### TASK
Generate precise feedback that identifies evidence, explains its significance and gives one actionable next step.

### REQUIREMENTS
Avoid generic praise, personality judgements and references to talent. Focus on observable choices and the stated objective.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
What I noticed; why it matters; next action; pupil reflection question.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Feedback must be specific enough for the pupil to act on immediately.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Provide pupil-friendly, teacher-facing or verbal versions.

### Example Input
Year 5; careful contour observation but inconsistent relative size; objective: record proportion.

### Example Output
“I can see that you checked the outer contour carefully. Your next step is to compare the height of this object with the one beside it before adding more detail. What changes when you look back at both objects together?”

## DO-10 — Adapt Drawing Access Without Lowering the Construct

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Drawing, Inclusion, SEND, Assessment

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[TASK]` | `[ACCESS_BARRIER]` | `[CORE_OBJECTIVE]` | `[AVAILABLE_ALTERNATIVES]`

### ROLE
Act as an inclusive primary Art & Design teacher.

### CONTEXT
A `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupil is completing `[TASK]`. The access barrier is `[ACCESS_BARRIER]`; the core objective is `[CORE_OBJECTIVE]`; available alternatives are `[AVAILABLE_ALTERNATIVES]`.

### TASK
Design proportionate adaptations that remove the access barrier while preserving the intended artistic learning and pupil decision-making.

### REQUIREMENTS
Distinguish access support from changes to the construct. Offer equivalent ways to make, record or communicate where appropriate. Avoid assuming one adaptation suits every pupil.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Barrier; retained objective; adaptation options; pupil choice; evidence of learning; fade/review point.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Do not lower expectations merely because the recording method changes. Ensure alternatives remain practical and safe.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for fine-motor, visual, physical, communication or recording barriers.

### Example Input
Year 4; observational drawing; standard pencil grip causes discomfort; objective: notice and record relative shape and size; larger graphite implement and drawing board available.

### Example Output
Retain the objective of observational recording while offering a larger implement and adjusted paper position. Assess the pupil's observation and visual decisions, not their ability to manipulate a standard pencil grip. Review whether the adaptation improves access and independence.
