# Digital Art & Cross-Curricular Integration Workflows

## DC-01 — Plan a Digital Art Exploration

**Prompt Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Digital Art, Experimentation, Creative Process

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[DIGITAL_TOOLS]` | `[ARTISTIC_OBJECTIVE]` | `[TIME]` | `[PRIOR_EXPERIENCE]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary Art & Design teacher who treats digital technology as an artistic medium rather than an end in itself.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` have access to `[DIGITAL_TOOLS]`. The artistic objective is `[ARTISTIC_OBJECTIVE]`, available time is `[TIME]` and prior experience is `[PRIOR_EXPERIENCE]`.

### TASK
Design an exploration in which pupils investigate visual possibilities offered by the digital medium before developing a more intentional response.

### REQUIREMENTS
Begin with visual or creative intent, not software novelty. Include purposeful experimentation, meaningful pupil choices and reflection on what the medium enables or constrains.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Objective; tool capabilities; exploration sequence; experiments; pupil choices; reflection; evidence; adaptations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure the activity is technically realistic and that digital operations are connected to artistic decisions.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for KS1, KS2, tablets, laptops, drawing apps or image-making software.

### Example Input
Year 5; tablets with drawing software; objective: investigate texture and atmosphere; 60 minutes; mixed prior experience.

### Example Output
Begin with a small set of texture experiments, ask pupils to compare their visual effects and select or combine approaches that support an intended atmosphere rather than rewarding use of the greatest number of software features.

## DC-02 — Compare Digital and Physical Media

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Digital Art, Media, Comparison

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[VISUAL_OBJECTIVE]` | `[PHYSICAL_MEDIA]` | `[DIGITAL_MEDIA]` | `[TIME]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary Art & Design teacher designing medium-specific enquiry.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` are pursuing `[VISUAL_OBJECTIVE]` using `[PHYSICAL_MEDIA]` and `[DIGITAL_MEDIA]` over `[TIME]`.

### TASK
Create an investigation into what each medium enables, constrains and changes.

### REQUIREMENTS
Avoid presenting digital work as inherently superior or merely as a substitute for physical media. Keep comparison tied to observable evidence and artistic intention.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Objective; comparison criteria; matched experiments; observations; medium-specific affordances; pupil choice; reflection.

### QUALITY CHECKS
The investigation must support genuine medium-specific reasoning rather than a technology preference survey.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for drawing, painting, collage, image manipulation or animation.

### Example Input
Year 6; objective: create depth and atmosphere; charcoal and digital drawing; two lessons.

### Example Output
Pupils test comparable visual strategies in both media, record differences in layering, reversibility and texture, then choose a medium or combination based on the intended effect.

## DC-03 — Teach Layers, Selection and Revision

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, Digital Art, Layers, Revision

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[DIGITAL_TOOL]` | `[ARTISTIC_TASK]` | `[SUPPORTED_CAPABILITIES]` | `[TIME]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary Art & Design teacher with accurate knowledge of the stated digital tool.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` are using `[DIGITAL_TOOL]` for `[ARTISTIC_TASK]`. Relevant capabilities are `[SUPPORTED_CAPABILITIES]`; available time is `[TIME]`.

### TASK
Sequence practical learning using layers, selection and reversible editing where the tool supports them.

### REQUIREMENTS
Connect each technical operation to a visual decision. Include demonstration, guided practice, independent choices and review. Do not invent features that the stated tool may not possess.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Objective; capability check; demonstration; guided practice; independent task; revision point; reflection; assessment.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Technical instructions must match the stated tool and revision must serve artistic purpose.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for software, device or pupil experience level.

### Example Input
Year 5; tablet drawing app with layers and selection; task: develop a poster composition; 50 minutes.

### Example Output
Demonstrate how separating visual elements onto layers allows a composition to be tested and revised, then require pupils to make and justify at least one deliberate compositional change.

## DC-04 — Plan a Digital Image Manipulation Enquiry

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 600–900 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, Digital Art, Image Manipulation, Ethics

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[SOURCE_IMAGE_CONTEXT]` | `[LEARNING_OBJECTIVE]` | `[PERMITTED_OPERATIONS]` | `[TOOL]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary Art & Design teacher who combines creative practice with age-appropriate attention to authorship, permissions and representation.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` will work with `[SOURCE_IMAGE_CONTEXT]`. The objective is `[LEARNING_OBJECTIVE]`; permitted operations are `[PERMITTED_OPERATIONS]`; the tool is `[TOOL]`.

### TASK
Design an enquiry using cropping, colour, layering, scale or other permitted manipulations to investigate how visual meaning changes.

### REQUIREMENTS
Clarify source context and permitted use. Address authorship, permissions and representation where relevant. Do not imply unrestricted reuse of images or treat editing as inherently deceptive.

### Example Input
Year 6; school-owned photographs; objective: investigate how cropping changes emphasis; cropping, colour adjustment and layering permitted; tablet editor.

### Example Output
Pupils create controlled variations from the same source, compare how attention and interpretation change and record both the visual evidence and the source information relevant to responsible use.

## DC-06 — Integrate Art With Another Subject Meaningfully

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 600–900 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Cross-Curricular, Curriculum Design

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[ART_OBJECTIVE]` | `[OTHER_SUBJECT]` | `[OTHER_SUBJECT_OBJECTIVE]` | `[SHARED_CONTEXT]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary curriculum designer with expertise in Art & Design and `[OTHER_SUBJECT]`.

### CONTEXT
For `[YEAR_GROUP]`, the Art objective is `[ART_OBJECTIVE]` and the `[OTHER_SUBJECT]` objective is `[OTHER_SUBJECT_OBJECTIVE]`. The shared context is `[SHARED_CONTEXT]`.

### TASK
Design an integrated activity that advances both disciplines where integration is genuinely educationally useful.

### REQUIREMENTS
Identify the authentic conceptual connection. Do not use decorative artwork as evidence of unrelated subject learning. State what evidence belongs to Art, what belongs to `[OTHER_SUBJECT]`, and what cannot be inferred from the activity.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Dual objectives; authentic connection; activity sequence; Art evidence; subject evidence; construct boundaries; assessment; adaptations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Neither discipline should be diluted, and assessment claims must respect subject boundaries.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for history, science, geography or English.

### Example Input
Year 5; Art objective: use visual hierarchy; geography objective: communicate a place-based pattern; shared context: local land use.

### Example Output
Pupils analyse and visualise spatial patterns using composition and visual hierarchy, with geography assessed through the accuracy and interpretation of the concept and Art assessed through intentional visual organisation.

## DC-08 — Diagnose Digital Art Access or Process Problems

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Digital Art, Diagnosis, Inclusion

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[PUPIL_PROCESS]` | `[TOOL]` | `[OBSERVED_DIFFICULTY]` | `[INTENDED_LEARNING]`

### ROLE
Act as a diagnostic primary Art & Design teacher.

### CONTEXT
A pupil or group in `[YEAR_GROUP]` is using `[TOOL]`. Their process is `[PUPIL_PROCESS]`; observed difficulty is `[OBSERVED_DIFFICULTY]`; intended learning is `[INTENDED_LEARNING]`.

### TASK
Identify plausible causes involving access, technical understanding, visual decision-making, task interpretation or task design, then create a discriminating check.

### REQUIREMENTS
Do not assume a technical difficulty indicates weak artistic understanding. Separate direct evidence from inference and test competing hypotheses where feasible.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Observed evidence; hypotheses; discriminating check; interpretation; targeted response; reassessment.

### QUALITY CHECKS
The diagnosis must be testable and the response must address the identified barrier rather than simply repeating the entire lesson.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for individual, pair or group work.

### Example Input
Year 5; pupil repeatedly undoes changes; tablet editor; difficulty: cannot progress beyond initial layout; intended learning: revise composition intentionally.

### Example Output
Consider technical uncertainty, fear of irreversible loss, decision overload and composition understanding. Use a copy or duplicate workflow to test whether reversibility changes the pupil's willingness to experiment before diagnosing the artistic barrier.

## DC-09 — Evaluate Digital Art Ethically and Critically

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, Digital Art, Ethics, Critical Thinking

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[DIGITAL_ARTWORK_OR_PRACTICE]` | `[CONTEXT]` | `[ETHICAL_FOCUS]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary Art & Design teacher facilitating nuanced, age-appropriate critical discussion.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` are examining `[DIGITAL_ARTWORK_OR_PRACTICE]` in `[CONTEXT]`. The ethical focus is `[ETHICAL_FOCUS]`.

### TASK
Create questions and discussion boundaries around authorship, editing, representation, ownership, audience or other relevant issues.

### REQUIREMENTS
Avoid simplistic claims that editing is always deceptive or that digital work is inherently less authentic. Distinguish factual questions from interpretive and ethical questions.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Context; factual checks; discussion questions; evidence prompts; competing considerations; boundaries; reflection.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Maintain nuance, age-appropriateness and respect for legitimate differences in interpretation where facts do not settle the question.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for KS2, photography, social images, digital illustration or animation.

### Example Input
Year 6; heavily edited portrait image; classroom discussion; ethical focus: representation and audience expectations.

### Example Output
Ask what changes can be observed, what information is known about the editing and how different audiences might interpret the image, while separating evidence about the image from broader ethical judgements.

## DC-10 — Adapt Digital and Cross-Curricular Art for Inclusion

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Inclusion, Digital Art, Cross-Curricular

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[ACTIVITY]` | `[CORE_ART_OBJECTIVE]` | `[OTHER_OBJECTIVE]` | `[ACCESS_BARRIER]` | `[AVAILABLE_TECHNOLOGY]`

### ROLE
Act as an inclusive primary teacher with expertise in Art & Design and accessible curriculum design.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` are undertaking `[ACTIVITY]`. The core Art objective is `[CORE_ART_OBJECTIVE]`; where relevant, the additional objective is `[OTHER_OBJECTIVE]`. The access barrier is `[ACCESS_BARRIER]`; available technology is `[AVAILABLE_TECHNOLOGY]`.

### TASK
Remove barriers to participation, decision-making, making or evidence collection while preserving the relevant artistic and disciplinary constructs.

### REQUIREMENTS
Provide alternatives when technology itself becomes the barrier. Distinguish access adaptation from lowering the core learning. Preserve meaningful pupil choices and specify equivalent evidence routes.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Barrier; retained objectives; adaptation options; alternative route; equivalent evidence; independence plan; review point.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure access improves without unintentionally changing what is being assessed or allowing one subject objective to disappear inside the other.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for SEND, EAL, communication, motor, sensory or digital-access barriers.

### Example Input
Year 4; digital map-inspired artwork linked to geography; Art objective: organise visual elements for emphasis; geography objective: represent selected local features; barrier: fine motor difficulty using a touchscreen; technology: tablet and large-trackpad laptop.

### Example Output
Allow alternative input hardware or physical arrangement before digital capture while retaining pupil decisions about visual hierarchy and feature selection. Assess the Art and geography evidence separately and avoid treating touchscreen speed as part of either construct.
