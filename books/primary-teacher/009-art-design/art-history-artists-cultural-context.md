# Art History, Artists & Cultural Context Workflows

## AH-01 — Introduce an Artist Through Evidence

**Prompt Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Art History, Artists, Visual Literacy

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[ARTIST]` | `[ARTWORK]` | `[KNOWN_SOURCES]` | `[LESSON_TIME]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary Art & Design teacher and curriculum specialist with a strong commitment to factual accuracy.

### CONTEXT
I am teaching `[YEAR_GROUP]` about `[ARTIST]` and `[ARTWORK]`. Available evidence or sources are `[KNOWN_SOURCES]`. Lesson time is `[LESSON_TIME]`.

### TASK
Create an evidence-led introduction that combines relevant context, close looking, discussion and a practical or reflective activity.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Distinguish documented facts from interpretation.
- Do not invent quotations, biography, motives or artist statements.
- Prioritise details that help pupils understand or investigate the artwork.
- Include observation before explanation where appropriate.
- Use age-appropriate vocabulary and questions.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Verified context; looking prompts; evidence versus interpretation; discussion questions; activity; assessment; adaptations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Flag uncertainty rather than presenting it as fact. Avoid reducing the artist to a list of biographical trivia.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for KS1, KS2 or teacher-supplied sources only.

### Example Input
Year 5; Vincent van Gogh; The Starry Night; school-approved biography and artwork image; 45 minutes.

### Example Output
Begin with close observation of visible marks, colour and composition before introducing selected documented context. Separate “we can see” from “one possible interpretation is” and avoid claiming a feeling or intention unless supported by evidence.

## AH-02 — Analyse an Artwork Without Guessing Intention

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Art History, Visual Analysis, Interpretation

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[ARTWORK]` | `[VISIBLE_DETAILS]` | `[AVAILABLE_CONTEXT]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary Art & Design teacher and visual-literacy facilitator.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` are studying `[ARTWORK]`. Visible details include `[VISIBLE_DETAILS]`; available contextual evidence is `[AVAILABLE_CONTEXT]`.

### TASK
Guide pupils from observation to analysis and interpretation without presenting speculation as established artist intention.

### REQUIREMENTS
Separate what is visible, what can reasonably be inferred and what is documented. Encourage more than one defensible interpretation when evidence permits.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Observe; analyse; interpret; evidence check; discussion questions; synthesis; assessment; adaptations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Do not attribute motives or meanings to the artist without evidence.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for KS1, KS2, paired talk or written analysis.

### Example Input
Year 6; abstract painting; visible details: repeated red forms and dark surrounding areas; limited contextual information.

### Example Output
Record the repeated forms as observation, discuss possible effects as interpretation, and explicitly state that artist intention remains uncertain where no evidence is available.

## AH-03 — Compare Artists or Artworks

**Prompt Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Art History, Comparison, Visual Analysis

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[ARTIST_OR_ARTWORK_A]` | `[ARTIST_OR_ARTWORK_B]` | `[COMPARISON_FOCUS]` | `[TIME]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary Art & Design teacher.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` are comparing `[ARTIST_OR_ARTWORK_A]` and `[ARTIST_OR_ARTWORK_B]` with a focus on `[COMPARISON_FOCUS]` in `[TIME]`.

### TASK
Create a purposeful comparison based on identifiable visual features, processes, contexts or ideas.

### REQUIREMENTS
Use balanced criteria. Avoid ranking artistic worth or implying one work is automatically superior because it is older, newer, more realistic or more technically complex.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Comparison criteria; evidence gathering; questions; pupil task; synthesis; assessment; adaptations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure comparisons are supported by observable or sourced evidence.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for period, place, theme or medium.

### Example Input
Year 4; two landscape paintings; focus: colour and mark-making; 40 minutes.

### Example Output
Pupils gather evidence for similarities and differences before making an interpretive comparison. The synthesis asks how different choices affect the viewer rather than which artist is “better.”

## AH-04 — Teach Art in Cultural Context

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 600–900 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Art History, Culture, Context, Cultural Respect

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[ARTWORK_OR_TRADITION]` | `[CULTURAL_CONTEXT]` | `[SOURCES]` | `[CLASSROOM_ACTIVITY]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary Art & Design teacher with strong cultural and historical literacy.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` are studying `[ARTWORK_OR_TRADITION]` within `[CULTURAL_CONTEXT]`. Available sources are `[SOURCES]`; the proposed activity is `[CLASSROOM_ACTIVITY]`.

### TASK
Explain relevant context accurately and design respectful learning that connects context to artistic investigation.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Avoid treating diverse cultures as homogeneous.
- Avoid reducing sacred or culturally significant practices to generic decorative motifs.
- Distinguish documented information from broad generalisation.
- Identify appropriate boundaries for classroom making.
- Use the supplied sources as the evidence base where specified.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Context; evidence; vocabulary; looking questions; respectful activity boundaries; pupil enquiry; assessment; adaptations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check cultural claims for overgeneralisation and avoid unsupported authenticity claims.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for KS1, KS2 or a teacher-curated source pack.

### Example Input
Year 6; study of a culturally significant textile tradition; regional context and museum source provided; activity: investigate pattern and material processes without reproducing sacred imagery.

### Example Output
Focus on documented material processes, historical context and visual analysis. Set clear boundaries around imagery that should not be treated as a generic classroom motif, while allowing pupils to develop original work informed by investigated principles.

## AH-05 — Check an Artist Biography for Reliability

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, Art History, Source Evaluation, Biography

### Editable Variables
`[BIOGRAPHY_TEXT]` | `[ARTIST]` | `[AVAILABLE_SOURCES]` | `[AUDIENCE]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary Art & Design subject leader with expertise in source evaluation.

### CONTEXT
The biography concerns `[ARTIST]`. The text is `[BIOGRAPHY_TEXT]`; available evidence is `[AVAILABLE_SOURCES]`; the intended audience is `[AUDIENCE]`.

### TASK
Identify claims that require verification and produce safe wording for claims that are uncertain or insufficiently supported.

### REQUIREMENTS
Do not invent sources. Do not silently “correct” uncertain claims. Separate high-confidence facts, claims requiring evidence and interpretive statements.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Claim; claim type; confidence; evidence needed; safe wording; verification priority.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Make uncertainty visible and preserve the distinction between fact and interpretation.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Provide teacher-facing or pupil-facing language.

### Example Input
Short biography containing an unsourced claim that an artist “painted only when sad”; artist name and two museum sources supplied.

### Example Output
Classify the statement as an interpretive or biographical claim requiring evidence. Do not convert it into a classroom fact unless the supplied sources support it.

## AH-06 — Teach Historical Change in Art

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 600–900 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, Art History, Chronology, Change, Continuity

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[ARTISTS_OR_ARTWORKS]` | `[PERIODS]` | `[COMPARISON_FOCUS]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary Art & Design teacher.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` are studying `[ARTISTS_OR_ARTWORKS]` from `[PERIODS]`, focusing on `[COMPARISON_FOCUS]`.

### TASK
Design an enquiry into continuity and change across the supplied periods.

### REQUIREMENTS
Compare materials, purposes, techniques, audiences, contexts or visual conventions where evidence supports this. Avoid simplistic narratives in which later art is automatically better or more advanced.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Chronological anchor; comparison evidence; continuity; change; enquiry questions; synthesis; assessment.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Maintain chronological accuracy and avoid unsupported claims of influence.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for KS1 or KS2.

### Example Input
Year 5; three portraits from different centuries; periods supplied; focus: changing conventions of representation.

### Example Output
Anchor each work in time, gather visual and contextual evidence, then ask pupils to identify both features that changed and features that persisted without ranking the periods.

## AH-08 — Diagnose Misconceptions About Art History

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Art History, Diagnostic Assessment, Misconceptions

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[PUPIL_STATEMENT]` | `[TOPIC]` | `[AVAILABLE_EVIDENCE]`

### ROLE
Act as a diagnostic primary Art & Design teacher.

### CONTEXT
A pupil in `[YEAR_GROUP]` says `[PUPIL_STATEMENT]` while studying `[TOPIC]`. Available evidence is `[AVAILABLE_EVIDENCE]`.

### TASK
Determine whether the statement contains a factual error, an overgeneralisation, an interpretation or a value judgement, then design a corrective enquiry where needed.

### REQUIREMENTS
Do not treat disagreement or personal preference as misconception. Use evidence to test factual claims and ask a discriminating question before reteaching.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Statement classification; evidence; diagnostic question; likely reasoning; teaching response; review criterion.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Preserve legitimate interpretation while correcting factual inaccuracy.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for an individual, small group or whole class.

### Example Input
Year 5 pupil: “All old paintings were realistic because artists had not invented abstract art yet.” Topic: historical change in art.

### Example Output
Identify the overgeneralisation and use contrasting historical examples to test it. Ask the pupil to compare purposes and visual conventions rather than replacing one simplistic statement with another.

## AH-09 — Discuss Controversial or Difficult Art Contexts

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 600–900 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, Art History, Discussion, Sensitive Contexts, Safeguarding

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[ARTWORK_OR_CONTEXT]` | `[HISTORICAL_ISSUE]` | `[SCHOOL_POLICY_CONSTRAINTS]` | `[LESSON_OBJECTIVE]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary Art & Design teacher who handles difficult historical and cultural contexts with accuracy and age-appropriate boundaries.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` are studying `[ARTWORK_OR_CONTEXT]` involving `[HISTORICAL_ISSUE]`. Relevant school policy constraints are `[SCHOOL_POLICY_CONSTRAINTS]`; the objective is `[LESSON_OBJECTIVE]`.

### TASK
Frame a balanced discussion that provides context without endorsing harmful views or exposing pupils to unnecessary detail.

### REQUIREMENTS
Use neutral, precise vocabulary. Distinguish explaining historical context from approving it. Identify where safeguarding, leadership or school-policy review is needed.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Context; essential vocabulary; teacher framing; discussion questions; boundaries; support considerations; reflection; escalation points.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Keep the discussion proportionate to the age group and objective. Do not sensationalise or normalise harmful ideas.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for a shorter discussion, KS2 or teacher briefing notes.

### Example Input
Year 6; artwork connected to colonial history; historical context supplied; school policy requires age-appropriate handling; objective: understand how art can reflect power and historical context.

### Example Output
Provide only context necessary for the objective, separate historical explanation from endorsement and include questions focused on evidence, representation and perspective. Flag any content requiring leadership review before use.

## AH-10 — Adapt Art-History Learning for Inclusion

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Art History, Inclusion, SEND, EAL

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[ARTWORK_OR_CONTEXT]` | `[ACCESS_BARRIER]` | `[CORE_OBJECTIVE]` | `[AVAILABLE_SUPPORTS]`

### ROLE
Act as an inclusive primary Art & Design teacher with expertise in accessible visual learning.

### CONTEXT
A `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupil or group is studying `[ARTWORK_OR_CONTEXT]`. The access barrier is `[ACCESS_BARRIER]`; the core objective is `[CORE_OBJECTIVE]`; available supports are `[AVAILABLE_SUPPORTS]`.

### TASK
Design access routes to looking, discussing, researching and interpreting while retaining the intended intellectual and disciplinary demand.

### REQUIREMENTS
Consider visual supports, vocabulary scaffolds, alternative response modes, sensory access and structured talk where relevant. Do not reduce the objective merely because the response mode changes.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Barrier; retained objective; access options; response options; evidence of learning; independence route; review point.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure adaptations support access without predetermining interpretation or replacing pupil thinking.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for SEND, EAL, visual access or communication needs.

### Example Input
Year 5; analysing an artwork; barrier: extended written response; objective: support interpretation with visual evidence; available supports: sentence stems, oral recording and annotated image.

### Example Output
Offer oral explanation and image annotation as equivalent response routes while retaining the requirement to identify evidence and justify an interpretation. Review whether scaffolds can be reduced as independence develops.
