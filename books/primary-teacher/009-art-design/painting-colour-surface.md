# Painting, Colour & Surface Workflows

## PC-01 — Plan a Colour Exploration

**Prompt Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Painting, Colour, Experimentation

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[THEME]` | `[MEDIA]` | `[TIME]` | `[COLOUR_FOCUS]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary Art & Design teacher specialising in colour and painting.

### CONTEXT
I teach `[YEAR_GROUP]`. Pupils are exploring `[THEME]` using `[MEDIA]` in `[TIME]`. The colour focus is `[COLOUR_FOCUS]`.

### TASK
Design a practical colour investigation in which pupils make, compare and apply colour choices.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Begin with purposeful experimentation rather than a prescribed finished image.
- Include a manageable number of colour variables.
- Require pupils to compare results and explain at least one choice.
- Connect experiments to a subsequent artistic application.
- Allow multiple valid outcomes.
- Account for the properties of the supplied media.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Objective; preparation; demonstration; colour experiments; pupil choices; application; reflection; assessment; adaptations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check age appropriateness, feasibility, media accuracy and genuine pupil choice. Do not imply that one palette is inherently correct.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for KS1, KS2, limited paint colours or a 20-minute session.

### Example Input
Year 3; imagined landscape; tempera paint; 45 minutes; explore contrasting colour choices.

### Example Output
Pupils create two small palette experiments, compare the visual effects and choose one relationship to develop in a landscape. The teacher models mixing and comparison rather than presenting a finished colour scheme.

## PC-02 — Teach Primary, Secondary & Mixed Colours

**Prompt Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Painting, Colour Mixing

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[KNOWN_PUPIL_KNOWLEDGE]` | `[PAINT_MEDIA]` | `[TIME]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary Art & Design teacher with sound knowledge of practical colour mixing.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` have `[KNOWN_PUPIL_KNOWLEDGE]` and will use `[PAINT_MEDIA]` for `[TIME]`.

### TASK
Build a practical investigation of primary, secondary and mixed colours appropriate to the supplied media.

### REQUIREMENTS
Explain the relevant colour model simply. Avoid claiming that all pigments behave identically. Include observation and comparison of actual mixtures.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Learning objective; teacher demonstration; pupil investigation; recording; application; questions; assessment; adaptations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure technical claims are appropriate to the medium and distinguish classroom colour vocabulary from universal claims about pigment behaviour.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for poster paint, tempera, watercolour or digital colour mixing.

### Example Input
Year 2; pupils know red, yellow and blue but mix them randomly; poster paint; 30 minutes.

### Example Output
Model controlled pairwise mixing and have pupils record what actually happens. Treat unexpected mixtures as useful evidence about the materials rather than automatically as pupil error.

## PC-03 — Explore Warm and Cool Colour Effects

**Prompt Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, Painting, Colour, Expression

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[THEME]` | `[MEDIA]` | `[TIME]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary Art & Design teacher.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` are exploring `[THEME]` using `[MEDIA]` for `[TIME]`.

### TASK
Design an investigation into how warm and cool colour choices can alter visual effect.

### REQUIREMENTS
Use comparison rather than fixed emotional rules. Invite pupils to describe what they notice and to justify choices. Acknowledge that colour effects can depend on context and individual interpretation.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Objective; experiments; comparison task; discussion prompts; application; assessment; adaptations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Do not present claims such as “blue always feels calm” as universal psychological facts.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Provide KS1 or KS2 versions and landscape, portrait or abstract variants.

### Example Input
Year 4; two versions of the same imagined scene; paint; 45 minutes.

### Example Output
Pupils make two palette studies, compare the visual effects and explain which choices alter emphasis, atmosphere or contrast for them. The teacher uses “might” and “could” when discussing expressive effects.

## PC-04 — Teach Colour Value and Contrast

**Prompt Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, Painting, Colour, Value, Contrast

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[SUBJECT]` | `[MEDIA]` | `[OBJECTIVE]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary painting teacher.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` will paint `[SUBJECT]` using `[MEDIA]` with the objective `[OBJECTIVE]`.

### TASK
Create a sequence for identifying and manipulating light/dark relationships.

### REQUIREMENTS
Distinguish hue from value. Connect contrast to visibility, emphasis or composition where relevant. Include looking, testing and revising.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Objective; demonstration; guided practice; application; review; assessment; adaptations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Use technically accurate colour terminology and do not equate a large tonal range with artistic quality in every context.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for monochrome studies, limited palettes or digital media.

### Example Input
Year 5; still life; acrylic-style classroom paint; objective: use contrast to emphasise a focal area.

### Example Output
Pupils make small value tests before applying paint. They compare which areas attract attention and adjust contrast intentionally rather than simply making everything as dark as possible.

Act as a primary Art & Design teacher experienced in practical painting processes.

Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` are completing `[PROJECT]` using `[MEDIA]` across `[LESSON_COUNT]`. The drying constraint is `[DRYING_CONSTRAINT]`.

### REQUIREMENTS

## PC-08 — Diagnose Colour-Mixing Problems

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 600–900 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Painting, Colour Mixing, Diagnostic Assessment

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[EXPECTED_RESULT]` | `[ACTUAL_RESULT]` | `[MEDIA]` | `[PROCESS]`

### ROLE
Act as a diagnostic primary Art & Design teacher.

### CONTEXT
A pupil in `[YEAR_GROUP]` expected `[EXPECTED_RESULT]` but produced `[ACTUAL_RESULT]` using `[MEDIA]` and `[PROCESS]`.

### TASK
Identify plausible causes and design a discriminating experiment before recommending reteaching.

### REQUIREMENTS
Consider pigment properties, contamination, ratios, mixing method and pupil conceptual understanding. Separate evidence from hypotheses.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Observed evidence; hypotheses; discriminating test; teaching response; review criterion.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Do not assume pupil misunderstanding when material behaviour could explain the result.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for individual, pair or whole-class diagnosis.

### Example Input
Year 2; expected green; produced brown; poster paint; pupils mixed three colours together in one pot.

### Example Output
First test controlled two-colour mixing using clean tools. If the result changes, contamination or uncontrolled mixing becomes a stronger explanation than lack of colour knowledge. Reteach only the concept the evidence indicates is insecure.

## PC-09 — Critique Painting Without Prescribing Style

**Prompt Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 400–700 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, Painting, Critique, Feedback, Artistic Agency

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[OBJECTIVE]` | `[PUPIL_CHOICES]` | `[NEXT_EXPERIMENT]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary Art & Design teacher and critique facilitator.

### CONTEXT
A `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupil has made `[PUPIL_CHOICES]` while working towards `[OBJECTIVE]`. A possible area for further exploration is `[NEXT_EXPERIMENT]`.

### TASK
Generate specific, constructive critique that focuses on artistic decisions and opens a useful next step.

### REQUIREMENTS
Do not treat resemblance to a teacher exemplar, neatness or conventional style as the sole measure. Include a question that returns decision-making to the pupil.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Evidence; strength/decision; question; next experiment; pupil reflection.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Feedback must be specific, respectful and agency-preserving.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Provide verbal, written or peer-critique versions.

### Example Input
Year 5; pupil deliberately used a restricted palette and visible brush marks; objective: communicate atmosphere.

### Example Output
Identify how the restricted palette and brush marks affect the image, ask what atmosphere the pupil intended, and suggest a small comparison experiment rather than instructing them to “paint it more neatly.”

## PC-10 — Adapt Painting for Inclusion

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Painting, Inclusion, SEND, Assessment

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[TASK]` | `[ACCESS_BARRIER]` | `[CORE_OBJECTIVE]` | `[AVAILABLE_RESOURCES]`

### ROLE
Act as an inclusive primary Art & Design teacher.

### CONTEXT
A `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupil is completing `[TASK]`. The access barrier is `[ACCESS_BARRIER]`; the core objective is `[CORE_OBJECTIVE]`; available resources are `[AVAILABLE_RESOURCES]`.

### TASK
Design proportionate adaptations that remove access barriers while preserving meaningful colour, surface and artistic decisions.

### REQUIREMENTS
Consider tools, scale, positioning, timing, containers, support and alternative response methods. Do not assume a single adaptation is universally appropriate.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Barrier; retained objective; adaptation options; pupil choice; evidence of learning; independence route; review point.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Preserve the artistic construct and agency wherever possible. Ensure adaptations are practical and safe.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for fine-motor, physical, visual, communication or recording barriers.

### Example Input
Year 4; painting; hand fatigue with small brushes; objective: use colour and surface intentionally; larger brushes and palette available.

### Example Output
Offer larger-handled brushes, adjusted working scale and a stable palette position while retaining the pupil's choices about colour and surface. Review whether the support increases independent control rather than replacing it.
