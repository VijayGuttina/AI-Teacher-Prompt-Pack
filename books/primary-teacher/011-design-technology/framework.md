# Primary Design & Technology Framework

## Purpose

This module provides reusable AI-assisted teacher workflows for planning, teaching, assessing and adapting primary Design & Technology. Workflows are organised around authentic classroom jobs and the design process rather than isolated prompt ideas.

## Workflow architecture

The initial release contains 10 workflow families, each with 10 workflows: 100 workflows in total.

## Core principles

1. Preserve the design-and-make construct when adapting tasks.
2. Distinguish designing, making, technical knowledge and evaluation rather than treating them as one generic skill.
3. Keep design decisions connected to the intended user, purpose and design criteria.
4. Do not invent material properties, product specifications, tool capabilities or technical facts.
5. Treat prototypes as testable representations, not automatically as finished products.
6. Use observable evidence when diagnosing pupil difficulties; do not infer motivation, effort or ability without evidence.
7. Preserve pupil agency and allow multiple valid design solutions where the brief permits them.
8. Apply appropriate practical safety guidance for tools, materials, electrical components and food preparation.
9. Adapt access without unnecessarily reducing the intended design, technical or problem-solving demand.
10. Keep generated technical explanations, measurements, calculations and procedural advice accurate and clearly qualified where assumptions matter.

## Standard workflow structure

Every workflow should use the established library structure:

**ROLE → CONTEXT → TASK → REQUIREMENTS → OUTPUT FORMAT → QUALITY CHECKS → OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION**

Inherited global and primary-teacher instructions should not be unnecessarily repeated.

## Primary Design & Technology quality gates

Every generated result should be checked for:

1. Design intent and user/purpose alignment
2. Technical accuracy
3. Age and curriculum appropriateness
4. Practical feasibility
5. Material/tool/equipment assumptions
6. Health and safety
7. Evidence-based diagnosis and assessment
8. Preservation of pupil agency
9. Accessibility and construct-preserving adaptation
10. Clear teacher usability

## Design-process discipline

Where relevant, workflows should make the relationship between these stages explicit:

**research → brief/specification → ideas → planning → prototype/make → test → evaluate → improve**

Not every lesson must contain every stage, but outputs should identify which stage is being addressed.

## Verification model

Structural QA, representative execution testing and targeted spot checks are separate stages. Authorship does not constitute model testing. Subject freeze requires recorded representative execution evidence and risk-based spot checks.
