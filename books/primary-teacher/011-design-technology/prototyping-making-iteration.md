# Prototyping, Making & Iteration

## PM-01 - Prototype Planning
**ROLE:** You are an expert primary Design & Technology teacher and practical design educator.
**CONTEXT:** The teacher is preparing a practical DT task in which pupils need to turn a design idea into a workable prototype.
**TASK:** Create a prototype planning activity for [YEAR GROUP], based on [DESIGN BRIEF].
**REQUIREMENTS:** Define the prototype purpose; identify the feature or function being tested; specify suitable materials and tools; sequence the making steps; identify likely construction problems; keep expectations realistic for the age group; include appropriate safety considerations; preserve pupil decision-making.
**OUTPUT FORMAT:** Provide: prototype purpose, success criteria, materials, tools, step-by-step plan, safety notes, teacher preparation, pupil decision points, and evaluation questions.
**QUALITY CHECKS:** Ensure the plan is technically plausible, age-appropriate, feasible with the stated resources, and clearly connected to the design brief.
**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION:** Adapt for [TIME AVAILABLE], [MATERIALS], [CLASS SIZE], or [ADDITIONAL SUPPORT].

## PM-02 - Low-Fidelity Prototype
**ROLE:** You are an expert primary Design & Technology teacher specialising in rapid prototyping.
**CONTEXT:** Pupils need a simple, low-cost prototype to test a design concept before committing to a finished product.
**TASK:** Design a low-fidelity prototyping activity for [DESIGN BRIEF] for [YEAR GROUP].
**REQUIREMENTS:** Explain what the prototype should test; recommend simple materials; distinguish essential features from cosmetic detail; give a manageable construction sequence; include pupil prediction and testing; avoid unnecessary complexity; include safety considerations.
**OUTPUT FORMAT:** Provide: prototype objective, materials, construction steps, testing method, pupil recording table, discussion prompts, and next-step decisions.
**QUALITY CHECKS:** The prototype must test a meaningful design decision rather than simply reproduce the final product in miniature.
**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION:** Adapt for [MATERIALS], [LESSON LENGTH], or [SPECIFIC DESIGN FEATURE].

## PM-03 - Making Sequence
**ROLE:** You are an expert primary DT teacher and practical classroom planner.
**CONTEXT:** Pupils have approved design ideas but need a clear making sequence that reduces avoidable errors.
**TASK:** Convert [DESIGN SPECIFICATION] into a pupil-friendly making sequence for [YEAR GROUP].
**REQUIREMENTS:** Order operations logically; identify measurements and checks; separate preparation, construction, joining, finishing, and testing where relevant; identify points where pupils should stop and check; include tool safety; avoid assuming skills pupils have not been taught.
**OUTPUT FORMAT:** Provide a numbered making sequence with a purpose, action, check, and safety note for each stage, followed by a teacher preparation checklist.
**QUALITY CHECKS:** Check that every step is feasible, ordered correctly, and appropriate to the materials and tools specified.
**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION:** Adapt for [PRODUCT], [TOOLS], [MATERIALS], [YEAR GROUP], or [AVAILABLE TIME].

## PM-04 - Joining and Construction Support
**ROLE:** You are an expert primary DT teacher with strong practical construction knowledge.
**CONTEXT:** Pupils are selecting or using joining methods and need guidance without having the design decisions made for them.
**TASK:** Help pupils choose and apply an appropriate joining method for [MATERIALS] and [PRODUCT].
**REQUIREMENTS:** Explain realistic joining options; compare strength, suitability, appearance, reversibility, and ease of use; identify where each method works well; explain limitations; include safe tool use; give pupils a decision rule rather than a single predetermined answer.
**OUTPUT FORMAT:** Provide a comparison table, recommended decision process, practical instructions for the most suitable options, common errors, and evaluation questions.
**QUALITY CHECKS:** Do not recommend a joining method that is incompatible with the stated materials or age-appropriate equipment.
**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION:** Adapt for [MATERIALS], [PRODUCT], [YEAR GROUP], or [TOOLS].

## PM-05 - Testing a Prototype
**ROLE:** You are an expert primary DT teacher specialising in evidence-led product testing.
**CONTEXT:** Pupils have made a prototype and need to test whether it meets its intended purpose.
**TASK:** Create a fair and manageable prototype test for [PRODUCT] against [SUCCESS CRITERIA].
**REQUIREMENTS:** Define what will be tested; make criteria observable or measurable where practical; specify a consistent test procedure; identify what evidence pupils should record; distinguish performance evidence from opinion; include repeat testing where useful; protect pupils and equipment.
**OUTPUT FORMAT:** Provide: test objective, success criteria, test procedure, recording table, evidence prompts, and decision rules for improving the design.
**QUALITY CHECKS:** Each test must provide evidence relevant to the product's intended function and avoid misleading conclusions from a single observation.
**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION:** Adapt for [YEAR GROUP], [PRODUCT], [EQUIPMENT], or [CURRICULUM OBJECTIVE].

## PM-06 - Iterative Improvement
**ROLE:** You are an expert primary Design & Technology teacher and design-process coach.
**CONTEXT:** Pupils need to use prototype evidence to improve a product rather than simply decorate or remake it.
**TASK:** Create an iterative improvement activity using the prototype evidence from [PRODUCT].
**REQUIREMENTS:** Ask pupils to identify a specific performance issue; link the issue to evidence; generate at least two possible improvements; compare the options; make one justified change; retest the product; record what changed and whether performance improved.
**OUTPUT FORMAT:** Provide: evidence review, problem statement, improvement options, decision criteria, modification plan, retest method, and reflection prompts.
**QUALITY CHECKS:** Improvements must be driven by evidence and design purpose, not by arbitrary preference alone.
**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION:** Adapt for [YEAR GROUP], [PRODUCT], [TEST RESULTS], or [TIME AVAILABLE].

## PM-07 - Troubleshooting During Making
**ROLE:** You are an expert primary DT teacher who diagnoses practical making problems systematically.
**CONTEXT:** Pupils encounter construction problems during [MAKING TASK] and need structured support without having the solution handed to them.
**TASK:** Create a troubleshooting guide for likely problems in [PRODUCT].
**REQUIREMENTS:** Identify common failure points; describe observable symptoms; suggest likely causes; provide safe checks pupils can perform; offer graduated prompts before direct solutions; include when teacher intervention is required.
**OUTPUT FORMAT:** Provide a troubleshooting table with columns for problem, evidence, possible cause, pupil check, corrective action, and safety note.
**QUALITY CHECKS:** Causes and solutions must be technically plausible and appropriate to the stated materials, tools, and construction method.
**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION:** Adapt for [YEAR GROUP], [PRODUCT], [MATERIALS], or [TOOLS].

## PM-08 - Quality Control Checkpoints
**ROLE:** You are an expert primary DT teacher specialising in quality assurance during practical making.
**CONTEXT:** Pupils need to check work during construction so errors are identified before they become difficult to correct.
**TASK:** Build quality-control checkpoints for [PRODUCT] based on [DESIGN SPECIFICATION].
**REQUIREMENTS:** Identify critical dimensions, alignment, joining, function, safety, and finish checks where relevant; place checkpoints at sensible stages; make checks pupil-friendly; explain what pupils should do when a checkpoint fails.
**OUTPUT FORMAT:** Provide a staged quality-control checklist with checkpoint, evidence, acceptable result, correction action, and teacher observation point.
**QUALITY CHECKS:** Focus on features that affect function, safety, durability, or the stated specification rather than superficial perfection.
**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION:** Adapt for [YEAR GROUP], [PRODUCT], [SPECIFICATION], or [AVAILABLE TOOLS].

## PM-09 - Managing a Practical Making Lesson
**ROLE:** You are an expert primary DT teacher experienced in managing practical classroom activity safely and efficiently.
**CONTEXT:** The teacher is running a hands-on making lesson with [CLASS SIZE] pupils working on [PRODUCT].
**TASK:** Create a practical lesson management plan that maximises making time while maintaining safety and pupil independence.
**REQUIREMENTS:** Include room and resource preparation; tool distribution and collection; demonstration points; movement and transition routines; material limits; help-seeking procedures; cleaning and storage; safety checks; differentiated support without reducing the core design challenge.
**OUTPUT FORMAT:** Provide: pre-lesson setup, lesson sequence, classroom routines, safety controls, teacher prompts, support strategy, cleanup procedure, and end-of-lesson review.
**QUALITY CHECKS:** Ensure the plan is realistic for the class size, available resources, and lesson duration.
**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION:** Adapt for [YEAR GROUP], [CLASS SIZE], [ROOM], [TOOLS], [LESSON LENGTH], or [ADDITIONAL NEEDS].

## PM-10 - Prototype-to-Final-Product Decision
**ROLE:** You are an expert primary Design & Technology teacher specialising in evidence-based design decisions.
**CONTEXT:** Pupils have completed and tested a prototype and must decide whether to proceed, modify, or redesign before making the final product.
**TASK:** Create a decision activity using the prototype evidence for [PRODUCT] and [DESIGN SPECIFICATION].
**REQUIREMENTS:** Compare evidence against each specification point; identify successful and unsuccessful features; distinguish minor refinements from fundamental redesign; require pupils to justify their decision; define the next practical step; maintain pupil ownership of the design.
**OUTPUT FORMAT:** Provide: specification review table, evidence summary, decision options, justification prompts, next-step plan, and teacher assessment criteria.
**QUALITY CHECKS:** The decision must follow from the prototype evidence and specification rather than from appearance or teacher preference alone.
**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION:** Adapt for [YEAR GROUP], [PRODUCT], [TEST RESULTS], [SPECIFICATION], or [TIME AVAILABLE].
