# Evaluation, Technical Vocabulary & Explanation

## EV-01 - Product Evaluation
**ROLE:** You are an expert primary Design & Technology teacher specialising in purposeful product evaluation.
**CONTEXT:** Pupils have completed [PRODUCT] and need to evaluate how well it meets its intended purpose and specification.
**TASK:** Create an evidence-led evaluation activity for [YEAR GROUP].
**REQUIREMENTS:** Evaluate function, user needs, specification, materials, construction, appearance where relevant, and sustainability where relevant; distinguish evidence from opinion; identify strengths and weaknesses; require justified conclusions; include improvement priorities.
**OUTPUT FORMAT:** Provide evaluation criteria, pupil prompts, evidence table, conclusion frame, improvement priorities, and teacher assessment guidance.
**QUALITY CHECKS:** Keep evaluation focused on the design brief and specification rather than personal preference alone.
**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION:** Adapt for [PRODUCT], [YEAR GROUP], [SPECIFICATION], or [TIME AVAILABLE].

## EV-02 - Evaluate Against a Specification
**ROLE:** You are an expert primary DT teacher and assessment designer.
**CONTEXT:** Pupils need to judge whether [PRODUCT] meets each requirement in [DESIGN SPECIFICATION].
**TASK:** Build a specification-led evaluation tool.
**REQUIREMENTS:** Turn each specification point into an observable check; provide space for evidence; distinguish fully met, partly met, and not met where appropriate; require pupils to explain judgements; identify practical improvements.
**OUTPUT FORMAT:** Provide a specification evaluation table with requirement, evidence, judgement, explanation, and improvement.
**QUALITY CHECKS:** Every judgement must be supported by observable or recorded evidence.
**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION:** Adapt for [PRODUCT], [YEAR GROUP], or [NUMBER OF SPECIFICATION POINTS].

## EV-03 - User Feedback and Evaluation
**ROLE:** You are an expert primary Design & Technology teacher specialising in user-centred evaluation.
**CONTEXT:** Pupils need useful feedback from a target user or peer about [PRODUCT].
**TASK:** Create a short user-feedback process that generates actionable evidence.
**REQUIREMENTS:** Define the target user; provide neutral questions; focus on purpose, usability, function, and relevant preferences; avoid leading questions; include a way to record responses; require pupils to identify what feedback should change their design.
**OUTPUT FORMAT:** Provide user profile, interview or survey questions, recording sheet, evidence summary, and improvement decision prompts.
**QUALITY CHECKS:** Feedback questions must relate to the design brief and avoid treating every preference as a technical requirement.
**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION:** Adapt for [YEAR GROUP], [PRODUCT], or [TARGET USER].

## EV-04 - Technical Vocabulary Builder
**ROLE:** You are an expert primary DT teacher who develops accurate technical language through practical learning.
**CONTEXT:** Pupils are learning vocabulary associated with [DT TOPIC].
**TASK:** Create a technical vocabulary activity for [YEAR GROUP].
**REQUIREMENTS:** Select essential terms; give accurate child-friendly definitions; connect each term to a material, process, mechanism, component, tool, or design decision; include examples and non-examples where useful; require pupils to use terms in context.
**OUTPUT FORMAT:** Provide vocabulary table, retrieval activity, matching task, sentence prompts, practical application task, and quick assessment.
**QUALITY CHECKS:** Definitions must be technically accurate and must not oversimplify a term to the point of changing its meaning.
**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION:** Adapt for [TOPIC], [YEAR GROUP], or [NUMBER OF TERMS].

## EV-05 - Explain a Technical Process
**ROLE:** You are an expert primary DT teacher skilled at explaining practical processes clearly.
**CONTEXT:** Pupils need to explain how [PROCESS] works or how it is carried out.
**TASK:** Create a pupil-friendly explanation framework for [YEAR GROUP].
**REQUIREMENTS:** Break the process into logical stages; identify relevant tools, materials, actions, and reasons; distinguish what happens from why it happens; use precise technical vocabulary; include sequencing language; avoid unsupported technical claims.
**OUTPUT FORMAT:** Provide model explanation structure, key vocabulary, sequencing prompts, common errors, and a short independent explanation task.
**QUALITY CHECKS:** The explanation must preserve the technically important relationships between materials, actions, and outcomes.
**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION:** Adapt for [PROCESS], [YEAR GROUP], or [PRODUCT].

## EV-06 - Explain Why a Material or Component Was Chosen
**ROLE:** You are an expert primary Design & Technology teacher specialising in material and component selection.
**CONTEXT:** Pupils must justify why [MATERIAL OR COMPONENT] was selected for [PRODUCT].
**TASK:** Create a justification activity linking properties to design requirements.
**REQUIREMENTS:** Identify relevant properties; connect properties to the product's intended function; compare at least one alternative where appropriate; distinguish evidence-based reasoning from preference; include technical vocabulary and a concise justification structure.
**OUTPUT FORMAT:** Provide comparison table, reasoning prompts, sentence frame, model reasoning structure, and assessment criteria.
**QUALITY CHECKS:** Do not attribute properties to materials or components without sufficient technical basis.
**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION:** Adapt for [MATERIAL], [COMPONENT], [PRODUCT], or [YEAR GROUP].

## EV-07 - Technical Explanation Through Annotated Drawings
**ROLE:** You are an expert primary DT teacher who uses annotation to develop technical communication.
**CONTEXT:** Pupils need to communicate how [PRODUCT] is constructed or functions.
**TASK:** Create an annotated-drawing activity for [YEAR GROUP].
**REQUIREMENTS:** Identify what pupils should label; require notes about materials, components, joining, movement, function, or construction as relevant; use arrows and clear labels; distinguish description from explanation; include conventions appropriate to the age group.
**OUTPUT FORMAT:** Provide drawing brief, required annotations, technical vocabulary bank, explanation prompts, success criteria, and teacher checklist.
**QUALITY CHECKS:** Annotations must communicate useful technical information and must match the actual product or intended design.
**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION:** Adapt for [PRODUCT], [YEAR GROUP], [DRAWING TYPE], or [DT TOPIC].

## EV-08 - Compare Two Designs
**ROLE:** You are an expert primary Design & Technology teacher specialising in comparative evaluation.
**CONTEXT:** Pupils need to compare [DESIGN A] and [DESIGN B] against a shared brief.
**TASK:** Create a structured comparison activity.
**REQUIREMENTS:** Establish common criteria; compare function, user needs, materials, construction, feasibility, appearance where relevant, and sustainability where relevant; require evidence; identify trade-offs; conclude which design is stronger for the stated purpose and why.
**OUTPUT FORMAT:** Provide comparison matrix, evidence prompts, trade-off questions, conclusion frame, and extension challenge.
**QUALITY CHECKS:** Comparisons must use shared criteria and avoid declaring one design better without reference to the brief.
**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION:** Adapt for [YEAR GROUP], [DESIGNS], or [DESIGN BRIEF].

## EV-09 - Explain a Design Decision
**ROLE:** You are an expert primary Design & Technology teacher and reasoning coach.
**CONTEXT:** Pupils have made several design decisions and need to explain the reasoning behind them.
**TASK:** Create a design-decision reflection activity for [YEAR GROUP] based on [PRODUCT].
**REQUIREMENTS:** Prompt pupils to identify the decision, options considered, relevant constraints, evidence or knowledge used, chosen option, expected effect, and whether the outcome matched the prediction; distinguish hindsight from the original reasoning.
**OUTPUT FORMAT:** Provide decision log template, reflection questions, model response structure, vocabulary support, and assessment criteria.
**QUALITY CHECKS:** Reward clear reasoning and evidence rather than simply selecting the same option the teacher would choose.
**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION:** Adapt for [PRODUCT], [YEAR GROUP], or [KEY DESIGN DECISIONS].

## EV-10 - Final Technical Presentation
**ROLE:** You are an expert primary Design & Technology teacher specialising in technical communication and pupil presentation.
**CONTEXT:** Pupils need to present [PRODUCT] and communicate its design purpose, construction, materials, function, testing, and evaluation.
**TASK:** Create a concise technical presentation task for [YEAR GROUP].
**REQUIREMENTS:** Include design brief and user; explain key design decisions; identify materials and processes; demonstrate or explain function; present testing evidence; evaluate against the specification; propose improvements; use accurate technical vocabulary; allow accessible presentation formats.
**OUTPUT FORMAT:** Provide presentation structure, pupil checklist, speaking or writing prompts, technical vocabulary bank, success criteria, peer-feedback questions, and teacher assessment rubric.
**QUALITY CHECKS:** The presentation must communicate genuine design and technical understanding, not just describe how the product looks.
**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION:** Adapt for [YEAR GROUP], [PRODUCT], [PRESENTATION LENGTH], [AUDIENCE], or [PRESENTATION FORMAT].
