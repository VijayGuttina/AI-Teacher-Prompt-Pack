# Worship, Practice & Lived Religion

This family provides ten copy/paste-ready prompts for exploring worship, religious practice, observance and lived religion in primary Religious Education. The prompts prioritise accurate description, internal diversity, context and respectful enquiry. They do not ask pupils to disclose personal beliefs, pretend to be religious believers, or simulate sacred practices inappropriately.

## WP-01 Plan a Worship and Practice Enquiry Lesson

### Metadata
- **Prompt ID:** WP-01
- **Difficulty:** Intermediate
- **AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
- **Expected output length:** 1,000–1,500 words
- **Typical generation time:** 30–60 seconds
- **Curriculum tags:** RE, KS1, KS2, worship, practice, observance, lived religion, enquiry

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary Religious Education curriculum specialist with expertise in religious practice, lived religion, religious literacy, enquiry pedagogy and inclusive classroom practice.

### CONTEXT
I am planning an RE lesson about worship, religious practice or observance.

Use:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Tradition/worldview: [TRADITION OR WORLDVIEW]
- Practice or form of worship: [PRACTICE]
- Enquiry question or theme: [THEME]
- Prior learning: [PRIOR LEARNING]
- Agreed syllabus/curriculum reference: [REFERENCE OR NOT PROVIDED]
- Lesson length: [MINUTES]
- Resources: [RESOURCES]
- School-specific requirements: [REQUIREMENTS OR NOT PROVIDED]

### TASK
Create a complete enquiry lesson that helps pupils understand what the practice is, why it may matter, how it is experienced and how practice can vary between people and communities.

### REQUIREMENTS
1. Begin with a clear enquiry question.
2. Define the practice accurately and distinguish worship, observance, ritual, custom and everyday lived religion where relevant.
3. Explain the purpose or significance of the practice without implying that every believer understands it identically.
4. Include internal diversity where it materially affects practice.
5. Distinguish descriptive claims from interpretations and participant perspectives.
6. Do not invent quotations, prayers, rituals, community views or theological explanations.
7. Do not require pupils to perform, imitate or role-play a sacred practice.
8. Do not ask pupils to disclose their own religion, beliefs or family practice.
9. Include appropriate use of images, artefacts or first-person accounts only when their provenance is clear.
10. Include evidence-based questioning and observable assessment.
11. Identify sensitive content and give a respectful alternative route where needed.
12. Flag local school procedures for visitors, artefacts, places of worship or off-site activities rather than inventing them.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Produce:
1. Lesson overview
2. Enquiry question
3. Key knowledge
4. Accurate practice/context briefing
5. Lesson sequence with timings
6. Teacher explanation
7. Evidence and perspective activity
8. Inclusion and adaptation
9. Misconceptions
10. Formative assessment
11. Exit task
12. Teacher quality-control checklist

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check accuracy, internal diversity, respectful language, source provenance, avoidance of stereotypes, avoidance of personal disclosure, appropriate handling of sacred practice and observable assessment.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for [KS1 / LOWER KS2 / UPPER KS2 / EAL / SEND / LIMITED RESOURCES / VISUAL LEARNERS].

### Example Input
Year 3, Sikhism, langar, 60-minute lesson, focus on how a shared practice can express values and community.

### Example Output Excerpt
Pupils investigate what langar is using identified evidence and then explain how the practice may communicate ideas about equality, service and community. The lesson does not ask pupils to reproduce the practice or disclose whether their own families participate in anything similar.

## WP-02 Explain a Religious Practice Accurately and Accessibly

- **AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
- **Expected output length:** 900–1,300 words
- **Typical generation time:** 30–60 seconds
- **Curriculum tags:** RE, KS1, KS2, religious practice, explanation, vocabulary, religious literacy

### ROLE
You are a religious-literacy specialist creating accurate, child-friendly explanations of religious practices.

### CONTEXT
Explain:
- Tradition/worldview: [TRADITION]
- Practice: [PRACTICE]
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Purpose of teaching it: [PURPOSE]
- Supplied evidence: [EVIDENCE]

### TASK
Create a clear explanation that enables pupils to understand what happens, relevant terminology, possible significance and variation in practice.

### REQUIREMENTS
1. State what the practice is before explaining why it may matter.
2. Explain key vocabulary accurately.
3. Distinguish observable practice from theological interpretation.
4. Represent variation between communities and individuals where relevant.
5. Do not claim that all members of the tradition practise it in the same way.
6. Do not invent participant quotations or explanations.
7. Distinguish religion from ethnicity, nationality and culture.
8. Avoid stereotypes and tokenistic language.
9. Do not require pupils to imitate the practice.
10. Identify any claims that require verification.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Teacher explanation
2. Pupil-friendly explanation
3. Vocabulary table
4. What pupils can observe
5. Why it may matter
6. Variation and diversity note
7. Common misconceptions
8. Three evidence-based questions
9. Verification notes

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check accuracy, neutrality, internal diversity, age suitability, terminology and distinction between description and interpretation.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Create versions for [YEAR 1 / YEAR 2 / YEAR 4 / YEAR 6].

### Example Input
Year 4, Judaism, Shabbat, focus on understanding the practice and why it can have significance.

### Example Output Excerpt
The explanation first describes selected Shabbat practices, then explains that their meaning can be understood through Jewish teachings and lived experience. It explicitly avoids suggesting that every Jewish household observes Shabbat in exactly the same way.

### ROLE
You are a primary RE specialist skilled in using first-person perspectives without turning one individual into a representative of an entire religion.

### CONTEXT
I have [SUPPLIED FIRST-PERSON ACCOUNT / INTERVIEW / VIDEO TRANSCRIPT / QUOTE] about:
- Tradition/worldview: [TRADITION]
- Practice: [PRACTICE]
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Enquiry focus: [FOCUS]

### TASK
Design an enquiry using the account as evidence of one lived experience while helping pupils understand the difference between an individual perspective and a wider religious claim.

### REQUIREMENTS
1. Preserve the supplied account accurately.
2. Do not fabricate quotations or fill gaps in the speaker's account.
3. Identify what the person says about their own experience.
4. Separate first-person testimony from broader claims about the tradition.
5. Include questions about what the account can and cannot tell us.
6. Introduce internal diversity where appropriate.
7. Avoid asking pupils to compare the account with their own religious identity or family practice.
8. Include respectful discussion structures.
9. Include a source-evaluation task.
10. Provide observable assessment.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Produce:
1. Source introduction
2. Context note
3. Perspective-analysis routine
4. Pupil questions
5. “One person versus wider tradition” activity
6. Inclusion guidance
7. Misconceptions
8. Assessment criteria
9. Reflection task

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check provenance, quotation accuracy, representativeness, respectful language and evidence use.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for [INTERVIEW TRANSCRIPT / WRITTEN TESTIMONY / VIDEO / VISITOR ACCOUNT].

### Example Input
Year 6, Muslim first-person account about Ramadan, focus on how lived experience can vary while remaining connected to religious teachings.

### Example Output Excerpt
Pupils identify statements that describe the speaker's own experience and separately identify statements that make broader claims. They then discuss what additional evidence would be needed before making a claim about Muslims more generally.

## WP-04 Compare Religious Practices Without Stereotyping

### Metadata
- **Prompt ID:** WP-04
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
- **Expected output length:** 1,100–1,500 words
- **Typical generation time:** 30–60 seconds
- **Curriculum tags:** RE, KS2, comparison, worship, practice, diversity, religious literacy

### ROLE
You are an expert primary RE teacher specialising in fair comparison of religious and worldview practices.

### CONTEXT
Compare:
- Tradition/worldview A: [A]
- Practice A: [PRACTICE A]
- Tradition/worldview B: [B]
- Practice B: [PRACTICE B]
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Enquiry focus: [FOCUS]
- Evidence available: [EVIDENCE]

### TASK
Create a balanced comparison that identifies meaningful similarities and differences without implying that the practices are equivalent or that either tradition is internally uniform.

### REQUIREMENTS
1. Compare like with like where possible.
2. Explain context before comparison.
3. Distinguish outward similarity from underlying meaning.
4. Represent internal diversity.
5. Do not invent practices or participant explanations.
6. Avoid “religion A does X, religion B does Y” oversimplification where evidence shows variation.
7. Include questions about purpose, setting, participants and lived experience.
8. Do not ask pupils which practice they prefer because of their personal beliefs.
9. Include an evidence-based comparison table.
10. Assess accurate comparison and explanation.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Context for each practice
2. Comparison matrix
3. Guided enquiry
4. Similarity questions
5. Difference questions
6. Meaning/context questions
7. Misconceptions
8. Assessment rubric

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check fairness, proportionality, internal diversity, terminology and evidence.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for [LOWER KS2 / UPPER KS2 / WRITTEN COMPARISON / ORAL DISCUSSION].

### Example Input
Year 5, Christian Eucharist and Jewish Shabbat, focus on how practices can express ideas about community and relationship with God.

### Example Output Excerpt
The comparison identifies specific observable similarities and differences but avoids saying that one practice is simply the equivalent of the other. Pupils use supplied evidence to explain why each practice has its own context and significance.

- Tradition/worldview: [TRADITION]
- Practice: [PRACTICE]
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Pupil statements/work: [RESPONSES]

### TASK
Identify misconceptions and design targeted corrective teaching.

### REQUIREMENTS

3. Identify confusion between religious teaching and individual practice.
4. Identify confusion between religion, culture, ethnicity and nationality.
5. Use evidence-led corrective explanations.

2. Misconception category
3. Explanation
4. Corrective teaching
5. Re-check question
6. Extension task

Use [QUIZ DATA / BOOK WORK / ORAL RESPONSES / EXIT TICKETS].

### Example Input

### Example Output Excerpt
The intervention corrects both the frequency misconception and the overgeneralisation. Pupils learn what Ramadan is, what fasting can involve, and why individual circumstances and religious exemptions matter without being asked about their own families.

13. Include practical alternatives for pupils unable to participate in a proposed visit or activity.
