# Beliefs, Teachings & Concepts

This family provides ten copy/paste-ready workflows for teaching and planning substantive RE knowledge about beliefs, teachings and concepts. Each workflow is designed to help pupils move beyond isolated facts towards accurate explanation, connection, interpretation and justified understanding while avoiding overgeneralisation.

---

## BT-01 | Build a Knowledge-Rich Beliefs and Teachings Lesson

**Prompt ID:** PT-RE-BT-01  
**Family:** Beliefs, Teachings & Concepts  
**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 900 to 1,300 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 30 to 60 seconds  
**Curriculum Tags:** RE, beliefs, teachings, concepts, substantive knowledge, primary, lesson planning

### ROLE
You are an expert primary Religious Education teacher and curriculum designer. Your specialist focus is accurate substantive knowledge about religious and non-religious worldviews, taught through enquiry and age-appropriate explanation.

### CONTEXT
Year group: **[YEAR GROUP]**  
Tradition/worldview: **[RELIGION / WORLDVIEW]**  
Belief, teaching or concept: **[TOPIC]**  
Unit context: **[UNIT]**  
Prior knowledge: **[PRIOR KNOWLEDGE]**  
Curriculum requirement: **[REQUIREMENT, IF KNOWN]**  
Lesson length: **[MINUTES]**

### TASK
Create a complete lesson that teaches the selected belief, teaching or concept accurately and helps pupils explain its significance using appropriate evidence and examples.

### REQUIREMENTS
1. Define the key belief, teaching or concept in language suitable for the year group.
2. Identify essential substantive knowledge.
3. Explain relevant vocabulary precisely.
4. Distinguish belief, teaching, interpretation, practice and lived experience.
5. Explain why the concept matters within the relevant tradition or worldview without claiming that every follower understands or applies it identically.
6. Include appropriate evidence, such as a supplied source, teaching, story, example or practice.
7. Include a retrieval activity.
8. Include a misconception check.
9. Include questions that progress from identification to explanation and reasoning.
10. Include an independent or paired task that produces observable learning.
11. Include a model answer that is accurate but does not imply internal uniformity.
12. Avoid asking pupils to disclose personal beliefs.
13. Avoid requiring pupils to role-play believers or simulate sacred practice.
14. Flag claims that require source verification.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Use:
1. Lesson overview
2. Key belief or concept
3. Essential knowledge
4. Vocabulary
5. Enquiry question
6. Lesson sequence with timings
7. Teacher questions
8. Pupil task
9. Misconceptions
10. Assessment evidence
11. Inclusion and adaptation
12. Model response
13. Source verification notes
14. Quality assurance check

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check accuracy, age suitability, terminology, internal diversity, distinction between belief and practice, evidence use and neutrality. Do not present a single example as universal.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add **[SUPPLIED SOURCE / KNOWLEDGE ORGANISER / AGREED SYLLABUS]** and treat it as the primary evidence base.

### EXAMPLE INPUT
Year 4, Christianity, forgiveness, pupils know the word but tend to define it only as "not being angry".

### EXAMPLE OUTPUT
The lesson would establish forgiveness as a concept, examine an appropriate Christian teaching or source, distinguish forgiveness from simply forgetting an event, and ask pupils to explain how the teaching may influence Christian thinking without claiming all Christians respond identically.

## BT-07 | Create a Belief and Teaching Misconception Diagnostic

### ROLE
You are a primary RE assessment specialist.

### CONTEXT
Year group: **[YEAR GROUP]**  
Tradition/worldview: **[TRADITION / WORLDVIEW]**  
Topic: **[BELIEF / TEACHING / CONCEPT]**  
Known pupil errors: **[ERRORS, IF KNOWN]**

### TASK
Create a diagnostic assessment that identifies whether pupils understand the belief or concept accurately and can distinguish it from common stereotypes or overgeneralisations.

### REQUIREMENTS
1. Produce 10 to 15 diagnostic items.
2. Mix multiple-choice, classification, short explanation and evidence-based questions.
3. Include plausible misconceptions rather than trivial distractors.
4. Provide answers and diagnostic interpretations.
5. Include a distinction between knowledge errors, vocabulary errors and reasoning errors.

8. Avoid asking pupils to identify themselves with a religion or worldview.

Use a table with:
- Item
- Expected answer
- Misconception revealed
- Teacher response

Then provide intervention groups and a short reteaching sequence.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure distractors are plausible but not misleading, and that assessment targets taught knowledge rather than assumptions about pupils' backgrounds.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add **[PUPIL RESPONSES]** to diagnose actual errors rather than predicted ones.

### EXAMPLE INPUT
Year 5, Hindu traditions, karma, with pupils sometimes interpreting karma as "good things always happen to good people".

### EXAMPLE OUTPUT
The diagnostic would distinguish the simplified classroom misconception from a more careful explanation and identify which pupils need vocabulary, conceptual or reasoning support.
