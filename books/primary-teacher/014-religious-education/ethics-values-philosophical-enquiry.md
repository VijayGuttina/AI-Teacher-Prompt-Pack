# Ethics, Values & Philosophical Enquiry

This family provides ten copy/paste-ready prompts for ethical questions, values and philosophical enquiry in primary Religious Education. The prompts develop reasoning, explanation, evidence use, listening and reflection while maintaining a clear distinction between religious/worldview perspectives and pupils' personal identities. They do not assume that every religion or worldview gives one identical answer to an ethical question.

## EV-01 Plan an Ethical and Philosophical Enquiry Lesson

### Metadata
- **Prompt ID:** EV-01
- **Difficulty:** Advanced
- **AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
- **Expected output length:** 1,100–1,600 words
- **Typical generation time:** 30–60 seconds
- **Curriculum tags:** RE, KS1, KS2, ethics, values, philosophy, enquiry, reasoning

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary Religious Education curriculum specialist with expertise in ethical reasoning, philosophical enquiry, religious literacy and inclusive classroom discussion.

### CONTEXT
Plan an enquiry about:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Ethical/philosophical question: [QUESTION]
- Relevant religion(s)/worldview(s): [TRADITIONS/WORLDVIEWS]
- Prior learning: [PRIOR LEARNING]
- Sources/evidence: [EVIDENCE]
- Agreed syllabus/curriculum reference: [REFERENCE OR NOT PROVIDED]
- Lesson length: [MINUTES]
- School-specific requirements: [REQUIREMENTS OR NOT PROVIDED]

### TASK
Create a complete lesson in which pupils encounter relevant perspectives, identify reasons and evidence, consider alternatives and formulate a justified response.

### REQUIREMENTS
1. Frame a genuine enquiry rather than a question with a predetermined teacher answer.
2. Identify the substantive religious/worldview knowledge pupils need.
3. Distinguish factual claims, value judgements, reasons, evidence and conclusions.
4. Represent relevant religious and non-religious perspectives accurately.
5. Acknowledge internal diversity where it materially affects the question.
6. Do not invent quotations, beliefs, community positions or philosophical arguments.
7. Do not imply that one religion has one universally accepted ethical answer unless evidence establishes that.
8. Do not require pupils to reveal personal beliefs or family circumstances.
9. Use hypothetical or supplied scenarios when personal disclosure could create pressure.
10. Include structured dialogue that values disagreement without treating every claim as equally well supported.
11. Include observable assessment of reasoning and explanation.
12. Flag sensitive or contested issues and identify appropriate teacher judgement.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Produce:
1. Lesson overview
2. Enquiry question
3. Knowledge prerequisites
4. Key vocabulary
5. Perspective/evidence briefing
6. Lesson sequence with timings
7. Teacher modelling of reasoning
8. Pupil philosophical enquiry
9. Discussion structures
10. Differentiation and inclusion
11. Misconceptions
12. Formative assessment
13. Exit task and success criteria
14. Teacher quality-control checklist

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that the lesson is enquiry-led, religiously literate, balanced, evidence-based, age-appropriate, inclusive and free from pressure to disclose personal beliefs.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for [KS1 / LOWER KS2 / UPPER KS2 / EAL / SEND / ORAL-HEAVY / WRITING-HEAVY].

### Example Input
Year 5, stewardship and care for the environment, Christianity and Sikhism plus a non-religious perspective, 60 minutes.

### Example Output Excerpt
Pupils examine identified perspectives and then distinguish “a reason given by this perspective” from “my own conclusion”. They justify a response using evidence rather than being assessed on whether they agree with a religious teaching.

## EV-02 Explain How a Religious or Worldview Perspective Supports an Ethical Position

- **Expected output length:** 1,000–1,400 words
- **Typical generation time:** 30–60 seconds
- **Curriculum tags:** RE, KS2, ethics, beliefs, values, reasoning, explanation

### ROLE
You are a religious-literacy specialist explaining ethical perspectives accurately and without overgeneralisation.

### CONTEXT
Explain the reasoning behind:
- Religion/worldview: [TRADITION/WORLDVIEW]
- Ethical position: [POSITION]
- Question: [QUESTION]
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Evidence supplied: [EVIDENCE]

### TASK
Create a pupil-accessible explanation showing how a perspective can connect beliefs, teachings, values and ethical conclusions.

### REQUIREMENTS
1. Start with the ethical question.
2. Identify relevant beliefs, teachings, values or principles.
3. Show the reasoning chain explicitly.
4. Distinguish what the source or identified perspective supports from an interpretation added by the teacher.
5. Represent internal diversity where appropriate.
6. Do not claim that every member of a tradition reaches the same conclusion.
7. Do not invent quotations or religious authorities.
8. Explain uncertainty or disagreement where relevant.
9. Include a non-religious perspective if supplied or pedagogically relevant.
10. Avoid asking pupils whether they personally hold the position.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Teacher explanation
2. Pupil explanation
3. Reasoning chain
4. Evidence table
5. Vocabulary
6. Internal diversity note
7. Misconceptions
8. Enquiry questions
9. Assessment task

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check factual accuracy, evidence traceability, internal diversity and separation of perspective from personal opinion.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Create versions for [YEAR 3 / YEAR 4 / YEAR 5 / YEAR 6].

### Example Input
Year 4, Christianity, forgiveness, supplied Gospel teaching, focus on how a teaching can inform an ethical response.

### Example Output Excerpt
The explanation links the supplied teaching to forgiveness as an ethical value but makes clear that the exact application to a modern scenario is an interpretive step, not a direct quotation from the source.

### CONTEXT

- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Ethical question: [QUESTION]
- Relevant perspectives: [PERSPECTIVES]
- Pupil responses: [RESPONSES]
- Intended learning: [INTENDED LEARNING]

### TASK
Identify misconceptions in pupils' ethical reasoning and design targeted intervention.

### REQUIREMENTS
1. Distinguish factual RE misconceptions from reasoning errors.
2. Identify unsupported generalisations.
3. Identify false equivalence and false dichotomies.
4. Identify conclusions that lack evidence or reasons.
5. Identify confusion between a person's practice and a tradition's teaching.
6. Identify misunderstandings of philosophical terms.
7. Do not treat a pupil's personal belief as a misconception to be corrected.
8. Provide a targeted corrective activity for each error.
9. Include a re-check question.
10. Assess improved reasoning using observable criteria.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

1. Diagnostic table
2. Error classification
3. Explanation
4. Corrective teaching
5. Re-check question
6. Improved-response model
7. Assessment criteria

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that interventions address the reasoning problem precisely and do not impose teacher-preferred ethical conclusions.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Use [BOOK WORK / DISCUSSION / WRITTEN ARGUMENT / QUIZ / EXIT TICKET] evidence.

### Example Input
A pupil writes, “People who believe X must always think Y.”

### Example Output Excerpt
The intervention teaches pupils to distinguish “some identified perspectives argue…” from “everyone who belongs to this religion believes…”. The pupil then revises the statement using appropriate qualification and evidence.
