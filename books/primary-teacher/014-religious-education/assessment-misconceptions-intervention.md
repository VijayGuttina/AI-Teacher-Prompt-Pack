# Assessment, Misconceptions & Intervention

This family provides teacher-ready workflows for assessing RE knowledge, identifying misconceptions and planning proportionate intervention. Assessment should focus on observable RE learning such as knowledge, vocabulary, source interpretation, explanation, comparison, reasoning and respectful dialogue. It must not assess personal faith, family practice, religious identity, willingness to identify with a worldview or conformity with a teacher-preferred conclusion.

## AM-01 Plan an RE Assessment Lesson

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 900 to 1,300 words  
**Typical generation time:** 30 to 60 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** RE, assessment, primary, KS1, KS2, knowledge, explanation

### ROLE

Act as an experienced primary RE subject leader and assessment specialist. Design assessment that captures genuine progress in religious and worldview knowledge without assessing pupils' personal beliefs or identities.

### CONTEXT

I am assessing **[YEAR GROUP]** after learning about **[UNIT/TOPIC]**. The relevant traditions/worldviews are **[TRADITIONS/WORLDVIEWS]**. The intended knowledge and skills are **[LEARNING OUTCOMES]**. Key vocabulary is **[TERMS]**. Sources used were **[SOURCES]**. Previous assessment evidence is **[EVIDENCE]**.

### TASK

Create a complete assessment lesson that reveals what pupils know, understand, can explain and can support with evidence, then converts the evidence into actionable next teaching.

### REQUIREMENTS

1. Assess substantive RE knowledge and disciplinary skills.
2. Include vocabulary, beliefs/teachings, texts/sources, practices/lived religion and explanation where relevant.
3. Include questions at different levels of cognitive demand.
4. Include at least one evidence-based explanation task.
5. Include at least one task that distinguishes related concepts or terms.
6. Include misconception probes rather than relying only on correct/incorrect scores.
7. Provide observable success criteria.
8. Provide guidance for acceptable variation where interpretations legitimately differ.
9. Do not ask pupils to reveal personal beliefs, family practices or religious identity.
10. Do not treat confidence, extroversion or willingness to speak about religion as evidence of RE attainment.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Use these headings:
1. Assessment purpose
2. Assessed knowledge and skills
3. Assessment sequence
4. Questions and tasks
5. Marking guidance
6. Misconception probes
7. Success criteria
8. Evidence collection
9. Intervention decisions
10. Teacher QA check

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check construct validity, age suitability, source accuracy, terminology and neutrality. Ensure the assessment measures RE learning rather than personal faith or identity.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Adapt for **[KS1/KS2]**, **[FORMATIVE/SUMMATIVE]**, **[UNIT]**, **[LESSON LENGTH]**, **[ASSESSMENT MODEL]** and **[PUPIL OUTCOME]**.

**Example Input:** Year 5, Christianity and Humanism, end-of-unit assessment on how worldviews influence ideas about responsibility.  
**Example Output excerpt:** The assessment separates knowledge recall from explanation and evidence use, then maps outcomes to specific reteaching decisions.

## AM-02 Diagnose RE Misconceptions From Pupil Responses

**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 1,000 to 1,500 words  
**Typical generation time:** 45 to 90 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** RE, misconceptions, assessment, intervention, primary, KS1, KS2

### ROLE

Act as a primary RE assessment specialist with expertise in diagnosing misconceptions.

### CONTEXT

Analyse these pupil responses from **[YEAR GROUP]** studying **[UNIT]**: **[PASTE RESPONSES]**. The intended knowledge is **[EXPECTED KNOWLEDGE]**. Key sources are **[SOURCES]** and key vocabulary is **[TERMS]**.

### TASK

Diagnose the underlying misconception in each response and distinguish factual error, incomplete understanding, vocabulary confusion, source misunderstanding, overgeneralisation and weak reasoning.

### REQUIREMENTS

1. Analyse each response individually.
2. Identify what is secure before identifying what is wrong or missing.
3. Classify the problem precisely.
4. Distinguish vocabulary errors from conceptual errors.
5. Identify confusion between belief, teaching, practice, text, interpretation, lived experience, identity and community.
6. Flag stereotypes or claims that incorrectly generalise across a religion or worldview.
7. Identify whether the error arises from the source, pupil reasoning or an oversimplified teaching representation.
8. Provide a short diagnostic question.
9. Provide a proportionate corrective explanation or activity.
10. Do not infer personal faith from pupil answers.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Use a diagnostic table with: Pupil response, Secure understanding, Error/misconception, Classification, Likely cause, Diagnostic question, Intervention, Success indicator.

Then provide a class-wide reteaching plan and a targeted plan for pupils requiring additional support.

### QUALITY CHECKS

Do not label a pupil as holding a religious misconception merely because their personal view differs. Diagnose against the intended curriculum knowledge and supplied evidence.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Adapt for **[YEAR GROUP]**, **[CLASS SIZE]**, **[UNIT]**, **[INTERVENTION TIME]** and **[PUPIL RESPONSES]**.

**Example Input:** Year 4 responses containing claims about Hindu festivals and the concept of karma.  
**Example Output excerpt:** The analysis distinguishes inaccurate generalisation from incomplete vocabulary knowledge and gives a separate teaching response for each.

## AM-03 Create an RE Misconception Diagnostic Quiz

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 800 to 1,200 words  
**Typical generation time:** 30 to 60 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** RE, diagnostic assessment, misconceptions, primary, KS1, KS2

### ROLE

Act as a primary RE curriculum and assessment designer.

### CONTEXT

Create a diagnostic quiz for **[YEAR GROUP]** studying **[UNIT/TOPIC]**. The traditions/worldviews are **[TRADITIONS/WORLDVIEWS]**. Common misconceptions are **[MISCONCEPTIONS]**. Key vocabulary is **[TERMS]**.

### TASK

Create a diagnostic assessment in which distractors and response options reveal specific misunderstandings rather than merely testing recall.

### REQUIREMENTS

1. Create 15 to 20 questions.
2. Map each question to a specific curriculum concept or skill.
3. Include plausible misconception-based distractors where appropriate.
4. Include short-answer explanation questions.
5. Include at least one source/evidence question.
6. Include at least one distinction between belief, teaching, practice, text or lived experience.
7. Include internal diversity where relevant.
8. Avoid questions that imply one believer speaks for an entire tradition.
9. Do not ask pupils to identify their own religion or personal beliefs.
10. Provide a diagnostic key explaining what each incorrect answer may indicate.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Provide the quiz first, followed by an answer key and misconception map.

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check that distractors are plausible but fair, content is accurate, and every item has a clear diagnostic purpose.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Adapt for **[KS1/KS2]**, **[NUMBER OF QUESTIONS]**, **[UNIT]**, **[DIGITAL/PRINT]** and **[QUESTION TYPES]**.

**Example Input:** Year 6, Islam, misconceptions about the Five Pillars, diversity within Muslim communities and the relationship between belief and practice.  
**Example Output excerpt:** Incorrect options correspond to defined misconceptions, allowing the teacher to decide what needs reteaching rather than simply recording a score.

Act as a primary RE intervention specialist.

### CONTEXT

Pupils need support with **[KNOWLEDGE/SKILL]** following assessment of **[UNIT]**. The year group is **[YEAR GROUP]**. Evidence of difficulty is **[PUPIL EVIDENCE]**. Available intervention time is **[TIME]**.

### TASK

Create a short, targeted intervention that addresses the diagnosed barrier without lowering the intellectual ambition of the RE curriculum.

### REQUIREMENTS

1. State the precise barrier.
2. Separate language/access barriers from substantive knowledge gaps.
3. Select the minimum prerequisite knowledge required.
4. Include explicit teaching and guided practice.
5. Include retrieval and immediate application.
6. Use accurate sources and terminology.
7. Include misconception checks before and after intervention.
8. Provide a transfer task showing whether the pupil can use the learning in a new context.
9. Avoid requiring personal belief disclosure.
10. Define an observable exit criterion.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Provide: diagnosis, prerequisite knowledge, intervention sequence, teacher prompts, pupil tasks, resources, misconception checks, exit task and next decision.

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check that intervention addresses the actual barrier, preserves curriculum rigour and does not turn religious literacy difficulties into assumptions about pupils' identities.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Adapt for **[INDIVIDUAL/SMALL GROUP/WHOLE CLASS]**, **[TIME]**, **[YEAR GROUP]**, **[UNIT]** and **[ACCESS NEEDS]**.

**Example Input:** Three Year 5 pupils confuse religious practice with personal identity and need support distinguishing the categories using supplied examples.  
**Example Output excerpt:** The intervention explicitly teaches the conceptual distinction, checks it with new examples and avoids asking pupils to describe their own identity.

## AM-08 Diagnose RE Comparison and Reasoning Errors

**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 1,000 to 1,500 words  
**Typical generation time:** 45 to 90 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** RE, comparison, reasoning, assessment, misconceptions, primary, KS2

### ROLE

Act as a primary RE assessment specialist with expertise in comparative and philosophical reasoning.

### CONTEXT

Pupils in **[YEAR GROUP]** have completed a comparison or reasoning task: **[TASK]**. Their responses are **[RESPONSES]**. The intended comparison is **[INTENDED LEARNING]** and sources are **[SOURCES]**.

### TASK

Diagnose errors in comparison and reasoning and identify whether pupils are making false equivalences, unfair rankings, unsupported claims or category errors.

### REQUIREMENTS

1. Identify what comparison dimension the pupil is using.
2. Check whether compared items are genuinely comparable.
3. Identify false equivalence and false difference.
4. Identify ranking or superiority claims unsupported by evidence.
5. Check whether pupils distinguish belief, teaching, practice, interpretation and lived experience.
6. Identify unsupported generalisations about a religion or worldview.
7. Check use of evidence.
8. Provide a corrective task using the same or a new evidence set.
9. Do not require personal belief disclosure.
10. Define an observable success criterion.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Use: Response, Secure reasoning, Error, Classification, Evidence issue, Corrective prompt, Follow-up task, Success criterion.

### QUALITY CHECKS

Ensure the intervention improves reasoning rather than coaching pupils towards a preferred conclusion.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Adapt for **[YEAR GROUP]**, **[TRADITIONS/WORLDVIEWS]**, **[TASK TYPE]** and **[SOURCE SET]**.

**Example Input:** Year 6 comparison of Christian and Humanist responses to charitable action.  
**Example Output excerpt:** A response that says one worldview is “more caring” is diagnosed as an unsupported evaluative ranking rather than accepted as a comparison.

Create a delayed reassessment and retrieval sequence that tests whether the learning has transferred and been retained.

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check that reassessment measures durable understanding and transfer rather than short-term recall alone.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Adapt for **[INDIVIDUAL/CLASS]**, **[TIME AVAILABLE]**, **[UNIT]**, **[MISCONCEPTION]** and **[RETRIEVAL INTERVALS]**.

**Example Input:** Year 5 pupils previously confused the terms “belief” and “practice”.

### TASK

Create a complete cycle from baseline assessment through formative checks, misconception diagnosis, intervention, reassessment and end-of-unit evaluation.

### REQUIREMENTS

1. Define assessment purposes and intended constructs.
2. Create a baseline task.
3. Create formative checks and diagnostic exit tickets.
4. Create a summative or end-point assessment appropriate to the unit.
5. Build misconception probes into the assessment sequence.
6. Provide analytic marking guidance.
7. Distinguish knowledge, vocabulary, source interpretation, explanation, comparison and reasoning.
8. Include intervention pathways for common barriers.
9. Include delayed reassessment and retrieval.
10. Include clear progression indicators.
11. Distinguish legitimate interpretive variation from factual inaccuracy.
12. Avoid stereotypes and overgeneralisations about religious or non-religious worldviews.
13. Include internal diversity where relevant.
14. Do not require pupils to disclose personal beliefs, religious identity or family practice.
15. Do not assess confidence or willingness to discuss religion as attainment.

1. Assessment rationale

4. Formative assessment sequence

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

**Example Input:** Year 6, eight-lesson unit comparing Christian and Humanist approaches to responsibility and community, with baseline writing, three formative checks and an end-of-unit explanation.
