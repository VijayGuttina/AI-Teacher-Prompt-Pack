# RE Enquiry & Curriculum Planning

This family provides ten copy/paste-ready workflows for planning high-quality primary Religious Education lessons and sequences. The workflows are designed around enquiry, religious literacy, lived religion, internal diversity, evidence, respectful dialogue and observable learning. They do not assume a particular locally agreed syllabus unless the teacher supplies it.

---

## RE-01 | Plan an RE Enquiry Lesson

**Prompt ID:** PT-RE-RE-01  
**Family:** RE Enquiry & Curriculum Planning  
**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 900 to 1,300 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 30 to 60 seconds  
**Curriculum Tags:** RE, Religious Education, enquiry, lesson planning, primary, knowledge, disciplinary thinking  

### ROLE
You are an expert primary Religious Education curriculum designer and experienced classroom teacher. Design rigorous, age-appropriate RE through enquiry rather than asking pupils to accept unsupported assertions. Represent religions and non-religious worldviews accurately, respectfully and with attention to internal diversity.

### CONTEXT
I am planning an RE lesson for **[YEAR GROUP]** on **[TOPIC / RELIGION / WORLDVIEW]**.

The lesson length is **[MINUTES]**.  
The learning context is **[UNIT / SEQUENCE / TERM CONTEXT]**.  
Pupils already know **[PRIOR KNOWLEDGE]**.  
The curriculum or agreed syllabus requirement is **[REQUIREMENT, IF KNOWN]**.  
Available resources are **[RESOURCES]**.  
Important classroom considerations are **[SEND / EAL / SENSITIVITY / OTHER]**.

### TASK
Create a complete enquiry-based RE lesson that develops knowledge and disciplinary thinking. Use the enquiry sequence:

**Question → Prior understanding → Encounter evidence/perspectives → Interpret → Compare/connect → Reason → Explain → Reflect → Revisit**

Choose one clear enquiry question that is intellectually worthwhile and suitable for the pupils' age.

### REQUIREMENTS
1. State the enquiry question and explain why it matters.
2. Define 3 to 6 precise learning outcomes using observable verbs.
3. Identify essential substantive knowledge pupils need.
4. Identify relevant disciplinary knowledge, such as describing, interpreting, comparing, reasoning or evaluating.
5. Include a short retrieval or prior-understanding activity.
6. Introduce evidence, perspectives, texts, artefacts or examples appropriately for the topic.
7. Distinguish clearly between a belief or teaching, a practice, an interpretation and a lived experience.
8. Avoid presenting one individual, denomination, community or source as representative of everyone.
9. Acknowledge internal diversity where it materially affects the lesson.
10. Include teacher questions that move from recall towards reasoning.
11. Include at least one misconception check.
12. Provide an inclusive independent or paired task.
13. Do not require pupils to disclose or defend their own religious or non-religious beliefs.
14. Do not require inappropriate role-play of believers or simulated sacred practices.
15. Include an assessment method that produces observable evidence of learning.
16. Include adaptations for pupils who need greater language, reading, processing or response support.
17. Identify any information that must be confirmed against the school's agreed syllabus, local guidance or reliable source before teaching.
18. Do not invent quotations, sacred practices, community views or local procedures.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Use these headings:
1. Lesson at a glance
2. Enquiry question
3. Learning outcomes
4. Essential knowledge
5. Disciplinary thinking
6. Lesson sequence with timings
7. Teacher questions
8. Pupil task
9. Misconceptions to check
10. Assessment evidence
11. Inclusion and adaptation
12. Resources and source notes
13. Teacher preparation checks
14. Example pupil response
15. Quality assurance check

### QUALITY CHECKS
Before finalising, check that the lesson is enquiry-led, factually cautious, age-appropriate, non-stereotyped, inclusive and assessable. Check that knowledge and interpretation are not blurred, that the enquiry question cannot be answered adequately by a single-word fact, and that no pupil is pressured to reveal personal beliefs.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
If **[CURRICULUM / AGREED SYLLABUS]** is supplied, align the lesson directly to it. If **[PUPIL PROFILE]** is supplied, adapt language, participation and evidence access without lowering the intellectual demand.

### EXAMPLE INPUT
Year 4, 50 minutes, Christianity, "What can the story of the Good Samaritan teach about how people respond to others?", pupils know the story but tend to think it simply means "be kind".

### EXAMPLE OUTPUT
A strong response would build from the pupils' existing knowledge of the parable, examine the social context and the significance of the Samaritan, compare possible interpretations, and require pupils to justify what the story may communicate about neighbourliness. It would distinguish the biblical text from later interpretations and avoid claiming that all Christians interpret it identically.

## RE-02 | Build a Short RE Enquiry Sequence

**Prompt ID:** PT-RE-RE-02  
**Family:** RE Enquiry & Curriculum Planning  
**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 1,500 to 2,000 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 45 to 75 seconds  
**Curriculum Tags:** RE, sequence, progression, enquiry, primary, curriculum planning  

### ROLE
You are a primary RE curriculum leader specialising in coherent progression, enquiry design and religious literacy.

### CONTEXT
I need a **[NUMBER]-lesson** RE sequence for **[YEAR GROUP]** about **[RELIGION / WORLDVIEW / THEME]**.

The intended curriculum requirement is **[REQUIREMENT, IF KNOWN]**.  
Pupils' prior knowledge is **[PRIOR KNOWLEDGE]**.  
The sequence should prepare pupils for **[NEXT LEARNING / OUTCOME]**.

### TASK
Design a coherent sequence in which each lesson contributes to a larger enquiry. Show how pupils move from noticing and identifying towards describing, explaining, interpreting, comparing, analysing and evaluating as appropriate for the year group.

### REQUIREMENTS
1. Give the sequence a clear overarching enquiry question.
2. Give every lesson a subsidiary enquiry question.
3. Map substantive knowledge and disciplinary development across the sequence.
4. Ensure lessons build rather than repeat knowledge.
5. Include opportunities to encounter evidence and perspectives.
6. Include explicit misconception planning.
7. Build vocabulary progressively.
8. Include internal diversity and lived religion where relevant.
9. Include at least one meaningful comparison or connection only where educationally justified.
10. Include assessment checkpoints and a final outcome.
11. Identify likely weak sequencing choices and replace them with stronger alternatives.
12. Do not assume pupils should reach a preferred theological or personal conclusion.
13. Identify syllabus-specific information that must be checked before implementation.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
- Sequence rationale
- Prior knowledge
- Overarching enquiry
- Progression map
- Lesson-by-lesson table
- Vocabulary progression
- Misconception map
- Assessment plan
- Inclusion plan
- Final enquiry outcome
- Teacher verification checklist

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check progression, coherence, factual accuracy, disciplinary balance, internal diversity, age suitability and assessment validity. Confirm that each lesson has a distinct purpose.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add **[SCHOOL SCHEME / AGREED SYLLABUS / PREVIOUS UNIT]** and use it as the primary sequencing constraint.

### EXAMPLE INPUT
Year 5, six lessons, Islam, belief and practice, pupils have basic knowledge of the Five Pillars but limited understanding of how beliefs connect with lived practice.

### EXAMPLE OUTPUT
The sequence would move from key beliefs and vocabulary into evidence about practices, then interpretation of how beliefs and practices connect, before considering diversity within Muslim communities and ending with a justified response to the overarching enquiry.

**Expected Output Length:** 1,000 to 1,500 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 30 to 60 seconds  
**Curriculum Tags:** RE, agreed syllabus, curriculum alignment, lesson planning, primary  

### ROLE
You are an RE curriculum alignment specialist. Your priority is to translate supplied curriculum requirements into teachable, assessable learning without adding unsupported claims.

### CONTEXT

**[PASTE AGREED SYLLABUS / CURRICULUM REQUIREMENT]**

Year group: **[YEAR GROUP]**  
Lesson/unit context: **[CONTEXT]**  
Available time: **[TIME]**

### TASK
Translate the supplied requirement into a practical lesson plan and show exactly where each curriculum expectation is addressed.

### REQUIREMENTS
1. Quote or paraphrase only the supplied requirement accurately.
2. Separate mandatory content from your suggested teaching choices.
3. Identify knowledge pupils need.
4. Identify disciplinary outcomes.
5. Create an enquiry question.
6. Map each activity to an explicit curriculum expectation.
7. Include assessment evidence for each major expectation.
8. Flag any requirement that is ambiguous rather than guessing.
9. Avoid adding denominational, theological or worldview claims not justified by the supplied material.
10. Include adaptations without changing the intended learning.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Use:
- Requirement summary
- Alignment matrix
- Enquiry question
- Learning outcomes
- Lesson sequence
- Assessment evidence
- Inclusion and adaptation
- Ambiguities requiring teacher confirmation
- Final alignment audit

### QUALITY CHECKS
Every major activity must have a clear curriculum purpose. Distinguish what comes from the supplied syllabus from what you have designed.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Include **[SCHOOL'S EXISTING SCHEME]** to align terminology and sequence with current practice.

### EXAMPLE INPUT
A supplied local syllabus requirement asks Year 3 pupils to explore beliefs and practices within Christianity and another religious tradition, with opportunities to identify similarities and differences.

### EXAMPLE OUTPUT
The response would map knowledge, comparison and enquiry separately, ensuring that "similarities and differences" does not become superficial matching of isolated facts.

### EXAMPLE OUTPUT

## RE-05 | Design an RE Retrieval and Prior Knowledge Routine

**Prompt ID:** PT-RE-RE-05  
**Family:** RE Enquiry & Curriculum Planning  
**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 800 to 1,200 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 30 to 45 seconds  
**Curriculum Tags:** RE, retrieval, prior knowledge, misconceptions, formative assessment, primary  

### ROLE
You are an expert primary RE teacher specialising in retrieval practice and formative assessment.

### CONTEXT
Year group: **[YEAR GROUP]**  
Current unit: **[UNIT]**  
Previous learning: **[PREVIOUS LEARNING]**  
Current learning goal: **[GOAL]**  
Known misconceptions: **[MISCONCEPTIONS, IF KNOWN]**

### TASK
Create a short retrieval routine that checks prerequisite knowledge without turning RE into a memory-only subject.

### REQUIREMENTS
1. Include 8 to 12 retrieval questions.
2. Mix vocabulary, knowledge, connections and one misconception check.
3. Include at least two questions requiring explanation rather than recall.
4. Give answers and acceptable answer variations.
5. Explain how each item prepares pupils for today's enquiry.
6. Avoid assessing personal beliefs.
7. Include a quick teacher diagnosis system.
8. Provide follow-up actions for common errors.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Use:
- Retrieval routine
- Questions
- Answers
- Diagnostic categories
- Immediate response actions
- Bridge into today's enquiry

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that retrieval reflects genuinely taught knowledge and does not rely on assumptions about pupils' family or religious backgrounds.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add **[LAST LESSON'S EXIT TICKET]** to make the routine responsive to recent evidence.

### EXAMPLE INPUT
Year 4, Hindu traditions, previous lesson on some forms of worship and symbolism, current lesson on how beliefs may influence practice.

### EXAMPLE OUTPUT
Questions would retrieve key vocabulary and previous examples, then ask pupils to explain one possible connection between belief and practice without implying that every Hindu person practises in the same way.

### ROLE
You are an expert RE enquiry designer.

### CONTEXT
Topic: **[TOPIC]**  
Year group: **[YEAR GROUP]**  
Key knowledge: **[KNOWLEDGE]**  
Intended disciplinary focus: **[DESCRIBE / EXPLAIN / INTERPRET / COMPARE / ANALYSE / EVALUATE]**

### TASK
Create a sequenced set of questions that begins with secure knowledge and develops towards meaningful RE enquiry.

### REQUIREMENTS
1. Produce 12 to 18 questions.
2. Group them by cognitive and disciplinary purpose.
3. Include retrieval questions.
4. Include evidence and interpretation questions.
5. Include comparison questions only where justified.
6. Include at least three questions requiring justification.
7. Include misconception probes.
8. Include teacher follow-up prompts.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

### QUALITY CHECKS

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Specify **[SOURCE / TEXT / ARTEFACT]** to make the questions evidence-led.

### EXAMPLE INPUT
Year 6, Sikhism, the concept of seva, with a focus on interpreting how a concept may relate to lived practice.

### EXAMPLE OUTPUT
The sequence would move from defining seva to examining examples, identifying possible connections between belief and action, considering variation in lived practice and justifying a carefully worded conclusion.

## RE-08 | Design a Cross-Tradition Comparison Enquiry

**Prompt ID:** PT-RE-RE-08  
**Family:** RE Enquiry & Curriculum Planning  
**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 1,200 to 1,700 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 40 to 70 seconds  
**Curriculum Tags:** RE, comparison, religions, worldviews, dialogue, enquiry, primary  

### ROLE
You are an expert primary RE curriculum designer specialising in balanced comparative enquiry.

### CONTEXT
Year group: **[YEAR GROUP]**  
Traditions/worldviews: **[A]** and **[B]**  
Theme: **[THEME]**  
Prior knowledge: **[PRIOR KNOWLEDGE]**  
Curriculum purpose: **[PURPOSE]**

### TASK
Design a comparison enquiry that helps pupils identify meaningful similarities and differences without reducing traditions to a checklist of facts.

### REQUIREMENTS
1. Establish secure knowledge of each tradition/worldview before comparison.
2. Use comparable categories only when educationally justified.
3. Include evidence or examples for each comparison.
4. Acknowledge internal diversity.
5. Distinguish belief, teaching, practice and lived experience.
6. Avoid implying that traditions are internally uniform.
7. Include questions about why similarities or differences may matter.
8. Include an evidence-based final response.
9. Do not rank traditions or imply that one is more rational, moral or authentic.
10. Do not ask pupils to state which worldview they personally prefer.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
- Comparison rationale
- Enquiry question
- Knowledge foundations
- Comparison matrix
- Lesson sequence
- Evidence prompts
- Misconception checks
- Final reasoning task
- Assessment criteria
- Inclusion guidance

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check balance, accuracy, internal diversity and evidence quality. Ensure the comparison develops understanding rather than tokenistic matching.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add **[SCHOOL'S PREVIOUS LEARNING]** so the comparison builds on established knowledge.

### EXAMPLE INPUT
Year 6, Christianity and Islam, theme of charity, with pupils already knowing selected beliefs and practices from both traditions.

### EXAMPLE OUTPUT
The comparison would examine concepts and practices using evidence, then ask pupils to explain both similarities and meaningful differences rather than producing a simple list.
