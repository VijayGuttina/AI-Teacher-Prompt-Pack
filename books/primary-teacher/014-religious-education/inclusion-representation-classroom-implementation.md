# Inclusion, Representation & Classroom Implementation

This family supports inclusive, accurate and educationally purposeful RE. It helps teachers represent religious and non-religious worldviews without stereotyping, avoid treating pupils as representatives of communities, plan safe enquiry and implement RE routines that remain academically rigorous and accessible.

## IR-01 Audit an RE Lesson for Inclusion and Representation

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 900 to 1,300 words  
**Typical generation time:** 30 to 60 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** RE, inclusion, representation, primary, KS1, KS2

### ROLE
Act as a primary RE subject leader and inclusion specialist.

### CONTEXT
Audit this lesson: **[PASTE LESSON]** for **[YEAR GROUP]**. It covers **[TOPIC/TRADITIONS/WORLDVIEWS]**. Curriculum guidance is **[GUIDANCE]** and available sources are **[SOURCES]**.

### TASK
Identify strengths, representation risks, inclusion barriers and practical improvements while preserving the intended learning.

### REQUIREMENTS
1. Check factual and terminological accuracy.
2. Check for stereotypes and overgeneralisations.
3. Check whether internal diversity is represented where relevant.
4. Check whether any pupil is implicitly positioned as spokesperson for a religion or worldview.
5. Check accessibility without diluting substantive knowledge.
6. Check assumptions about family practice, identity or belief.
7. Check source provenance and authenticity.
8. Check age suitability and classroom sensitivity.
9. Check whether activities simulate sacred practices inappropriately.
10. Recommend proportionate revisions linked to evidence.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Use: Strengths, Risks, Why it matters, Recommended revision, Priority, Teacher action.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Do not recommend generic inclusion language. Tie every recommendation to a specific feature of the lesson.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for **[KS1/KS2]**, **[TRADITION/WORLDVIEW]**, **[LOCAL COMMUNITY CONTEXT]** and **[ACCESS NEEDS]**.

## IR-02 Represent Internal Diversity Accurately

**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 900 to 1,400 words  
**Typical generation time:** 45 to 90 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** RE, diversity, representation, lived religion, primary, KS1, KS2

### ROLE
Act as a religious studies specialist supporting primary curriculum design.

### CONTEXT
The unit covers **[TRADITION/WORLDVIEW]**, focusing on **[TOPIC]**. Pupils are **[YEAR GROUP]** and current materials are **[MATERIALS]**.

### TASK
Create a practical representation plan that shows meaningful diversity without overwhelming pupils or turning the lesson into a catalogue of differences.

### REQUIREMENTS
1. Identify where variation is essential to accurate understanding.
2. Distinguish broad tradition-level claims from individual or community practice.
3. Use representative examples cautiously.
4. Avoid presenting one individual as representative of everyone.
5. Identify distinctions in interpretation, practice, culture or community only when relevant.
6. Include source guidance.
7. Explain what simplification is acceptable for this age group.
8. Flag claims requiring verification.
9. Avoid tokenism and “festival-only” representation.
10. Provide pupil language for making careful claims.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide representation principles, essential diversity points, examples, risky oversimplifications, source plan and teacher wording.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that diversity is relevant, evidence-based and manageable within the lesson's disciplinary purpose.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for **[YEAR GROUP]**, **[TOPIC]**, **[SOURCE SET]** and **[UNIT LENGTH]**.

## IR-03 Design an Accessible RE Lesson Without Lowering Ambition

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 900 to 1,300 words  
**Typical generation time:** 30 to 60 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** RE, SEND, accessibility, scaffolding, primary, KS1, KS2

### ROLE
Act as a primary RE teacher with expertise in adaptive teaching and disciplinary literacy.

### CONTEXT
The lesson is **[TOPIC]** for **[YEAR GROUP]**. Intended learning is **[LEARNING]**. Barriers include **[ACCESS NEEDS]**. Key vocabulary is **[TERMS]**.

### TASK
Adapt the lesson so pupils can access the same substantive RE learning through appropriate scaffolds and routes of participation.

### REQUIREMENTS
1. Preserve the core knowledge and enquiry.
2. Identify language, reading, working-memory, sensory and communication barriers where supplied.
3. Use scaffolds rather than lower-value substitute tasks.
4. Make vocabulary explicit and contextual.
5. Provide multiple response modes where appropriate.
6. Keep assessment aligned to the intended knowledge.
7. Avoid assumptions based on identity or religion.
8. Avoid asking pupils to disclose personal beliefs to participate.
9. Include gradual removal of scaffolds where feasible.
10. Provide observable success criteria.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide core learning, barriers, adaptations, teacher prompts, pupil supports, assessment access and review criteria.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that differentiation changes access, not the intellectual purpose of the lesson.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for **[SEND PROFILE/NEED]**, **[EAL]**, **[KS1/KS2]** and **[LESSON LENGTH]**.

## IR-04 Create Safe Classroom Dialogue Protocols for RE

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 800 to 1,200 words  
**Typical generation time:** 30 to 60 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** RE, dialogue, classroom culture, inclusion, primary, KS2

### ROLE
Act as a primary RE teacher experienced in facilitating respectful academic dialogue.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in **[YEAR GROUP]** will discuss **[QUESTION/TOPIC]**. Relevant traditions/worldviews are **[TRADITIONS/WORLDVIEWS]** and known sensitivities are **[CONSIDERATIONS]**.

### TASK
Create a dialogue protocol that enables enquiry, disagreement and evidence-based reasoning without requiring personal disclosure.

### REQUIREMENTS
1. Establish clear participation norms.
2. Distinguish critique of an idea from judgement of a person or community.
3. Require evidence for factual claims.
4. Allow pupils to pass on personal questions.
5. Prevent pupils being positioned as community representatives.
6. Include sentence stems for careful disagreement.
7. Include a teacher response protocol for misinformation or stereotyping.
8. Include a procedure for contested or emotionally charged claims.
9. Avoid forcing consensus.
10. End with an evidence-based synthesis.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide norms, discussion sequence, teacher prompts, pupil stems, intervention responses and reflection questions.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure dialogue remains academically purposeful and does not become a debate about which worldview is superior.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for **[KS1/KS2]**, **[TOPIC]**, **[CLASS PROFILE]** and **[DISCUSSION LENGTH]**.

## IR-05 Plan the Use of Visitors, Interviews or Community Voices

**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 900 to 1,400 words  
**Typical generation time:** 45 to 90 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** RE, visitors, community voices, lived religion, primary, KS1, KS2

### ROLE
Act as a primary RE subject leader planning educational use of first-hand voices.

### CONTEXT
I may use **[VISITOR/INTERVIEW/COMMUNITY VOICE]** for **[UNIT]**, Year **[YEAR GROUP]**. The learning purpose is **[PURPOSE]** and school requirements are **[POLICIES]**.

### TASK
Create a plan that uses the contribution as one situated perspective rather than as a spokesperson account for an entire religion or worldview.

### REQUIREMENTS
1. Define the curricular purpose.
2. Prepare pupils with prior knowledge.
3. Frame the contribution as an individual or situated perspective.
4. Create respectful, curriculum-linked questions.
5. Avoid intrusive questions about identity or personal practice.
6. Provide opt-out/alternative participation routes where needed.
7. Plan teacher safeguarding and school policy checks.
8. Separate visitor testimony from broader evidence.
9. Include post-visit evaluation and comparison with other sources where appropriate.
10. Do not imply the visitor is an authority on every interpretation or community.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide preparation, question bank, framing language, session protocol, follow-up learning and evaluation.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check educational purpose, safeguarding, representation and source triangulation.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for **[VISITOR TYPE]**, **[YEAR GROUP]**, **[UNIT]** and **[SCHOOL POLICY]**.

## IR-10 Build a Complete Inclusive RE Classroom Implementation Plan

**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 1,800 to 2,600 words  
**Typical generation time:** 60 to 120 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** RE, inclusion, representation, implementation, primary, KS1, KS2

### ROLE
Act as a primary RE subject leader, curriculum implementation specialist and inclusion adviser.

### CONTEXT
Create an implementation plan for **[YEAR GROUP/PHASE]**, **[UNIT OR CURRICULUM AREA]**, covering **[TRADITIONS/WORLDVIEWS]**. Curriculum guidance is **[GUIDANCE]**, resources are **[RESOURCES]**, staff considerations are **[STAFF NEEDS]** and access needs are **[PUPIL NEEDS]**.

### TASK
Build a complete plan for delivering rigorous, accurate and inclusive RE from teacher preparation through classroom implementation, assessment and review.

### REQUIREMENTS
1. Define non-negotiable curriculum knowledge.
2. Map inclusion and representation risks.
3. Provide accurate framing for religious and non-religious worldviews.
4. Build internal diversity into relevant teaching points.
5. Include source verification and provenance checks.
6. Include vocabulary and disciplinary literacy supports.
7. Include accessible participation routes.
8. Explicitly prevent pupils being treated as spokespersons for communities.
9. Provide alternatives to personal disclosure.
10. Address sensitive or controversial content proportionately.
11. Include teacher language for correcting stereotypes and misinformation.
12. Include guidance for visitors and first-hand voices.
13. Include lesson routines and classroom norms.
14. Include formative assessment and misconception response.
15. Include staff QA and post-unit review.
16. Do not prescribe religious participation or simulation of sacred practice.
17. Do not invent local demographic information, school policy or community views.
18. Flag information that must be verified before implementation.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Use these headings:
1. Implementation purpose
2. Curriculum non-negotiables
3. Representation and inclusion risk assessment
4. Teacher preparation
5. Source and resource quality assurance
6. Vocabulary and access plan
7. Classroom participation routines
8. Dialogue and sensitive-content protocol
9. Visitors and community voices
10. Lesson implementation sequence
11. Assessment and misconception response
12. Staff support and moderation
13. Post-unit review
14. Red flags and escalation points
15. Final implementation checklist

### QUALITY CHECKS
Run a final audit for curriculum integrity, factual accuracy, source provenance, internal diversity, avoidance of stereotypes and tokenism, meaningful representation of relevant non-religious worldviews, accessibility, personal-disclosure safeguards, age suitability, classroom manageability, appropriate treatment of sensitive content and realistic teacher workload.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for **[KS1/KS2]**, **[WHOLE-SCHOOL/UNIT]**, **[TRADITIONS/WORLDVIEWS]**, **[LOCAL SYLLABUS]**, **[STAFF EXPERIENCE]**, **[ACCESS NEEDS]** and **[SCHOOL QA MODEL]**.

**Example Input:** Whole KS2 implementation plan for an RE curriculum covering Christianity, Judaism, Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism, Sikhism and relevant non-religious worldviews, with mixed staff confidence and varied reading needs.

**Example Output excerpt:** The plan defines common implementation standards while allowing teachers to adapt access and examples. It separates substantive curriculum requirements from local information that must be verified and prevents pupils from being positioned as representatives of their families or communities.
