# 014 Religious Education Framework

## Purpose

This framework defines the architecture, quality standards and verification approach for the Religious Education prompt pack. The prompts are designed to help primary teachers plan, teach, assess, adapt and reflect on RE learning, including religious and non-religious worldviews, lived experience, texts, beliefs, practices, ethics, identity, community and interpretation.

The pack supports teacher judgement and locally agreed curriculum requirements. It must not assume that one syllabus, religion or interpretation applies universally.

**Framework target: 100 workflows across 10 families.**

## Core Principles

1. **Enquiry before assertion**: encourage pupils to investigate questions, evidence, interpretations and lived perspectives rather than simply memorising claims.
2. **Religious literacy**: use accurate terminology, names, practices, texts and concepts appropriate to the tradition or worldview being studied.
3. **Lived religion and worldview diversity**: distinguish formal teachings from how individuals and communities may actually live, interpret or express them.
4. **Internal diversity**: never imply that a religion or non-religious worldview is internally uniform when meaningful differences exist.
5. **Respect without loss of rigour**: treat pupils and traditions respectfully while allowing evidence-based questioning, comparison and critical evaluation.
6. **Non-religious worldviews**: include non-religious perspectives where relevant and represent them accurately rather than treating them as a single homogeneous position.
7. **Pupil dignity and neutrality**: do not pressure pupils to disclose personal beliefs, practices or family circumstances.
8. **Evidence and interpretation**: distinguish what a source or tradition states from teacher explanation, scholarly interpretation and pupil inference.
9. **Cultural and contextual accuracy**: explain practices and beliefs in appropriate historical, geographical and social context.
10. **Age-appropriate philosophical enquiry**: support reasoning, listening, questioning and justification without turning complex theological or philosophical questions into simplistic binaries.
11. **Inclusive participation**: provide alternative ways to engage where activities could create religious, cultural, linguistic, sensory or personal barriers.
12. **Appropriate uncertainty**: where the locally agreed syllabus, school policy or community context is unknown, state assumptions and do not invent requirements.

## Standard Workflow Architecture

Every workflow follows the agreed prompt architecture:

1. `ROLE`
2. `CONTEXT`
3. `TASK`
4. `REQUIREMENTS`
5. `OUTPUT FORMAT`
6. `QUALITY CHECKS`
7. `OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION`

Prompts should also include the agreed metadata and, where specified by the master specification, Example Input and Example Output.

## RE Quality Gates

Every workflow should be checked for:

- alignment with the supplied or locally agreed curriculum
- accurate representation of religions and non-religious worldviews
- appropriate distinction between belief, teaching, practice and lived experience
- internal diversity and avoidance of overgeneralisation
- accurate use of religious and disciplinary vocabulary
- age and developmental suitability
- enquiry, reasoning and evidence rather than unsupported assertion
- respectful and non-stereotyped representation
- clear distinction between source evidence and interpretation
- meaningful opportunities for pupil questioning and explanation
- balanced treatment of similarity and difference
- appropriate handling of sensitive or contested issues
- inclusive participation without requiring personal disclosure
- realistic classroom implementation
- observable assessment evidence
- proportionate intervention and misconception handling
- explicit handling of missing school-specific information

## Enquiry Model

Use the following sequence where appropriate:

**Question → Prior understanding → Encounter evidence/perspectives → Interpret → Compare/connect → Reason → Explain → Reflect → Revisit**

The sequence may vary by enquiry. Prompts should make the disciplinary purpose clear and avoid reducing RE to colouring, craft or generic moral discussion.

## Disciplinary Development Model

Prompts should support progression through:

**Notice → Identify → Describe → Explain → Interpret → Compare → Analyse → Evaluate → Reflect**

The exact demand should match the age, curriculum and learning objective. Pupils should not be expected to produce abstract theological or philosophical analysis beyond the intended curriculum level.

## Assessment Model

Assessment should distinguish, where relevant:

- religious and worldview knowledge
- accurate vocabulary
- understanding of beliefs and teachings
- understanding of texts and sources
- knowledge of practices and lived religion
- interpretation and use of evidence
- comparison and connection
- ethical and philosophical reasoning
- explanation and justification
- respectful dialogue and listening
- reflection and metacognition
- progress over time

Do not assess pupils' personal faith, family practice, willingness to identify with a religion, confidence in discussing sensitive topics or conformity with a teacher's preferred conclusion.

## Inclusion and Sensitivity Model

Adaptation may address:

- reading age and language load
- unfamiliar terminology
- source accessibility
- visual, aural and tactile access
- response mode
- discussion structures
- group participation
- sensitive or personal subject matter
- prior knowledge
- misconceptions and stereotypes
- alternative routes into enquiry

Do not require pupils to role-play a religious believer, simulate sacred practice inappropriately or disclose personal beliefs. Where a curriculum activity involves artefacts, sacred texts, places of worship or community visitors, prompts should encourage respectful handling and use of locally appropriate guidance.

## Representation and Safety Model

RE prompts should:

- avoid stereotypes, caricatures and tokenistic representation
- avoid presenting one individual or source as representative of every member of a tradition
- distinguish religion from ethnicity, nationality and culture
- acknowledge diversity within traditions when relevant
- avoid inventing quotations, practices, community views or theological claims
- flag contested or uncertain interpretations where appropriate
- use school procedures when supplied
- avoid inventing safeguarding, visitor, worship or off-site arrangements

Where a prompt asks for current or local information, it should use supplied evidence or reliable current sources rather than fabricate details.

## Verification Model

The RE pack will be verified using three layers:

### 1. Structural QA

All workflows must contain the required metadata and agreed prompt architecture. Target: **100/100 workflows structurally complete**.

### 2. Representative In-Model Execution

Execute exactly two representative workflows from each family, giving **20/20 representative executions**. Check that outputs are usable, coherent, age-appropriate, accurate, balanced and aligned with the stated task.

### 3. Targeted Risk Checks

Complete 16 targeted checks:

1. Accurate representation of religious traditions
2. Accurate representation of non-religious worldviews
3. Internal diversity and avoidance of overgeneralisation
4. Religious terminology accuracy
5. Sacred text and quotation accuracy
6. Distinction between belief, teaching, practice and lived experience
7. Historical and contextual accuracy
8. Respectful handling of sensitive topics
9. Avoidance of requiring personal belief disclosure
10. Fair comparison of religions and worldviews
11. Ethical and philosophical reasoning construct validity
12. Appropriate handling of contested interpretations
13. Inclusive classroom participation
14. Avoidance of stereotypes and tokenism
15. Evidence-led misconception and intervention work
16. Safe handling of missing syllabus or school-specific procedures

A workflow is not considered frozen solely because its structure is valid. Execution verification must test whether the prompt actually produces a useful result.
