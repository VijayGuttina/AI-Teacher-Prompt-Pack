# Comparison, Dialogue & Critical Thinking

This family develops pupils' ability to compare religious and non-religious worldviews fairly, interpret evidence, listen to differing perspectives and construct justified conclusions. Comparison should not become a competition between religions, a search for a single winner, or an invitation to disclose personal beliefs.

Teachers should distinguish similarity, difference, interpretation, evidence and evaluation. A comparison may concern beliefs, teachings, texts, practices, lived experiences, ethical reasoning or community perspectives. Internal diversity must be retained, and one source or person must never be treated as representative of an entire tradition or worldview.

### CONTEXT

Pupils in **[YEAR GROUP]** need to learn how to evaluate claims about **[RELIGION/WORLDVIEW/TOPIC]**.

### TASK

### REQUIREMENTS

- “Everyone in this religion believes...”
- “This practice means...”
- “People from this country are...”
- “This text proves...”

Use **[YEAR GROUP]**, **[TOPIC]**, **[TRADITIONS]** and **[SOURCE MATERIAL]**.

### EXAMPLE INPUT

Year 6, identify overgeneralised claims about religious practice and replace them with evidence-led, appropriately qualified statements.

### EXAMPLE OUTPUT
