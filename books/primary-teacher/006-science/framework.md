# Primary Science Framework

This framework is the subject-specific operating layer for the Primary Science Workflow Library. It inherits from the Global Prompt Standard, Master Prompt Framework and Primary Teacher Framework.

## Purpose

Generate practical, curriculum-aware science workflows that help primary teachers plan, teach, assess, diagnose misconceptions, design scientific enquiry and develop secure scientific knowledge.

## Curriculum context

The default context is the National Curriculum for England, KS1 and KS2. Schools retain flexibility over sequencing within a key stage, so workflows should align to the supplied school sequence when one is provided rather than assuming a single year-by-year order.

## Core principles

1. **Substantive science and working scientifically are connected** — enquiry skills should normally be embedded in meaningful science content rather than treated as a disconnected activity.
2. **Conceptual accuracy comes first** — scientific explanations, terminology, diagrams, examples and conclusions must be checked.
3. **Evidence before inference** — distinguish observations and data from explanations, predictions and hypotheses.
4. **Enquiry type must fit the question** — use observing over time, pattern seeking, classification, comparative/fair testing or secondary research where appropriate.
5. **Practical work has a scientific purpose** — do not add experiments merely to make a lesson look practical.
6. **Variables and controls matter when appropriate** — older pupils should increasingly identify what changes, what is measured and what is kept the same.
7. **Models are useful but are not reality** — identify simplifications when a model could create a misconception.
8. **Vocabulary is precise and purposeful** — introduce technical terms in context and distinguish everyday from scientific meanings where relevant.
9. **Mathematics supports science** — tables, units, scales, graphs and calculations must be appropriate to the science and the year group.
10. **Misconceptions are evidence-led** — never present a plausible explanation for an error as established fact without evidence.
11. **Adaptive teaching preserves the science** — reduce unnecessary access barriers without automatically reducing conceptual or enquiry demand.
12. **Safety is part of planning** — practical workflows should identify foreseeable hazards and sensible control measures, while recognising that school risk assessments and local procedures take precedence.

## Scientific enquiry categories

The library treats the following as genuine teacher jobs-to-be-done:

- Asking and refining scientific questions
- Planning enquiries
- Fair and comparative testing
- Observation over time
- Pattern seeking
- Classification and identification
- Research using secondary sources
- Measuring and recording data
- Interpreting data and drawing conclusions
- Evaluating evidence and improving an enquiry

## Substantive science categories

- Living things, plants, animals and humans
- Materials and their properties / changes
- Forces and motion
- Light and sound
- Electricity and energy
- Earth, space and seasonal phenomena
- Habitats, environments and interdependence
- Evolution, adaptation and scientific classification at the appropriate primary level

## Quality gate

Before returning an output, check:

1. Is the science factually correct?
2. Is the explanation appropriate to the stated year group and prior knowledge?
3. Is scientific terminology used accurately?
4. Does the enquiry type actually answer the question posed?
5. Are variables, controls and measurements appropriate where relevant?
6. Are data, units, tables, graphs and conclusions internally consistent?
7. Are observations clearly separated from interpretations?
8. Are predictions and hypotheses distinguished from conclusions based on evidence?
9. Does the practical activity have a clear scientific purpose?
10. Are safety considerations sensible without pretending to replace the school's risk assessment?
11. If diagnosing a misconception, is the proposed cause supported by evidence?
12. If adapting a task, has the intended scientific knowledge or enquiry demand been preserved?

## AI verification

Science workflows must be tested against the recorded model before publication. Verification must include representative inputs, factual accuracy checking, enquiry-design review and output-quality review. Published metadata should record the exact model, test date, result and next review date.

## Worked examples

The published Science book should include worked examples by workflow category showing:

1. Teacher input
2. Workflow used
3. Representative AI output
4. Why the output is useful
5. What the teacher must check or customise

Worked examples should demonstrate quality rather than inflate the page count.
