# Primary Science Worked Examples

These examples are category-level demonstrations. They are intended to show the quality and teacher value of the workflow library rather than inflate the number of examples.

## 1. Scientific enquiry

**Teacher input:** Year 5, investigate whether the surface material affects how far a toy car travels.

**Workflow:** WS-03 — Fair test planner

**Representative output:** Identifies surface material as the independent variable, distance travelled as the dependent variable, and recommends controlling the car, starting point, ramp height and release method. It provides a results table and conclusion prompt.

**Why useful:** Converts a broad practical idea into a controlled enquiry while making the variables explicit.

**Teacher check:** Confirm that the equipment and method are realistic for the classroom and apply the school's practical risk assessment.

## 2. Living things

**Teacher input:** Year 4, teach how plants transport water.

**Workflow:** LH-02 — Plant structure and function

**Representative output:** Connects roots, stem and leaves to their roles and proposes an observation using coloured water to make movement through a cut stem visible.

**Why useful:** Links structure and function rather than presenting plant parts as a vocabulary list.

**Teacher check:** Make clear that the practical observation is evidence of water movement, not a complete explanation of all plant transport mechanisms.

## 3. Materials

**Teacher input:** Year 5, investigate which material is easiest to separate from a mixture of iron filings, sand and water.

**Workflow:** MC-07 — Separation techniques

**Representative output:** Selects magnetic separation, filtration and evaporation/other suitable methods according to the stated mixture and purpose, explaining the property each method exploits.

**Why useful:** Makes the scientific principle behind each technique explicit.

**Teacher check:** Ensure the chosen sequence matches the actual mixture and equipment available.

## 4. Forces

**Teacher input:** Year 5, compare how different parachute designs affect descent time.

**Workflow:** FM-06 — Air and water resistance

**Representative output:** Defines a testable question, identifies design features as the variable to change, descent time as the outcome, and controls material, release height and mass where appropriate.

**Why useful:** Turns a familiar practical into an enquiry with measurable evidence.

**Teacher check:** Decide whether the proposed controls are feasible and whether repeat readings are needed.

## 5. Light, sound and electricity

**Teacher input:** Year 3, investigate which materials are transparent, translucent or opaque.

**Workflow:** LE-02 — Shadows investigation, adapted to the stated objective

**Representative output:** Uses a light source and object to compare transmitted light and resulting shadow evidence, with a structured observation table.

**Why useful:** Keeps the activity evidence-led rather than relying only on vocabulary recall.

**Teacher check:** Ensure the activity distinguishes the relevant material properties clearly.

## 6. Earth and space

**Teacher input:** Year 5, explain why we have day and night.

**Workflow:** ES-03 — Earth rotation and day/night

**Representative output:** Uses a globe/light-source model to show Earth rotating relative to the Sun, then asks pupils to explain what an observer at different locations would experience.

**Why useful:** Makes the model causal and exposes the common misconception that the Sun travels around Earth each day.

**Teacher check:** Explicitly identify the classroom model as a simplified representation.

## 7. Habitats and classification

**Teacher input:** Year 4, classify organisms found around the school grounds.

**Workflow:** CH-03 — Classification key builder

**Representative output:** Builds a branching key from observable characteristics and asks pupils to justify each classification decision.

**Why useful:** Develops classification reasoning rather than simple naming.

**Teacher check:** Verify identification features before using the key with pupils.

## 8. Assessment and diagnosis

**Teacher input:** Year 6, several pupils state that heavier objects always fall faster than lighter objects.

**Workflow:** ADI-05 — Science misconception diagnosis

**Representative output:** Treats the statement as evidence of a possible misconception, proposes discriminating questions and practical/model-based evidence before recommending reteaching.

**Why useful:** Prevents the teacher from treating a single statement as proof of a fixed misconception and creates a route to gather better evidence.

**Teacher check:** Ensure the diagnostic task controls relevant variables and is scientifically appropriate.
