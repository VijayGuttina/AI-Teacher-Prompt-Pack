# Assessment, Diagnosis and Intervention Workflows

## ADI-01 — Science Exit Ticket

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Science, Assessment, Formative Assessment

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[OBJECTIVE]` | `[RECENT_TEACHING]` | `[NUMBER_OF_QUESTIONS]` | `[COMMON_ERRORS]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary science assessment specialist with strong subject knowledge and expertise in formative assessment.

### CONTEXT
Create an exit ticket for `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils after teaching `[OBJECTIVE]`. Take account of `[RECENT_TEACHING]`, `[NUMBER_OF_QUESTIONS]` and any known `[COMMON_ERRORS]`.

### TASK
Create a concise assessment that samples the intended knowledge and, where appropriate, application, explanation or evidence interpretation needed to determine the most useful next teaching step.

### REQUIREMENTS
Every item must map to the objective. Include a purposeful mix of item types only where each adds diagnostic value. Avoid generic science trivia and avoid testing reading complexity when scientific understanding is the target. Include one item capable of revealing a likely misconception where appropriate.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Assessment objective; item map; pupil questions; answer key; what each item assesses; likely interpretations; immediate next-step options.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Verify every scientific answer. Check that success or failure on each item supports a meaningful interpretation and that the total assessment can realistically be completed at the end of the lesson.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for whole-class use, mini-whiteboards, digital forms, practical lessons or a two-minute paper check.

### Example Input
Year 4; objective: recognise that sounds are made by vibrations; recent teaching: tuning fork and drum demonstrations; questions: 4; common error: thinking sound can be produced without vibration.

### Example Output
Include direct recall, explanation of an observed demonstration and a diagnostic statement requiring pupils to identify whether vibration is present.

## ADI-02 — Science Hinge Question

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
**Expected Output Length:** 450–700 words
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Science, Formative Assessment, Misconceptions

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[OBJECTIVE]` | `[MISCONCEPTION]` | `[PRIOR_TEACHING]` | `[RESPONSE_FORMAT]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary science assessment specialist designing high-discrimination hinge questions for live teaching.

### CONTEXT
Create a hinge question for `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils on `[OBJECTIVE]`. The suspected misconception is `[MISCONCEPTION]`, following `[PRIOR_TEACHING]`, using `[RESPONSE_FORMAT]`.

### TASK
Design one decisive question that can quickly distinguish secure understanding from several plausible alternative models and guide an immediate teaching decision.

### REQUIREMENTS
Use one unambiguous correct answer unless the task explicitly asks for open reasoning. Make each distractor represent a plausible interpretation, misconception or procedural error. Explain what each response pattern may indicate and provide a specific teacher action for each major pattern.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Objective; hinge question; response options; correct answer; distractor logic; interpretation grid; immediate teacher response; follow-up check.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that the correct answer is scientifically and linguistically unambiguous and that distractors are diagnostically meaningful rather than obviously absurd.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for mini-whiteboards, multiple choice, diagram selection, practical prediction or paired discussion.

### Example Input
Year 5; objective: explain day and night using Earth’s rotation; misconception: the Sun moves around Earth once each day; response format: four-option multiple choice.

### Example Output
Use options representing rotation, apparent Sun movement and other plausible confusions, then specify whether to proceed, pause for re-teaching or investigate a mixed response pattern.

## ADI-03 — Science Objective Assessment

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
**Expected Output Length:** 800–1,200 words
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Science, Assessment, Curriculum

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[OBJECTIVES]` | `[COVERAGE]` | `[ITEM_COUNT]` | `[DURATION]` | `[KNOWN_MISCONCEPTIONS]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary science assessment designer with expertise in curriculum alignment and diagnostic interpretation.

### CONTEXT
Build an assessment for `[YEAR_GROUP]` covering `[OBJECTIVES]` with `[COVERAGE]`, `[ITEM_COUNT]`, `[DURATION]` and known `[KNOWN_MISCONCEPTIONS]`.

### TASK
Create a balanced assessment blueprint and complete question set that samples recall, application, explanation and evidence interpretation only where these are genuinely relevant to the stated objectives.

### REQUIREMENTS
Map each item to an objective and cognitive demand. Avoid duplicate items that measure the same narrow fact unless repetition is required for reliability. Include marking guidance and define what patterns of performance may indicate, without treating one error as proof of a fixed misconception.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Assessment blueprint; objective coverage; questions; answer key; marking guidance; item-by-item interpretation; cohort analysis guidance; next teaching actions.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Verify science accuracy, objective coverage, age appropriateness and whether each item actually measures its claimed learning target.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for end-of-lesson, end-of-topic, practical science or mixed written and oral assessment.

### Example Input
Year 6; objectives: complete circuits, conductors and circuit symbols; coverage: balanced across all objectives; item count: 12; duration: 25 minutes.

### Example Output
Provide a blueprint before the questions, ensuring no single objective dominates merely because it is easier to assess with closed questions.

## ADI-04 — Analyse Pupil Science Work

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
**Expected Output Length:** 700–1,000 words
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Science, Diagnosis, Assessment, Feedback

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[OBJECTIVE]` | `[PUPIL_WORK]` | `[RECENT_TEACHING]` | `[EXPECTED_STANDARD]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary science assessment and diagnostic specialist.

### CONTEXT
Analyse `[PUPIL_WORK]` from `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils against `[OBJECTIVE]`, taking account of `[RECENT_TEACHING]` and `[EXPECTED_STANDARD]`.

### TASK
Identify strengths, observable errors, response patterns and the smallest useful next teaching step. Generate hypotheses about causes only where the evidence does not establish them directly.

### REQUIREMENTS
Separate observation from interpretation. Consider vocabulary, scientific knowledge, representation, procedural understanding, evidence reasoning and retrieval as distinct possible causes. Identify what additional question or task would discriminate between competing explanations.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Strengths; observable evidence; errors or gaps; response pattern; possible explanations; evidence for and against; diagnostic follow-up; recommended next step; feedback language; review check.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Do not infer effort, ability, motivation or a stable misconception from insufficient evidence. Keep recommendations proportionate to the amount and quality of work supplied.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for an individual pupil, small group, whole class, practical write-up or science explanation.

### Example Input
Year 4; objective: identify that sounds are made by vibrations; pupil work: “The drum makes sound because you hit it. The air makes the noise.”; recent teaching: observing rice move on a drum skin.

### Example Output
Record the observable omission of vibration, propose alternative explanations for the error and create a short discriminating follow-up before prescribing extended re-teaching.

## ADI-05 — Science Misconception Diagnosis

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Science, Misconceptions, Diagnosis

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[TOPIC]` | `[SUSPECTED_MISCONCEPTION]` | `[EXISTING_EVIDENCE]` | `[ALTERNATIVE_EXPLANATIONS]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary science diagnostic specialist with expertise in misconception research and discriminating assessment design.

### CONTEXT
Investigate whether `[SUSPECTED_MISCONCEPTION]` is present in `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils studying `[TOPIC]`. Current evidence is `[EXISTING_EVIDENCE]`; consider `[ALTERNATIVE_EXPLANATIONS]`.

### TASK
Create diagnostic questions, demonstrations or representation tasks that generate new evidence capable of distinguishing the suspected misconception from plausible alternatives.

### REQUIREMENTS
Do not simply repeat the original question. Vary the context or representation where useful. State the response patterns expected under each competing explanation and recommend a proportionate teaching response for each interpretation.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Diagnostic hypothesis; current evidence; competing explanations; diagnostic items; expected responses; interpretation matrix; confidence limits; teaching response; follow-up verification.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Every diagnostic item must add information beyond the original evidence. Verify scientific accuracy and avoid treating one incorrect answer as conclusive proof of a particular mental model.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for one-to-one conferencing, small-group interviews, whole-class checks or practical demonstrations.

### Example Input
Year 3; topic: light; suspected misconception: shiny objects are light sources; existing evidence: pupils labelled a mirror as a source of light; alternatives: vocabulary confusion or misunderstanding reflection.

### Example Output
Use contrasting shiny, dull, luminous and non-luminous objects to determine whether the pupil is using brightness, reflectivity or actual light production as the classification criterion.

## ADI-06 — Targeted Science Intervention

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
**Expected Output Length:** 800–1,200 words
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Science, Intervention, Adaptive Teaching

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[IDENTIFIED_GAP]` | `[EVIDENCE]` | `[PREREQUISITES]` | `[NUMBER_OF_SESSIONS]` | `[TIME_PER_SESSION]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary science intervention teacher specialising in precise, evidence-led conceptual re-teaching.

### CONTEXT
Plan an intervention for `[YEAR_GROUP]` addressing `[IDENTIFIED_GAP]`, supported by `[EVIDENCE]`. Relevant `[PREREQUISITES]` must be considered. The intervention has `[NUMBER_OF_SESSIONS]` sessions of `[TIME_PER_SESSION]`.

### TASK
Design a focused sequence that checks prerequisites, rebuilds the target concept through appropriate explanation or representation, provides guided and independent practice, and verifies whether understanding has changed.

### REQUIREMENTS
Do not design around an unverified diagnosis. Use the smallest intervention likely to address the evidence. Include retrieval and transfer so success is not limited to copying a familiar example. Define an exit criterion based on evidence rather than time completed.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Need and evidence; prerequisite check; session sequence; representation choices; teacher explanation; guided practice; independent application; diagnostic checks; exit criterion; escalation route.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure each activity addresses the identified gap and that the final check differs sufficiently from practice to test transferable understanding.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for individual tutoring, small groups, short daily sessions or practical science intervention.

### Example Input
Year 5; identified gap: explaining day and night; evidence: repeated claims that the Sun circles Earth; prerequisites: Earth as a spherical body and relative position; sessions: 3; time: 20 minutes.

### Example Output
Begin by checking the prerequisite ideas, use a physical model with explicit viewpoint changes, then assess transfer through a new location-based prediction rather than repeating the demonstration question.

## ADI-07 — Science Retrieval Planner

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
**Expected Output Length:** 600–900 words
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Science, Retrieval Practice, Curriculum Sequencing

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[CURRENT_TOPIC]` | `[PREREQUISITE_KNOWLEDGE]` | `[NUMBER_OF_ITEMS]` | `[TIME_AVAILABLE]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary science teacher with expertise in curriculum sequencing and purposeful retrieval practice.

### CONTEXT
Plan retrieval for `[YEAR_GROUP]` before or during teaching of `[CURRENT_TOPIC]`. The required `[PREREQUISITE_KNOWLEDGE]` should be sampled through `[NUMBER_OF_ITEMS]` within `[TIME_AVAILABLE]`.

### TASK
Create a retrieval set that reactivates knowledge needed for the current learning and helps reveal whether a prerequisite requires immediate re-teaching.

### REQUIREMENTS
Prioritise relevance over generic science trivia. Mix direct recall and application only where both are useful. Include answers, likely errors and a concise re-teach note for each high-value misconception or prerequisite gap.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that every question has a clear purpose in relation to the upcoming learning and that prior concepts are represented accurately rather than oversimplified.

Adapt for starter activities, low-stakes quizzes, oral retrieval, cumulative review or spaced retrieval.

### Example Input
Year 6; current topic: electrical circuits; prerequisite knowledge: conductors, material properties and complete pathways; items: 6; time: 7 minutes.

### Example Output
Include retrieval that reveals whether pupils can apply prior material knowledge to an electrical context, not simply define “conductor” in isolation.

## ADI-08 — Adapt Science Task

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Science, Adaptive Teaching, Inclusion

### Editable Variables
`[ORIGINAL_TASK]` | `[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[SCIENTIFIC_OBJECTIVE]` | `[ACCESS_BARRIER]` | `[SUPPORT_AVAILABLE]` | `[RESOURCES]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary science teacher specialising in adaptive teaching and inclusive science learning.

### CONTEXT
The original task is `[ORIGINAL_TASK]` for `[YEAR_GROUP]`. The scientific objective is `[SCIENTIFIC_OBJECTIVE]`; the access barrier is `[ACCESS_BARRIER]`; available support is `[SUPPORT_AVAILABLE]`; resources include `[RESOURCES]`.

### TASK
Adapt language, visuals, equipment access, recording or task sequencing so the pupil can access the task while preserving the same central science concept, observation or reasoning demand.

### REQUIREMENTS
Analyse the barrier before selecting support. Consider language, working memory, sensory access, motor demands, reading, recording and prior knowledge separately. Do not provide the key scientific observation, variable choice or conclusion. Include scaffold fading and a route towards increasing independence.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Original objective; barrier analysis; adapted task; support strategy; preserved scientific demand; alternative recording; independence route; safety; review point.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Confirm that the adaptation changes access rather than silently changing the assessed scientific objective.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for SEND, EAL, sensory access, motor access, dyslexia, working-memory or communication barriers.

### Example Input
Year 4; original task: record observations from a sound investigation in a table; objective: compare evidence and identify a pattern; barrier: difficulty with extended handwriting; support available: tablet; resources: audio recorder and prepared table template.

### Example Output
Allow voice-recorded observations or selection-based entries while preserving the pupil's responsibility for deciding what counts as evidence and identifying the pattern.

## ADI-09 — Science Evidence Summary

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
**Expected Output Length:** 600–900 words
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Science, Assessment Analysis, Subject Leadership

### Editable Variables
`[DATA]` | `[OBJECTIVE]` | `[PUPIL_OR_GROUP_CONTEXT]` | `[NEXT_LESSON_CONSTRAINTS]` | `[THRESHOLDS_IF_ANY]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary science subject leader analysing assessment or enquiry evidence for proportionate teaching decisions.

### CONTEXT
Analyse `[DATA]` against `[OBJECTIVE]` for `[PUPIL_OR_GROUP_CONTEXT]`. The next lesson is constrained by `[NEXT_LESSON_CONSTRAINTS]`, with `[THRESHOLDS_IF_ANY]` if supplied.

### TASK
Summarise what the evidence establishes, what it suggests but does not prove, and what cannot yet be concluded. Prioritise the next actions by likely educational value and confidence in the evidence.

### REQUIREMENTS
Separate secure evidence, developing evidence, observable errors and interpretation hypotheses. Distinguish cohort patterns from isolated anomalies. Recommend diagnostic follow-up where uncertainty remains instead of inventing certainty.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Evidence overview; secure understanding; developing understanding; errors and patterns; evidence limits; priority actions; diagnostic follow-up; next-lesson plan.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Every recommendation must be traceable to supplied evidence. Do not overgeneralise from small samples or use attainment labels unsupported by the data.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for a class teacher, science subject leader, intervention team or concise leadership summary.

### Example Input
Data: 24 exit tickets on shadow size; objective: explain how distance between light source and object affects shadow size; context: whole class; next lesson: 45 minutes; threshold: none.

### Example Output
Separate the majority pattern from isolated errors, identify where additional diagnosis is needed and recommend whether the next lesson should proceed, revisit or use a short targeted re-teach.

## ADI-10 — Unit Review and Reteach Planner

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
**Expected Output Length:** 800–1,200 words
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Science, Unit Review, Reteaching, Curriculum Planning

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[UNIT_OBJECTIVES]` | `[ASSESSMENT_RESULTS]` | `[OBSERVED_ERRORS]` | `[NEXT_UNIT]` | `[AVAILABLE_TIME]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary science subject leader and curriculum planner.

### CONTEXT
Review a completed science unit for `[YEAR_GROUP]` using `[UNIT_OBJECTIVES]`, `[ASSESSMENT_RESULTS]` and `[OBSERVED_ERRORS]`. Plan for `[NEXT_UNIT]` within `[AVAILABLE_TIME]`.

### TASK
Identify secure learning, unresolved gaps, likely prerequisite risks and high-value reteaching priorities. Produce a proportionate plan for retrieval, targeted reteaching and future sequencing.

### REQUIREMENTS
Map evidence to individual objectives. Distinguish widespread patterns from isolated errors and temporary performance variation. Prioritise concepts that are foundational, persistently insecure or likely to obstruct future learning. Avoid reteaching the entire unit by default.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Evidence summary; objective-by-objective status; secure learning; unresolved gaps; priority misconceptions; reteach plan; retrieval schedule; reassessment checks; links to next unit; future sequencing notes.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Keep claims proportionate to the evidence. Ensure reteaching targets the underlying concept rather than simply repeating the original activity, and define how improvement will be verified.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for whole-class review, intervention planning, subject-leader analysis or a short pre-next-unit preparation plan.

### Example Input
Year 5; unit objectives: Earth and space; assessment results: objective-level class summary; observed errors: seasons explained by Earth-Sun distance and Moon phases explained by Earth’s shadow; next unit: forces; available time: two 45-minute retrieval/reteach sessions.

### Example Output
Prioritise unresolved foundational misconceptions, avoid reteaching objectives already secure for most pupils, and include a brief delayed check that tests transfer to a new diagram or scenario.
