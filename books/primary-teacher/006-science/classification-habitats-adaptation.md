## CH-01 — Habitat Concept Lesson

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 700–1,000 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Science, Biology, Habitats, Interdependence, Lesson Planning

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[HABITAT]` | `[ORGANISMS]` | `[OBJECTIVE]` | `[PRIOR_KNOWLEDGE]` | `[RESOURCES]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary biology teacher with strong subject knowledge of habitats, organism needs and interdependence.

### CONTEXT
Teach `[OBJECTIVE]` to `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils using `[HABITAT]`, `[ORGANISMS]` and `[RESOURCES]`, building from `[PRIOR_KNOWLEDGE]`.

### TASK
Design a concept sequence linking habitat conditions and available resources to the needs and survival of living things.

### REQUIREMENTS
Use accurate, age-appropriate language. Include examples of both large habitats and microhabitats where relevant. ### QUALITY CHECKS

### Example Input
Year 2; habitat: school field and log pile; organisms: worms, woodlice and plants; objective: describe how different habitats provide for basic needs.

### Example Output
Use direct observations to identify conditions and resources, then ask pupils to justify why particular organisms may be found in different parts of the environment.

### CONTEXT
Plan a survey for `[YEAR_GROUP]` at `[LOCATION]` to investigate `[QUESTION]`, using `[TARGET_ORGANISMS]`, `[EQUIPMENT]` and `[TIME]`.

## CH-03 — Classification Key Builder

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Science, Biology, Classification, Identification Keys

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[ORGANISMS_OR_OBJECTS]` | `[OBSERVABLE_CHARACTERISTICS]` | `[DIFFICULTY]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary biology specialist with expertise in classification and identification reasoning.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` will classify `[ORGANISMS_OR_OBJECTS]` using `[OBSERVABLE_CHARACTERISTICS]` at `[DIFFICULTY]` level.

### TASK
Create a branching classification or identification key that pupils can construct, test, debug and explain.

### REQUIREMENTS
Base every branch on a clear, observable and reliably distinguishable characteristic. Avoid ambiguous wording and ensure each route leads consistently to the intended group or identification. Include at least one debugging task involving an incorrect or inefficient branch.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Objective; vocabulary; characteristic set; completed key; partially completed pupil key; test cases; debugging challenge; questions; answers; limitations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Trace every item through the complete key. Ensure a characteristic is not used in a way that produces contradictory or overlapping routes.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for animals, plants, rocks, everyday objects or fictional organisms.

### Example Input
Year 6; organisms: six local invertebrates; characteristics: number of legs, wings, body sections and antennae; difficulty: intermediate.

### Example Output
Provide a dichotomous key, a partially completed version and one deliberately flawed branch for pupils to identify and repair using the observable characteristics.

## CH-04 — Food-Web Reasoning

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, Science, Biology, Food Chains, Food Webs, Interdependence

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[HABITAT]` | `[ORGANISMS]` | `[CHANGE_SCENARIO]` | `[DIFFICULTY]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary biology teacher specialising in ecological relationships and evidence-based reasoning.

### CONTEXT
Create a food-web reasoning task for `[YEAR_GROUP]` using `[HABITAT]`, `[ORGANISMS]` and `[CHANGE_SCENARIO]` at `[DIFFICULTY]` level.

### TASK
Design a food web and scenario requiring pupils to reason through possible direct and indirect effects when one population or resource changes.

### REQUIREMENTS
Check every feeding relationship. Make arrow conventions explicit. Distinguish plausible outcomes from guaranteed outcomes when multiple ecological factors could influence the result. Require pupils to show a chain of reasoning rather than simply naming organisms affected.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Context; food web; key; scenario; reasoning scaffold; questions; model answers; alternative plausible outcomes; evidence limitations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Do not treat simplified classroom food webs as complete representations of real ecosystems. Avoid deterministic claims where the supplied evidence supports only a possible outcome.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for woodland, pond, grassland, marine or urban ecosystems.

### Example Input
Year 5; habitat: pond; organisms: algae, snails, tadpoles, insects, fish and herons; change: a reduction in algae after prolonged shading.

### Example Output
Ask pupils to trace possible consequences through the web, distinguish direct from indirect effects and identify what additional evidence would be needed to predict actual population change confidently.

**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Science, Biology, Adaptation, Comparison

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[ORGANISMS]` | `[HABITATS]` | `[FEATURES]` | `[OBJECTIVE]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary biology specialist with strong knowledge of adaptations and age-appropriate evolutionary language.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` will compare `[ORGANISMS]` from `[HABITATS]`, focusing on `[FEATURES]` to achieve `[OBJECTIVE]`.

### TASK
Create a comparison sequence linking observable structural or behavioural features to plausible functions and environmental conditions.

### REQUIREMENTS
Avoid teleological explanations suggesting organisms consciously choose or deliberately develop features because they need them. Distinguish an observed feature from an inferred function. Include comparison and transfer to an unfamiliar organism or environment.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Objective; organism information; feature table; habitat conditions; function reasoning; comparison questions; model answers; misconception check; limitations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Verify biological claims. Do not imply that every individual organism has identical adaptations or that a feature guarantees survival in every circumstance.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

### Example Input
Year 6; organisms: camel and polar bear; habitats: desert and Arctic; features: insulation, body surface, water conservation and feet; objective: explain how features can support survival.

### Example Output
Ask pupils to connect each feature to a stated environmental challenge, distinguish strong evidence from speculation and correct explanations implying intentional adaptation.

### CONTEXT
Plan an investigation for `[YEAR_GROUP]` comparing `[MICROHABITATS]` to answer `[QUESTION]`, using `[TARGET_OBSERVATIONS]` and `[EQUIPMENT]`.

### TASK
Design an observation and comparison enquiry in which pupils gather comparable evidence about conditions or organisms across microhabitats.

### Example Input
Year 3; microhabitats: under logs and open grass; question: where are more invertebrates observed?; equipment: trays, timers and tally sheets.

### Example Output
Use matched observation periods and a consistent search area, then ask pupils to explain the observed pattern while identifying one reason the survey may not represent the whole site.

**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Science, Biology, Classification, Assessment

`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[ITEMS]` | `[CLASSIFICATION_SYSTEM]` | `[DIFFICULTY]` | `[PUPIL_ERRORS]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary science assessment specialist with expertise in classification reasoning.

### CONTEXT
Create a classification reasoning task for `[YEAR_GROUP]` using `[ITEMS]` and `[CLASSIFICATION_SYSTEM]` at `[DIFFICULTY]` level. Incorporate `[PUPIL_ERRORS]` if supplied.

### TASK
Require pupils to classify items, identify the characteristics supporting their decisions, compare competing classifications and evaluate whether the available evidence is sufficient.

### REQUIREMENTS
Ensure each classification can be justified from stated characteristics. Include plausible distractors and at least one item for which classification requires additional information or a carefully stated assumption.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

### QUALITY CHECKS

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for living things, materials, rocks, organisms from images or mixed data sets.

### Example Input
Year 6; items: eight organisms; classification system: vertebrate and invertebrate, then subgroups; difficulty: high; pupil errors: classifying by habitat instead of characteristics.

### Example Output
Include an evidence table, classification challenge and a diagnostic comparison showing why habitat is not the defining characteristic in the requested system.

### CONTEXT
Analyse `[PUPIL_RESPONSES]` from `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils working on `[OBJECTIVE]` within `[TOPIC]`. Consider `[RECENT_TEACHING]` when interpreting the evidence.

### TASK
Identify observable errors, generate competing explanations, design discriminating diagnostic questions and recommend a focused teaching response.

### REQUIREMENTS

### QUALITY CHECKS

### Example Input

### Example Output
Use questions about moisture, shelter and evidence to distinguish everyday preference language from a lack of understanding about habitat conditions and organism needs.

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary science teacher specialising in adaptive teaching, inclusive fieldwork and practical biology.

### CONTEXT
The original task is `[ORIGINAL_TASK]` for `[YEAR_GROUP]`. The scientific objective is `[SCIENTIFIC_OBJECTIVE]`; the access barrier is `[ACCESS_BARRIER]`; available `[LOCATION_OR_EQUIPMENT]` and `[EXISTING_SUPPORT]` must be considered.

### TASK
Adapt access, observation, handling, representation or recording while preserving the central scientific question and the pupil's responsibility for gathering or interpreting evidence.

### REQUIREMENTS
Analyse equipment access, mobility, sensory access, language, working memory and recording separately. Do not automatically reduce the enquiry demand or provide the key classification or conclusion. Include scaffold fading and a route towards increasing independence.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

### QUALITY CHECKS
Confirm that the adaptation still produces evidence relevant to the same scientific question and that support does not perform the central observation or reasoning for the pupil.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for SEND, EAL, mobility access, sensory access, motor access, working-memory or language barriers.

### Example Input
Year 4; original task: compare invertebrates in two microhabitats; objective: use observations to compare habitats; barrier: difficulty moving across uneven outdoor ground; location: school nature area; existing support: adult verbal prompting.

### Example Output
Use an accessible observation station, prepared but authentic sample areas or shared class evidence where necessary, while preserving the pupil's responsibility for applying consistent criteria, recording observations and interpreting the comparison.
