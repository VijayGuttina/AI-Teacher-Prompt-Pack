### ROLE

### CONTEXT
Teach `[OBJECTIVE]` to `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils, building from `[PRIOR_KNOWLEDGE]` and using `[EXAMPLES]` within `[DURATION]`.

### TASK
Design a concept lesson linking forces to observable effects and requiring pupils to distinguish observations from explanations.

### REQUIREMENTS
Use scientifically accurate, age-appropriate force language. Include retrieval, demonstration or model, guided reasoning, independent application and formative assessment. Address a relevant misconception such as the idea that a force is required to keep an object moving.

### Example Input

### Example Output
Use familiar observations to establish friction as a force opposing relative motion, then require pupils to predict and explain how changing surfaces can affect movement.

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[CONTEXTS]` | `[OBJECTS]` | `[OBJECTIVE]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary science teacher introducing force concepts through familiar physical situations.

### CONTEXT
Teach `[OBJECTIVE]` to `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils using `[CONTEXTS]` and `[OBJECTS]`.

### TASK
Create classification, observation and reasoning activities that develop the idea that pushes and pulls are interactions that can change an object's motion or shape.

### REQUIREMENTS

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Objective; examples; classification; reasoning questions; practical activity; answers; misconception; assessment.

### Example Input
Year 2; contexts: opening a drawer, kicking a ball, stretching elastic and pulling a toy; objective: identify pushes and pulls.

### Example Output
Classify the examples while asking pupils what the force does to the object, including changes in movement or shape.

**Expected Output Length:** 600–900 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, Science, Physics, Forces, Balanced Forces, Reasoning

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[CONTEXTS]` | `[REPRESENTATIONS]` | `[OBJECTIVE]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary physics teacher developing an age-appropriate model of balanced and unbalanced forces.

### CONTEXT
Teach `[OBJECTIVE]` to `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils using `[CONTEXTS]` and `[REPRESENTATIONS]`.

### TASK
Create examples and reasoning tasks showing how the balance of forces relates to changes or lack of changes in motion.

### REQUIREMENTS
Use force arrows or other representations only when they accurately communicate the intended idea. Make clear that balanced forces do not mean that no forces are acting. Avoid introducing vector mathematics beyond the curriculum requirement.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Objective; vocabulary; model; examples; representations; reasoning questions; answers; misconception; assessment.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check every force diagram and ensure the stated motion is consistent with the force balance described.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for stationary objects, moving objects, friction, gravity, air resistance or simple force diagrams.

### Example Input
Year 6; contexts: book on a table, cyclist moving at constant speed and falling object; objective: reason about balanced and unbalanced forces.

### Example Output
Use contrasting cases to show that balanced forces can coexist with motion, provided the motion is not changing, and require pupils to explain the difference.
