## ES-01 — Earth and Space Concept Lesson

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 700–1,000 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Science, Earth Science, Space, Lesson Planning

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[OBJECTIVE]` | `[PRIOR_KNOWLEDGE]` | `[REPRESENTATIONS]` | `[EXAMPLES]` | `[DURATION]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary science teacher with strong subject knowledge of Earth and space science and of model-based primary science teaching.

### CONTEXT
Teach `[OBJECTIVE]` to `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils. Build from `[PRIOR_KNOWLEDGE]`, using `[REPRESENTATIONS]` and `[EXAMPLES]`, within `[DURATION]`.

### TASK
Design a concept sequence that moves from observation or familiar phenomena to a scientifically appropriate model, explanation, application and formative assessment.

### REQUIREMENTS
Use accurate, age-appropriate vocabulary. Explicitly distinguish direct observations from explanations based on scientific models. State where a classroom model is useful but incomplete. Address the most likely misconception without overloading pupils with unnecessary secondary-level detail.

### Example Output

## ES-02 — Solar System Model

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, Science, Astronomy, Solar System, Models

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[OBJECTS]` | `[MODEL_TYPE]` | `[SCALE_CONSTRAINTS]` | `[LEARNING_OBJECTIVE]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary astronomy specialist designing model-based learning that makes simplifications explicit.

### CONTEXT
Create an activity for `[YEAR_GROUP]` involving `[OBJECTS]` using `[MODEL_TYPE]`. The intended learning objective is `[LEARNING_OBJECTIVE]`, with `[SCALE_CONSTRAINTS]`.

### TASK
Design a Solar System model and learning sequence that makes the represented relationship clear while explicitly identifying what the model cannot represent faithfully.

### REQUIREMENTS
Specify what pupils should infer from the model and what they should not infer. Distinguish size, distance, orbit and relative position where relevant. Do not present a convenient classroom layout as physically proportional unless the stated scale genuinely supports that claim.

### Example Input
Year 5; objects: Sun and eight planets; model type: playground distance model; scale constraints: walking distances must fit within the available space; objective: understand relative distance is much greater than planet size representations suggest.

### Example Output
State clearly which quantities are represented and which are compressed, then ask pupils to identify how the model would change if distance and diameter had to use one exact shared scale.

## ES-03 — Earth’s Rotation and Day/Night

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, Science, Astronomy, Earth, Rotation, Day and Night

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[MODEL]` | `[LOCATIONS]` | `[QUESTIONS]` | `[PRIOR_KNOWLEDGE]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary science teacher with accurate astronomy knowledge and expertise in physical models.

### CONTEXT
Teach day and night to `[YEAR_GROUP]` using `[MODEL]`, `[LOCATIONS]` and `[QUESTIONS]`, building from `[PRIOR_KNOWLEDGE]`.

### TASK
Create a demonstration and reasoning sequence explaining how Earth’s rotation relates to changing day and night at different locations.

### REQUIREMENTS
Make the observer’s viewpoint explicit. Distinguish apparent daily movement of the Sun from the model-based explanation involving Earth’s rotation. Include prediction questions that require pupils to manipulate or interpret the model.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Objective; retrieval; vocabulary; model setup; demonstration; explanation; prediction tasks; questions; answers; misconception diagnosis; model limitations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Do not reinforce the misconception that the Sun physically travels around Earth each day. Check that the direction and sequence of the model are internally consistent.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for different world locations, time differences, apparent movement of the Sun or comparative model work.

### Example Input
Year 5; model: lamp and globe; locations: UK and Australia; questions: where is daylight and what changes after rotation?; prior knowledge: Earth is approximately spherical.

### Example Output
Require pupils to predict which location faces the light before rotating the globe, then use the result to explain why locations can experience different times of day.

### ROLE
Act as a primary astronomy specialist teaching changing lunar appearance through accurate model-based reasoning.

### CONTEXT
Teach `[PHASE_SET]` to `[YEAR_GROUP]` using `[MODEL]` and `[OBSERVATION_CONTEXT]`, building from `[PRIOR_KNOWLEDGE]`.

### TASK
Create a model and observation sequence explaining why the visible illuminated portion of the Moon changes from Earth.

### REQUIREMENTS
Distinguish the illuminated half of the Moon from the portion visible from a particular viewpoint. Explicitly address the misconception that ordinary Moon phases are caused by Earth’s shadow. Use the model to support prediction, not only demonstration.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Objective; vocabulary; model setup; observation sequence; explanation; prediction tasks; phase ordering; questions; answers; misconception check; model limitations.

Adapt for phase sequencing, observation diaries, physical models or diagram interpretation.

### Example Input
Year 5; model: lamp, ball and observer; phase set: new, quarter, full and intermediate appearances; observation context: classroom model followed by home observations.

### Example Output
Ask pupils to predict what the observer will see from different positions before revealing the result, then require them to explain why the visible illuminated portion changes.

### CONTEXT
Design an investigation for `[YEAR_GROUP]` using `[LOCAL_OBSERVATIONS]` over `[TIME_PERIOD]`. Available evidence includes `[DATA_AVAILABLE]`, and the enquiry question is `[QUESTION]`.

### TASK
Create a structured observation-over-time investigation that enables pupils to record, represent and interpret seasonal patterns.

### Example Input
Year 2; local observations: tree leaves and weekly temperature; time period: autumn term; data: class measurements and photographs; question: what changes can we observe as autumn progresses?

### Example Output

## ES-07 — Environmental Change Evidence Task

**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Science, Environment, Environmental Change, Evidence

`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[ENVIRONMENT]` | `[CHANGE]` | `[EVIDENCE]` | `[TIMEFRAME]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary science teacher designing evidence-led environmental learning.

### CONTEXT
Pupils in `[YEAR_GROUP]` will examine `[CHANGE]` in `[ENVIRONMENT]` using `[EVIDENCE]` over `[TIMEFRAME]`.

### TASK
Create a case study or investigation requiring pupils to distinguish evidence, inference, uncertainty and value judgement when considering environmental change and its effects on living things.

### REQUIREMENTS

### QUALITY CHECKS

### Example Input
Year 6; environment: local pond; change: increased litter and reduced visible aquatic plants; evidence: photographs, counts and pupil observations; timeframe: one term.

### Example Output
Require pupils to identify direct evidence separately from possible explanations and propose additional observations before claiming that one specific cause produced every observed change.

## ES-08 — Sustainability Science Decision Task

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Science, Sustainability, Evidence-Based Decision Making

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[ISSUE]` | `[EVIDENCE]` | `[OPTIONS]` | `[CONSTRAINTS]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary science teacher designing evidence-informed decision tasks.

### CONTEXT
Create a decision task for `[YEAR_GROUP]` concerning `[ISSUE]`. Pupils will use `[EVIDENCE]` to compare `[OPTIONS]` under `[CONSTRAINTS]`.

### TASK
Design a structured sustainability decision in which pupils apply relevant science, weigh stated criteria and justify a recommendation with evidence.

### REQUIREMENTS
Separate scientific evidence from preferences and values. Make decision criteria explicit. Allow more than one defensible conclusion where the evidence and priorities support it, rather than manufacturing a single “correct” opinion.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Scenario; evidence; options; decision criteria; pupil task; reasoning framework; questions; model response; alternative defensible conclusions; limitations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Do not present contested, ethical or value-based claims as simple scientific facts. Check that the evidence supplied is sufficient for the requested reasoning.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for recycling, energy use, transport, water use, biodiversity or school sustainability decisions.

### Example Input
Year 5; issue: reducing lunchtime waste; evidence: one-week waste audit; options: reusable containers, composting and reducing packaging; constraints: limited budget and staff time.

### Example Output
Require pupils to use the audit data, compare trade-offs against explicit criteria and justify a recommendation while acknowledging limitations in the available evidence.

### ROLE
Act as a primary astronomy and Earth-science diagnostic specialist.

### CONTEXT
Analyse `[PUPIL_RESPONSES]` from `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils working on `[OBJECTIVE]`. Consider `[RECENT_TEACHING]` and `[MODELS_USED]` when interpreting the evidence.

### TASK
Identify observable misconceptions, generate competing explanations, design discriminating questions or model tasks and recommend a focused teaching response.

### REQUIREMENTS
Separate the pupil evidence from hypotheses about why the response occurred. Consider vocabulary, perspective, model interpretation, conceptual understanding and retrieval as distinct possibilities. Do not infer fixed ability from limited evidence.

### QUALITY CHECKS

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for individual conferencing, small-group intervention or whole-class hinge questions.

### Example Input
Year 5; response: “It is winter because Earth is farther from the Sun”; objective: explain seasonal patterns; recent teaching: Earth-Sun model; models used: globe and lamp.

### Example Output
Create contrasting diagnostic questions that test whether the pupil is confusing distance with Earth’s orientation or simply recalling an oversimplified diagram, then provide a model-based re-teaching sequence.

## ES-10 — Adapt Earth, Space or Environmental Science Task

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Science, Earth Science, Space, Adaptive Teaching, Inclusion

### Editable Variables
`[ORIGINAL_TASK]` | `[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[SCIENTIFIC_OBJECTIVE]` | `[ACCESS_BARRIER]` | `[RESOURCES]` | `[EXISTING_SUPPORT]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary science teacher specialising in adaptive teaching and inclusive model-based learning.

### CONTEXT
The original task is `[ORIGINAL_TASK]` for `[YEAR_GROUP]`. The scientific objective is `[SCIENTIFIC_OBJECTIVE]`; the access barrier is `[ACCESS_BARRIER]`; available resources are `[RESOURCES]`; existing support is `[EXISTING_SUPPORT]`.

### TASK
Adapt language, representation, physical models, observation methods or recording so pupils can access the task while preserving the intended scientific evidence or reasoning demand.

### REQUIREMENTS
Analyse the barrier before proposing support. Consider vocabulary, spatial reasoning, working memory, sensory access, motor demands and recording separately. Do not automatically simplify the scientific concept or provide the key inference. Include scaffold fading and a route towards increasing independence.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Original objective; barrier analysis; adapted task; representation or equipment changes; support; preserved scientific demand; recording alternatives; independence route; safety; review point.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Confirm that the adaptation preserves the central concept, observation or evidence-based decision. Ensure support does not perform the key scientific reasoning for the pupil.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for SEND, EAL, visual or sensory access, motor access, working-memory or language barriers.

### Example Input
Year 5; original task: use a globe and lamp to explain day and night; objective: connect Earth’s rotation to changing illumination; barrier: difficulty manipulating the globe while tracking location; resources: labelled globe, lamp and location markers.

### Example Output
Stabilise the globe and use removable location markers or staged photographs while retaining the pupil’s responsibility for predicting, observing and explaining what changes when the model is rotated.
