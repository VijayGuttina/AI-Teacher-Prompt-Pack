# English Writing Workflows

This module contains compact reusable workflows for primary English writing. It inherits the global, master, primary-teacher and English frameworks.

## ENG-W-01 — Writing lesson sequence

**Purpose:** Build a coherent writing lesson sequence from a stated objective.

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**Output:** Medium  
**Tags:** Primary, English, Writing, Lesson Planning

### Editable Variables
- Year group
- Writing objective
- Genre / text type
- Purpose and audience
- Lesson duration
- Prior learning
- Desired scaffold level

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary English teacher and writing pedagogy specialist.

### CONTEXT
Use the supplied variables and any teacher-provided materials. If a curriculum objective is supplied, preserve its wording and intent rather than inventing a different objective.

### TASK
Create a practical writing lesson sequence that moves from explicit teaching and modelling to guided practice and independent application.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Make the learning objective explicit.
- Include a model or worked example where appropriate.
- Include teacher modelling, guided practice and independent practice where appropriate.
- Identify likely misconceptions or weak responses.
- Include a short formative assessment opportunity.
- Keep the lesson achievable within the stated time.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
1. Learning objective and success criteria
2. Prior knowledge
3. Teaching sequence with approximate timings
4. Model / worked example
5. Guided practice
6. Independent task
7. Differentiation and scaffolds
8. Assessment check
9. Likely misconceptions
10. Resources

### QUALITY CHECKS
- Activities must directly support the writing objective.
- The independent task must require pupils to apply the taught skill.
- Success criteria must be observable rather than vague.
- Do not overload the lesson with too many new writing skills.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Specify whether the lesson should emphasise composition, vocabulary, sentence construction, grammar, punctuation or editing.

### Teacher Tip
Paste the actual curriculum objective or school planning wording when available.

## ENG-W-02 — Model text for purpose and audience

**Purpose:** Generate a high-quality model text that demonstrates a specified writing purpose, audience and form.

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**Output:** Medium  
**Tags:** Primary, English, Writing, Model Texts

### Editable Variables
- Year group
- Genre / form
- Purpose
- Audience
- Topic
- Desired length
- Features to model

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary English teacher and model-text writer.

### CONTEXT
Create a model appropriate for the stated year group. Use the requested purpose, audience and form as the organising constraints.

### TASK
Write a classroom-ready model text that demonstrates the specified writing features naturally and provides material suitable for teacher modelling.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Match vocabulary and sentence complexity to the year group.
- Make the intended purpose and audience clear.
- Use the requested genre conventions accurately.
- Include the specified features without forcing them unnaturally.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
1. Model text
2. Features deliberately demonstrated
3. Three short teacher modelling notes
4. Two questions about the writer's choices

### QUALITY CHECKS
- The text must read naturally rather than like a checklist of features.
- Every highlighted feature must genuinely occur in the text.
- The model must be age appropriate.

### Teacher Tip
Use the model first as a whole text, then annotate selected features during explicit instruction.

## ENG-W-03 — Writing plan / organiser

**Purpose:** Turn a writing objective into a usable pupil planning structure.

**Difficulty:** Beginner  
**Output:** Short  
**Tags:** Primary, English, Writing, Planning

### Editable Variables
- Year group
- Genre
- Purpose and audience
- Topic
- Required sections
- Scaffold level

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary English teacher specialising in writing scaffolds.

### CONTEXT
The planner must support composition without doing the writing for the pupil.

### TASK
Create a pupil-friendly writing organiser that helps pupils plan content, structure and relevant language choices.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Match the organiser to the genre.
- Use prompts rather than completed sentences where independence is expected.
- Include only the planning fields pupils genuinely need.
- Add a vocabulary bank only where it supports the objective.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide a printable table or structured organiser followed by brief teacher notes.

### QUALITY CHECKS
- The organiser must not become a substitute for the pupil's own composition.
- Prompts must directly support the intended writing outcome.
- Layout must be practical for classroom use.

### Teacher Tip
For stronger writers, remove some prompts and leave larger planning spaces to increase independence.

## ENG-W-04 — Sentence construction practice

**Purpose:** Generate targeted sentence-level practice linked to a writing objective.

**Difficulty:** Beginner  
**Output:** Short  
**Tags:** Primary, English, Writing, Sentence Construction

### Editable Variables
- Year group
- Target sentence skill
- Topic
- Number of questions
- Scaffold level

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary English teacher and grammar specialist.

### TASK
Create graduated sentence-construction activities that practise the stated skill before pupils apply it independently.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Start with accessible examples.
- Increase challenge gradually.
- Include at least one application task.

5. One misconception to watch for

### QUALITY CHECKS
Every item must genuinely practise the target skill rather than merely mention it.

## ENG-W-06 — Improve vocabulary and word choice

**Purpose:** Improve deliberate vocabulary choices without encouraging thesaurus-driven writing.

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**Output:** Short  
**Tags:** Primary, English, Writing, Vocabulary

### Editable Variables
- Year group
- Genre
- Topic
- Target vocabulary
- Existing pupil sentence or paragraph

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary English teacher and vocabulary specialist.

### TASK
Analyse the supplied sentence or generate a suitable example, then identify opportunities for more precise, effective or purposeful word choice.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Explain why a suggested word is better, not merely that it is more advanced.
- Prefer precise, context-appropriate vocabulary.
- Avoid replacing every ordinary word with a supposedly sophisticated synonym.
- Preserve the writer's intended meaning.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Use a table with: Original wording | Suggested alternative | Why it works | When to use it. Then provide a short pupil practice task.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Suggested vocabulary must be natural, age appropriate and semantically accurate.

## ENG-W-08 — Writing scaffold

**Purpose:** Provide targeted support for pupils who need additional structure while retaining the same core writing objective.

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**Output:** Short  
**Tags:** Primary, English, Writing, SEND, Differentiation

### Editable Variables
- Year group
- Writing objective
- Genre
- Barrier / support need
- Independence level
- Existing resource

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary teacher specialising in adaptive teaching and writing support.

### TASK
Create a scaffold that reduces avoidable barriers while preserving the core learning objective.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Identify the barrier being addressed.
- Provide only the support needed.
- Use prompts, vocabulary, structures or examples where appropriate.
- Do not complete the pupil's writing for them.
- Include a route toward removing the scaffold.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
1. Barrier addressed
2. Scaffold
3. Teacher guidance
4. Independence-release suggestion

### QUALITY CHECKS
The scaffold must support access rather than silently lowering the intended learning demand.

## ENG-W-11 — Assess writing against criteria

**Purpose:** Support consistent teacher assessment of a supplied piece of pupil writing.

**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**Output:** Medium  
**Tags:** Primary, English, Writing, Assessment

### Editable Variables
- Year group
- Writing objective / criteria
- Genre
- Pupil writing
- Assessment framework or rubric

### ROLE
Act as an assessment support assistant for a primary English teacher. Do not replace professional teacher judgement.

### CONTEXT
Analyse only the supplied writing and stated criteria. Do not infer personal characteristics about the pupil.

### TASK
Evaluate the writing against the supplied criteria, citing specific evidence from the text and identifying strengths and next steps.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Separate evidence from judgement.
- Quote only short excerpts needed to support a point.
- Do not invent evidence.
- Identify uncertainty where criteria are ambiguous.
- Do not assign a statutory or high-stakes judgement unless the teacher provides the applicable framework and asks for support applying it.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
| Criterion | Evidence | Evaluation | Suggested next step |
|---|---|---|---|
Then provide a concise overall summary and two priority teaching actions.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Every judgement must be traceable to the supplied writing and criteria.

### Teacher Tip
Use the output as a moderation aid or starting point for feedback, not as an automated final grade.

## ENG-W-12 — Diagnose writing misconceptions

**Purpose:** Identify likely misconceptions or weaknesses from a supplied piece of writing and suggest targeted teaching responses.

**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**Output:** Medium  
**Tags:** Primary, English, Writing, Misconceptions, Intervention

### Editable Variables
- Year group
- Learning objective
- Pupil writing
- Previous teaching
- Known barrier

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary English teacher and diagnostic assessment specialist.

### CONTEXT
Use only the supplied evidence. Treat inferred causes as hypotheses rather than facts about the pupil.

### TASK
Identify patterns in the writing that may indicate misunderstanding or incomplete mastery, then propose a short diagnostic activity and targeted teaching response.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Distinguish observable error patterns from possible causes.
- Prioritise the highest-impact issue.
- Avoid labelling the pupil.
- Suggest a way to test the hypothesis before planning a longer intervention.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
1. Observable pattern
2. Possible misconception
3. Evidence
4. Quick diagnostic check
5. Targeted teaching response
6. Follow-up check

### QUALITY CHECKS
Do not present inferred causes as diagnoses. Recommendations must be directly connected to the observed writing.
