# SEND Adaptive Teaching & Scaffolding

This family contains ten copy/paste-ready workflows for designing, applying and reviewing adaptive teaching and scaffolding for pupils with different access needs. The workflows are designed to preserve curriculum ambition, maintain the intended learning construct and avoid unsupported diagnostic assumptions.

## SEND-specific adaptive teaching rules

Use these controls in every workflow:

- Start with the intended curriculum objective and learning construct before deciding how teaching should be adapted.
- Treat adaptive teaching as responsive classroom practice, not as a fixed package attached to a pupil label.
- Use supplied pupil information, classroom evidence, work samples and school or specialist guidance where provided. Do not diagnose conditions, impairments, neurodivergence, speech and language needs, medical conditions or mental health conditions.
- Identify the specific access barrier before selecting an adaptation. Do not assume that a named SEND category predicts the support a pupil needs.
- Distinguish access support from reducing the cognitive or disciplinary demand of the curriculum.
- Preserve the intended construct wherever possible. If an adaptation changes the construct, state this explicitly and explain the consequence for assessment.
- Use the least intrusive effective support and plan how support can be reviewed, reduced or faded when appropriate.
- Consider reading age, language load, vocabulary, instruction density, modelling, chunking, examples, visual structure, processing time, response mode, sensory access, communication, physical access, grouping and assistive technology where relevant.
- Avoid creating unnecessary adult dependency. Support should increase access and independence rather than replace the pupil's thinking.
- Do not equate slower processing, movement, limited eye contact, reduced verbal output, handwriting difficulty, non-compliance or alternative communication with lack of understanding unless there is direct evidence that the behaviour is affecting the intended learning.
- Do not use deficit language, stereotypes or assumptions about what a pupil can or cannot achieve.
- Do not invent EHCP provision, specialist recommendations, reasonable adjustments, medical restrictions, safeguarding procedures or school policy.
- Where a decision depends on information not supplied, identify the decision point for the teacher rather than inventing an answer.
- Do not require pupils to disclose diagnoses, disability, family circumstances or personal experiences.
- Keep recommendations practical for a real primary classroom, including staffing, time, resources and classroom routines.
- Use no em dash character in generated output.

## SEND-21: Design Barrier-Led Adaptive Teaching

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT and comparable general-purpose models  
**Expected output length:** 800 to 1,300 words  
**Typical generation time:** 40 to 70 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** SEND, adaptive teaching, barriers to learning, inclusion, lesson planning

### ROLE

Act as an experienced primary teacher and SEND-informed adaptive teaching specialist. Design support from observable barriers to learning rather than from labels or assumptions.

### CONTEXT

Year group: [YEAR_GROUP]

Subject: [SUBJECT]

Curriculum objective: [CURRICULUM_OBJECTIVE]

Learning construct to preserve: [LEARNING_CONSTRUCT]

Lesson or task: [LESSON_OR_TASK]

Observed access barriers: [OBSERVED_ACCESS_BARRIERS]

Relevant pupil information supplied by the teacher: [PUPIL_INFORMATION]

Available resources and adult support: [RESOURCES_AND_ADULT_SUPPORT]

School guidance: [SCHOOL_GUIDANCE]

### TASK

Create a barrier-led adaptive teaching plan that explains how the teacher should respond to each identified barrier while keeping the intended learning ambitious and assessable.

### REQUIREMENTS

For each barrier:

1. describe the observable evidence
2. distinguish the barrier from any unsupported explanation of why it occurs
3. identify the part of the lesson affected
4. propose one or more access adaptations
5. explain why each adaptation addresses the barrier
6. state what aspect of learning remains unchanged
7. identify evidence that would show whether the adaptation is working
8. state when support should be reviewed, reduced or changed

Include universal classroom supports, targeted adaptations and a short decision tree for responding when the initial adaptation is ineffective.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Use:

- Learning construct
- Barrier evidence
- Adaptive teaching plan
- Universal supports
- Targeted supports
- Review and fading decisions
- Assessment implications
- Contingency decisions
- Teacher implementation checklist

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check that recommendations are barrier-led, non-diagnostic, practical, respectful and construct-valid. Do not assume that a pupil needs easier content because they need additional access support.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Add [PUPIL_GROUPS] if the teacher needs to plan for several pupils with different barriers without creating unnecessary individualised plans.

**Example Input:** Year 5 English; objective: write a persuasive paragraph; evidence shows one pupil loses track of multi-step instructions and another finds dense source text difficult to navigate.

**Example Output excerpt:** Break the task into visible stages and reduce unnecessary source-text density while retaining the requirement to select evidence and construct a persuasive argument.

## SEND-22: Build a Scaffold From Supported to Independent Performance

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT and comparable general-purpose models  
**Expected output length:** 800 to 1,200 words  
**Typical generation time:** 40 to 70 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** SEND, scaffolding, independence, adaptive teaching, learning support

### ROLE

Act as a primary teacher who designs temporary scaffolds that enable pupils to perform the intended learning and then deliberately reduces support as independence develops.

### CONTEXT

Year group: [YEAR_GROUP]

Subject: [SUBJECT]

Learning objective: [OBJECTIVE]

Task: [TASK]

Current evidence of performance: [CURRENT_EVIDENCE]

Access barriers: [ACCESS_BARRIERS]

Existing support: [EXISTING_SUPPORT]

### TASK

Create a graduated scaffold that moves from high support towards independent performance.

### REQUIREMENTS

Create up to five stages, for example:

1. teacher modelling
2. worked example or shared construction
3. guided practice
4. partially completed support
5. independent application

For each stage specify the teacher action, pupil action, prompt level, resource or representation, evidence of readiness to fade and what to do if removing support reveals a genuine access barrier.

Separate scaffolding from task simplification. If a scaffold risks changing the construct, explain how to redesign it.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Provide a scaffold ladder followed by:

- teacher prompts
- pupil independence indicators
- fading criteria
- reintroduction criteria
- assessment implications
- common implementation errors

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check that the scaffold supports thinking rather than doing the thinking for the pupil. Avoid permanent prompting where temporary support would be sufficient.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Add [GROUP_SIZE], [ADULT_SUPPORT] and [TIME_AVAILABLE].

**Example Input:** Year 4 maths; objective: explain a multi-step problem-solving method; pupils currently rely on teacher prompts for each step.

**Example Output excerpt:** Move from a fully modelled example to a visual step sequence, then remove individual prompts while retaining the mathematical reasoning requirement.

**Typical generation time:** 30 to 60 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** SEND, instructions, language load, adaptive teaching, accessibility

Act as a primary teacher specialising in clear instruction design and accessible classroom communication.

### CONTEXT

Year group: [YEAR_GROUP]

Subject: [SUBJECT]

Learning objective: [OBJECTIVE]

Original instructions: [ORIGINAL_INSTRUCTIONS]

Observed instruction barrier: [INSTRUCTION_BARRIER]

Pupil access information: [ACCESS_INFORMATION]

### TASK

Rewrite and redesign the teaching instructions so pupils can access the task without unnecessarily reducing its intellectual demand.

### REQUIREMENTS

Analyse the original instructions for:

- vocabulary load
- number of steps
- sequencing
- abstract language
- unnecessary wording
- working-memory demand
- visual organisation

- Check for understanding

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

**Example Input:** Year 2 science; pupils must sort materials, record observations and explain one classification decision; several pupils struggle with long verbal instructions.

**Example Output excerpt:** Present the same three-part scientific task as a numbered visual sequence, model the first classification decision, then check pupils can explain what they must do before independent work.

## Family-level quality assurance

Before treating this family as complete, verify every workflow against the following controls:

### 1. Barrier-led practice

Adaptations must respond to observable access barriers or supplied evidence, not to SEND labels alone.

### 2. No diagnosis

The workflows must never diagnose, infer or confirm a medical, developmental, neurological, speech and language or mental health condition from classroom evidence.

### 3. Curriculum ambition

Support should remove unnecessary barriers without automatically reducing curriculum entitlement, cognitive demand or disciplinary expectations.

### 4. Construct validity

The intended learning construct must be explicit. Alternative access routes must not accidentally replace the construct being taught or assessed.

### 5. Independence and fading

Scaffolds should have a purpose, review point and appropriate fading route. Support must not create unnecessary adult or prompt dependency.

### 6. Communication and response access

Alternative response modes should be used where they preserve the intended construct. Communication differences must not automatically be treated as learning deficits.

### 7. Reading and language access

Language-load adaptations must distinguish incidental literacy demands from vocabulary or language that is essential to the subject construct.

### 8. Sensory, physical and processing access

Adaptations should address genuine access requirements without making assumptions about pupils based on diagnosis, appearance or behaviour.

### 9. Dignity and inclusion

Grouping, adult support and classroom routines must provide meaningful participation and avoid segregation, tokenism, deficit language or passive helper roles.

### 10. Evidence-led review

Recommendations should specify what evidence will be collected, what conclusions are justified and when support should be continued, modified, faded or replaced.

### 11. Statutory and specialist boundaries

Do not invent EHCP provision, specialist recommendations, reasonable adjustments, medical restrictions or school procedures. Identify missing information for teacher or specialist judgement.

### 12. Safeguarding and personal information

Do not require pupils to disclose diagnoses, disability, family circumstances or sensitive experiences. Use established school procedures where safeguarding information is supplied.

### 13. Practical classroom feasibility

Recommendations should account for class size, lesson time, adult availability, resources, routines and teacher workload.

### 14. Respectful terminology

Use respectful, precise SEND language. Avoid deficit-based descriptions and unsupported assumptions about capability.

### 15. Assessment integrity

Do not confuse performance on incidental barriers such as handwriting speed, verbal fluency, reading load or response speed with the intended learning unless those features are part of the construct.

### 16. Missing-information protocol

Where a recommendation depends on school policy, specialist advice, statutory information, current guidance or pupil-specific evidence that has not been supplied, flag the gap rather than inventing an answer.

All ten workflows in this family should pass these quality gates before the family is submitted for structural, representative and targeted verification.
