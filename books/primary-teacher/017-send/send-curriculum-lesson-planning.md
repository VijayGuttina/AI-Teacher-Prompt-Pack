# SEND Curriculum & Lesson Planning

This family contains ten copy/paste-ready workflows for planning inclusive primary teaching while maintaining curriculum ambition and avoiding unsupported diagnostic assumptions.

## SEND-specific planning rules

Use these controls in every workflow:

- Start with the curriculum objective and intended learning construct, then determine what access support is needed.
- Use supplied pupil information, classroom evidence and school guidance. Do not diagnose SEND, neurodivergence, speech and language conditions, medical conditions or mental health conditions.
- Distinguish a barrier to learning from a pupil characteristic. Do not infer a cause simply because a pupil is struggling.
- Adapt access without automatically reducing cognitive demand, curriculum entitlement or expected outcome.
- Consider reading age, vocabulary load, instructions, visual layout, modelling, chunking, processing time, response mode, sensory access, communication, grouping and assistive technology where relevant.
- Preserve the intended construct. If an adaptation changes what is being assessed, state the change explicitly.
- Use precise, respectful language and avoid deficit-based descriptions, stereotypes or assumptions about disability.
- Do not invent EHCP provision, reasonable adjustments, specialist advice, safeguarding procedures or school policy.
- Where school-specific information is required but not supplied, identify it as a decision point for the teacher rather than inventing an answer.
- Do not require pupils to disclose personal information about disability, diagnosis, family circumstances or support needs.
- Keep outputs practical for a real primary classroom, including time, resources, grouping and likely barriers.
- Use no em dash character in generated output.

## SEND-01: Plan a Lesson From a Curriculum Objective

**Difficulty:** Beginner  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT and comparable general-purpose models  
**Expected output length:** 700 to 1,100 words  
**Typical generation time:** 30 to 60 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** curriculum planning, SEND, adaptive teaching, inclusion, lesson planning

### ROLE

Act as an experienced primary teacher and SEND-informed curriculum planner. Your job is to turn the supplied curriculum objective into an ambitious, accessible lesson without making diagnostic assumptions about pupils.

### CONTEXT

Year group: [YEAR GROUP]

Subject: [SUBJECT]

Curriculum objective: [CURRICULUM OBJECTIVE]

Lesson length: [LESSON LENGTH]

Prior learning: [PRIOR LEARNING]

Known barriers or access needs supplied by the teacher: [BARRIERS_OR_ACCESS_NEEDS]

Available resources: [AVAILABLE_RESOURCES]

School guidance that must be followed: [SCHOOL_GUIDANCE]

### TASK

Create a complete lesson plan that preserves the intended curriculum ambition while removing unnecessary access barriers.

### REQUIREMENTS

Include:

1. learning intention and measurable success criteria
2. prior-knowledge check
3. key vocabulary
4. explicit teacher modelling
5. guided practice
6. independent or collaborative application
7. formative assessment points
8. likely misconceptions or barriers
9. adaptive teaching responses linked to the supplied barriers
10. support that can be faded rather than permanently attached
11. an appropriate extension route that increases depth rather than simply adding volume
12. a short contingency if the planned approach creates an access barrier

For every adaptation, explain which access barrier it addresses and how the intended learning remains intact.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Use these headings:

- Lesson overview
- Learning intention
- Success criteria
- Prior learning and diagnostic check
- Vocabulary
- Teacher modelling
- Guided practice
- Independent application
- Assessment opportunities
- Likely barriers and misconceptions
- Adaptive teaching plan
- Support to fade
- Extension
- Contingency
- Resources
- Teacher quality check

### QUALITY CHECKS

Before finalising, check that the lesson does not diagnose pupils, lower expectations by default, assess confidence instead of learning, require personal disclosure or invent school-specific procedures.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

If [BARRIERS_OR_ACCESS_NEEDS] is incomplete, produce a useful baseline plan and clearly mark which adaptations require teacher judgement.

**Example Input:** Year 4 science; objective: describe the functions of parts of a flowering plant; 60 minutes; one pupil benefits from reduced language load and visual sequencing; one pupil needs additional processing time.

**Example Output excerpt:** The core scientific construct remains identifying and explaining plant-part functions. Reduce instruction density, provide a visual sequence and allow additional processing time without replacing the explanation task with copying.

## SEND-02: Build an Inclusive Unit Sequence

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT and comparable general-purpose models  
**Expected output length:** 1,000 to 1,600 words  
**Typical generation time:** 45 to 75 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** SEND, unit planning, progression, adaptive teaching, inclusion

### ROLE

Act as a primary curriculum and SEND planning specialist who designs coherent sequences rather than isolated accommodations.

### CONTEXT

Year group: [YEAR GROUP]

Subject: [SUBJECT]

Unit topic: [UNIT TOPIC]

Curriculum objectives: [CURRICULUM_OBJECTIVES]

Number of lessons: [NUMBER_OF_LESSONS]

Prior learning: [PRIOR_LEARNING]

Known barriers or access needs: [BARRIERS_OR_ACCESS_NEEDS]

Resources: [AVAILABLE_RESOURCES]

### TASK

Sequence the unit so that knowledge and skills build cumulatively while access barriers are anticipated and addressed.

### REQUIREMENTS

Show for every lesson: objective, prerequisite knowledge, new content, vocabulary, modelling, practice, assessment, likely barrier, planned access support and how support will be reviewed or faded.

Do not create a separate low-expectation pathway merely because pupils need additional support. Where pupils need different routes to demonstrate the same construct, explain the route.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Provide:

- unit intent
- progression map
- lesson-by-lesson sequence
- cumulative vocabulary
- barrier and adaptation map
- assessment checkpoints
- intervention or revisit points
- teacher implementation notes
- final quality check

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check progression, curriculum ambition, construct validity, accessibility, realistic workload and absence of diagnostic claims.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Add [SCHOOL_TERM_DATES] if the sequence must fit a particular calendar.

**Example Input:** Year 5 history; topic: the Anglo-Saxons; eight lessons; mixed reading fluency; objectives supplied by teacher.

**Example Output excerpt:** Each lesson builds historical knowledge before requiring increasingly independent explanation. Reading support changes access to sources, not the historical reasoning being assessed.

## SEND-04: Create a Differentiation and Scaffolding Plan

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT and comparable general-purpose models  
**Expected output length:** 700 to 1,100 words  
**Typical generation time:** 30 to 60 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** differentiation, scaffolding, SEND, adaptive teaching

### ROLE

Act as a primary teacher who uses evidence-informed scaffolding and avoids creating permanent dependency.

### CONTEXT

Learning objective: [LEARNING_OBJECTIVE]

Year group: [YEAR_GROUP]

Task: [TASK]

Current pupil evidence: [PUPIL_EVIDENCE]

Known access barriers: [ACCESS_BARRIERS]

### TASK

Create a graduated scaffold from initial access support towards greater independence.

### REQUIREMENTS

For each scaffold, state its purpose, teacher action, pupil action, evidence of readiness to fade and what to do if fading exposes a genuine barrier.

Avoid assuming that more support is always better.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Provide a scaffold ladder with five levels where appropriate, followed by teacher prompts and fading criteria.

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check that scaffolding supports access and independence without changing the intended construct unless explicitly stated.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Use [PUPIL_GROUPS] to distinguish different access profiles without assigning unsupported diagnoses.

**Example Input:** Year 6 writing; objective: write a balanced argument; some pupils need planning support and sentence-level organisation.

**Example Output excerpt:** Begin with a planning structure, model how reasons connect to evidence, then remove prompts as pupils demonstrate independent organisation.

### CONTEXT

### TASK

### REQUIREMENTS

**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT and comparable general-purpose models  
**Expected output length:** 800 to 1,300 words  
**Typical generation time:** 40 to 70 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** SEND, assessment, re-teaching, adaptive teaching, intervention

### ROLE

Act as a primary teacher reviewing lesson evidence to decide what should be retaught, adapted or practised next.

### CONTEXT

Year group: [YEAR_GROUP]

Subject: [SUBJECT]

Objective: [OBJECTIVE]

Assessment evidence: [ASSESSMENT_EVIDENCE]

Known access barriers: [ACCESS_BARRIERS]

### TASK

Analyse the evidence without assuming that an unsuccessful response proves a specific SEND need.

### REQUIREMENTS

Separate possible knowledge gaps from access barriers, task design issues, vocabulary barriers and response-mode barriers. Recommend the smallest useful next teaching step and explain what evidence would confirm improvement.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Provide:

- evidence summary
- construct being assessed
- possible explanations to investigate
- immediate reteach
- access adaptation
- independent practice
- reassessment

### QUALITY CHECKS

Do not diagnose. Do not treat one assessment response as proof of an underlying condition.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Add [PUPIL_WORK_SAMPLES] where appropriate.

**Example Input:** Year 3 writing; pupils have weak explanations of cause and effect; some pupils also have high language load from the task.

**Example Output excerpt:** First reduce unnecessary language in the instruction and inspect whether the explanation construct remains weak. Then reteach causal connectives and reasoning where evidence supports that conclusion.

# Family Quality Control

Before releasing any output from this family, confirm all of the following:

- The curriculum objective is explicit.
- The intended construct is explicit.
- Adaptations are linked to observable or supplied access information.
- No unsupported diagnosis is made.
- SEND is not treated as synonymous with low attainment.
- Access support does not automatically reduce cognitive demand.
- Scaffolds can be reviewed and faded.
- Alternative response modes preserve the intended construct where possible.
- Reading, language, sensory, physical, communication and processing barriers are considered separately where relevant.
- Assessment does not measure confidence, personality, disclosure or conformity.
- Pupil dignity is protected.
- No stereotypes or deficit assumptions are used.
- No EHCP, safeguarding, specialist or school-specific arrangements are invented.
- Missing information is surfaced as a teacher decision point.
- The plan is feasible in a real primary classroom.
- Resources, time, grouping and contingencies are considered.
- The final output is copy/paste-ready.
