# 017 SEND Framework

## Purpose

This framework defines the architecture, quality standards and verification approach for the Primary Teacher Prompt Pack: Special Educational Needs and Disabilities (SEND).

The module is designed to help primary teachers plan inclusive teaching, understand and respond to barriers to learning, adapt instruction and resources, support participation, analyse evidence, plan intervention and communicate effectively with colleagues and families.

The prompts support teacher judgement. They do not diagnose disability, prescribe therapy, determine statutory entitlement, replace specialist advice, or invent school procedures. Where pupil-specific information, professional advice, an EHCP, school policy or current statutory guidance is required, the prompt must make that dependency explicit.

SEND support should begin with the pupil, the intended learning and the barriers affecting access or participation. A diagnosis must not become a substitute for understanding the actual learning need.

## Core Principles

1. **Start with the learner**: identify the intended learning, participation goal and observable barrier before selecting an adaptation.
2. **Inclusion is access, participation and progress**: avoid treating physical presence in a classroom as sufficient evidence of inclusion.
3. **Diagnosis is not destiny**: a diagnostic label can inform planning but must not replace evidence about the individual pupil.
4. **Do not diagnose**: prompts must not infer or assign a medical, psychological, developmental or educational diagnosis from limited classroom evidence.
5. **Preserve ambition**: SEND adaptation should not automatically mean easier work, reduced vocabulary, lower cognitive demand or removal of the intended curriculum construct.
6. **Adaptive teaching before unnecessary separation**: use flexible teaching and access strategies where these meet the need, while recognising when additional targeted provision may be appropriate.
7. **Barrier before label**: distinguish barriers caused by task design, language, environment, prior knowledge, communication, sensory demand, processing demand or other factors from assumptions about the pupil.
8. **Evidence before intervention**: interventions should have a defined purpose, baseline evidence, success criteria, review point and proportionate evaluation.
9. **Pupil dignity and agency**: avoid deficit language, public labelling, unnecessary disclosure and practices that remove pupil voice or choice.
10. **Strengths matter**: identify strengths, interests, successful strategies and existing independence alongside areas requiring support.
11. **Communication is access**: provide appropriate ways for pupils to understand, express, interact and demonstrate learning.
12. **Accessibility preserves meaning**: an alternative format should preserve the intended learning wherever possible.
13. **Reasonable adjustments require context**: do not invent statutory entitlement, school procedures or individual adjustments without supplied information.
14. **Professional boundaries**: distinguish classroom support from medical treatment, occupational therapy, speech and language therapy, counselling and clinical assessment.
15. **Safeguarding remains separate but connected**: SEND status does not itself explain safeguarding concerns and must not be used as a substitute for safeguarding procedures.
16. **Family partnership without blame**: communicate respectfully and avoid assumptions about parenting, home circumstances or family expertise.
17. **Current guidance must be verified**: statutory SEND requirements and local procedures can change and should be checked against authoritative current sources when relevant.
18. **Teacher judgement remains central**: generated outputs are planning support, not a replacement for professional judgement, specialist advice or school policy.

Where relevant, prompts should expose:

- year group
- pupil age
- curriculum subject
- curriculum objective
- intended learning construct
- current attainment or supplied evidence
- strengths and interests
- identified barriers to learning or participation
- supplied SEND information
- communication profile
- receptive and expressive language considerations
- sensory considerations
- physical access considerations
- attention and processing considerations
- memory and executive-function considerations
- reading and writing access
- social interaction considerations
- regulation considerations
- pupil voice
- successful existing strategies
- support already in place
- specialist advice supplied by the school
- EHCP or other formal-plan information where the teacher is authorised to use it
- school SEND policy
- lesson duration
- class context
- available resources
- assistive technology
- assessment purpose
- review date

Prompts must never require unnecessary personal or medical information. Use only the information needed for the task.

## Barrier-to-Access Model

Use this sequence where appropriate:

**Intended learning → Participation demand → Potential barrier → Evidence → Adaptation → Supported practice → Independent opportunity → Evidence of impact → Review**

The purpose is to avoid jumping directly from a label to a generic strategy.

For example, rather than assuming a pupil needs simplified work because of a diagnosis, identify whether the actual barrier is reading load, working-memory demand, unfamiliar vocabulary, task sequencing, visual access, processing time, motor demand or another evidenced factor.

## Adaptive Teaching Model

Prompts should support:

**Model → Scaffold → Guided practice → Check understanding → Adjust → Practise → Fade support → Apply independently → Review**

Adaptations may include:

- explicit modelling
- worked examples
- chunking
- visual supports
- concrete representations
- vocabulary pre-teaching
- dual coding where useful
- structured retrieval
- rehearsal
- repetition
- reduced extraneous information
- step-by-step instructions
- task sequencing
- processing time
- alternative response modes
- guided peer interaction
- environmental adjustments
- assistive technology where supplied and appropriate

Support should be faded when evidence shows increased independence. Permanent over-support can create dependency and obscure what a pupil can do independently.

## Communication, Language and Interaction Model

Communication support may address:

- receptive language
- expressive language
- vocabulary
- concept language
- instruction length
- question structure
- processing time
- visual communication
- AAC where supplied or professionally advised
- turn-taking
- interaction structure
- alternative response modes

Do not assume limited spoken language means limited understanding. Do not treat speech differences as evidence of lower cognitive ability.

Do not invent clinical communication advice or claim that a particular intervention is appropriate without relevant professional or school-supplied information.

## Cognition, Learning and Executive Function

Prompts may address:

- attention
- working memory
- retrieval
- processing speed
- sequencing
- planning
- organisation
- task initiation
- inhibition
- flexible thinking
- checking and self-monitoring
- metacognition

Strategies should be tied to the actual task demand. Avoid presenting broad claims about a diagnostic group as if they apply to every pupil.

## Sensory and Physical Access

Prompts may address:

- visual access
- auditory access
- sensory load
- classroom layout
- movement and positioning
- physical access to resources
- writing and motor demands
- alternative recording methods
- lighting, noise or environmental considerations where supplied
- accessible digital formats

Do not prescribe medical, physical or therapeutic interventions. Where specialist advice is required, explicitly defer to that advice.

## Neurodiversity, Regulation and Participation

Prompts should recognise variation in:

- communication
- attention
- sensory processing
- predictability needs
- transitions
- social interaction
- interests
- emotional regulation
- executive functioning
- response to environmental demands

Avoid deficit-only descriptions and stereotypes. Do not assume that all autistic pupils, pupils with ADHD, or other neurodivergent pupils share identical needs or strengths.

Regulation strategies should be educational and classroom-focused. They must not be presented as clinical treatment or used to justify removing a pupil from learning without appropriate context and school procedures.

## Assessment and Progress Model

Assessment should distinguish among:

- access to the task
- knowledge
- understanding
- application
- independence
- communication of learning
- fluency
- accuracy
- progress over time
- response to an intervention

Prompts should ask whether an adaptation changes the construct being assessed. For example, changing handwriting demands may preserve a writing-content construct in some contexts, while simplifying the language itself may change the construct.

Intervention evaluation should include:

**Baseline → Intended outcome → Strategy → Frequency/duration where supplied → Evidence measure → Review point → Outcome → Next decision**

Do not claim an intervention caused progress where the evidence does not support that conclusion.

## Resources and Technology Model

Accessible resources should be evaluated for:

- reading age
- language load
- visual clarity
- navigation
- contrast where relevant
- alternative text where relevant
- captions/transcripts where relevant
- audio access
- response method
- motor demand
- cognitive load
- compatibility with supplied assistive technology
- privacy
- copyright

Assistive technology should be treated as an access tool, not as evidence of reduced ability. Do not recommend specific medical or specialist equipment without appropriate context.

## Pupil Voice and Partnership

Pupil voice should be used to understand:

- what helps
- what creates barriers
- what the pupil can already do
- preferred ways of accessing or demonstrating learning
- successful routines
- goals for increasing independence

Pupil voice must not become an expectation that pupils disclose sensitive medical, family or emotional information.

Family communication should:

- describe observable learning and access needs
- recognise strengths
- avoid blame
- distinguish school evidence from professional opinion
- avoid jargon where it is unnecessary
- avoid promises about outcomes
- explain practical next steps
- respect confidentiality

## Safeguarding and Professional Boundaries

SEND prompts must not:

- diagnose a condition
- prescribe treatment
- recommend medication changes
- provide clinical assessment
- provide psychotherapy or counselling
- invent safeguarding procedures
- invent EHCP processes or statutory decisions
- assume a pupil's home circumstances
- require disclosure of private information

If a pupil discloses a safeguarding concern, the teacher must follow the school's current safeguarding procedure and designated safeguarding arrangements. The prompt may help structure neutral documentation or classroom planning only where appropriate, but must not invent the reporting route.

## SEND Language Standards

Use precise, respectful language. Avoid:

- describing pupils solely by diagnosis
- deficit-only descriptions
- "normal" versus "abnormal" pupil comparisons
- claims that a diagnosis automatically determines ability
- assumptions that all pupils with a named condition need the same support
- language that frames accommodations as unfair advantages
- language that implies independence means receiving no support

Prefer language that describes the pupil, context, barrier, strength, support and observed outcome.

## Missing Information Protocol

When essential pupil, school or professional information is missing:

1. Identify the missing information.
2. Explain why it matters.
3. Use a neutral placeholder or clearly labelled assumption where possible.
4. Provide a safe generic option that does not rely on the missing detail.
5. State what should be checked with the appropriate school professional, policy or authoritative guidance.

Never invent:

- diagnoses
- EHCP content
- reasonable adjustments
- specialist recommendations
- therapy programmes
- safeguarding routes
- school SEND policies
- local authority procedures
- statutory interpretations

## Quality Gates

Every SEND workflow must pass the following gates:

### Curriculum and construct
- Intended learning is explicit.
- Adaptations are linked to the barrier and task demand.
- Curriculum ambition is preserved unless a supplied plan explicitly requires otherwise.
- Any construct change is clearly identified.

### Evidence and identification
- Classroom evidence is distinguished from diagnosis.
- Observations are specific and contextual.
- Strengths and successful strategies are considered.
- Claims about causation are proportionate to the evidence.

### Inclusion and dignity
- Avoids deficit framing and stereotypes.
- Does not unnecessarily label pupils publicly.
- Protects privacy and dignity.
- Supports meaningful participation and independence.

### Communication and access
- Provides appropriate access to instructions, resources and response modes.
- Does not equate speech, handwriting or English proficiency with cognitive ability.
- Considers hearing, visual, communication and motor access where relevant.

### Adaptive teaching
- Strategies are practical and linked to the identified barrier.
- Scaffolds can be faded where appropriate.
- Support does not automatically reduce cognitive or linguistic demand.

### Assessment and intervention
- Assessment is construct-valid.
- Baseline, outcome and review are explicit for intervention work.
- Progress claims are evidence-led.
- Pupil independence is considered.

### Professional boundaries
- No diagnosis, clinical treatment or medication advice.
- Specialist advice is not invented.
- School policy and safeguarding procedures are not fabricated.

### Current guidance
- Current statutory or policy claims are flagged for authoritative verification when relevant.
- Local procedures are treated as variables rather than assumptions.

## Verification Model

SEND will follow the same release discipline as the other 100-workflow modules:

1. **Framework integrity**: exactly 10 families × 10 workflows = 100 workflows.
2. **Structural QA**: 100/100 workflows complete against the master specification.
3. **Representative execution testing**: exactly 20 workflows, two per family.
4. **Targeted risk checks**: exactly 16 SEND-specific checks.
5. **Verification record and freeze** only after all required stages pass.

Structural completeness does not constitute execution verification.

## Targeted Risk Checks

1. Avoidance of diagnosis from classroom evidence
2. Barrier-led rather than label-led adaptation
3. Preservation of curriculum ambition and intended construct
4. Accurate and respectful SEND terminology
5. Avoidance of deficit framing and stereotyping
6. Communication and language access without assumptions about cognition
7. Sensory and physical accessibility without clinical prescribing
8. Neurodiversity-aware practice without overgeneralisation
9. Regulation and participation strategies within educational boundaries
10. Construct-valid assessment and alternative response modes
11. Evidence-led intervention design and review
12. Appropriate use of assistive technology and accessible resources
13. Pupil voice, dignity, privacy and agency
14. Family and professional partnership without blame or invented advice
15. Safeguarding, EHCP and statutory-boundary handling
16. Safe handling of missing pupil, school, specialist and current-guidance information

- pupil dignity, privacy, agency and voice are protected

- safeguarding and statutory-boundary handling is explicit
- missing-information handling is explicit

- representative execution testing passes 20/20
