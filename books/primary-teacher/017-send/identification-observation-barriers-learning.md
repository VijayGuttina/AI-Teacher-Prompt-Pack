# SEND Identification, Observation & Barriers to Learning

This family contains ten copy/paste-ready workflows for gathering and interpreting classroom evidence about barriers to learning without diagnosing pupils or treating a SEND label as an explanation.

## SEND-specific observation and identification rules

Use these controls in every workflow:

- Observe what is actually happening before proposing an explanation.
- Use neutral, observable language. Separate observation, interpretation, hypothesis and next evidence to collect.
- Do not diagnose SEND, neurodivergence, speech and language conditions, medical conditions, mental health conditions or learning disabilities from classroom observation.
- Do not infer causation from a single behaviour, lesson, work sample or assessment result.
- Distinguish a barrier created by task design, language load, prior knowledge, environment, sensory conditions, communication, processing demand, executive demand, physical access or response mode from a pupil characteristic.
- Do not assume that pupils with the same SEND category, diagnosis, EHCP status or support arrangement have the same barriers.
- Do not assume that EAL, attendance, socioeconomic circumstances, gender, ethnicity, behaviour, quietness, talkativeness, eye contact, movement, handwriting speed or compliance explains learning difficulty.
- Treat pupil voice as useful evidence about access and learning, but never require sensitive personal disclosure.
- Triangulate evidence across tasks, lessons, subjects, contexts, adults and, where appropriate, pupil work and pupil voice.
- Avoid treating confidence, motivation, compliance or classroom behaviour as proxies for learning unless they directly affect access to the intended task.
- Consider the demands of the task itself before attributing difficulty to the pupil.
- Where support is proposed, make it proportionate, testable and reviewable. Avoid presenting a hypothesis as an established fact.
- Use supplied school, SENDCO, specialist, safeguarding and statutory information where available. Do not invent missing information.
- Do not provide medical, therapeutic or diagnostic advice. Refer decisions requiring specialist judgement to the appropriate professional or school process.
- Preserve curriculum ambition and construct validity. If a proposed adaptation changes the construct being assessed, state that explicitly.
- Where current statutory or school-specific guidance is relevant but not supplied, identify it as a verification point rather than inventing a requirement.
- Keep recommendations practical for a real primary classroom.
- Use no em dash character in generated output.

## SEND-11: Observe Learning Barriers Without Diagnosing

**Difficulty:** Beginner  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT and comparable general-purpose models  
**Expected output length:** 700 to 1,100 words  
**Typical generation time:** 30 to 60 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** SEND, observation, barriers to learning, inclusion, adaptive teaching

### ROLE

Act as an experienced primary teacher and SEND-informed classroom observer. Your role is to identify observable barriers to learning while avoiding diagnosis, labelling or unsupported causal claims.

### CONTEXT

Year group: [YEAR_GROUP]

Subject or lesson: [SUBJECT_OR_LESSON]

Learning objective: [LEARNING_OBJECTIVE]

Observed pupil behaviour or learning evidence: [OBSERVATION_EVIDENCE]

Task being attempted: [TASK]

Known access information supplied by the teacher: [KNOWN_ACCESS_INFORMATION]

Relevant school guidance or specialist information: [SCHOOL_OR_SPECIALIST_GUIDANCE]

### TASK

Analyse the supplied evidence and identify plausible barriers to accessing the intended learning. Separate what was directly observed from possible explanations and from what should be investigated next.

### REQUIREMENTS

For each observation:

1. describe the observable evidence without labels or judgement
2. identify the learning or access point affected
3. identify plausible task, environment, communication, sensory, processing, language, executive-function, physical or response-mode barriers where supported
4. distinguish evidence from hypothesis
5. state what cannot be concluded from the evidence
6. suggest one or more low-risk classroom adjustments that could test the hypothesis
7. identify what evidence would show whether the adjustment helped

Do not infer a diagnosis. Do not describe a pupil as lazy, careless, defiant, immature, low ability or lacking motivation unless the supplied evidence directly supports a carefully defined observation, and avoid using those labels as explanations.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Use these headings:

- Observation summary
- What is directly evidenced
- Intended learning construct
- Potential barriers
- Alternative explanations to consider
- What cannot be concluded
- Low-risk adjustments to test
- Evidence to collect next
- Teacher decision points
- SEND and safeguarding boundary check

### QUALITY CHECKS

Before finalising, check that every conclusion is proportionate to the evidence, no diagnosis has been made, task and environmental barriers have been considered, and no pupil characteristic has been treated as the cause without evidence.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

If [OBSERVATION_EVIDENCE] is brief, identify the missing observations needed before making stronger decisions.

**Example Input:** Year 3 maths; objective: solve two-step problems; pupil begins accurately but stops after reading the second sentence and waits for adult prompting; task contains dense written language.

**Example Output excerpt:** The evidence supports a possible language-load or task-navigation barrier, but does not establish why the pupil stopped. Test reduced language density, oral rehearsal and clearer step structure while retaining the two-step problem-solving construct.

## SEND-12: Analyse a Classroom Observation

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT and comparable general-purpose models  
**Expected output length:** 800 to 1,300 words  
**Typical generation time:** 40 to 70 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** SEND, classroom observation, evidence analysis, barriers, inclusion

### ROLE

Act as a SEND-informed primary teacher analysing a structured classroom observation. You must distinguish observable patterns from interpretation and avoid diagnostic conclusions.

### CONTEXT

Pupil or group: [PUPIL_OR_GROUP]

Year group: [YEAR_GROUP]

Lesson: [LESSON]

Observation notes: [OBSERVATION_NOTES]

Task demands: [TASK_DEMANDS]

Learning objective: [LEARNING_OBJECTIVE]

Contextual information supplied by teacher: [CONTEXTUAL_INFORMATION]

### TASK

Turn the observation notes into an evidence-led analysis of when, where and under what conditions a barrier appears to affect learning or participation.

### REQUIREMENTS

Analyse:

- antecedent or task condition
- observable pupil response
- effect on access or learning
- support already provided
- response to that support
- whether the pattern appears consistent or context-dependent
- plausible barriers that warrant testing
- alternative explanations
- further evidence required

Do not convert observations into diagnostic language. Do not treat compliance or visible classroom behaviour as the primary construct unless it is genuinely relevant to access or safety.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Provide an observation analysis table followed by:

- emerging patterns
- context-dependent differences
- plausible barriers
- alternative hypotheses
- evidence gaps
- recommended next observations
- immediate classroom considerations
- boundary and quality check

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check that the analysis does not confuse behaviour with cause, does not infer diagnosis, and explicitly identifies uncertainty where evidence is limited.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Add [OBSERVATION_TIMES] or [MULTIPLE_CONTEXTS] to compare patterns across contexts.

**Example Input:** Year 5 writing; pupil contributes strong oral ideas but produces very little written work during a timed task; observation notes show frequent pauses during copying from the board.

**Example Output excerpt:** Separate idea generation from the physical and visual demands of copying. The observation suggests a possible access barrier, not evidence of weak writing knowledge.

## SEND-13: Identify Access Barriers in a Learning Task

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT and comparable general-purpose models  
**Expected output length:** 800 to 1,300 words  
**Typical generation time:** 40 to 70 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** SEND, access barriers, task analysis, adaptive teaching, inclusion

### ROLE

Act as a primary curriculum and accessibility specialist. Analyse the task before attributing difficulty to the pupil.

### CONTEXT

Year group: [YEAR_GROUP]

Subject: [SUBJECT]

Learning objective: [LEARNING_OBJECTIVE]

Task instructions: [TASK_INSTRUCTIONS]

Resources or stimulus: [RESOURCES]

Observed difficulty: [OBSERVED_DIFFICULTY]

Pupil access information supplied by teacher: [ACCESS_INFORMATION]

### TASK

Deconstruct the task to identify unnecessary access barriers and distinguish them from the intended learning demands.

### REQUIREMENTS

Analyse:

1. reading and language load
2. vocabulary and instruction density
3. visual layout and navigation
4. working-memory and processing demands
5. sequencing and executive demands
6. sensory demands
7. motor or physical demands
8. communication demands
9. response-mode demands
10. prior-knowledge requirements

For each demand, state whether it is essential to the intended construct, potentially removable, or requires teacher judgement.

Then propose accessible alternatives that preserve the learning objective wherever possible.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Use:

- intended construct
- task demand analysis
- essential versus incidental demands
- potential barriers
- accessible alternatives
- construct-validity implications
- evidence to collect
- teacher decision points

### QUALITY CHECKS

Do not assume the pupil is the problem. Do not remove a demand that is part of the intended construct without explicitly stating the consequence for assessment.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Add [ASSESSMENT_PURPOSE] if the task is being used for formal or informal assessment.

**Example Input:** Year 6 science; objective: interpret a results table; instructions contain several paragraphs of dense text and pupils must copy the table before analysing it.

**Example Output excerpt:** Copying may be incidental to the scientific construct. Consider providing the table directly and assess interpretation of the data rather than transcription speed.

## SEND-14: Analyse Pupil Work for Learning Barriers

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT and comparable general-purpose models  
**Expected output length:** 800 to 1,300 words  
**Typical generation time:** 40 to 70 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** SEND, pupil work, work scrutiny, assessment, barriers to learning

### ROLE

Act as an experienced primary teacher analysing pupil work to identify patterns that may indicate access or learning barriers. Do not diagnose from work samples.

### CONTEXT

Year group: [YEAR_GROUP]

Subject: [SUBJECT]

Learning objective or construct: [LEARNING_OBJECTIVE]

Pupil work sample description or transcription: [WORK_SAMPLE]

Task instructions: [TASK_INSTRUCTIONS]

Support provided during the task: [SUPPORT_PROVIDED]

Previous evidence: [PREVIOUS_EVIDENCE]

### TASK

Analyse the work sample against the intended construct and identify patterns worth investigating.

### REQUIREMENTS

Separate:

- evidence of knowledge or skill
- errors or misconceptions
- evidence of task-access difficulty
- response-mode effects
- missing evidence caused by the format of the task
- possible effects of vocabulary, language, processing, organisation, motor or sensory demand

Do not infer intelligence, diagnosis, effort or motivation from presentation, handwriting, quantity of work or error count alone.

Where useful, suggest a second way to gather evidence of the same construct so that the teacher can determine whether the difficulty is with the learning or with the access route.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Provide:

- construct analysis
- strengths evidenced
- errors and misconceptions
- possible access barriers
- missing or ambiguous evidence
- alternative evidence-gathering route
- next teaching or observation step
- review criteria

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check that the analysis does not treat poor handwriting, short answers, incomplete work or presentation as diagnostic evidence and does not make unsupported causal claims.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Add [COMPARISON_WORK_SAMPLES] to identify whether a pattern is repeated across tasks.

**Example Input:** Year 4 history; objective: explain why a historical event happened; pupil gives a strong oral explanation but writes two short sentences containing accurate ideas.

**Example Output excerpt:** The written sample alone under-represents the pupil's historical reasoning. Compare oral and written evidence before concluding that the pupil lacks the target historical understanding.

## SEND-15: Map Environmental and Sensory Barriers

**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT and comparable general-purpose models  
**Expected output length:** 800 to 1,300 words  
**Typical generation time:** 40 to 70 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** SEND, sensory access, environment, accessibility, classroom inclusion

### ROLE

Act as a SEND-informed primary classroom accessibility reviewer. Identify environmental and sensory factors that may affect access without making medical or diagnostic assumptions.

### CONTEXT

Year group or class: [YEAR_GROUP_OR_CLASS]

Lesson or routine: [LESSON_OR_ROUTINE]

Observed access difficulty: [OBSERVED_DIFFICULTY]

Environment: [ENVIRONMENT_DESCRIPTION]

Sensory or physical information supplied by teacher: [SUPPLIED_ACCESS_INFORMATION]

Learning objective: [LEARNING_OBJECTIVE]

### TASK

Map environmental and sensory demands that may create barriers to accessing the intended learning.

### REQUIREMENTS

Consider where relevant:

- lighting and visual clutter
- noise and competing sound
- seating and viewing position
- transitions and movement
- crowding and personal space
- tactile materials
- smell or other environmental stimuli
- screen or display demands
- equipment layout
- physical navigation
- timing and processing opportunity

For each potential barrier, distinguish observed evidence from a hypothesis. Recommend low-risk classroom adjustments and identify how the teacher could test whether the change improves access.

Do not prescribe medical treatment, sensory therapy or specialist equipment unless it is already supplied as part of relevant professional guidance.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Use:

- environmental profile
- sensory and physical demand map
- observed evidence
- hypotheses to test
- low-risk adjustments
- evidence of impact
- what requires specialist or school guidance
- review plan

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check that environmental factors are considered before attributing difficulty to the pupil and that recommendations remain within classroom practice unless supplied professional guidance applies.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Add [CLASSROOM_LAYOUT] or [SCHOOL_ACCESS_GUIDANCE] where available.

**Example Input:** Year 2 classroom; pupil becomes less able to engage during carpet teaching when the room is noisy; objective is to listen and respond to a story.

**Example Output excerpt:** Treat noise as an access variable to test. Consider seating, proximity, visual support and quieter transition routines while preserving listening and comprehension as the intended construct.

## SEND-16: Analyse Communication and Interaction Barriers

**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT and comparable general-purpose models  
**Expected output length:** 900 to 1,400 words  
**Typical generation time:** 45 to 75 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** SEND, communication, interaction, language, accessibility, inclusion

### ROLE

Act as a primary teacher with expertise in inclusive communication and classroom interaction. Analyse communication demands without diagnosing speech, language or communication conditions.

### CONTEXT

Year group: [YEAR_GROUP]

Subject or lesson: [SUBJECT_OR_LESSON]

Learning objective: [LEARNING_OBJECTIVE]

Interaction or communication task: [COMMUNICATION_TASK]

Observed evidence: [OBSERVED_EVIDENCE]

Language demands: [LANGUAGE_DEMANDS]

Communication information supplied by teacher or specialist: [COMMUNICATION_INFORMATION]

### TASK

Identify where communication demands may be creating a barrier to learning, participation or demonstrating knowledge.

### REQUIREMENTS

Analyse:

- receptive language demands
- expressive language demands
- vocabulary and sentence complexity
- question structure
- turn-taking demands
- processing time
- peer interaction demands
- non-verbal response opportunities
- visual or written supports
- alternative response modes

Distinguish difficulty understanding the language of the task from difficulty with the curriculum content. Do not assume that limited verbal participation means limited understanding.

Do not diagnose or infer a communication condition. Where supplied specialist guidance exists, use it as context rather than replacing it with generic advice.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Provide:

- communication demand analysis
- observable evidence
- potential access barriers
- alternative response routes
- classroom adjustments
- evidence-gathering plan
- specialist or school decision points
- construct-validity check

### QUALITY CHECKS

Ensure communication style is not treated as a deficit in itself. Do not equate eye contact, speech volume, speed, frequency of contribution or conversational style with understanding or motivation.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Add [PREFERRED_COMMUNICATION_METHODS] only where supplied and appropriate.

**Example Input:** Year 3 English discussion; pupil rarely volunteers answers but gives accurate written responses and contributes when given preparation time.

**Example Output excerpt:** The evidence suggests that rapid public turn-taking may be an access barrier. Increase preparation time and offer structured response routes rather than interpreting low volunteering as low understanding.

## SEND-17: Analyse Task, Processing and Executive-Function Demands

**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT and comparable general-purpose models  
**Expected output length:** 900 to 1,400 words  
**Typical generation time:** 45 to 75 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** SEND, executive function, processing, working memory, task analysis, adaptive teaching

### ROLE

Act as an experienced primary teacher analysing the cognitive and organisational demands embedded in a task. Avoid diagnosing executive-function or cognitive conditions.

### CONTEXT

Year group: [YEAR_GROUP]

Subject: [SUBJECT]

Learning objective: [LEARNING_OBJECTIVE]

Task: [TASK]

Instructions: [INSTRUCTIONS]

Observed difficulty: [OBSERVED_DIFFICULTY]

Previous support or evidence: [PREVIOUS_SUPPORT_AND_EVIDENCE]

### TASK

Deconstruct the task to determine whether processing, sequencing, working-memory, initiation, planning, organisation, monitoring or switching demands are interfering with access to the intended learning.

### REQUIREMENTS

For each stage of the task identify:

1. what the pupil must understand
2. what must be held in mind
3. what must be sequenced
4. what must be initiated independently
5. what must be monitored or checked
6. which demands are essential to the curriculum construct
7. which demands may be incidental to the construct

Then create evidence-led adjustments such as chunking, visual sequencing, worked examples, external memory supports, reduced instruction density, checklists, rehearsal, prompts or planned pauses where appropriate.

Do not label a pupil as having a working-memory problem, executive-function difficulty or cognitive impairment from one observation.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Use:

- intended construct
- task-demand decomposition
- observed barrier
- plausible mechanisms to test
- essential versus incidental demands
- adjustments to test
- fading or independence plan
- evidence criteria
- boundaries and cautions

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check that cognitive-demand language describes the task and evidence rather than diagnosing the pupil. Ensure adjustments do not unnecessarily lower the intended level of thinking.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Add [KNOWN_CLASSROOM_SUPPORTS] and [PUPIL_WORK_EXAMPLES].

**Example Input:** Year 6 maths; objective: solve a multi-step problem; pupil understands each calculation when prompted but loses track of the sequence when working independently.

**Example Output excerpt:** Analyse the sequencing and external-memory demands first. A structured problem-solving frame can test whether reducing organisational load allows the pupil to demonstrate the intended mathematical reasoning.

## SEND-18: Triangulate Evidence Across Lessons and Contexts

**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT and comparable general-purpose models  
**Expected output length:** 900 to 1,500 words  
**Typical generation time:** 45 to 90 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** SEND, evidence triangulation, observation, assessment, barriers, inclusion

### ROLE

Act as a primary SEND evidence reviewer. Your job is to triangulate classroom evidence and prevent conclusions being drawn from a single lesson, task or behaviour.

### CONTEXT

Pupil or group: [PUPIL_OR_GROUP]

Year group: [YEAR_GROUP]

Evidence set: [EVIDENCE_SET]

Subjects or contexts represented: [SUBJECTS_AND_CONTEXTS]

Tasks represented: [TASKS]

Support already used: [SUPPORT_USED]

Relevant school or specialist information: [SCHOOL_OR_SPECIALIST_INFORMATION]

### TASK

Compare the evidence and determine which patterns are consistent, context-dependent, uncertain or contradicted by other evidence.

### REQUIREMENTS

Triangulate across as many supplied dimensions as possible:

- different subjects
- different task formats
- independent versus supported work
- oral versus written responses
- individual versus group contexts
- different times of day or lesson stages
- different adults
- different environments
- pupil work and assessment evidence
- pupil voice where appropriate

For every emerging pattern, state the evidence supporting it, evidence against it, possible alternative explanations and the next evidence required.

Do not infer diagnosis or causation. Do not treat a SEND label as confirmation of a hypothesised barrier.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Provide:

- evidence inventory
- cross-context comparison
- consistent patterns
- context-specific patterns
- conflicting evidence
- hypotheses that remain plausible
- hypotheses that should be rejected or held lightly
- next evidence to collect
- classroom action to test
- review criteria

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check that conclusions are based on multiple evidence sources where available and that uncertainty is explicit. Avoid turning correlations into causes.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Add [TIME_PERIOD] to define the evidence window and [ADULTS_INVOLVED] if multiple adults have supplied observations.

**Example Input:** Year 5 pupil struggles with independent written tasks but performs strongly in practical science, oral explanation and paired work; several observations supplied across two weeks.

**Example Output excerpt:** The pattern is context-dependent rather than a general inability to learn. Investigate the specific demands of independent written production before proposing broader explanations.

## SEND-19: Build an Evidence-Led Barrier and Support Profile

**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT and comparable general-purpose models  
**Expected output length:** 1,000 to 1,600 words  
**Typical generation time:** 50 to 90 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** SEND, barrier profile, support planning, evidence, adaptive teaching, inclusion

### ROLE

Act as an experienced primary teacher and SEND evidence reviewer building a practical, non-diagnostic profile of learning and access barriers.

### CONTEXT

Pupil or group: [PUPIL_OR_GROUP]

Year group: [YEAR_GROUP]

Strengths and successful learning conditions: [STRENGTHS_AND_SUCCESSFUL_CONDITIONS]

Evidence gathered: [EVIDENCE_GATHERED]

Known barriers: [KNOWN_BARRIERS]

Existing classroom supports: [EXISTING_SUPPORTS]

Relevant school or specialist guidance: [SCHOOL_OR_SPECIALIST_GUIDANCE]

Curriculum priorities: [CURRICULUM_PRIORITIES]

### TASK

Create an evidence-led barrier and support profile that explains where access is difficult, where learning is successful, what support has been tested and what should happen next.

### REQUIREMENTS

For each barrier include:

- observable evidence
- context in which it occurs
- effect on access or demonstration of learning
- strengths or successful conditions that provide useful contrast
- plausible explanation stated as a hypothesis where necessary
- support already tested
- evidence of impact
- recommended adjustment or next test
- how independence will be protected
- review date or review trigger

Include a clear distinction between classroom evidence and information requiring specialist or statutory judgement.

Do not create a diagnosis, medical profile or invented EHCP provision. Do not assume that support should be permanent. Do not use deficit language.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

- Pupil or group context
- Strengths and successful conditions
- Evidence summary
- Barrier profile
- Support already tested
- Recommended classroom adjustments
- Independence and fading plan

- Specialist or school decision points
- Review questions
- Non-diagnostic boundary check

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check that the profile is useful to teachers, evidence-led, respectful and reviewable. Confirm that barriers are described in relation to learning and access rather than as fixed pupil deficits.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Add [REVIEW_PERIOD], [STAFF_ROLES] or [TARGETED_CURRICULUM_PRIORITY] where supplied.

**Example Input:** Year 4 pupil; strong oral reasoning and practical learning; difficulty starting extended independent writing; supplied evidence includes work samples, two observations and pupil comments about task instructions.

**Example Output excerpt:** The profile identifies task initiation and written organisation as hypotheses to test, while recognising strong oral reasoning as evidence that the curriculum concept may be understood. Recommended support focuses on externalising planning and initiation rather than reducing the writing objective.

**Typical generation time:** 60 to 100 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** SEND, identification, observation, evidence review, barriers to learning, adaptive teaching, inclusion

### ROLE

Act as a senior primary SEND-informed teacher coordinating a structured classroom evidence review. Produce a complete review pack that helps teachers understand barriers to learning without diagnosing pupils.

### CONTEXT

Pupil or group: [PUPIL_OR_GROUP]

Year group: [YEAR_GROUP]

Subjects or contexts: [SUBJECTS_AND_CONTEXTS]

Primary concern or question: [REVIEW_QUESTION]

Learning objectives or curriculum priorities: [CURRICULUM_PRIORITIES]

Observation evidence: [OBSERVATION_EVIDENCE]

Pupil work or assessment evidence: [WORK_AND_ASSESSMENT_EVIDENCE]

Task and environmental information: [TASK_AND_ENVIRONMENT_INFORMATION]

Communication or access information supplied by teacher: [ACCESS_INFORMATION]

Existing support and response to support: [EXISTING_SUPPORT_AND_IMPACT]

Pupil voice where appropriate: [PUPIL_VOICE]

School or specialist guidance: [SCHOOL_OR_SPECIALIST_GUIDANCE]

### TASK

Create a complete, evidence-led identification and barriers review pack. The pack must help a teacher move from observation to proportionate classroom action and review without turning classroom evidence into diagnosis.

### REQUIREMENTS

Include all of the following:

1. review purpose and intended curriculum constructs
2. evidence inventory
3. neutral observation summary
4. task-demand analysis
5. environmental and sensory considerations where relevant
6. communication and interaction considerations where relevant
7. processing, sequencing and executive-demand considerations where relevant
8. pupil work and assessment analysis
9. strengths and successful learning conditions
10. cross-context evidence triangulation
11. plausible barriers stated as hypotheses rather than facts where necessary
12. alternative explanations
13. evidence gaps
14. low-risk classroom adjustments to test
15. expected evidence of impact
16. support-fading or independence plan
17. construct-validity implications
18. specialist, school-policy or statutory decision points that require verification
19. review questions and next-step criteria

Create a clear evidence hierarchy:

- Direct observation
- Work or assessment evidence
- Contextual information supplied by teacher or school
- Pupil voice
- Plausible hypothesis
- Evidence still required

Do not diagnose SEND or any medical, developmental, communication, cognitive or mental health condition. Do not infer a pupil's ability, motivation, family circumstances or character from limited evidence. Do not invent EHCP provision, specialist advice, safeguarding procedures or statutory requirements.

If the evidence is insufficient to identify a plausible barrier, say so and specify exactly what should be observed or collected next.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Use these headings:

- Review purpose
- Intended learning constructs
- Evidence inventory
- Neutral observation summary
- Task-demand analysis
- Environmental and sensory analysis
- Communication and interaction analysis
- Processing and executive-demand analysis
- Work and assessment evidence
- Strengths and successful conditions
- Cross-context triangulation
- Plausible barriers and hypotheses
- Alternative explanations
- Evidence gaps
- Classroom adjustments to test
- Evidence of impact
- Independence and fading plan
- Construct-validity implications
- School or specialist decision points
- Review questions
- Recommended next step
- Final SEND quality and boundary check

### QUALITY CHECKS

Before finalising, verify that:

- no diagnosis has been made
- no unsupported causal claim is presented as fact
- observation is separated from interpretation
- task and environmental barriers have been considered
- evidence has been triangulated where possible
- pupil strengths are represented alongside barriers
- no stereotypes or deficit descriptions are used
- personal disclosure is not required
- classroom support is practical and reviewable
- curriculum ambition is preserved wherever possible
- assessment remains construct-valid
- specialist and statutory boundaries are explicit
- missing school-specific information is flagged rather than invented
- no medical or therapeutic advice has been supplied
- the output contains no em dash character

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

If any evidence field is incomplete, do not fill the gap with assumptions. Mark the field as an evidence gap and specify the minimum additional information required to proceed safely and proportionately.

**Example Input:** Year 6 pupil; teacher is concerned about slow completion of independent maths tasks; supplied evidence includes three lesson observations, two work samples, one oral assessment and notes showing stronger performance when instructions are chunked.

**Example Output excerpt:** The review distinguishes mathematical understanding from the access demands of independent task navigation. Stronger performance with chunked instructions is treated as evidence supporting a hypothesis about task-processing demand, not as evidence of a diagnosis. The next step is a time-limited classroom adjustment with defined evidence criteria and a review point.

## Family-level quality check

Across SEND-11 to SEND-20, the generated workflows must consistently demonstrate:

- observation before explanation
- barrier-led rather than label-led reasoning
- distinction between evidence, hypothesis and diagnosis
- task and environment analysis before pupil attribution
- neutral and respectful language
- triangulation across contexts where available
- communication and sensory access awareness
- processing and executive-demand analysis without diagnosis
- construct validity and curriculum ambition
- testable, proportionate and reviewable classroom support
- pupil dignity and appropriate pupil voice
- no forced disclosure of sensitive personal information
- no invented EHCP, specialist, safeguarding, school-policy or statutory arrangements
- no medical, therapeutic or diagnostic advice
- explicit uncertainty when evidence is insufficient
- practical classroom implementation
- no em dash character
