# SEND Resources, Technology & Accessibility

## Family purpose

This family helps primary teachers audit, adapt and design learning resources, digital materials and technology so that pupils can access learning, participate meaningfully and demonstrate the intended curriculum construct. The prompts focus on practical accessibility rather than diagnosis or generic technology recommendations.

## Family-wide controls

1. Start with the intended learning construct and the actual access demand of the task.
2. Remove incidental barriers without automatically reducing curriculum ambition or cognitive demand.
3. Distinguish an access barrier from a learning difficulty, communication difference, cognition, motivation, behaviour or confidence.
4. Do not assume a pupil needs technology or assistive equipment solely because of a diagnosis, label or SEND category.
5. Use supplied pupil information, specialist advice, school accessibility guidance and device specifications as controlling context.
6. Do not invent device capabilities, software compatibility, licences, accessibility provision, school IT policy, data or privacy controls, safeguarding procedures or specialist recommendations.
7. Do not recommend clinical or therapeutic technology as if it were a classroom intervention.
8. Treat assistive technology as an access and independence tool, not as evidence of reduced ability.
9. Consider visual, auditory, physical, communication, cognitive, language and navigation access where relevant.
10. Consider reliability, usability, training burden, classroom feasibility, privacy, safety, compatibility and cost where information is supplied.
11. Current software specifications, operating-system features, device capabilities, licences and school policies must be verified rather than invented.
12. Alternative formats and response modes must preserve the intended construct wherever possible.
13. Technology should increase access and independence rather than create unnecessary adult dependency.
14. Do not require pupils to disclose medical, family or personal information to justify an adaptation.
15. Use respectful, strengths-aware language and protect pupil dignity, privacy and agency.
16. Do not use an em dash character.

## SEND-81: Audit Learning Resources for Accessibility

### Metadata
- Prompt ID: SEND-81
- Difficulty: Advanced
- AI model compatibility: ChatGPT
- Expected output length: 1,000 to 1,300 words
- Typical generation time: 2 to 3 minutes
- Curriculum tags: SEND, accessibility, resources, adaptive teaching, inclusion

### Editable variables
- Year group: `[YEAR GROUP]`
- Subject: `[SUBJECT]`
- Curriculum objective: `[CURRICULUM OBJECTIVE]`
- Resource: `[RESOURCE DESCRIPTION]`
- Intended construct: `[INTENDED CONSTRUCT]`
- Pupil access information: `[SUPPLIED ACCESS INFORMATION]`

### Example Input
Audit a Year 3 science worksheet about plant requirements. Pupils need to identify and explain what plants need to grow. One pupil has supplied information about visual access and processing time.

### Example Output excerpt
Separate the science construct from incidental demands such as dense text, small diagrams or unnecessary copying. Identify accessibility barriers and recommend changes that preserve the science learning.

### Prompt

**ROLE**

Act as an experienced primary SEND and accessibility adviser with strong curriculum and classroom practice knowledge.

**CONTEXT**

I teach `[YEAR GROUP]` in `[SUBJECT]`.

The curriculum objective is `[CURRICULUM OBJECTIVE]`.

The intended construct is `[INTENDED CONSTRUCT]`.

The resource is `[RESOURCE DESCRIPTION]`.

Supplied pupil access information is `[SUPPLIED ACCESS INFORMATION]`.

**TASK**

Audit the resource for accessibility barriers and recommend practical improvements that preserve the intended learning.

**REQUIREMENTS**

- Define the intended construct first.
- Identify visual, auditory, language, cognitive, motor, communication and navigation barriers where relevant.
- Distinguish essential task demands from incidental presentation demands.
- Consider reading age, vocabulary density, instruction length, layout, spacing, diagrams, colour dependence and response demands.
- Do not infer a diagnosis from a barrier.
- Recommend adaptations only where they are relevant to the supplied context.
- State where an adaptation could alter the intended construct.
- Avoid inventing specialist guidance or school accessibility requirements.

**OUTPUT FORMAT**

Produce:
1. Construct summary.
2. Accessibility audit table with barrier, evidence, impact and recommendation.
3. Priority fixes.
4. Revised resource design specification.
5. Construct-validity risks.
6. Teacher implementation notes.
7. Accessibility check before use.

**QUALITY CHECKS**

Check that recommendations remove unnecessary barriers without simply making the learning easier. Check that every pupil-specific recommendation is traceable to supplied information.

**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION**

If the actual resource has not been supplied, create an audit checklist and clearly label assumptions rather than pretending to have inspected the resource.
