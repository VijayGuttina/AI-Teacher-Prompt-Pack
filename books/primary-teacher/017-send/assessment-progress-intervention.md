# SEND Assessment, Progress & Intervention

## Family purpose

This family helps primary teachers assess learning accurately, interpret evidence, identify barriers, plan proportionate interventions and review whether support is making a difference. The prompts are designed to preserve curriculum ambition and assessment construct validity while avoiding diagnosis, unsupported causal claims and intervention creep.

## Family-wide controls

1. Start with the intended curriculum construct and the evidence needed to demonstrate it.
2. Separate attainment evidence from access barriers, behaviour, communication style and support dependency.
3. Do not diagnose conditions or infer medical, psychological or neurodevelopmental causes from assessment evidence.
4. Do not treat a SEND label, EHCP status, EAL status or intervention history as evidence of ability.
5. Distinguish a pupil's independent performance from supported performance and record the support provided.
6. Preserve assessment construct validity. Accessibility adaptations should remove incidental barriers without changing what is being assessed unless the change is explicitly intended and identified.
7. Do not reduce curriculum ambition simply because a pupil needs access support.
8. Use multiple sources of evidence rather than relying on a single score, task or observation.
9. Treat intervention as a hypothesis to test, not a guaranteed solution.
10. Define baseline, intended outcome, measurable success criteria, review point and decision rule.
11. Avoid interventions that merely increase adult prompting without building independence.
12. Do not invent EHCP provision, specialist recommendations, statutory requirements, school policies or safeguarding procedures.
13. Do not provide medical, diagnostic, therapeutic or clinical intervention advice.
14. Use supplied school and professional guidance as controlling context and flag missing information.
15. Use respectful, strengths-aware language and protect pupil dignity and privacy.
16. Do not use an em dash character.

## SEND-71: Build a SEND Assessment Blueprint

### Metadata
- Prompt ID: SEND-71
- Difficulty: Advanced
- AI model compatibility: ChatGPT
- Expected output length: 900 to 1,200 words
- Typical generation time: 2 to 3 minutes
- Curriculum tags: SEND, assessment, construct validity, curriculum, progress

### Editable variables
- Year group: `[YEAR GROUP]`
- Subject: `[SUBJECT]`
- Curriculum objective: `[CURRICULUM OBJECTIVE]`
- Assessment context: `[ASSESSMENT CONTEXT]`
- Known access information: `[SUPPLIED ACCESS INFORMATION]`

### Example Input
Year 5 mathematics objective: compare and order fractions. The pupil benefits from enlarged representations and additional processing time.

### Example Output excerpt
Define the mathematical construct first, then specify accessible representations and response options. Do not lower the fraction-comparison demand merely because visual or processing support is required.

### Prompt

**ROLE**

Act as an experienced primary SEND assessment and curriculum adviser.

**CONTEXT**

I am assessing `[CURRICULUM OBJECTIVE]` with `[YEAR GROUP]` pupils in `[SUBJECT]`.

The assessment context is `[ASSESSMENT CONTEXT]`.

Known access information is `[SUPPLIED ACCESS INFORMATION]`.

**TASK**

Build an assessment blueprint that identifies the intended construct, evidence required, appropriate accessibility adaptations and valid ways for pupils to demonstrate learning.

**REQUIREMENTS**

- Define the curriculum construct before discussing SEND adaptations.
- Identify knowledge, skills, reasoning or application being assessed.
- Separate incidental barriers from essential task demands.
- Provide accessible routes without changing the construct.
- Record support provided so independent and supported performance can be distinguished.
- Avoid diagnosis or assumptions based on SEND labels.

**OUTPUT FORMAT**

Produce:
1. Construct definition.
2. Evidence requirements.
3. Assessment task design.
4. Access adaptations.
5. Construct-validity risks.
6. Recording template.
7. Teacher moderation checklist.

**QUALITY CHECKS**

Check that adaptations do not unintentionally change the construct and that the assessment measures learning rather than handwriting, speed, memory load, communication style or another incidental barrier unless those are genuinely part of the objective.

**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION**

If `[SUPPLIED ACCESS INFORMATION]` is incomplete, provide practical options and state what must be confirmed.

- Subject: `[SUBJECT]`
- Assessment evidence: `[ASSESSMENT EVIDENCE]`
- Assessment construct: `[ASSESSMENT CONSTRUCT]`
- Existing support: `[EXISTING SUPPORT]`

### Example Input
A Year 4 writing sample shows strong ideas but inconsistent sentence punctuation and incomplete independent task completion. The pupil used a visual checklist.

### Example Output excerpt
Separate evidence of writing knowledge from evidence about task access. Analyse punctuation errors directly rather than attributing incomplete work to a diagnostic cause.

### Prompt

**ROLE**

Act as an experienced primary SEND assessment adviser who interprets evidence cautiously and objectively.

**CONTEXT**

The year group is `[YEAR GROUP]`.

The subject is `[SUBJECT]`.

The assessment construct is `[ASSESSMENT CONSTRUCT]`.

The evidence is `[ASSESSMENT EVIDENCE]`.

Existing support was `[EXISTING SUPPORT]`.

**TASK**

Analyse the evidence to identify demonstrated learning, misconceptions, access barriers and questions requiring further evidence.

**REQUIREMENTS**

- Separate observed evidence from interpretation.
- Do not diagnose or infer a condition.
- Do not assume errors are caused by SEND.
- Distinguish knowledge gaps from task-access barriers.
- Consider whether support affected the evidence.
- Identify what additional evidence would strengthen the judgement.

**OUTPUT FORMAT**

Produce:
1. Evidence summary.
2. Demonstrated strengths.
3. Misconceptions or learning gaps.
4. Possible access barriers, clearly labelled as hypotheses.
5. Effect of existing support.
6. Additional evidence needed.
7. Recommended next teaching step.

**QUALITY CHECKS**

Check that every conclusion is traceable to evidence and that no medical or diagnostic explanation has been introduced.

**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION**

Compare independent and supported work where both are supplied.

### Example Output excerpt

### Prompt

**REQUIREMENTS**

- State exactly what the assessment is intended to measure.
- Identify which features are incidental barriers.
- Offer alternative response modes only when construct-valid.
- Explain when an adaptation would change the construct.
- Record support used.
- Do not diagnose or make medical assumptions.
- Use supplied professional or school guidance where relevant.

Check that the adaptation does not make the assessment easier in the construct being measured and does not accidentally assess a different skill.

**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION**

### Example Input
A six-week spelling intervention shows improved performance on taught word patterns but limited transfer into independent writing.

### Example Output excerpt

### Prompt

**ROLE**

**CONTEXT**
