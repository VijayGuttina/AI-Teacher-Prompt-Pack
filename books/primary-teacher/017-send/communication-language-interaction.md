# SEND Communication, Language & Interaction

This family contains ten copy/paste-ready workflows for improving communication access, language comprehension, expression and classroom interaction. The workflows are designed to distinguish communication access from cognition, avoid diagnostic assumptions, preserve curriculum constructs and provide practical alternatives for pupils to understand, express and demonstrate learning.

## SEND-specific communication, language and interaction rules

Use these controls in every workflow:

- Start with the intended learning, participation demand and communication demand before selecting support.
- Use observable evidence and supplied pupil information. Do not diagnose speech, language, communication, hearing, developmental, neurological, psychological or medical conditions.
- Distinguish receptive language, expressive language, vocabulary, processing time, speech production, hearing or visual access, motor output, interaction structure, task design, confidence and behaviour rather than treating them as interchangeable.
- Never equate limited spoken output, unusual speech, alternative communication, delayed response, limited eye contact or reduced classroom talk with limited understanding or lower cognitive ability without direct evidence.
- Respect multilingualism and EAL. Do not equate English proficiency with cognition, intelligence, SEND status or MFL aptitude.
- Provide multiple appropriate routes for accessing instructions and expressing learning, including speech, writing, typing, symbols, visuals, gesture, drawing or other supplied communication systems where construct-valid and appropriate.
- Where AAC or specialist communication systems are supplied, use the supplied system and professional guidance as controlling context. Do not invent clinical programmes, device specifications or specialist recommendations.
- Do not require eye contact, forced speech, public speaking, performance or personal disclosure as a condition of participation unless the supplied curriculum construct genuinely requires that specific skill.
- Use explicit vocabulary, clear language, modelling, visuals, gesture, written support, repetition, rehearsal, wait time and structured interaction where they address the evidenced barrier.
- Preserve the intended curriculum construct. Separate language or output demands that are incidental from those that are essential to the learning being assessed.
- Avoid deficit language, stereotypes and assumptions based on diagnosis, EAL, ethnicity, gender, behaviour, confidence, attendance or family background.
- Do not invent EHCP provision, speech and language therapy advice, school communication policy, reasonable adjustments, safeguarding procedures or specialist recommendations.
- Do not require pupils to disclose diagnoses, family circumstances, medical information or personal experiences.
- Keep recommendations practical for a real primary classroom, including class size, staffing, time, resources and routines.
- Use no em dash character in generated output.

## SEND-31: Analyse Communication Access Needs in the Classroom

**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT and comparable general-purpose models  
**Expected output length:** 900 to 1,400 words  
**Typical generation time:** 45 to 75 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** SEND, communication, language, classroom access, inclusion

### ROLE

Act as an experienced primary teacher and SEND-informed communication-access specialist. Analyse communication demands and observable barriers without diagnosing the pupil.

### CONTEXT

Year group: [YEAR_GROUP]

Subject: [SUBJECT]

Curriculum objective: [CURRICULUM_OBJECTIVE]

Learning construct: [LEARNING_CONSTRUCT]

Lesson or classroom routine: [LESSON_OR_ROUTINE]

Observed communication evidence: [OBSERVED_COMMUNICATION_EVIDENCE]

Current successful strategies: [SUCCESSFUL_STRATEGIES]

Available communication resources or technology: [COMMUNICATION_RESOURCES]

School or specialist guidance supplied: [SCHOOL_OR_SPECIALIST_GUIDANCE]

### TASK

Analyse the communication access demands of the context and identify practical barriers and support options.

### REQUIREMENTS

Separate:

1. receptive language demands
2. expressive language demands
3. vocabulary and concept language
4. processing-time demands
5. speech or motor-output demands
6. visual or written communication demands
7. interaction and turn-taking demands
8. environmental or task-design barriers

For each, distinguish observable evidence from possible explanations and identify what should be tested next. State what learning demand must remain unchanged.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Use:

- Intended learning and participation demand
- Communication demand analysis
- Evidence table
- Potential access barriers
- Recommended classroom supports
- Construct-valid response routes
- Evidence to collect
- Review points
- Teacher checklist

### QUALITY CHECKS

Do not diagnose or infer a condition. Do not equate speech, silence, eye contact or response speed with understanding. Check that support addresses the actual communication demand.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Add [CLASSROOM_ROUTINES], [COMMUNICATION_MODES] and [PUPIL_STRENGTHS].

**Example Input:** Year 4 science; pupils must explain how a habitat supports an animal; one pupil understands the content but struggles to formulate extended spoken answers under time pressure.

**Example Output excerpt:** Separate scientific understanding from spoken formulation speed. Provide rehearsal and an appropriate alternative response route while retaining the requirement to explain the habitat relationship.
