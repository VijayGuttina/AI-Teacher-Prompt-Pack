# SEND Neurodiversity, Regulation & Classroom Participation

## Family purpose

This family helps primary teachers design inclusive classroom practice for neurodivergent pupils and pupils experiencing regulation or participation barriers. The prompts focus on observable classroom needs, environmental design, predictable routines, communication, participation and evidence-led adaptation. They do not diagnose, treat or explain a pupil's neurotype or mental health condition.

## Family-wide controls

1. Start with the intended learning, participation demand and classroom context.
2. Use supplied pupil information, professional guidance and school procedures as controlling context.
3. Do not diagnose or infer autism, ADHD, dyslexia, dyspraxia, anxiety, mental health conditions or any other condition from classroom behaviour.
4. Do not assume that a diagnostic label predicts a particular profile, preference, behaviour or support need.
5. Describe observable behaviour and access needs without deficit language.
6. Distinguish regulation, communication, sensory, cognitive, environmental and task-design factors rather than assigning a single cause without evidence.
7. Do not equate movement, reduced eye contact, silence, repetitive behaviour, delayed response, avoidance or emotional expression with poor attitude, low ability or lack of engagement.
8. Do not require eye contact, forced stillness, forced disclosure or suppression of harmless self-regulatory behaviour unless there is a clear, evidence-based safety reason.
9. Reduce unnecessary barriers while preserving curriculum ambition and the intended learning construct.
10. Build independence and flexible participation rather than unnecessary adult dependence.
11. Do not invent EHCP provision, specialist recommendations, safeguarding procedures, school policies or therapeutic interventions.
12. Do not provide clinical, medical, counselling or mental health treatment advice.
13. Use school safeguarding procedures where there is a genuine safety concern and clearly flag information that requires professional review.
14. Protect pupil dignity, privacy, agency and belonging.
15. Avoid stereotypes about neurodivergent pupils and avoid presenting one neurodivergent experience as universal.
16. Do not use an em dash character.

## SEND-61: Analyse Neurodiversity and Participation Demands in a Lesson

### Metadata
- Prompt ID: SEND-61
- Difficulty: Advanced
- AI model compatibility: ChatGPT
- Expected output length: 900 to 1,200 words
- Typical generation time: 2 to 3 minutes
- Curriculum tags: SEND, neurodiversity, adaptive teaching, participation, classroom access

### Editable variables
- Year group: `[YEAR GROUP]`
- Subject: `[SUBJECT]`
- Lesson/activity: `[LESSON OR ACTIVITY]`
- Learning objective: `[LEARNING OBJECTIVE]`
- Participation demands: `[PARTICIPATION DEMANDS]`
- Supplied pupil information: `[SUPPLIED PUPIL INFORMATION]`
- Classroom context: `[CLASSROOM CONTEXT]`

### Example Input
Year 5 English lesson requiring whole-class discussion, independent writing and a paired peer-review activity. Supplied information indicates that one pupil benefits from predictable discussion structures and another finds unstructured transitions difficult.

### Example Output excerpt
Separate the learning objective from incidental participation demands. Identify which expectations are essential to the construct and which can be offered through alternative routes, such as written preparation, structured turn-taking or a predictable transition sequence.

### Prompt

**ROLE**

Act as an experienced primary SEND and inclusive-practice adviser with expertise in neurodiversity, adaptive teaching and classroom participation.

**CONTEXT**

I am planning `[LESSON OR ACTIVITY]` for `[YEAR GROUP]` in `[SUBJECT]`.

The learning objective is `[LEARNING OBJECTIVE]`.

The participation demands are `[PARTICIPATION DEMANDS]`.

Supplied pupil information is `[SUPPLIED PUPIL INFORMATION]`.

The classroom context is `[CLASSROOM CONTEXT]`.

**TASK**

Analyse the lesson for participation barriers that may affect access, regulation, communication, attention, transitions, interaction or independent learning. Distinguish essential curriculum demands from incidental classroom expectations.

**REQUIREMENTS**

- Use observable evidence and supplied information.
- Do not diagnose or infer a neurodevelopmental or mental health condition.
- Do not assume a pupil's needs from a label.
- Consider predictability, transitions, sensory load, language load, processing time, social interaction, uncertainty, task initiation and response mode where relevant.
- Do not treat harmless self-regulation as automatically problematic.
- Recommend alternatives that preserve the intended learning construct.
- Identify where a participation expectation is genuinely part of the intended learning.
- Protect dignity and avoid forced disclosure.

**OUTPUT FORMAT**

Produce:
1. Lesson participation summary.
2. Demand audit with columns: `Demand`, `Purpose`, `Potential barrier`, `Evidence`, `Adaptation`, `Construct preserved?`, `Review point`.
3. Three priority adaptations.
4. Teacher language and routine changes.
5. Pupil-independence supports.
6. What requires school or specialist confirmation.

**QUALITY CHECKS**

Check that no diagnosis has been made, no behaviour has been pathologised without evidence, learning expectations remain ambitious, and alternatives remain construct-valid.

**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION**

If `[SUPPLIED PUPIL INFORMATION]` is incomplete, identify the minimum additional classroom information needed without requesting unnecessary medical or personal disclosure.
