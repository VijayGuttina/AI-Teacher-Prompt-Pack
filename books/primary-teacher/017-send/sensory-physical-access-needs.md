# SEND Sensory, Physical & Access Needs

## Family purpose

This family helps primary teachers identify and reduce sensory, physical and access barriers while preserving the intended curriculum construct. The prompts support classroom planning, resource adaptation, practical participation, digital accessibility and evidence-led review. They do not diagnose conditions or replace specialist, medical, occupational therapy, physiotherapy, vision, hearing, safeguarding or school risk-management advice.

## Family-wide controls

1. Start with the intended curriculum objective, learning construct and participation demand.
2. Describe observable access barriers rather than diagnosing or labelling pupils.
3. Use supplied pupil information, specialist guidance, school policy and risk assessments as controlling context.
4. Do not infer disability, impairment, medical condition or sensory profile from behaviour alone.
5. Distinguish sensory or physical access barriers from cognition, motivation, confidence, behaviour and learning ability.
6. Remove incidental barriers without automatically reducing curriculum ambition or changing the intended construct.
7. Do not invent EHCP provision, occupational therapy or physiotherapy recommendations, visual impairment or hearing impairment guidance, equipment specifications, medical restrictions, safeguarding procedures or school policies.
8. Flag where specialist or school review is needed rather than filling missing information with assumptions.
9. Avoid forced eye contact, forced sensory exposure or compulsory participation in an inaccessible activity.
10. Make reasonable classroom adaptations practical for ordinary primary settings.
11. Keep assessment construct-valid. An accessibility adaptation should not accidentally make the assessment measure something different.
12. Use respectful, non-deficit language and do not treat sensory preferences or physical differences as misbehaviour.
13. Protect pupil dignity, privacy and agency. Do not require personal medical or sensitive disclosure.
14. Where safety is affected, use supplied risk-assessment and school procedures and identify any information that must be confirmed before implementation.
15. Do not provide medical or therapeutic advice.
16. Do not use an em dash character.

## SEND-51: Analyse Sensory and Physical Access Demands in the Classroom

### Metadata
- Prompt ID: SEND-51
- Difficulty: Intermediate
- AI model compatibility: ChatGPT
- Expected output length: 700 to 1,000 words
- Typical generation time: 1 to 2 minutes
- Curriculum tags: SEND, accessibility, sensory needs, physical access, adaptive teaching, classroom environment

### Editable variables
- Year group: `[YEAR GROUP]`
- Subject: `[SUBJECT]`
- Lesson or activity: `[LESSON OR ACTIVITY]`
- Learning objective: `[LEARNING OBJECTIVE]`
- Known access information: `[SUPPLIED PUPIL OR SPECIALIST INFORMATION]`
- Classroom environment: `[CLASSROOM CONTEXT]`
- Available resources/equipment: `[AVAILABLE RESOURCES]`

### Example Input
Year 4 science lesson on separating materials. One pupil has supplied information stating that prolonged standing is difficult and another pupil benefits from reduced visual clutter. The class will rotate between three practical stations.

### Example Output excerpt
| Demand | Possible access barrier | Incidental or essential? | Adaptation to test | Information to confirm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Moving between stations | Long walking route | Incidental | Bring resources closer or reduce unnecessary movement | Check classroom risk assessment |
| Recording observations | Visually dense worksheet | Incidental | Simplify layout and increase spacing | Confirm preferred format |

### Prompt

**ROLE**

Act as an experienced primary SEND and adaptive-teaching adviser. Your role is to analyse classroom access demands without diagnosing pupils or making medical assumptions.

**CONTEXT**

I am planning `[LESSON OR ACTIVITY]` for `[YEAR GROUP]` in `[SUBJECT]`.

The learning objective is `[LEARNING OBJECTIVE]`.

Known access information supplied to me is: `[SUPPLIED PUPIL OR SPECIALIST INFORMATION]`.

The classroom environment is: `[CLASSROOM CONTEXT]`.

Available resources and equipment are: `[AVAILABLE RESOURCES]`.

**TASK**

Analyse the sensory and physical demands of this lesson. Identify barriers that could prevent a pupil from accessing the learning, participating safely or demonstrating the intended learning. Separate incidental access barriers from essential curriculum or safety demands.

**REQUIREMENTS**

- Analyse visual, auditory, physical, movement, seating, positioning, sensory-load and environmental demands where relevant.
- Distinguish observed or supplied evidence from hypotheses.
- Do not diagnose or infer a medical condition or disability.
- Do not assume that a behaviour such as movement, avoidance, fatigue, noise sensitivity or difficulty completing a physical task has a particular cause.
- Recommend adaptations that preserve the intended curriculum construct wherever possible.
- Identify any adaptation that could change what is being assessed.
- Do not invent specialist recommendations, equipment specifications, medical restrictions or risk controls.
- Flag safety-critical points that require confirmation from school policy, risk assessment or appropriate professionals.

**OUTPUT FORMAT**

Produce:
1. A short lesson-access summary.
2. A table with columns: `Lesson demand`, `Potential access barrier`, `Evidence or assumption`, `Impact on learning`, `Adaptation`, `Construct preserved?`, `Safety or information to confirm`.
3. A priority list of the three most important adaptations.
4. A short teacher implementation checklist.
5. A section titled `What I have not assumed`.

**QUALITY CHECKS**

Before finalising, check that every recommendation is linked to an identified access demand, no diagnosis has been made, no unsupported medical claim has been introduced, the intended learning has been preserved, and safety-critical gaps are clearly flagged.

**OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION**

If `[SUPPLIED PUPIL OR SPECIALIST INFORMATION]` is incomplete, state exactly what additional classroom or professional information would help, without asking the pupil to disclose sensitive medical information.

**TASK**

Build a complete, practical Sensory, Physical & Access Toolkit for primary teachers. It should cover universal classroom accessibility, targeted adaptations, resource accessibility, practical participation, sensory load, digital accessibility, evidence-led support planning, assessment integrity and review.

**REQUIREMENTS**

The toolkit must:

- Begin with curriculum intent and access rather than labels or diagnoses.
- Distinguish sensory and physical access barriers from cognition, motivation, confidence, behaviour and learning ability.
- Cover visual access, hearing and auditory access, physical access, sensory load, movement, positioning, transitions, practical activities, creative activities, physical activities and digital accessibility.
- Provide universal strategies before targeted strategies where appropriate.
- Preserve curriculum ambition and intended constructs wherever possible.
- Explicitly identify when an adaptation changes a curriculum or assessment construct.
- Include guidance for accessible instructions, resources, diagrams, tables, maps, audio, video, captions, transcripts and digital documents where relevant.
- Include practical alternatives for recording, participation and communication without assuming that an alternative is automatically equivalent.
- Include safety gates for practical activities, equipment, movement and environmental changes.
- Use supplied school policy, risk assessment and specialist guidance as controlling information.
- Never invent EHCP provision, specialist recommendations, medical restrictions, equipment specifications, risk controls or safeguarding procedures.
- Never diagnose a condition or infer one from classroom behaviour.
- Avoid forced eye contact, forced sensory exposure and compulsory participation in inaccessible activities.
- Avoid deficit language, stereotypes and assumptions based on disability category.
- Protect pupil dignity, privacy and agency.
- Include an evidence-led review cycle for targeted adaptations.
- Include assessment construct-validity safeguards.
- Include a missing-information protocol.
- Do not provide medical, therapeutic or clinical advice.
- Do not use an em dash character.

**OUTPUT FORMAT**

Produce the toolkit in the following structure:

### 1. How to Use This Toolkit
Explain the purpose, scope and boundaries in concise teacher-friendly language.

### 2. Universal Classroom Accessibility Audit
Provide a checklist covering environment, movement, seating, positioning, noise, visual clutter, lighting, routines, transitions, resource access and participation.

### 3. Visual Access Toolkit
Provide practical checks and adaptations for text, layout, diagrams, tables, maps, colour, spacing, positioning, magnification and alternative representations.

### 4. Hearing and Auditory Access Toolkit
Provide practical checks for teacher positioning, background noise, visual reinforcement, written instructions, captions/transcripts, turn-taking, audio alternatives and accessible discussion.

### 5. Physical Access Toolkit
Provide checks for movement routes, positioning, equipment access, recording, practical participation, fatigue and pacing. Clearly identify safety-critical decisions requiring confirmation.

### 6. Sensory Load Toolkit
Cover noise, light, crowding, touch, smell, visual clutter, unpredictability, transitions and access to lower-stimulation options without treating sensory preferences as behaviour problems.

### 7. Practical, Creative and Physical Activity Toolkit
Show how to separate essential learning or practical constructs from incidental physical or sensory demands and adapt activities without token participation.

### 8. Digital Accessibility Toolkit
Provide checks for text, contrast, keyboard/pointer access, captions, transcripts, timing, navigation, audio dependence, accessible documents and alternative response modes. Do not assume device features.

### 9. Targeted Access Support Plan Template
Provide a reusable template containing evidence, barrier, adaptation, intended outcome, construct-validity consideration, implementation owner, review measure, review date and information to confirm.

### 10. Assessment and Construct-Validity Safeguards
Explain how to ensure accessibility adaptations do not accidentally assess a different construct.

### 11. Safety and Professional Boundaries
Clearly separate classroom adaptation from medical, therapeutic, specialist, safeguarding and statutory decisions.

### 12. Missing-Information Protocol
Show what to do when pupil, specialist, school policy, risk-assessment or equipment information is missing.

### 13. Teacher Quick-Reference Checklist
Create a one-page checklist that can be used before, during and after a lesson.

### 14. Review and Improvement Cycle
Use a simple cycle such as `Identify → Adapt → Implement → Observe → Review → Refine → Fade where appropriate`.

**QUALITY CHECKS**

Before finalising, check all of the following:

- No diagnosis or medical inference has been made.
- No unsupported specialist recommendation has been invented.
- Sensory and physical access are treated as access and participation issues, not behaviour problems.
- The intended curriculum construct is preserved wherever possible.
- Any construct change is explicitly identified.
- Safety-critical decisions are flagged for confirmation.
- Visual, auditory, physical, sensory and digital access are covered.
- Practical activities preserve meaningful participation.
- Assessment remains construct-valid.
- The toolkit protects pupil dignity and privacy.
- Universal access is considered before unnecessary individualisation.
- Targeted adaptations are evidence-led.
- Independence and fading are considered.
- Missing information is handled transparently.
- Language is respectful, practical and suitable for primary teachers.
- No em dash is used.
