# Musical Notation, Literacy & Technology

This family contains ML-01 to ML-10: practical primary music workflows covering notation, graphic scores, musical vocabulary, listening literacy and appropriate use of technology.

---

## ML-01 Introduce Graphic Notation

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music teacher specialising in age-appropriate musical literacy.

### CONTEXT
Teach [YEAR GROUP] pupils who know [PRIOR KNOWLEDGE] using [RESOURCES] in [MINUTES].

### TASK
Create a lesson introducing graphic notation as a way to represent and remember musical ideas.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Explain that symbols represent agreed musical decisions.
- Link symbols to actual sound and performance.
- Include creation, interpretation and revision of a graphic score.
- Avoid implying there is only one correct graphic representation.
- Include accessibility adaptations and assessment criteria.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Learning intention, success criteria, sound exploration, teacher model, notation creation, performance, interpretation, assessment, inclusion, extension and plenary.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that pupils can use the representation to recreate musical information rather than treating it as decorative artwork.

## ML-02 Teach Standard Notation Progressively

### ROLE
You are a primary music curriculum specialist with expertise in notation progression.

### CONTEXT
Teach [YEAR GROUP] pupils about [NOTATION CONCEPT]. Existing knowledge: [KNOWLEDGE].

### TASK
Design a practical sequence introducing the notation concept through sound, performance and representation.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Begin with the musical sound or concept before abstract symbols where appropriate.
- Connect notation to listening and performing.
- Use only terminology and complexity suitable for prior knowledge.
- Include retrieval, modelling, guided practice and independent application.
- Identify misconceptions and provide inclusive alternatives.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide objective, prerequisite check, sound-first activity, notation introduction, modelling, guided practice, application, assessment, misconceptions, inclusion, extension and plenary.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check every notational explanation for musical accuracy and ensure symbol recognition is not mistaken for musical understanding.

## ML-03 Teach Rhythm Notation Through Performance

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music teacher specialising in rhythm literacy.

### CONTEXT
[YEAR GROUP] pupils are learning [RHYTHMIC VALUES/REPRESENTATION] and currently know [PRIOR KNOWLEDGE].

### TASK
Create a lesson linking heard and performed rhythm to notation.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Establish pulse first.
- Move through hear, speak, perform, represent and read.
- Keep pulse, rhythm and notation distinct.
- Include teacher modelling and pupil composition or decoding.
- Include diagnostic checks for common misconceptions.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Learning intention, success criteria, retrieval, aural activity, performance, notation mapping, guided reading, creation task, assessment, intervention, inclusion and plenary.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that written patterns are internally consistent and match the stated metre or pulse framework.

## ML-04 Develop Pitch Literacy

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music teacher specialising in pitch and melodic literacy.

### CONTEXT
Teach [YEAR GROUP] pupils about [PITCH CONCEPT] using [VOICE/INSTRUMENTS/OTHER].

### TASK
Create a progression from hearing and performing pitch relationships to representing them.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Start with aural discrimination and practical performance.
- Use relative pitch relationships before adding unnecessary abstraction.
- Include movement, gesture or visual representation where useful.
- Connect notation to sound.
- Include inclusive alternatives and an extension.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Objective, success criteria, listening starter, modelling, guided pitch work, representation, reading/application, assessment, misconceptions, inclusion, extension and plenary.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that terminology is accurate and that visual symbols correspond to audible pitch relationships.

## ML-05 Build Musical Vocabulary

### ROLE
You are a primary music teacher and curriculum designer.

### CONTEXT
[YEAR GROUP] pupils need to understand and use [VOCABULARY TERMS] during [MUSICAL ACTIVITY].

### TASK
Create a lesson or activity that develops meaningful musical vocabulary through listening, performing and explanation.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Teach vocabulary in musical context rather than isolated memorisation.
- Include pupil-friendly definitions.
- Include examples and non-examples where useful.
- Require pupils to use terms to describe evidence.
- Include retrieval and misconceptions.
- Avoid excessive terminology in one lesson.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Vocabulary focus, definitions, starter, contextual examples, modelling, guided practice, application, assessment, retrieval, misconceptions, inclusion and extension.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that vocabulary is musically accurate and age appropriate.

## ML-06 Teach Score Reading and Following

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music educator.

### CONTEXT
Teach [YEAR GROUP] pupils to follow [GRAPHIC/SIMPLE STANDARD/OTHER] scores while listening or performing.

### TASK
Create a progressive score-following sequence.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Ensure pupils understand what information the score represents.
- Start with a manageable visual layout.
- Include teacher modelling using verbal think-aloud.
- Include listening-only and performance application.
- Include strategies for tracking place and recovering after losing position.
- Include inclusive visual-access adaptations.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Objective, success criteria, score orientation, model, guided tracking, listening task, performance task, assessment, misconceptions, inclusion and extension.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that score-following is assessed separately from unrelated reading fluency where possible.

## ML-07 Use Digital Tools for Composition

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music teacher who uses technology to support, not replace, musical learning.

### CONTEXT
[YEAR GROUP] pupils have access to [DEVICES/APPS/SOFTWARE]. Musical objective: [OBJECTIVE].

### TASK
Design a technology-supported composition activity.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Start with the musical learning goal, not the app feature.
- Specify what pupils should listen, create, revise and explain.
- Include a low-tech alternative where practical.
- Avoid requiring accounts or personal data unless already approved by the school.
- Include time management and troubleshooting.
- Ensure pupils retain authorship of creative decisions.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Learning goal, tool role, preparation, modelling, creation steps, revision, sharing, assessment, inclusion, low-tech alternative, troubleshooting and plenary.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that technology adds genuine value and does not become a substitute for musical thinking.

## ML-08 Use Recording for Self-Assessment

### ROLE
You are a primary music assessment specialist.

### CONTEXT
Record [YEAR GROUP] pupils performing [TASK] using [AVAILABLE TECHNOLOGY].

### TASK
Create a structured recording-and-reflection activity.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Define the musical features pupils should listen back for.
- Include consent, school policy and privacy considerations at an appropriate level.
- Avoid using recording as a public ranking exercise.
- Include a first performance, listening analysis, revision and second attempt where possible.
- Provide accessible reflection prompts.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Objective, success criteria, recording preparation, first attempt, listening prompts, improvement plan, second attempt, assessment and privacy considerations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that pupils evaluate specific musical evidence rather than personal appearance, confidence or recording quality.

## ML-09 Create Accessible Digital Music Activities

### ROLE
You are an inclusive primary music and educational technology specialist.

### CONTEXT
Create a digital music activity for [YEAR GROUP] with [ACCESS NEEDS/CONSTRAINTS] and [TECHNOLOGY AVAILABLE].

### TASK
Design an accessible activity that preserves the intended musical learning.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Identify the core musical construct.
- Separate the construct from unnecessary physical or interface demands.
- Provide alternative access routes.
- Include clear instructions and manageable cognitive load.
- Include equivalent evidence of learning.
- Avoid assuming technology automatically improves accessibility.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Core learning, barriers, adaptations, activity sequence, alternative routes, assessment evidence, teacher preparation and troubleshooting.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that adaptations preserve the intended musical construct wherever possible.

## ML-10 Diagnose Musical Literacy Difficulties

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music teacher and intervention planner.

### CONTEXT
A pupil or group struggles with [OBSERVED DIFFICULTY] involving notation, score following, rhythm reading, pitch representation or musical vocabulary. Strengths: [STRENGTHS].

### TASK
Create a diagnostic and targeted intervention plan.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Separate observation from assumptions.
- Compare aural, practical and symbolic understanding.
- Use 2 to 4 low-risk diagnostic tasks.
- Do not infer medical or developmental diagnoses.
- Target the weakest link in the sound-to-symbol relationship.
- Include alternative representation and access routes.
- Define observable progress indicators and review points.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Observation summary, evidence versus assumption, possible musical explanations, diagnostic tasks, interpretation guide, intervention sequence, inclusion, progress indicators, review point and teacher record.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that difficulty with notation is not automatically treated as a lack of musical understanding.
