# Instruments, Notation & Musical Literacy

This family provides ten copy-paste-ready workflows for introducing instruments, developing technique, teaching notation and diagnosing difficulties in primary music. The workflows are designed for practical classroom use and should be adapted to the instruments, notation systems and resources actually available in the school.

## IN-01 Introduce Musical Instruments

```yaml
id: IN-01
title: Introduce Musical Instruments
family: Instruments, Notation & Musical Literacy
phase: Primary
prompt_difficulty: Intermediate
ai_model_compatibility: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
expected_output_length: Medium
typical_generation_time: 30-60 seconds
curriculum_tags: [KS1, KS2, instruments, listening, performing, vocabulary]
```

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music teacher planning an accessible first introduction to musical instruments.

### CONTEXT
I am teaching [YEAR GROUP] pupils. The instruments available are [INSTRUMENTS]. Pupils have [PRIOR EXPERIENCE]. The lesson length is [MINUTES].

### TASK
Create a practical first lesson that introduces the instruments and helps pupils recognise how they are played and how they sound.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Use accurate, age-appropriate musical vocabulary.
- Include active listening and practical music-making.
- Explain how each instrument produces sound in simple terms.
- Include safe handling and appropriate volume guidance.
- Provide an inclusive route for pupils who cannot or do not wish to play immediately.
- Do not invent school-specific safety procedures.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide: learning intention, success criteria, vocabulary, starter, teacher modelling, pupil exploration, listening task, short performance task, safety reminders, assessment checks, differentiation, and exit question.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that the activities are feasible with the stated instruments, technically accurate, age appropriate, inclusive and focused on musical learning rather than instrument novelty.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for [WHOLE CLASS / SMALL GROUP / INDIVIDUAL], [KS1 / KS2], and [INSTRUMENT SET].

### Example Input
Year 2, untuned percussion, 35 minutes, little prior experience.

### Example Output
A short sequence in which pupils hear, identify and safely explore three percussion instruments before copying and performing simple contrasting patterns.

## IN-02 Teach Instrument Technique

```yaml

title: Teach Instrument Technique

phase: Primary
prompt_difficulty: Intermediate
ai_model_compatibility: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
expected_output_length: Medium
typical_generation_time: 30-60 seconds
curriculum_tags: [KS1, KS2, performing, technique, progression]
```

### ROLE
You are a primary music specialist teaching a specific instrumental technique.

### CONTEXT
Pupils are [YEAR GROUP] and are learning [INSTRUMENT]. Their current technique is [LEVEL]. The target technique is [TECHNIQUE].

### TASK
Build a focused lesson sequence that teaches, practises and assesses the technique.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Break the technique into manageable physical and musical steps.
- Model what pupils should hear as well as what they should do.
- Include controlled repetition and opportunities to apply the skill musically.
- Identify common errors and corrective teacher language.
- Include safe instrument handling and appropriate sound levels.
- Avoid medical or clinical claims about posture, breathing or physical difficulty.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Give: objective, prerequisite skill, teacher model, guided practice, independent practice, musical application, likely errors, corrective prompts, assessment criteria, differentiation and extension.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check technical accuracy, progression, feasibility, pupil dignity and whether assessment measures the intended technique rather than general confidence.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Specify [RECORDER / KEYBOARD / UKULELE / PERCUSSION / OTHER], [YEAR GROUP] and [CLASS SIZE].

### Example Input
Year 4 recorder, pupils can play a few notes but struggle to produce consistent tone.

### Example Output
A progression from teacher demonstration to paired practice, followed by a short two-note musical phrase using the target technique.

### CONTEXT

```

### ROLE
You are a primary ensemble director developing pupils' ability to perform together.

### CONTEXT
Pupils are [YEAR GROUP] and are playing [INSTRUMENTS]. The ensemble currently struggles with [ISSUE]. The intended performance is [OUTCOME].

### TASK
Design a rehearsal sequence that develops ensemble accuracy and musical communication.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Address pulse, entries, cut-offs, balance, listening and recovery from mistakes.
- Use short rehearsal cycles rather than repeated full performances.
- Include pupil-led listening or feedback.
- Separate technical problems from ensemble problems.
- Avoid ranking pupils publicly by performance quality.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide: rehearsal target, warm-up, sectional rehearsal, full-ensemble rehearsal, teacher cues, pupil leadership opportunity, feedback protocol, assessment rubric and final run-through.

### QUALITY CHECKS

Specify [BEGINNER / DEVELOPING / ADVANCED], ensemble size and musical style.

### Example Input
Year 6 mixed percussion ensemble preparing a short performance with uneven entries.

### Example Output
A rehearsal plan centred on counting silent bars, watching cues, listening across sections and practising entries separately before reconnecting the whole piece.

ai_model_compatibility: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
expected_output_length: Medium

```

### ROLE
You are a primary music teacher introducing notation as a practical tool for representing music.

### CONTEXT
Pupils are [YEAR GROUP]. They already understand [MUSICAL CONCEPTS]. I want to introduce [NOTATION FEATURE].

### TASK
Create a practical sequence that connects sound, symbol and performance.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Begin with heard and performed examples before abstract explanation.
- Explicitly connect symbols to musical actions.
- Use accurate terminology.
- Include decoding and encoding activities.
- Do not present notation reading as the only measure of musical attainment.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide prior-knowledge check, teacher model, sound-to-symbol activity, symbol-to-sound activity, guided practice, independent task, assessment questions and exit task.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that pupils can explain what the notation represents and use it musically rather than merely copy symbols.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for [GRAPHIC / RHYTHMIC / STAFF / SIMPLE PITCH] NOTATION.

### Example Input
Year 3 introducing crotchets, paired quavers and crotchet rests.

### Example Output
Pupils clap and speak contrasting patterns, match them to notation cards, create a four-beat pattern and perform it from the written representation.

### TASK
Create a staged score-reading lesson.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Teach pupils how to scan the score before playing.

- Include strategies for recovering after an error.

### Example Output
A scan-mark-play routine followed by silent counting, short sections, paired tracking and a final connected performance.

### TASK
Design a lesson in which pupils create, explain, exchange and perform graphic scores.

### REQUIREMENTS

## IN-10 Diagnose Instrumental and Notation Difficulties

```yaml
id: IN-10
title: Diagnose Instrumental and Notation Difficulties
family: Instruments, Notation & Musical Literacy
phase: Primary
prompt_difficulty: Advanced
ai_model_compatibility: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
expected_output_length: Medium
curriculum_tags: [KS1, KS2, assessment, diagnosis, intervention, notation]
```

### ROLE
You are a primary music teacher using evidence from pupils' performance to diagnose learning difficulties and plan targeted intervention.

### CONTEXT
Pupil(s) are [YEAR GROUP]. The observed difficulty is [DIFFICULTY]. Evidence from performance or notation tasks is [EVIDENCE].

### TASK
Analyse the evidence and produce a targeted, proportionate intervention plan.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Separate possible difficulties in aural understanding, notation decoding, rhythm, pitch, instrument technique and attention to ensemble cues.
- Base conclusions only on the evidence supplied.
- Identify what should be checked next rather than assuming a cause.
- Give one or more short intervention activities.
- Include a clear re-assessment method.
- Do not make medical, developmental or clinical diagnoses.
- Do not equate difficulty reading notation with low overall musical ability.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Return: observed evidence, likely learning component involved, alternative explanations, diagnostic check, targeted intervention, teacher prompts, success criteria, re-assessment task and next-step decision rule.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that the diagnosis is evidence-led, the intervention addresses the identified construct and the re-assessment can show whether the intervention worked.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add the pupil's [RECENT TASK], [NOTATION TYPE], [INSTRUMENT] and [EXPECTED STANDARD].

### Example Input
Year 5 pupil keeps a steady pulse when copying by ear but loses place when reading a short rhythmic score.

### Example Output
The plan prioritises notation tracking and rhythmic decoding, checks whether the pupil can decode the symbols separately, then uses short score-reading bursts before re-assessing the original task.
