# Singing, Voice & Vocal Development

This family contains 10 copy/paste-ready workflows for safe, inclusive and progressive singing and vocal learning in primary classrooms.

## SV-01: Plan a Singing Lesson

**Prompt Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 900 to 1,400 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 1 to 2 minutes  
**Curriculum Tags:** Primary, Music, Singing, Voice, Lesson Planning

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music teacher with expertise in classroom singing.

### CONTEXT
The class is **[YEAR GROUP]**. The lesson is **[MINUTES]** minutes. Pupils are learning **[SONG / MUSICAL FOCUS]**. Prior singing experience is **[PRIOR LEARNING]**. Available resources are **[RESOURCES]**.

### TASK
Plan a practical singing lesson that develops musical understanding and vocal skill progressively.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Include a short, age-appropriate preparation or warm-up.
- Model the target musical feature clearly.
- Include opportunities for pupils to listen, imitate, practise and apply.
- Identify pitch, rhythm, diction, dynamics or phrasing goals where relevant.
- Avoid forcing pupils to sing at a pitch or volume that is uncomfortable.
- Include inclusive participation routes without humiliating pupils who are reluctant or developing singers.
- Include a clear assessment checkpoint.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide learning intention, success criteria, preparation, teacher model, guided practice, song-learning sequence, independent application, assessment, inclusion and reflection.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that vocal activities are age-appropriate, do not encourage vocal strain and are realistic for the class.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for **[CLASS PROFILE / SONG / PITCH RANGE / RESOURCE]**.

### Example Input
Year 4, 40 minutes, learning a new song with a focus on accurate pitch and clear diction.

### Example Output
A lesson beginning with gentle vocal preparation, followed by teacher modelling, phrase-by-phrase learning, supported singing and an assessed final performance.

**Prompt Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 700 to 1,100 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 1 to 2 minutes  
**Curriculum Tags:** Primary, Music, Singing, Vocal Warm-Up, Safety

### ROLE
You are a primary music specialist designing safe classroom vocal preparation.

### CONTEXT
I need a **[5 / 10] minute** vocal warm-up for **[YEAR GROUP]** before **[ACTIVITY]**.

### TASK
Create a short sequence that prepares pupils for singing without unnecessary strain.

### REQUIREMENTS
Use gentle breathing, posture, resonance, articulation and simple vocal patterns where appropriate. Keep pitch ranges developmentally sensible. Explain teacher cues and stopping points. Do not present the routine as medical treatment or claim that it prevents injury.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide sequence, timing, teacher language, pupil action, musical purpose and safety note for each activity.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that exercises do not require shouting, excessive volume, prolonged high notes or uncomfortable vocal extremes.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for **[YEAR GROUP / SONG / AVAILABLE SPACE]**.

### Example Input
A five-minute warm-up for Year 3 before whole-class singing.

### Example Output
A gentle progression from posture and breathing awareness to articulation and comfortable short vocal patterns.

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 1,000 to 1,500 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 1 to 3 minutes  
**Curriculum Tags:** Primary, Music, Singing, Pitch, Aural Development

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music teacher specialising in aural and singing development.

### CONTEXT
I have **[YEAR GROUP]** pupils who are developing their ability to match pitch. Current evidence is **[OBSERVATION]** and lesson time is **[MINUTES]**.

### TASK
Design a supportive teaching sequence for developing pitch matching.

### REQUIREMENTS
Begin with comfortable, accessible patterns and use repetition, listening and imitation. Include individual, paired and whole-class options so pupils are not singled out unnecessarily. Explain what the teacher should listen for and how to adjust the task if the pitch range is too difficult.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide learning intention, sequence, teacher modelling, practice variations, observation checklist, responsive adaptations and progression.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Avoid unsupported claims about why an individual pupil may struggle with pitch. Focus on observable musical behaviour and teachable next steps.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Use **[SONG / PITCH RANGE / CLASS SIZE]**.

### Example Input
Year 2 pupils developing matching of short two- or three-note patterns.

### Example Output
A low-pressure listening-and-response sequence using short patterns, gradual variation and teacher observation rather than public individual testing.

### CONTEXT

A sequence using rhythmic speaking, targeted consonant rehearsal and gradual reintegration into the sung phrase.

**Expected Output Length:** 900 to 1,400 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 1 to 2 minutes  
**Curriculum Tags:** Primary, Music, Singing, Expression, Interpretation

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music teacher developing expressive performance.

### CONTEXT
Pupils are **[YEAR GROUP]** and can already sing **[REPERTOIRE]** with reasonable accuracy. The next goal is **[EXPRESSIVE GOAL]**.

### TASK
Design a rehearsal sequence that helps pupils make deliberate expressive choices.

### REQUIREMENTS
Address dynamics, phrasing, tempo, articulation, tone or mood where relevant. Model contrasting interpretations and ask pupils to explain choices using musical vocabulary. Avoid treating subjective interpretation as a single objectively correct answer.

Use **[SONG / MUSICAL CONTEXT]**.

Year 6 pupils preparing a song and needing to improve phrasing and dynamic contrast.

### Example Output
A compare-and-rehearse sequence where pupils hear contrasting phrasing choices, justify their preferred approach and refine a final performance.

## SV-06: Support Reluctant Singers

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 1,000 to 1,500 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 1 to 3 minutes  
**Curriculum Tags:** Primary, Music, Singing, Inclusion, Participation

### ROLE
You are an inclusive primary music specialist.

### CONTEXT
A pupil or group of pupils is reluctant to sing publicly. Observed behaviour is **[OBSERVATION]**. The intended musical learning is **[OBJECTIVE]**.

### TASK
Create supportive strategies that maintain meaningful musical participation without coercion or embarrassment.

### REQUIREMENTS
Offer gradual participation routes such as singing within a group, humming where appropriate, echo responses, paired work, instrumental or rhythmic contribution and private rehearsal. Distinguish reluctance from lack of ability. Do not diagnose anxiety or other conditions from classroom behaviour.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide immediate strategies, short-term progression, teacher language, participation options, assessment approach and review points.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Protect pupil dignity and ensure adaptations still provide meaningful evidence of the intended musical learning.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for **[YEAR GROUP / CLASS CONTEXT / PERFORMANCE EVENT]**.

### Example Input
A Year 5 pupil participates in group singing but refuses solo singing.

### Example Output
A progression that keeps the pupil involved in ensemble singing while gradually introducing low-pressure paired or small-group opportunities if appropriate.

## SV-08: Assess Singing Progress

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 1,100 to 1,600 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 1 to 3 minutes  
**Curriculum Tags:** Primary, Music, Singing, Assessment, Progress

### ROLE
You are a primary music assessment specialist.

### CONTEXT
I need to assess singing progress for **[YEAR GROUP]**. The learning objectives are **[OBJECTIVES]** and the available evidence is **[OBSERVATIONS / RECORDINGS / PERFORMANCE]**.

### TASK
Create a fair assessment approach that separates relevant musical constructs.

### REQUIREMENTS
Assess pitch accuracy, rhythmic accuracy, diction, dynamics, phrasing, musical response and ensemble contribution only where relevant to the stated objectives. Do not equate loudness, confidence, prior private tuition or willingness to sing solo with overall attainment. Provide observable descriptors rather than vague labels.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide assessment criteria, a four-point progression rubric, evidence sources, teacher observation prompts, feedback language and next steps.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check construct validity, age suitability and respectful language.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Use **[SCHOOL ASSESSMENT MODEL]** if supplied.

### Example Input
Year 5 objective focused on accurate pitch, rhythm and expressive choices in a group song.

### Example Output
A rubric separating pitch/rhythm accuracy, expressive choices and ensemble contribution, with evidence collected through normal rehearsal and performance.

## SV-09: Diagnose a Singing Difficulty

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 1,000 to 1,500 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 1 to 3 minutes  
**Curriculum Tags:** Primary, Music, Singing, Diagnosis, Intervention

### ROLE
You are a primary music teacher using evidence-led musical intervention.

### CONTEXT
I have observed the following issue: **[OBSERVATION]**. The pupil/group is **[YEAR GROUP]** and the current task is **[TASK]**.

### TASK
Analyse the observable musical difficulty and recommend targeted teaching responses.

### REQUIREMENTS
Separate possible pitch, rhythm, memory, diction, listening, coordination or task-design issues. Do not diagnose medical or psychological conditions. Give a small number of practical interventions and explain what evidence would indicate improvement.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide observed issue, plausible musical explanations, immediate intervention, follow-up activity, evidence to collect and decision rule for the next lesson.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Do not infer causes that are not supported by the observation. Keep interventions proportionate and teachable.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add **[PUPIL WORK SAMPLE / RECORDING DESCRIPTION / REPERTOIRE]**.

### Example Input
A pupil consistently starts a sung phrase at the correct pitch but drifts away from it.

### Example Output
A set of listening, short-phrase imitation and reduced-complexity activities, with observation of whether pitch stability improves across repeated attempts.

## SV-10: Plan a Vocal Performance Progression

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 1,300 to 1,900 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 2 to 4 minutes  
**Curriculum Tags:** Primary, Music, Singing, Performance, Progression

### ROLE
You are a primary music curriculum leader planning a progressive vocal performance pathway.

### CONTEXT
The pupils are **[YEAR GROUP]** and have **[NUMBER]** lessons before a performance. Current singing skills are **[CURRENT POSITION]**. Repertoire is **[REPERTOIRE]**.

### TASK
Create a progression from initial learning to confident, musical and inclusive performance.

### REQUIREMENTS
Sequence repertoire learning, pitch/rhythm accuracy, diction, expressive choices, ensemble awareness, rehearsal discipline and performance reflection. Build in manageable rehearsal goals. Include alternative meaningful roles where appropriate. Do not require solo performance from pupils as a measure of progress unless it is an explicit objective and is approached appropriately.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide a rehearsal timeline, weekly goals, teacher observation criteria, pupil reflection prompts, inclusion strategies and final assessment.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check vocal safety, feasibility, musical progression and pupil dignity throughout the plan.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for **[PERFORMANCE VENUE / CLASS SIZE / REHEARSAL TIME]**.

### Example Input
Year 6 pupils have four lessons to prepare two songs for a school event.

### Example Output
A four-lesson progression from secure repertoire learning through ensemble refinement, expressive rehearsal, full run-through and reflective final performance.
