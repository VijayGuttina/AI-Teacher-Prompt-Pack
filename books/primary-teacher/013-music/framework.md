# 013 Music Framework

## Purpose

This framework defines the architecture, quality standards and verification approach for the Music prompt pack. The prompts are designed to help primary teachers plan, teach, assess, adapt and reflect on music learning across singing, listening, composing, performing, musical literacy, instruments, technology and cultural context.

The pack is intended to support teacher judgement, not replace it. Prompts should produce practical classroom-ready material while making assumptions visible and avoiding unsupported claims.

## Core Principles

1. **Active music-making**: prioritise meaningful opportunities to sing, play, compose, improvise, listen and perform.
2. **Progression**: build musical knowledge and skill cumulatively rather than treating activities as isolated experiences.
3. **Musical understanding**: connect practical activity with listening, vocabulary, concepts and reflection.
4. **Technical accuracy**: use correct musical terminology and avoid presenting uncertain technique as universal fact.
5. **Inclusive participation**: provide accessible routes into sound-making, listening, composing, performing and responding.
6. **Notation as a means, not the sole measure**: musical learning may be demonstrated through a range of practical, aural, creative, verbal and notational responses.
7. **Pupil agency and dignity**: avoid humiliating pupils through public comparison or deficit language, particularly in singing and performance.
8. **Cultural respect**: represent musical traditions accurately and respectfully. Do not reduce cultures to stereotypes or imply that one musical tradition represents an entire community.
9. **Age-appropriate care**: singing and instrumental activities should reflect pupils' developmental stage and avoid unnecessary strain.
10. **Safe sound and equipment use**: consider volume, hearing, electrical equipment, cables, instruments and classroom movement where relevant.
11. **Evidence-led intervention**: identify the specific musical barrier before recommending support or reteaching.
12. **Appropriate uncertainty**: where school procedures, available instruments, software or local curriculum decisions are unknown, state assumptions and request or use supplied information rather than inventing details.

## Standard Workflow Architecture

Every workflow follows the agreed prompt architecture:

1. `ROLE`
2. `CONTEXT`
3. `TASK`
4. `REQUIREMENTS`
5. `OUTPUT FORMAT`
6. `QUALITY CHECKS`
7. `OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION`

Prompts should also include the agreed metadata and, where specified by the master specification, Example Input and Example Output.

## Music Quality Gates

Every workflow should be checked for:

- curriculum and learning-intent alignment
- age and developmental suitability
- accurate musical terminology
- clear progression from prior learning
- meaningful music-making or listening activity
- realistic classroom implementation
- appropriate assumptions about instruments, space and technology
- observable success criteria
- useful teacher actions and pupil actions
- inclusive access and sensible adaptation
- respectful treatment of performance differences
- culturally accurate and non-stereotyped representation
- safe use of voice, instruments, sound and equipment
- feasible teacher workload
- evidence-led assessment and intervention
- explicit handling of missing or school-specific information

## Planning Model

Use the following sequence where appropriate:

**Learning intention → Model/listen/demonstrate → Guided music-making → Independent or collaborative application → Feedback → Reflection → Revisit/progress**

Planning prompts should identify what pupils will know, hear, do or create, how the teacher will model it, how pupils will practise it, and how progress will be observed.

## Musical Development Model

Prompts should support progression through:

**Explore → Notice → Imitate → Practise → Stabilise → Apply → Adapt → Combine → Create/Perform → Evaluate**

The exact sequence may vary by learning objective. The framework should not imply that every pupil must progress through identical stages at identical speed.

## Assessment Model

Assessment should distinguish, where relevant:

- musical knowledge and vocabulary
- aural recognition and listening
- pulse and rhythmic control
- melodic understanding
- vocal or instrumental technique
- notation and musical literacy
- composing and developing musical ideas
- improvisation and creative decision-making
- performance and expressive choices
- ensemble awareness and collaboration
- safe and appropriate participation
- progress over time

Do not treat confidence, prior extracurricular tuition, access to instruments outside school, singing volume, speed of reading notation or public performance style as automatic measures of overall musical attainment.

## Inclusion Model

Adaptation may address:

- instrument choice
- pitch range
- rhythmic complexity
- notation density
- modelling and demonstration
- visual, aural and tactile supports
- repetition and rehearsal time
- grouping and ensemble role
- response mode
- volume and sensory load
- physical access to instruments and equipment
- recording and reflection methods

Prompts should preserve the intended musical construct wherever possible. If an adaptation changes the construct being assessed, the output should say so.

## Safety, Wellbeing & Equipment Model

Music prompts should integrate proportionate safety guidance. Consider:

- safe and age-appropriate vocal use
- sensible sound levels and hearing considerations
- safe handling and storage of instruments
- cables, electrical equipment and digital devices
- classroom movement and trip hazards
- appropriate use of specialist instruments or equipment
- pupil dignity during singing and performance

Use school procedures when supplied. Do not invent emergency arrangements, medical protocols, supervision ratios or specialist safeguarding procedures. Avoid unsupported therapeutic or medical claims about music, singing, hearing or voice.

## Verification Model

The Music pack will be verified using three layers:

### 1. Structural QA

All workflows must contain the required metadata and agreed prompt architecture. Target: **100/100 workflows structurally complete**.

### 2. Representative In-Model Execution

Execute exactly two representative workflows from each family, giving **20/20 representative executions**. Check that outputs are usable, coherent, age-appropriate, musically accurate and aligned with the stated task.

### 3. Targeted Risk Checks

Complete 16 targeted checks:

1. Vocal safety and age-appropriate singing
2. Musical terminology accuracy
3. Pulse, rhythm and notation accuracy
4. Instrument technique accuracy
5. Safe handling of instruments and equipment
6. Appropriate progression
7. Composition and improvisation construct validity
8. Fair performance assessment
9. Inclusive access to music-making
10. Culturally respectful representation
11. Listening and appraising accuracy
12. Digital audio and recording claims
13. Safe sound levels and hearing boundaries
14. Avoidance of unsupported therapeutic or medical claims
15. Evidence-led intervention
16. Safe handling of missing school-specific procedures

A workflow is not considered frozen solely because its structure is valid. Execution verification must test whether the prompt actually produces a useful result.
