# Digital Music, Technology & Recording

This family provides ten copy-paste-ready workflows for using digital tools to support primary music-making, composition, recording, editing and reflection. Prompts should be adapted to the software and devices actually available in the school.

## DM-01 Explore Digital Music Tools

```yaml
id: DM-01
title: Explore Digital Music Tools
family: Digital Music, Technology & Recording
phase: Primary
prompt_difficulty: Intermediate
ai_model_compatibility: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
expected_output_length: Medium
typical_generation_time: 30-60 seconds
curriculum_tags: [KS1, KS2, digital music, technology, composition]
```

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music teacher introducing pupils to digital music-making.

### CONTEXT
Pupils are [YEAR GROUP]. The available device or software is [TOOL]. They have [PRIOR EXPERIENCE]. The lesson lasts [MINUTES].

### TASK
Create a practical lesson in which pupils learn the minimum technology skills needed to make a simple musical outcome.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Prioritise musical learning over software features.
- Give short, sequential instructions suitable for the age group.
- Include an unplugged or low-tech alternative where practical.
- Use only features that can reasonably be assumed from the stated tool, and flag anything that needs checking.
- Include safe and responsible device use.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide learning intention, vocabulary, teacher demonstration, guided task, independent music-making, troubleshooting, assessment, differentiation and exit reflection.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that the technology serves a clear musical purpose and that no unsupported software feature is presented as fact.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Specify [APP/SOFTWARE], device type and whether pupils work individually, in pairs or groups.

### Example Input
Year 4 using a tablet music app for a first introduction to sequencing.

### Example Output
A short sequence in which pupils create a repeating rhythmic pattern, test it, make one musical change and explain what changed.

```

### ROLE
You are a primary music teacher using digital technology to support composition.

### CONTEXT
Pupils are [YEAR GROUP]. They are composing around [MUSICAL IDEA]. The available tool is [SOFTWARE].

### TASK
Design a composition lesson that uses the technology to test, develop and refine musical ideas.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Start with a clear musical brief.
- Require pupils to make intentional choices about rhythm, melody, texture, dynamics, timbre or structure as appropriate.
- Avoid treating clicking or arranging pre-made loops as sufficient evidence of composition without musical decision-making.
- Include revision after listening.
- Provide an accessible non-digital route.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide composition brief, stimulus, planning, digital workflow, musical checkpoints, revision cycle, sharing task and assessment criteria.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that the assessed outcome measures musical composition rather than software fluency alone.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add [STYLE], [DURATION], [MUSICAL ELEMENTS] and [SOFTWARE].

### Example Input
Year 6 composing a 60-second piece using layered loops and recorded sounds.

### Example Output
Pupils select a brief, plan contrasting sections, justify their layers, listen critically and revise one section for clearer structure.

```

### ROLE
You are a primary music teacher teaching pupils to make purposeful edits to a recording.

### CONTEXT
Pupils have recorded [MUSICAL MATERIAL]. They are using [SOFTWARE] and currently understand [SKILLS].

### TASK
Create a lesson in which pupils listen critically and make limited, purposeful edits.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Separate musical editing from technical editing.
- Focus on achievable edits such as trimming, ordering sections, adjusting obvious balance problems or removing an unwanted recording segment where appropriate.
- Require pupils to listen before and after each significant edit.
- Avoid presenting automated processing as inherently improving music.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide listening criteria, teacher model, guided edit, pupil edit task, before-and-after comparison, reflection and assessment.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that edits support the musical intention and do not obscure the distinction between performance and production skills.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Specify the available editing features and the musical outcome.

### Example Input
Year 6 has a recording with a long unwanted gap between two sections.

### Example Output
Pupils identify the gap, make a clean edit, listen for continuity and explain why the edit improves the recording's structure.

You are a primary music teacher using sequencing technology to strengthen pupils' rhythmic understanding.

### CONTEXT
Pupils are [YEAR GROUP]. They know [RHYTHMIC CONCEPTS]. The sequencer is [TOOL].

### TASK
Create a lesson connecting visual sequencing with heard and performed rhythm.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Establish the underlying pulse before sequencing.
- Require pupils to predict what a sequence will sound like.
- Include a physical or spoken rhythm activity alongside the software.
- Address common confusion between grid positions, pulse and rhythm.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide aural starter, physical rhythm task, sequencer demonstration, prediction task, creation task, playback analysis and assessment.

### QUALITY CHECKS

### Example Input
Year 5 using a step sequencer to create four-bar percussion patterns in 4/4.

### Example Output
Pupils clap a pattern first, predict its grid representation, build it, listen back and revise one rhythmic feature deliberately.

## DM-10 Diagnose Digital Music and Recording Difficulties

```yaml
id: DM-10
title: Diagnose Digital Music and Recording Difficulties
family: Digital Music, Technology & Recording
phase: Primary
prompt_difficulty: Advanced
ai_model_compatibility: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
expected_output_length: Medium
typical_generation_time: 30-60 seconds
curriculum_tags: [KS1, KS2, assessment, diagnosis, technology, recording]
```

### ROLE
You are a primary music teacher diagnosing whether a difficulty in digital music-making is primarily musical, technical or a combination of both.

### CONTEXT
Pupils are [YEAR GROUP]. They are using [TOOL]. The observed problem is [PROBLEM]. Evidence is [EVIDENCE].

### TASK
Analyse the evidence and produce a short diagnostic and intervention plan.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Distinguish musical understanding from software navigation difficulty.
- Check whether the pupil can demonstrate the musical idea without the technology where appropriate.
- Identify what evidence is still needed before concluding a cause.
- Give a short intervention and re-test.
- Do not make clinical or developmental diagnoses.
- Do not assume a device or software fault without checking.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Return: observed evidence, possible causes, diagnostic check, intervention, teacher prompts, re-test, success criteria and next decision.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that conclusions are evidence-led and that the intervention targets the actual barrier.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add [SOFTWARE], [TASK], [PUPIL RESPONSE] and any known technical constraints.

### Example Input
Year 5 pupil can clap a rhythm accurately but places it incorrectly in the digital sequencer.

### Example Output
First test whether the pupil can map the rhythm to a printed grid, then teach the specific sequencing representation and re-test the original pattern.
