# Pulse, Rhythm & Musical Elements

This workflow family contains 10 copy/paste-ready ChatGPT workflows for teaching pulse, rhythm and core musical elements in primary music.

The prompts are designed to produce practical classroom-ready outputs while protecting musical accuracy, pupil dignity, inclusion, progression and safe participation.

## PR-01 Teach Pulse and Beat

```yaml
id: PR-01
title: Teach Pulse and Beat
category: Pulse, Rhythm & Musical Elements
tags: [pulse, beat, rhythm, KS1, KS2, music]
prompt_difficulty: Intermediate
ai_model_compatibility: [ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot]
expected_output_length: Medium
typical_generation_time: 1-2 minutes
```

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music teacher and curriculum specialist. You understand age-appropriate musical development, active music-making and practical classroom teaching.

### CONTEXT
I am teaching primary pupils who need to understand pulse and beat through listening, movement, speaking and music-making. The pupils are aged [AGE/YEAR GROUP]. The lesson length is [MINUTES]. Available resources are [RESOURCES]. Their current musical experience is [EXPERIENCE].

### TASK
Create a practical lesson for teaching the difference between a steady pulse and the changing rhythms performed over it.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Begin with a clear learning intention and 2 to 4 pupil-friendly success criteria.
- Use active music-making rather than relying on explanation.
- Include a progression from feeling the pulse through movement to keeping a pulse while speaking, clapping or playing.
- Explain pulse and beat accurately in language appropriate for the pupils.
- Include at least one call-and-response activity and one independent or paired activity.
- Include a short musical listening example or describe the characteristics the teacher should look for when selecting one.
- Include likely misconceptions, especially confusing pulse with rhythm.
- Provide teacher modelling language and concise prompts.
- Include an adaptation for pupils who find maintaining a steady pulse difficult.
- Include an extension for pupils who can maintain a pulse securely.
- Do not require specialist instruments unless they are listed in the resources.
- Keep movement safe and suitable for the stated age group.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Lesson overview
2. Learning intention
3. Success criteria
4. Starter
5. Teacher modelling
6. Guided practice
7. Independent or paired practice
8. Musical application
9. Assessment opportunities
10. Misconceptions and responses
11. Inclusion and adaptation
12. Extension
13. Plenary
14. Teacher preparation checklist

### QUALITY CHECKS
Before giving the answer, check that pulse is treated as a regular underlying beat rather than interchangeable with rhythm, that activities create meaningful opportunities to experience and maintain pulse, and that all activities are realistic for a primary classroom.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
If useful, also provide a version using only body percussion and voice, or adapt the lesson for [KS1/KS2], [CLASS SIZE] and [AVAILABLE SPACE].

### Example Input
Year 2, 30 minutes, body percussion and classroom instruments available, pupils have some previous singing experience but limited rhythm vocabulary.

### Example Output
A 30-minute lesson beginning with walking to a steady pulse, moving to teacher-led pulse copying, then maintaining a pulse while speaking a short chant. Pupils distinguish pulse from rhythm by comparing a steady hand tap with changing spoken patterns, followed by paired pulse-keeping and a short ensemble performance. Assessment focuses on whether pupils can maintain a steady pulse while another rhythm is performed.

### CONTEXT

### TASK
Create a practical lesson that introduces or develops syncopation through listening, movement, speaking and performance.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Define syncopation accurately without making it unnecessarily technical.
- Start from a secure pulse and use the pulse to help pupils hear displaced rhythmic emphasis.
- Use an accessible musical or spoken example.
- Include teacher modelling and pupil imitation.
- Include a movement or body-percussion activity that helps pupils experience the relationship between pulse and syncopated rhythm.
- Progress from imitation to reading or representation where appropriate.
- Include a short creation task.
- Identify the difference between simply placing notes off the beat and the broader idea of syncopated emphasis.
- Include misconceptions and precise corrective strategies.
- Provide an inclusive reduced-complexity version.
- Avoid assuming pupils can read notation unless their prior knowledge supports it.

10. Assessment
11. Misconceptions and corrections
12. Inclusion and extension
13. Plenary

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that the explanation of syncopation is musically accurate, that activities make the pulse audible or physically clear, and that the task does not reduce syncopation to a vague instruction to "clap between the beats."

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt the lesson for [YEAR GROUP], or provide rhythm grids and short patterns using notation already taught to the class.

### Example Input
Year 5, 45 minutes, pupils can perform simple crotchet and quaver patterns and can maintain a steady pulse.

### Example Output
Pupils establish a four-beat pulse, listen to contrasting on-beat and syncopated patterns, perform a teacher-modelled pattern using body percussion, then create and perform a short syncopated rhythm over a steady class pulse.

Year 3, 35 minutes, pupils understand steady pulse and basic dynamics.

## PR-10 Diagnose Rhythm and Pulse Difficulties

```yaml
id: PR-10
title: Diagnose Rhythm and Pulse Difficulties
category: Pulse, Rhythm & Musical Elements
tags: [assessment, diagnosis, pulse, rhythm, intervention, KS1, KS2]
prompt_difficulty: Advanced
ai_model_compatibility: [ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot]
expected_output_length: Long
typical_generation_time: 2-3 minutes
```

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music teacher, assessment specialist and intervention planner. You distinguish between different musical difficulties and design proportionate, evidence-informed classroom responses.

### CONTEXT
I have observed the following pupil difficulty: [OBSERVATION]. The pupil is in [YEAR GROUP]. They can currently [STRENGTHS]. The difficulty occurs during [ACTIVITY]. Available time for intervention is [TIME]. Resources are [RESOURCES].

### TASK
Analyse the observed difficulty and create a short diagnostic and intervention plan focused on pulse and/or rhythm.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Separate observable evidence from assumptions about the cause.
- Identify whether the main difficulty appears to involve pulse, rhythm, coordination, listening, musical memory, notation, attention to the ensemble or another factor.
- Do not diagnose a medical, developmental or neurological condition from classroom observation.
- Provide 2 to 4 low-risk diagnostic activities that help distinguish possible musical causes.
- Explain what the teacher should observe in each activity.
- Recommend a short sequence of targeted interventions.
- Begin with the simplest useful musical construct before adding complexity.
- Include repetition and gradual progression.
- Use voice, movement, body percussion or instruments as appropriate.
- Include an inclusive alternative where movement, sensory input or fine-motor demands may interfere with demonstrating musical understanding.
- Define observable indicators of improvement.
- State when the teacher should stop, review the evidence or seek appropriate school-based support rather than infer a cause.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Observation summary
2. Evidence versus assumption
3. Possible musical explanations
4. Diagnostic activity 1
5. Diagnostic activity 2
6. Diagnostic activity 3, if needed
7. Interpretation guide
8. Targeted intervention sequence
9. Differentiation and inclusion
10. Progress indicators
11. Review point
12. Teacher recording template

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that the response does not make unsupported diagnoses, that interventions target the observed musical construct, that pulse and rhythm are not conflated, and that success is measured through observable musical behaviour.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
If I provide several observations from different lessons, compare them and identify the most defensible intervention priority without making clinical claims.

### Example Input
Year 4 pupil can copy short spoken rhythms accurately when working alone but regularly loses the pulse when performing with a group.

### Example Output
The plan identifies ensemble pulse maintenance as the priority rather than assuming a general rhythm deficit. Diagnostic tasks compare solo pulse keeping, pulse keeping while listening to another rhythm, and pulse keeping within a small ensemble. Intervention then moves from a secure external pulse to reduced teacher support and finally to ensemble application, with progress measured by sustained pulse across increasingly independent activities.
