# Music Enquiry & Curriculum Planning

This family contains 10 copy/paste-ready workflows for curriculum planning, sequencing, lesson design and musical learning intentions.

## MU-01: Plan a Music Unit

**Prompt Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 1,200 to 1,800 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 1 to 3 minutes  
**Curriculum Tags:** Primary, Music, Curriculum Planning, Unit Planning

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music curriculum specialist and classroom teacher.

### CONTEXT
I am planning a music unit for **[YEAR GROUP]** pupils. The unit will run for **[NUMBER] lessons** of approximately **[LESSON LENGTH] minutes**. The main musical focus is **[FOCUS]**. Available instruments/resources are **[RESOURCES]**. Prior learning is **[PRIOR LEARNING]**. The curriculum expectations or school objectives are **[OBJECTIVES]**.

### TASK
Design a coherent unit that develops pupils' musical knowledge and practical skills progressively.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Identify a precise learning intention for each lesson.
- Sequence knowledge and practical activity so that later learning builds on earlier learning.
- Include singing, listening, performing, composing or improvising where genuinely relevant rather than adding activities for variety alone.
- Identify the musical vocabulary pupils should encounter and use.
- Include teacher modelling, guided practice and independent or collaborative application.
- Provide realistic equipment and classroom-management assumptions.
- Include an observable success criterion for each lesson.
- Build in retrieval or revisiting of important prior learning.
- Include inclusive adaptations that preserve the intended musical learning.
- Do not assume specialist instruments, notation skills or prior tuition unless supplied.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Use this structure:
1. Unit overview
2. Prior learning
3. End-of-unit learning goals
4. Lesson-by-lesson sequence table with: learning intention, key vocabulary, musical activity, teacher modelling, pupil practice, assessment evidence and progression
5. Inclusion and adaptation notes
6. Resources
7. End-of-unit assessment approach
8. Teacher preparation checklist

### QUALITY CHECKS
Before finalising, check that the sequence is progressive, age-appropriate, musically accurate, practical within the stated lesson time and does not rely on resources that were not supplied.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
If information is missing, make only reasonable assumptions and label them clearly. Adapt the sequence for **[SCHOOL PRIORITY / CLASS PROFILE]** if supplied.

### Example Input
Year 4, six 45-minute lessons, focus on rhythm and texture, using untuned percussion and body percussion. Pupils can maintain a simple pulse and read basic rhythmic notation.

### Example Output
A six-lesson progression beginning with pulse and rhythmic pattern recognition, moving through layered rhythms and texture, then developing a short group composition that pupils rehearse, perform and evaluate.

## MU-02: Create a Lesson Sequence from a Learning Objective

**Prompt Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 900 to 1,400 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 1 to 2 minutes  
**Curriculum Tags:** Primary, Music, Lesson Planning, Progression

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music teacher specialising in progression and practical lesson design.

### CONTEXT
My learning objective is: **[LEARNING OBJECTIVE]**. The class is **[YEAR GROUP]**. Lesson length is **[MINUTES]**. Pupils already know **[PRIOR KNOWLEDGE]** and have access to **[RESOURCES]**.

### TASK
Turn the objective into a logically sequenced music lesson.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Start with retrieval or connection to prior learning.
- Model the new musical idea clearly.
- Include guided practice before independent application.
- Include a meaningful opportunity to hear or make music.
- Specify what the teacher should listen for or observe.
- Include a short assessment checkpoint and a response to likely errors.
- End with reflection or performance evidence.
- Keep activities realistic for the stated time.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide: learning intention, success criteria, prior-learning retrieval, teacher model, guided practice, independent/collaborative task, assessment checkpoint, misconception responses, plenary and extension/support.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that every activity contributes directly to the objective and that the final task provides evidence of the intended learning.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for **[INSTRUMENTS / CLASS SIZE / SPACE / ADDITIONAL NEEDS]**.

### Example Input
Year 3 pupils learning to identify and perform contrasting dynamics using classroom percussion.

### Example Output
A practical lesson that moves from listening and vocabulary retrieval to teacher modelling of contrasting dynamics, guided performance, paired application and a short assessed performance.

## MU-03: Build a Progression Map

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 1,500 to 2,200 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 2 to 4 minutes  
**Curriculum Tags:** Primary, Music, Progression, Curriculum Mapping

### ROLE
You are a primary music curriculum leader.

### CONTEXT
I need progression across **[YEAR GROUPS / KEY STAGE]** for **[MUSICAL DOMAIN]**. Current learning is **[CURRENT POSITION]** and the intended endpoint is **[ENDPOINT]**.

### TASK
Create a progression map that shows how knowledge and practical skill should develop over time.

### REQUIREMENTS
Distinguish knowledge, aural skill, practical technique, creativity and vocabulary. Identify prerequisites, increasing complexity and likely transition points. Avoid equating prior instrumental tuition with general musical attainment.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide a progression table followed by teacher guidance, prerequisite knowledge, common gaps and assessment evidence.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check for genuine progression rather than simply increasing activity difficulty. Ensure terminology is accurate and age-appropriate.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Use **[SCHOOL CURRICULUM / INSTRUMENTAL PROGRAMME]** if supplied.

### Example Input
Years 1 to 6 progression in rhythm and pulse.

### Example Output
A year-by-year progression from maintaining and responding to pulse through increasingly complex rhythmic patterns, notation and independent rhythmic creation.

## MU-04: Design a Retrieval Starter Bank

**Prompt Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 900 to 1,300 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 1 to 2 minutes  
**Curriculum Tags:** Primary, Music, Retrieval Practice, Vocabulary

### ROLE
You are a primary music teacher designing efficient retrieval practice.

### CONTEXT
The class is **[YEAR GROUP]** and has recently studied **[TOPICS]**. The next unit is **[NEXT TOPIC]**.

### TASK
Create a bank of short retrieval starters that strengthen musical memory without consuming most of the lesson.

### REQUIREMENTS
Include a mixture of aural, practical, verbal and visual retrieval. Include answers or teacher notes. Revisit essential vocabulary and prior concepts. Avoid testing knowledge pupils have not been taught.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Create 15 starters grouped by difficulty, each with estimated time, pupil response and answer/teacher note.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure each starter has a clear connection to prior learning and can be completed efficiently.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add **[INSTRUMENT / NOTATION / SONG / COMPOSER]** content if supplied.

### Example Input
Year 5, prior learning on pulse, metre, dynamics and tempo.

### Example Output
Short listening-identification, vocabulary, rhythm-performance and comparison activities suitable for the opening five minutes of lessons.

## MU-05: Adapt a Unit for Limited Resources

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 1,200 to 1,700 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 1 to 3 minutes  
**Curriculum Tags:** Primary, Music, Adaptation, Resource Management

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music teacher skilled in resource-light teaching.

### CONTEXT
I have this intended unit: **[UNIT DESCRIPTION]**. Available resources are limited to **[AVAILABLE RESOURCES]**. Class size is **[SIZE]** and lesson length is **[MINUTES]**.

### TASK
Redesign the unit so the core musical learning remains intact despite the resource constraints.

### REQUIREMENTS
Prioritise body percussion, voice, listening, movement, found or available classroom resources and turn-taking where appropriate. Do not replace practical music-making with worksheets unless necessary. Explain any change to the musical construct being assessed.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide a lesson-by-lesson adapted plan, resource strategy, grouping model, assessment approach and preparation checklist.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure the adaptations preserve the original learning intention and remain safe and manageable.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for **[SPECIFIC RESOURCE CONSTRAINT]**.

### Example Input
A Year 4 percussion unit with only six instruments for a class of 30.

### Example Output
A rotating ensemble structure combining body percussion, voice, listening and six-instrument stations while preserving the rhythmic and ensemble objectives.

## MU-06: Plan a Cross-Curricular Music Lesson

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 1,100 to 1,600 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 1 to 3 minutes  
**Curriculum Tags:** Primary, Music, Cross-Curricular, Curriculum Planning

### ROLE
You are a primary teacher and music curriculum specialist.

### CONTEXT
The main curriculum subject is **[SUBJECT]** and the topic is **[TOPIC]**. The music learning objective is **[MUSIC OBJECTIVE]**. Pupils are **[YEAR GROUP]**.

### TASK
Design a lesson where music makes a genuine contribution to the wider learning rather than being an added decoration.

### REQUIREMENTS
State the distinct music learning and the cross-curricular learning. Include practical music-making, listening or creation. Avoid superficial links and factual inaccuracies.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide lesson rationale, dual learning intentions, sequence, resources, assessment and differentiation.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Confirm that the musical objective could still be assessed independently of the cross-curricular topic.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Use **[TEXT / ARTWORK / HISTORICAL CONTEXT / SCIENCE CONCEPT]** if supplied.

### Example Input
Year 6 history topic on World War II, with a music objective focused on developing melodic motifs.

### Example Output
A lesson using historical context as stimulus while assessing pupils' ability to develop and organise melodic motifs, without reducing the music to historical trivia.

## MU-10: Audit a Music Curriculum Plan

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 1,500 to 2,200 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 2 to 4 minutes  
**Curriculum Tags:** Primary, Music, Curriculum Audit, Subject Leadership

### ROLE
You are a primary music subject leader conducting a rigorous curriculum review.

### CONTEXT
Here is my current music curriculum plan: **[PASTE PLAN]**. The intended curriculum principles are **[PRINCIPLES]**. Resources and staffing are **[DETAILS]**.

### TASK
Audit the plan for curriculum coherence, progression, musical breadth and classroom feasibility.

### REQUIREMENTS
Assess coverage of listening, singing, performing, composing/improvising, musical elements, instruments, notation/literacy and cultural context as relevant. Identify repetition, gaps, unrealistic assumptions and weak progression. Separate high-priority issues from refinements. Do not claim compliance with a curriculum document unless the relevant requirements are supplied.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Use: executive summary, strengths, priority gaps, progression audit, breadth audit, inclusion audit, resource/workload audit, recommended changes and a prioritised action plan.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Make every recommendation specific and actionable. Distinguish evidence from inference and identify missing information that prevents a firm judgement.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
If I provide curriculum documentation, school plans or assessment information, use those sources as the basis for the audit.

### Example Input
A Years 1 to 6 music curriculum showing units, songs, listening repertoire, instruments and assessment approaches.

### Example Output
A structured curriculum audit identifying where progression is strong, where practical music-making is uneven, where vocabulary is not revisited and which changes would have the greatest impact.
