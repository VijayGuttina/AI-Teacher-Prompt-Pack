# Composition, Improvisation & Creativity

This workflow family contains 10 copy/paste-ready ChatGPT workflows for developing composition, improvisation and creative music-making in primary classrooms.

The prompts are designed to support genuine pupil musical decision-making, practical experimentation, progression, inclusion and respectful assessment of creative work.

---

## CI-01 Plan a Composition Lesson

```yaml
id: CI-01
title: Plan a Composition Lesson
category: Composition, Improvisation & Creativity
tags: [composition, creativity, music, KS1, KS2]
prompt_difficulty: Intermediate
ai_model_compatibility: [ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot]
expected_output_length: Long
typical_generation_time: 2-3 minutes
```

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music teacher and curriculum specialist with expertise in composition, improvisation and creative music-making.

### CONTEXT
I am planning a composition lesson for [YEAR GROUP]. The lesson lasts [MINUTES]. Pupils already know [PRIOR MUSICAL KNOWLEDGE]. Available resources are [RESOURCES]. The musical focus is [FOCUS].

### TASK
Create a practical lesson in which pupils make meaningful musical decisions and create an original short piece or section of music.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Start with a clear learning intention and 2 to 4 pupil-friendly success criteria.
- Establish the musical knowledge pupils need before composing.
- Include listening or model examples that illuminate the musical idea without requiring pupils to copy a finished piece.
- Use a clear creative constraint such as a rhythm, scale, motif, ostinato, text, structure or set of available sounds.
- Give pupils genuine choices within the constraint.
- Include teacher modelling of the process rather than producing the composition for pupils.
- Include exploration, selection, refinement and performance.
- Make the composition manageable within the available time and resources.
- Include an inclusive route using voice, body percussion, instruments or digital tools as appropriate.
- Include an extension that increases musical decision-making rather than simply increasing task length.
- Define how the teacher will assess the musical learning without judging pupils' work solely by polish or complexity.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Lesson overview
2. Learning intention
3. Success criteria
4. Prior knowledge check
5. Musical stimulus
6. Teacher modelling
7. Exploration phase
8. Composition task
9. Refinement process
10. Performance or sharing
11. Assessment opportunities
12. Inclusion and adaptation
13. Extension
14. Misconceptions and teacher responses
15. Plenary

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that pupils retain meaningful ownership of musical decisions, that the task is achievable within the stated time, and that assessment focuses on relevant musical understanding and creative decision-making.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt the composition task for [YEAR GROUP], [AVAILABLE INSTRUMENTS], [MUSICAL STYLE] or [CURRICULUM OBJECTIVE].

### Example Input
Year 4, 45 minutes, classroom percussion and voices, pupils understand pulse and simple rhythmic patterns, focus on composing a short rhythmic piece with contrasting sections.

### Example Output
Pupils explore a four-beat rhythmic motif, generate two contrasting variations, select ideas, arrange them into two sections and perform the finished piece. Assessment focuses on deliberate rhythmic choices, contrast, structure and the ability to explain decisions.

```yaml

title: Create a Structured Improvisation Activity
category: Composition, Improvisation & Creativity

prompt_difficulty: Intermediate
ai_model_compatibility: [ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot]
expected_output_length: Medium
typical_generation_time: 1-2 minutes
```

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music teacher who uses structured improvisation to develop listening, musical response and pupil confidence.

### CONTEXT
I am teaching [YEAR GROUP]. Pupils currently understand [PRIOR KNOWLEDGE]. I have [RESOURCES] available and [MINUTES] for the activity. I want the improvisation to develop [MUSICAL FOCUS].

### TASK
Create a structured improvisation activity that gives pupils freedom to make musical choices within clear boundaries.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Define the improvisation constraint clearly.
- Establish pulse, tonal material, rhythmic material or another appropriate musical framework before pupils improvise.
- Include teacher modelling.
- Use short, low-risk turns before moving to longer or more independent improvisation.
- Include active listening and musical response between pupils.
- Avoid requiring every pupil to perform solo publicly if this would undermine participation or dignity.
- Include paired, small-group or whole-class routes.
- Include an adaptation with reduced musical choices for pupils who need more structure.
- Include an extension that develops variation, response, phrasing or interaction.
- State what the teacher should listen for when assessing the musical construct.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Musical goal
2. Success criteria
3. Framework or constraint
4. Teacher model
5. Guided improvisation
6. Partner or group improvisation
7. Independent challenge
8. Assessment criteria
9. Inclusion and confidence-building adaptations
10. Extension
11. Troubleshooting
12. Plenary

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that the activity is genuinely improvisational, that the framework provides enough security for pupils to succeed, and that the assessment does not equate confidence or novelty with musical quality.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Create versions based on [RHYTHM], [PITCH MATERIAL], [SONG], [MUSICAL STYLE] or [INSTRUMENT].

### Example Input
Year 3, 30 minutes, untuned percussion and voices, focus on rhythmic question-and-answer improvisation.

### Example Output
Pupils establish a four-beat pulse, copy short rhythmic questions, then improvise four-beat answers using a limited rhythm set. Pairs take turns while classmates maintain the pulse, followed by a small-group challenge involving variation.

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music educator specialising in musical ideas, motifs, variation and composition.

### CONTEXT
I am working with [YEAR GROUP] pupils. They know [PRIOR KNOWLEDGE]. The lesson lasts [MINUTES] and uses [RESOURCES]. The intended musical focus is [RHYTHMIC/Melodic/COMBINED] motif development.

### TASK
Design a lesson in which pupils create a short musical motif and develop it through deliberate variation.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Explain motif in accessible, age-appropriate language.
- Begin with a manageable rhythmic or melodic idea.
- Model how one musical idea can be changed while remaining recognisable.
- Include at least three variation strategies such as changing rhythm, pitch direction, repetition, dynamics, articulation, register or instrumentation where appropriate.
- Distinguish variation from simply creating an unrelated new idea.
- Include pupil experimentation before selection.
- Include notation or graphic representation only at an appropriate level.
- Include an opportunity to perform the original and developed motif.
- Include an inclusive route using fewer notes, fewer rhythmic values or non-notated representation.
- Include extension through combining or sequencing motifs.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

1. Learning intention
2. Success criteria

4. Teacher model
5. Exploration activities
6. Variation strategies
7. Pupil composition task
8. Performance task
9. Assessment
10. Misconceptions
11. Inclusion and extension
12. Plenary

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that motif and variation are explained accurately, that each variation remains musically connected to the original where intended, and that the task remains suitable for primary pupils.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Generate a starting motif suitable for [YEAR GROUP] using [PITCH/RHYTHM MATERIAL] without completing the pupils' creative work for them.

### Example Input
Year 5, 45 minutes, pupils read simple notation, focus on developing a four-note melodic motif through rhythmic and pitch variation.

### Example Output
Pupils create a short motif, perform it, then alter rhythm, pitch direction and dynamics while preserving a recognisable connection to the original. They compare versions and explain which changes were most effective.

## CI-04 Compose Using a Musical Structure

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music teacher with expertise in musical form, structure and creative composition.

### CONTEXT
I am teaching [YEAR GROUP] pupils to organise musical ideas into a coherent structure. They know [PRIOR KNOWLEDGE]. The lesson lasts [MINUTES]. Resources are [RESOURCES].

### TASK
Create a composition sequence in which pupils organise original musical ideas into a clear structure such as AB, ABA, rondo or another structure appropriate to their age and prior learning.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Select a structure appropriate to the pupils' stage.
- Explain the structure through listening, movement, visual representation or performance before pupils compose.
- Make each section musically purposeful.
- Provide manageable constraints for each section.
- Include a process for generating, selecting and ordering ideas.
- Include transition strategies between sections.
- Require pupils to perform or hear the structure as it develops.
- Include assessment of structural understanding as well as musical execution.
- Provide inclusive alternatives for pupils who need fewer compositional decisions at once.
- Include extension through contrast, recurring material or transition design.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Learning intention
2. Success criteria
3. Structure introduction
4. Listening example criteria
5. Teacher modelling
6. Idea-generation task
7. Section-building task
8. Ordering and transition task
9. Rehearsal
10. Performance
11. Assessment
12. Inclusion and extension
13. Misconceptions and troubleshooting
14. Plenary

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that the named structure is used accurately, that sections are distinguishable for musical reasons, and that pupils are not simply assembling unrelated activities under structural labels.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for [AB/ABA/RONDO/OTHER] and [YEAR GROUP], using [AVAILABLE RESOURCES].

### Example Input
Year 6, 50 minutes, pupils can compose short rhythmic and melodic ideas, focus on ABA form.

### Example Output
Pupils study how a returning section can create coherence, compose an A idea, develop a contrasting B section, return to A with an intentional change, then perform and annotate the resulting structure.

### Example Input
Year 2, image of a busy city, 35 minutes, voices and percussion.

### Example Output

Create a pupil-friendly decision grid for [MUSICAL FOCUS].

### Example Input


### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music assessment specialist who understands how to evaluate composition without reducing creativity to subjective judgement.

### CONTEXT
I need to assess pupil compositions from [YEAR GROUP]. The intended musical learning is [LEARNING INTENTION]. Pupils were asked to [TASK]. Evidence available includes [PERFORMANCE/NOTATION/RECORDING/EXPLANATION/OTHER].

### TASK
Create a fair assessment and feedback framework for the compositions.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Identify the musical constructs being assessed before judging the work.
- Separate musical understanding, use of musical elements, structure, creative decision-making, technical execution and explanation where relevant.
- Avoid scoring pupils primarily on complexity, instrumental virtuosity or confidence.
- Use observable descriptors rather than vague labels such as "very creative".
- Include formative feedback that tells pupils what to keep, what to improve and what musical action to try next.
- Include an opportunity for pupil reflection or explanation of choices.
- Provide differentiated evidence expectations where access needs require alternative ways to demonstrate the same construct.
- State when a limitation in performance technique should not be mistaken for a limitation in compositional understanding.
- Include a concise rubric suitable for classroom use.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Assessment purpose
2. Constructs being assessed
3. Four-level rubric
4. Evidence sources
5. Teacher observation prompts
6. Feedback sentence stems
7. Pupil reflection questions
8. Inclusion and access adjustments
9. Moderation guidance
10. Example annotated evidence

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that the rubric distinguishes creative decision-making from performance skill, avoids subjective aesthetic ranking as the primary measure, and uses age-appropriate descriptors.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt the rubric for [YEAR GROUP], [COMPOSITION TASK] and [CURRICULUM EXPECTATION].

### Example Input
Year 4 pupils composed a short piece using repeated rhythm, contrast and a clear ending. Some pupils performed their ideas confidently while others needed teacher support to perform them.

### Example Output
The rubric assesses deliberate use of repetition, contrast, structure and musical vocabulary separately from performance fluency, allowing a pupil to demonstrate strong compositional understanding even where instrumental execution is still developing.

## CI-10 Diagnose and Improve Creative Blocks

```yaml
id: CI-10
title: Diagnose and Improve Creative Blocks
category: Composition, Improvisation & Creativity
tags: [composition, creativity, intervention, assessment, KS1, KS2]
prompt_difficulty: Advanced
ai_model_compatibility: [ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot]
expected_output_length: Long
typical_generation_time: 2-3 minutes
```

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music teacher and intervention specialist. You diagnose classroom-observable barriers to creative music-making without making unsupported psychological or clinical claims.

### CONTEXT
A pupil or group in [YEAR GROUP] is struggling with [OBSERVED DIFFICULTY], for example producing no ideas, repeating one idea without developing it, copying peers, avoiding improvisation or becoming overwhelmed by too many choices. They can currently [STRENGTHS]. The activity is [TASK].

### TASK
Analyse the observable difficulty and create a short diagnostic and intervention sequence that increases successful creative participation.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Separate observation from assumptions about the reason for the difficulty.
- Identify possible musical barriers such as unclear constraints, insufficient prior musical vocabulary, excessive choice, weak pulse security, limited technical access, difficulty recording ideas or uncertainty about the task.
- Do not infer a clinical condition from creative behaviour.
- Provide 2 to 4 low-risk diagnostic activities.
- Explain what the teacher should observe in each activity.
- Recommend a progression from highly structured choices to greater independence where appropriate.
- Use musical scaffolds such as rhythm banks, motifs, call-and-response, limited pitch sets, visual structures or recorded idea banks.
- Preserve genuine pupil choice rather than replacing composition with teacher-created answers.
- Include inclusive alternatives for notation, movement, performance or fine-motor demands.
- Define observable indicators of improvement and a review point.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Observation summary
2. Evidence versus assumption
3. Possible musical barriers
4. Diagnostic activity 1
5. Diagnostic activity 2
6. Diagnostic activity 3, if needed
7. Interpretation guide
8. Intervention sequence
9. Scaffolding to independence
10. Inclusion and access adaptations
11. Progress indicators
12. Review point
13. Teacher recording template

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that the plan does not make unsupported psychological or medical claims, that interventions address observable musical barriers, and that scaffolding increases access without taking away pupil ownership.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
If I provide several observations across lessons, compare them and identify the most defensible musical intervention priority.

### Example Input
Year 3 pupil becomes stuck when asked to compose freely but creates effective rhythms when given three choices and can explain why they prefer one.

### Example Output
The plan identifies choice overload or insufficient task structure as a plausible classroom barrier rather than assuming a general lack of creativity. Intervention begins with three constrained rhythmic choices, moves to combining and modifying choices, and gradually increases independent decision-making while monitoring whether the pupil retains ownership of the final ideas.
