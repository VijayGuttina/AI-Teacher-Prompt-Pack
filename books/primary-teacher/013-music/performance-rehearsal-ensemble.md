# Performance, Rehearsal & Ensemble

Ten classroom-ready workflows for planning, rehearsing, improving and assessing primary music performance.

---

## PF-01 Plan a Performance Lesson

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music teacher and ensemble director.

### CONTEXT
Teach [YEAR GROUP] to perform [PIECE/SONG/ARRANGEMENT] in [MINUTES] using [RESOURCES]. Current strengths and gaps: [DETAILS].

### TASK
Create a practical performance lesson that develops secure musical execution and expressive communication.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Define a specific musical performance goal.
- Include warm-up, modelling, guided rehearsal, focused repetition and a complete run-through.
- Prioritise pulse, rhythm and accurate entries before adding expressive detail.
- Include concise teacher cues.
- Build in listening to other performers.
- Provide adaptations and extension.
- Include observable assessment criteria.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
1. Performance goal
2. Success criteria
3. Warm-up
4. Teacher model
5. Sectional rehearsal
6. Ensemble rehearsal
7. Focused improvement loop
8. Run-through
9. Assessment
10. Inclusion and extension
11. Plenary

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that rehearsal time is purposeful and that pupils know what to improve on each repetition.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for singing, instruments, body percussion or mixed ensemble.

## PF-02 Design an Efficient Rehearsal Sequence

### ROLE
You are a skilled primary ensemble director.

### CONTEXT
Prepare [YEAR GROUP] for [PERFORMANCE] over [NUMBER] sessions of [MINUTES]. Pupils are currently [READINESS].

### TASK
Create a session-by-session rehearsal plan that moves from learning material to secure ensemble performance.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Sequence learning logically.
- Identify rehearsal priorities for each session.
- Avoid repeated full performances when a sectional strategy would be more effective.
- Include checkpoints and contingency actions.
- Plan transitions and endings.
- Include time for independent listening and self-correction.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide a table for each session with objective, rehearsal focus, activities, teacher cues, evidence of progress and next-step criteria.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure later expressive work does not mask unresolved rhythmic or structural problems.

## PF-03 Improve Ensemble Timing

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music teacher specialising in ensemble coordination.

### CONTEXT
[YEAR GROUP] pupils struggle with [OBSERVED TIMING ISSUE] while performing [ACTIVITY].

### TASK
Diagnose the musical issue and create a targeted intervention.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Separate evidence from assumptions.
- Consider pulse, listening, entries, coordination and rhythmic understanding.
- Use short diagnostic tasks.
- Progress from simplified parts to full ensemble performance.
- Include clear success indicators.
- Do not make clinical claims from classroom observations.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
1. Observation summary
2. Possible musical causes
3. Diagnostics
4. Intervention sequence
5. Rehearsal drills
6. Progress indicators
7. Review point

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that the intervention targets the actual timing problem rather than simply repeating the whole piece.

## PF-04 Teach Ensemble Listening

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music educator specialising in collaborative performance.

### CONTEXT
Teach [YEAR GROUP] pupils to listen and respond while performing [MUSICAL ACTIVITY].

### TASK
Create a lesson that makes ensemble listening explicit and observable.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Define what pupils should listen for.
- Include activities requiring response to another part.
- Develop awareness of balance, timing, entries and endings where relevant.
- Avoid assessing pupils solely on confidence or volume.
- Include inclusive roles and adaptations.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Learning intention, success criteria, starter, modelling, guided ensemble tasks, independent task, assessment, inclusion, extension and plenary.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure pupils must use auditory information to succeed rather than merely perform alongside one another.

## PF-05 Develop Expressive Performance

### ROLE
You are a primary music teacher with expertise in expressive interpretation.

### CONTEXT
[YEAR GROUP] pupils can perform the notes or rhythms of [PIECE] but need to improve [EXPRESSION FOCUS].

### TASK
Create a rehearsal sequence that develops intentional expression through appropriate musical elements.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Connect expressive choices to dynamics, tempo, articulation, phrasing, texture or timbre where relevant.
- Require pupils to compare alternatives.
- Ask pupils to justify choices with musical evidence.
- Avoid presenting one emotional interpretation as universally correct.
- Include a before-and-after performance task.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide rehearsal goal, success criteria, listening/model task, experimentation, selection, rehearsal, performance comparison, assessment and reflection.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that expression is created through deliberate musical choices, not vague instructions to "perform with more feeling."

## PF-06 Plan a Class Performance

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music coordinator and performance planner.

### CONTEXT
Plan a performance of [EVENT/PIECE] for [YEAR GROUP/CLASS] with [TIME AVAILABLE], [VENUE] and [RESOURCES].

### TASK
Create an inclusive performance plan from preparation to event delivery.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Cover repertoire suitability, rehearsal, roles, transitions and practical logistics.
- Include equitable participation without forcing identical roles.
- Identify sound, space and safeguarding considerations.
- Include contingency planning.
- Avoid unnecessary public pressure on individual pupils.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
1. Performance objective
2. Timeline
3. Repertoire/arrangement considerations
4. Roles
5. Rehearsal plan
6. Venue and technical checklist
7. Inclusion plan
8. Risk and contingency points
9. Day-of-performance sequence
10. Evaluation

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that the plan is realistic for a primary setting and that logistical complexity does not overwhelm musical learning.

## PF-07 Prepare Pupils for Solo or Small-Group Performance

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music teacher who develops performance confidence without coercion.

### CONTEXT
Support [YEAR GROUP] pupils preparing [SOLO/DUO/SMALL-GROUP] performances of [MATERIAL].

### TASK
Create a graduated preparation sequence.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Start with low-stakes rehearsal.
- Offer choice of participation routes where possible.
- Include technical preparation and musical interpretation.
- Include constructive feedback routines.
- Avoid equating willingness to perform publicly with musical attainment.
- Include support for performance anxiety without making medical assumptions.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide preparation stages, teacher language, rehearsal tasks, feedback prompts, assessment criteria, adaptations and review questions.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure dignity is protected and that assessment measures the intended musical learning.

## PF-08 Give High-Quality Performance Feedback

### ROLE
You are a primary music assessment specialist.

### CONTEXT
Give feedback on a pupil or group performance of [TASK]. Intended learning: [OBJECTIVES]. Observed evidence: [NOTES].

### TASK
Produce concise formative feedback and a practical next step.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Base comments on observable evidence.
- Identify what is secure and one or two high-value improvements.
- Use age-appropriate musical vocabulary.
- Give an actionable rehearsal strategy.
- Separate musical performance skill from confidence or personality.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
1. Evidence observed
2. Strength
3. Improvement priority
4. Specific action
5. Success indicator
6. Pupil-friendly version

### QUALITY CHECKS
Do not invent observations not supplied in the context.

## PF-09 Create a Performance Assessment Rubric

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music assessment designer.

### CONTEXT
Create an assessment rubric for [YEAR GROUP] performing [TASK]. Curriculum focus: [FOCUS].

### TASK
Design a concise four-level rubric aligned to the intended musical constructs.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Assess only relevant constructs such as pulse, rhythm, pitch, ensemble listening, technique and expressive control where appropriate.
- Use observable descriptors.
- Do not make confidence, extroversion or volume a proxy for attainment.
- Allow equivalent evidence routes where access needs affect how pupils perform.
- Include formative next steps.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide assessment purpose, constructs, four-level rubric, evidence notes, pupil self-assessment and moderation guidance.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check construct validity and age-appropriate language.

## PF-10 Diagnose Performance Difficulties

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music teacher and intervention planner.

### CONTEXT
A pupil or group shows [OBSERVED DIFFICULTY] during [PERFORMANCE CONTEXT]. Strengths include [STRENGTHS].

### TASK
Create a diagnostic and intervention plan.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Distinguish observable evidence from assumptions.
- Consider musical, technical, memory, coordination, listening and task-design factors.
- Use low-risk diagnostic activities.
- Do not diagnose medical, developmental or psychological conditions.
- Recommend targeted rehearsal strategies with progression.
- Include inclusion adaptations that preserve the intended musical construct.
- Define observable improvement indicators and a review point.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
1. Observation summary
2. Evidence versus assumption
3. Possible contributing factors
4. Diagnostic tasks
5. Interpretation guide
6. Intervention sequence
7. Inclusion adaptations
8. Progress indicators
9. Review point
10. Teacher record template

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that recommendations remain proportionate to the evidence and target the specific musical difficulty.
