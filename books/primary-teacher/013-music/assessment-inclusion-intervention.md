# Assessment, Inclusion & Intervention

## AI-01 Assess Musical Understanding

### Metadata
- Prompt ID: AI-01
- Difficulty: Intermediate
- AI Model Compatibility: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
- Expected Output Length: Medium
- Typical Generation Time: 30-60 seconds
- Curriculum Tags: KS1, KS2, assessment, listening, performance, composition

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary music teacher and assessment specialist.

### CONTEXT
I need to assess pupils' understanding of [MUSICAL CONCEPT] in [YEAR GROUP]. Evidence currently available includes [EVIDENCE].

### TASK
Create a proportionate assessment activity that reveals pupils' musical understanding.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Define the intended construct clearly.
- Use observable musical evidence.
- Avoid assessing writing fluency, confidence or technology skill when these are not the learning objective.
- Include formative follow-up questions.
- Provide accessible response routes.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide: learning focus, success criteria, assessment task, evidence indicators, teacher questions, likely misconceptions, recording method, next-step decisions and adaptations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check construct validity, age appropriateness, feasibility and whether evidence supports the conclusions drawn.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Specify [YEAR], [CONCEPT], [CLASS SIZE] and [AVAILABLE TIME].

### Example Input
Year 3, assessing understanding of pulse and rhythm in a 20-minute lesson.

### Example Output
A short listen-clap-explain task with separate evidence for maintaining pulse, reproducing rhythm and explaining the difference.

## AI-02 Build a Music Assessment Rubric

### Metadata
- Prompt ID: AI-02
- Difficulty: Advanced
- AI Model Compatibility: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
- Expected Output Length: Medium
- Typical Generation Time: 30-60 seconds
- Curriculum Tags: KS1, KS2, rubric, assessment, performance, composition

### ROLE
You are a primary music assessment designer.

### CONTEXT
I need a rubric for [TASK] focused on [MUSICAL LEARNING]. Pupils are [YEAR GROUP].

### TASK
Create a concise, pupil-appropriate assessment rubric.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Assess only the intended musical constructs.
- Use observable descriptors rather than vague labels such as good or excellent.
- Keep the number of criteria manageable.
- Include emerging, developing, secure and extending descriptors only if those labels fit the school's approach.
- Allow alternative forms of participation where appropriate.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide: assessment purpose, criteria table, observable descriptors, evidence examples, pupil-friendly version, teacher guidance and common misinterpretations to avoid.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that descriptors are distinguishable, progressive and do not reward confidence or compliance instead of musical understanding.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add [YEAR GROUP], [TASK] and [ASSESSMENT SCALE].

### Example Input
Year 5 group composition assessed for rhythmic control, structure and purposeful use of dynamics.

### Example Output
A three-criterion rubric with evidence-based descriptors and separate guidance for assessing group contribution fairly.

## AI-03 Diagnose Musical Misconceptions

### Metadata
- Prompt ID: AI-03
- Difficulty: Advanced
- AI Model Compatibility: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
- Expected Output Length: Medium
- Typical Generation Time: 30-60 seconds
- Curriculum Tags: KS1, KS2, misconceptions, diagnosis, intervention

### ROLE
You are a primary music teacher analysing pupil misconceptions.

### CONTEXT
The observed responses are [RESPONSES/EVIDENCE]. The intended learning is [CONCEPT].

### TASK
Diagnose likely misconceptions and design a targeted re-teaching sequence.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Base conclusions on supplied evidence only.
- Distinguish misconception from lack of exposure, vocabulary difficulty or performance difficulty.
- Propose a short diagnostic check before intervention where uncertainty remains.
- Include practical re-teaching and a re-assessment.
- Do not make developmental or clinical diagnoses.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide: evidence summary, likely misconception, alternative explanations, diagnostic question, intervention activity, teacher language, re-assessment and next decision.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that intervention addresses the underlying musical concept and not merely the visible error.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Specify [YEAR], [CONCEPT] and [PUPIL RESPONSES].

### Example Input
Pupils can clap rhythms but identify the fastest sound as the pulse.

### Example Output
A diagnostic sequence distinguishing steady beat from changing rhythm, followed by movement, listening and a short re-check.

## AI-04 Differentiate a Music Lesson

### Metadata
- Prompt ID: AI-04
- Difficulty: Intermediate
- AI Model Compatibility: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
- Expected Output Length: Medium
- Typical Generation Time: 30-60 seconds
- Curriculum Tags: KS1, KS2, differentiation, inclusion, planning

### ROLE
You are an inclusive primary music teacher.

### CONTEXT
I am teaching [LESSON/TASK] to [YEAR GROUP]. Pupils have different levels of experience with [SKILL].

### TASK
Create differentiation that preserves a shared musical objective.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Do not lower expectations indiscriminately.
- Provide access, scaffolding and extension routes.
- Avoid permanently assigning pupils to fixed ability groups.
- Include options for simplifying complexity while preserving the intended construct.
- Identify what remains non-negotiable in the learning objective.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide: common objective, barriers, universal supports, scaffolded route, core route, extension, teacher prompts, assessment adjustments and review questions.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that differentiation changes support or complexity rather than changing the subject being assessed.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add [YEAR GROUP], [SKILL], [INSTRUMENTS] and [CLASS PROFILE].

### Example Input
Year 4 ensemble lesson with wide differences in rhythmic confidence.

### Example Output
A shared pulse and ensemble goal with reduced-pattern scaffolds, visual supports and optional extension through independent rhythmic variation.

## AI-05 Adapt Music Learning for Additional Needs

### Metadata
- Prompt ID: AI-05
- Difficulty: Advanced
- AI Model Compatibility: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
- Expected Output Length: Medium
- Typical Generation Time: 30-60 seconds
- Curriculum Tags: KS1, KS2, SEND, accessibility, inclusion

### ROLE
You are a primary music teacher experienced in inclusive and accessible teaching.

### CONTEXT
The musical task is [TASK]. Relevant learning or access needs described by the teacher are [NEEDS]. Available support is [SUPPORT].

### TASK
Adapt the lesson so pupils can access meaningful musical learning while preserving the intended objective wherever possible.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Focus on functional barriers described in the context rather than assuming needs not provided.
- Offer sensory, visual, auditory, physical and communication adaptations where relevant.
- Avoid medical or diagnostic claims.
- Avoid assuming that a disability determines musical preference or ability.
- Include multiple participation routes and dignified alternatives.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide: intended learning, barriers, reasonable adaptations, resources, teacher prompts, participation options, assessment evidence and review points.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that adaptations improve access without unnecessarily removing musical challenge.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Specify [TASK], [YEAR GROUP], [AVAILABLE SUPPORT] and [BARRIERS].

### Example Input
A pupil needs an alternative way to participate in a fast-paced percussion activity.

### Example Output
Several equivalent roles and adapted patterns that maintain the musical objective without requiring the same physical action from every pupil.

## AI-06 Support Pupils with Limited Prior Musical Experience

### Metadata
- Prompt ID: AI-06
- Difficulty: Intermediate
- AI Model Compatibility: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
- Expected Output Length: Medium
- Typical Generation Time: 30-60 seconds
- Curriculum Tags: KS1, KS2, inclusion, prior knowledge, intervention

### ROLE
You are a primary music teacher planning equitable access for pupils with differing prior musical experience.

### CONTEXT
Some pupils have extensive experience of [SKILL] while others have limited prior exposure. The shared objective is [OBJECTIVE].

### TASK
Create a lesson sequence that establishes common ground and enables meaningful progression for all pupils.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Do not equate prior private tuition with greater musical potential.
- Establish shared prerequisite knowledge.
- Provide challenge through musical complexity rather than extra workload.
- Avoid public comparisons of prior experience.
- Include a low-stakes diagnostic activity.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide: prerequisite check, common foundation, guided practice, access supports, extension, assessment, feedback language and next steps.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check fairness, progression and whether pupils can demonstrate learning regardless of prior access to music tuition.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Specify [YEAR], [SKILL] and [OBJECTIVE].

### Example Input
Year 5 pupils have very different levels of keyboard experience.

### Example Output
A common orientation and short musical pattern, with optional extension through harmony, variation or independence rather than separate status groups.

## AI-07 Plan a Targeted Music Intervention

### Metadata
- Prompt ID: AI-07
- Difficulty: Advanced
- AI Model Compatibility: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
- Expected Output Length: Medium
- Typical Generation Time: 30-60 seconds
- Curriculum Tags: KS1, KS2, intervention, assessment, progression

### ROLE
You are a primary music teacher designing a short evidence-led intervention.

### CONTEXT
Pupil(s) are struggling with [MUSICAL SKILL]. Evidence includes [EVIDENCE]. Available intervention time is [TIME].

### TASK
Create a short intervention sequence with a clear entry point and re-assessment.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Identify the smallest useful learning target.
- Use practical, active music-making.
- Include spaced repetition or retrieval where appropriate.
- Include an immediate check and a delayed check if feasible.
- Do not label pupils globally as weak at music.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide: evidence, target, prerequisite check, intervention steps, teacher prompts, practice schedule, success criteria, re-assessment and next-step rule.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that the intervention is proportionate and addresses the identified barrier rather than reteaching the entire unit.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add [YEAR], [SKILL], [TIME AVAILABLE] and [GROUP SIZE].

### Example Input
A small Year 3 group cannot reliably maintain a steady pulse during ensemble work.

### Example Output
A brief multi-session sequence using movement, body percussion, gradual layering and repeated ensemble transfer, followed by a re-check in the original task.

## AI-08 Give High-Quality Feedback in Music

### Metadata
- Prompt ID: AI-08
- Difficulty: Intermediate
- AI Model Compatibility: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
- Expected Output Length: Medium
- Typical Generation Time: 30-60 seconds
- Curriculum Tags: KS1, KS2, feedback, assessment, performance, composition

### ROLE
You are a primary music teacher giving specific, actionable feedback.

### CONTEXT
The pupil or group completed [TASK]. The intended learning was [OBJECTIVE]. Evidence observed was [EVIDENCE].

### TASK
Generate concise feedback that identifies what the evidence shows and the most useful next step.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Refer to observable musical evidence.
- Keep feedback age-appropriate.
- Avoid personality judgements.
- Avoid comparing pupils publicly.
- Give one priority improvement unless multiple steps are essential.
- Include a practical action for acting on the feedback.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide: evidence noticed, what this shows, priority next step, teacher wording, pupil action and quick follow-up check.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check specificity, accuracy, tone and whether the feedback can realistically be acted upon.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Specify [YEAR GROUP], [TASK] and [OBJECTIVE].

### Example Input
Year 6 ensemble kept the pulse securely but endings were inconsistent.

### Example Output
Feedback recognising secure pulse and directing pupils to watch the agreed cue and rehearse the final two bars three times with a clear success check.

## AI-09 Assess Group Music-Making Fairly

### Metadata
- Prompt ID: AI-09
- Difficulty: Advanced
- AI Model Compatibility: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
- Expected Output Length: Long
- Typical Generation Time: 45-75 seconds
- Curriculum Tags: KS1, KS2, group work, assessment, collaboration, performance, composition

### ROLE
You are a primary music assessment specialist.

### CONTEXT
Pupils are completing [GROUP TASK]. I need to assess both the musical outcome and, where appropriate, individual learning.

### TASK
Design a fair assessment approach for group music-making.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Separate group outcome from individual evidence.
- Do not assume the most visible or confident pupil contributed most musically.
- Include multiple evidence sources where practical, such as observation, short explanation, rehearsal evidence or individual demonstration.
- Keep assessment workload proportionate.
- Include pupil self- or peer-assessment only with clear criteria.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide: assessment constructs, group evidence, individual evidence, observation checklist, pupil reflection questions, moderation considerations, rubric and next-step decisions.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check fairness, construct validity, workload and whether quieter pupils have meaningful routes to demonstrate learning.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add [YEAR], [GROUP SIZE], [TASK] and [ASSESSMENT PRIORITY].

### Example Input
Year 5 groups composing and performing an eight-bar piece.

### Example Output
A combined approach assessing the finished composition as a group outcome while gathering individual evidence through rehearsal observation and short pupil explanations.

## AI-10 Review Music Progression and Identify Gaps

### Metadata
- Prompt ID: AI-10
- Difficulty: Advanced
- AI Model Compatibility: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
- Expected Output Length: Long
- Typical Generation Time: 45-75 seconds
- Curriculum Tags: KS1, KS2, curriculum, progression, assessment, intervention

### ROLE
You are a primary music curriculum leader conducting a progression and attainment review.

### CONTEXT
The current curriculum or evidence set is [CURRICULUM/EVIDENCE]. The focus is [MUSICAL AREA]. Pupils are [YEAR GROUP/PHASE].

### TASK
Analyse progression, identify likely gaps and recommend targeted next steps.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Identify intended knowledge and skill progression explicitly.
- Separate curriculum gaps from gaps in individual pupil learning.
- Avoid assuming that missing evidence proves absence of learning.
- Identify evidence still needed before making strong conclusions.
- Recommend proportionate curriculum changes and interventions.
- Include inclusion and access considerations.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide: intended progression, available evidence, strengths, possible gaps, confidence level for each finding, further evidence needed, curriculum recommendations, intervention priorities and review schedule.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check evidence quality, progression logic, construct validity, workload and whether recommendations are justified by the evidence.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Specify [PHASE], [MUSICAL AREA], [CURRICULUM DOCUMENTS] and [ASSESSMENT EVIDENCE].

### Example Input
Review Year 3 to Year 6 progression in rhythmic literacy using curriculum plans and sample assessment evidence.

### Example Output
A progression review distinguishing confirmed strengths from uncertain conclusions, identifying missing prerequisite steps and proposing targeted curriculum adjustments and evidence collection.
