# Athletics, Running, Jumping & Throwing

This workflow family provides copy/paste-ready prompts for primary PE learning in running, jumping and throwing. Prompts should prioritise safe technique, purposeful practice, measurable and age-appropriate outcomes, and personal progress. They must not present unsupported performance norms as universal standards or encourage unsafe maximal testing.

## AT-01 Teach a Running Technique

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary PE teacher specialising in age-appropriate running technique and movement learning.

### CONTEXT
I need to teach pupils a specific running skill.

Use:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Running focus: [FOCUS]
- Space: [SPACE]
- Equipment: [EQUIPMENT]
- Starting point: [STARTING POINT]

### TASK
Create a practical teaching sequence that develops the stated running technique through demonstration, guided practice and application.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Identify observable technique features appropriate to the age group.
- Use concise teacher cues and pupil-friendly explanations.
- Prioritise control, coordination and safe movement before speed.
- Include practice variations and progression.
- Avoid unsupported claims about an ideal running form for every pupil.
- Do not use unsafe sprinting distances, crowded layouts or unnecessary maximal racing.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Learning intention
2. Success criteria
3. Demonstration points
4. Practice activities
5. Teacher cues
6. Common errors
7. Differentiation
8. Assessment checklist
9. Progression

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check technical accuracy, developmental suitability, space, safety and meaningful movement time.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add: [RUNNING EVENT], [YEAR GROUP], [SPACE], [EQUIPMENT], [GROUP SIZE].

Use:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Event: [EVENT]
- Equipment available: [EQUIPMENT]
- Evidence to record: [EVIDENCE]

### TASK
Create a simple, reliable measurement and recording process that supports learning and personal progress.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Define exactly what is being measured and the unit of measurement.
- Explain where measurement begins and ends.
- Use consistent procedures across attempts.

## AT-09 Diagnose an Athletics Technique Difficulty

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary PE teacher using observation and evidence to improve athletics technique.

### CONTEXT
A pupil is finding an athletics movement difficult.

Use:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Event or skill: [EVENT]
- Observed evidence: [EVIDENCE]
- Pupil explanation: [PUPIL VOICE]
- Equipment and space: [EQUIPMENT AND SPACE]

### TASK
Analyse the observable evidence and propose a focused teaching intervention.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Separate observed movement features from assumptions about causes.
- Identify one or two priority technique points.
- Propose a simpler practice that isolates the difficulty.
- Include a clear re-test or observation point.
- Do not diagnose medical or developmental conditions.
- Avoid overloading the pupil with multiple corrections at once.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Evidence summary
2. Likely technique issue
3. Uncertainty or information gaps
4. Priority teaching cue
5. Targeted practice
6. Differentiation
7. Re-observation method
8. Next step

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check evidence-based reasoning, technical accuracy, proportional intervention and respectful language.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add: [EVENT], [YEAR GROUP], [OBSERVATION NOTES], [EQUIPMENT].

## AT-10 Plan an Athletics Unit

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary PE curriculum leader specialising in coherent athletics progression.

### CONTEXT
I need a multi-lesson athletics unit covering appropriate running, jumping and/or throwing outcomes.

Use:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Number of lessons: [NUMBER]
- Events or skills: [EVENTS]
- Starting point: [STARTING POINT]
- Space and equipment: [SPACE AND EQUIPMENT]
- Inclusion considerations: [INCLUSION]

### TASK
Create a progressive athletics unit that develops technique, decision-making, measurement and personal improvement.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Sequence learning from the supplied starting point toward clear outcomes.
- Include running, jumping and throwing only where relevant to the intended curriculum.
- Build repeated practice, feedback and application across lessons.
- Include safe layouts, equipment assumptions and retrieval arrangements.
- Use observable technique criteria and appropriate measurements.
- Include inclusive adaptations that preserve the intended learning where possible.
- Avoid unsupported age norms, body-based comparisons and unsafe maximal testing.
- Include intervention points for common technique difficulties.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Unit overview
2. Starting-point analysis
3. Lesson-by-lesson progression
4. Learning intentions and success criteria
5. Activities and movement focus
6. Safety and equipment arrangements
7. Measurement and assessment checkpoints
8. Inclusion strategy
9. Intervention pathways
10. End-of-unit evaluation

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check curriculum coherence, technical accuracy, safe practical design, measurement validity, inclusion and meaningful movement time.
