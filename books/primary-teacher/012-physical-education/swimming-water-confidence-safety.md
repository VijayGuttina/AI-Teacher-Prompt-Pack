# Swimming, Water Confidence & Safety

This workflow family provides copy/paste-ready prompts for planning, teaching, assessing and adapting primary swimming and water-confidence learning. Swimming prompts must use school and pool procedures supplied by the teacher or setting. They must not invent supervision ratios, lifeguard arrangements, emergency procedures, medical protocols, pool dimensions, water depths or facility rules.

## SW-01 Plan a Swimming Lesson

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary PE teacher and swimming curriculum planner. You understand age-appropriate aquatic learning, progression, water confidence and safe participation.

### CONTEXT
I am planning a swimming lesson for primary pupils.

Use these details:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Lesson length: [LENGTH]
- Learning focus: [FOCUS]
- Current pupil ability or prior learning: [PRIOR LEARNING]
- Pool or facility information: [FACILITY INFORMATION]
- Equipment available: [EQUIPMENT]
- School or pool safety procedures: [PROCEDURES]
- Inclusion considerations: [INCLUSION NEEDS]

### TASK
Create a complete, practical swimming lesson plan that develops the stated learning focus while keeping safe participation central to the lesson design.

### REQUIREMENTS
- State one clear learning intention and observable success criteria.
- Sequence the lesson from entry and orientation through teaching, practice, application and reflection.
- Include concise teacher explanations and demonstrations.
- Prioritise meaningful pupil participation without assuming facility arrangements that have not been supplied.
- Include appropriate differentiation and progression.
- Use only the equipment and procedures provided.
- If a safety-critical detail is missing, identify it as a local-setting check rather than inventing an answer.
- Do not claim that the lesson certifies swimming competence.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Lesson overview
2. Learning intention
3. Success criteria
4. Safety and local-setting checks
5. Lesson sequence with approximate timings
6. Teacher demonstrations and cues
7. Pupil practice activities
8. Differentiation and inclusion
9. Assessment opportunities
10. Reflection and next step

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that the plan is age-appropriate, technically coherent, practical, progressive and safe. Check that no unsupported pool procedures, supervision arrangements or medical claims have been introduced.

## SW-02 Teach Water Confidence and Safe Pool Behaviour

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary swimming teacher focused on water confidence, safe behaviour and clear pupil communication.

### CONTEXT
I need to teach primary pupils how to behave safely and confidently in a swimming environment.

Use:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Existing water-confidence level: [LEVEL]
- School or pool rules: [RULES]
- Relevant equipment: [EQUIPMENT]
- Inclusion considerations: [INCLUSION NEEDS]

### TASK
Create a short teaching sequence that develops water confidence and safe pool behaviour without overstating what any single activity can achieve.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Teach pupils to listen to and follow the instructions of the responsible adults and pool staff specified by the setting.
- Cover appropriate awareness of boundaries, personal space, entry and exit expectations, equipment use and behaviour around water.
- Use calm, age-appropriate language that avoids fear-based messaging.
- Distinguish general water-safety principles from the school's or pool's specific procedures.
- Do not invent emergency actions, supervision ratios or rescue procedures.
- Include opportunities for pupils to explain why a behaviour supports safer participation.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Learning intention
2. Key safety messages
3. Teacher script
4. Demonstration or modelling activity
5. Guided pupil activities
6. Questions to check understanding
7. Misconceptions to watch for
8. Assessment and reflection

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that safety guidance is accurate, proportionate and setting-dependent where necessary. Remove any invented facility-specific procedures or unsupported drowning-prevention claims.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add: [POOL RULES], [AGE RANGE], [KNOWN BARRIERS TO CONFIDENCE], [EQUIPMENT].

- Current skill level: [CURRENT LEVEL]
- Learning focus: [AQUATIC MOVEMENT]
- Equipment: [EQUIPMENT]
- Setting procedures: [PROCEDURES]

### TASK
Design a progressive sequence of aquatic movement activities that develops control, orientation and confidence in water.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Begin from the pupils' stated starting point.
- Break the skill into manageable components.
- Include teacher cues that describe observable movement.
- Progress from exploration and controlled practice toward purposeful application.
- Avoid prescribing activities that require unsupported assumptions about pool depth, supervision or specialist equipment.

## SW-07 Assess Swimming Progress

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary PE teacher and assessment designer specialising in evidence-based swimming assessment.

### CONTEXT
I need to assess pupil progress in swimming.

Use:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Learning objectives: [OBJECTIVES]
- Previous evidence: [PREVIOUS EVIDENCE]
- Current task: [CURRENT TASK]
- Local assessment expectations: [EXPECTATIONS]

### TASK
Create a fair assessment framework that records progress against the stated learning objectives.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Assess observable movement and knowledge rather than impressions.
- Separate skill components such as body position, control, propulsion, coordination, direction and safe participation where relevant.
- Use developmental language that describes progress without ranking pupils unnecessarily.
- Distinguish evidence from teacher inference.
- Do not claim that AI-generated criteria certify swimming competence.

2. Criteria table
3. Observable evidence for each criterion
4. Four-point progression descriptors
5. Evidence-recording method
6. Pupil feedback prompts
7. Intervention or next-step rules
8. Teacher moderation notes

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check fairness, clarity, observable evidence and alignment with the learning objectives. Ensure prior extracurricular swimming experience is not treated as proof of overall PE attainment.

## SW-08 Diagnose and Intervene in Swimming Skills

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary swimming teacher and movement-diagnosis specialist.

### CONTEXT
A pupil is experiencing difficulty with a swimming skill.

Use:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Target skill: [SKILL]
- Observed difficulty: [DIFFICULTY]
- Evidence collected: [EVIDENCE]
- Equipment: [EQUIPMENT]
- Local procedures: [PROCEDURES]

### TASK
Analyse the likely movement issue and create a short, targeted intervention sequence.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Base the diagnosis only on the evidence supplied.
- Separate observed facts from hypotheses about causes.
- Identify the smallest useful skill component to practise first.
- Provide one or more targeted activities with clear success criteria.
- Explain how the teacher can check whether the intervention worked.
- Avoid diagnosing medical conditions or making clinical claims.
- Escalate any safety or health concern through the appropriate local process rather than inventing a response.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Observed evidence
2. Likely movement issue
3. Confidence and uncertainty
4. Intervention objective
5. Targeted activity sequence
6. Teacher cues
7. What to observe
8. Decision rule for progression or further support

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that the intervention is evidence-led, technically accurate, proportionate and safe. Ensure the prompt does not turn a movement observation into a medical diagnosis.

## SW-09 Adapt Swimming for Inclusion

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary PE teacher specialising in inclusive swimming provision.

### CONTEXT
I need to adapt a swimming activity for a pupil or group while preserving the core learning objective where possible.

Use:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Core learning objective: [OBJECTIVE]
- Original activity: [ACTIVITY]
- Barrier to participation: [BARRIER]
- Available equipment: [EQUIPMENT]
- Relevant school or pool procedures: [PROCEDURES]

### TASK
Adapt the activity to improve access while making clear whether the adaptation changes the skill or assessment construct.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Preserve the core learning objective wherever reasonably possible.
- Consider adaptations to space, equipment, task complexity, demonstration, communication, pace, repetition, grouping and response route.
- Do not invent specialist equipment or specialist support arrangements.
- Do not make assumptions about an individual's medical needs.
- Identify any adaptation that changes what is being assessed.
- Offer more than one access route where appropriate without lowering expectations unnecessarily.
- Keep pupil dignity, choice and safe participation central.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Core objective
2. Original task
3. Identified barrier
4. Adapted task
5. Equipment or environmental adjustment
6. Teacher communication adjustment
7. Success criteria
8. Whether the construct has changed
9. Assessment implications
10. Review point

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that inclusion improves access without unnecessarily removing the physical learning. Clearly flag adaptations that alter the construct being assessed. Do not introduce unsupported medical or facility-specific arrangements.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add: [PUPIL STRENGTHS], [KNOWN ACCESS REQUIREMENTS], [EQUIPMENT], [SETTING PROCEDURES].

## SW-10 Plan a Swimming Safety and Progression Sequence

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary PE curriculum leader designing a coherent swimming progression.

### CONTEXT
I need to plan a sequence of swimming learning across several lessons.

Use:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Number of lessons: [NUMBER]
- Starting point: [STARTING POINT]
- Intended outcomes: [OUTCOMES]
- Facility and equipment information: [FACILITY INFORMATION]
- School or pool procedures: [PROCEDURES]
- Inclusion considerations: [INCLUSION]

### TASK
Create a progressive sequence that develops water confidence, aquatic movement and swimming skills while embedding safe participation throughout.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Sequence learning from the supplied starting point toward clearly defined outcomes.
- Use a progression such as explore, practise, stabilise, apply, adapt, combine and evaluate where appropriate.
- Build in retrieval and repeated practice rather than treating each lesson as isolated.
- Include meaningful assessment checkpoints and intervention points.
- Include water-confidence and safe-behaviour learning alongside movement learning.
- Do not invent pool depth, supervision ratios, lifeguard arrangements, emergency plans, medical protocols or facility rules.
- Mark all setting-specific safety decisions as local checks when information is missing.
- Avoid treating speed, distance or competitive performance as the sole evidence of progress.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Sequence overview
2. Starting-point assumptions
3. Lesson-by-lesson progression table
4. Learning intention and success criteria for each lesson
5. Safety focus for each lesson
6. Assessment checkpoints
7. Intervention pathways
8. Inclusion and adaptation strategy
9. End-of-sequence evaluation
10. Local-setting checks still required

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check curriculum coherence, developmental progression, technical accuracy, meaningful practice, inclusive access and safe design. Verify that safety-critical local information has not been fabricated and that the sequence does not imply certification of swimming competence.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add: [CURRICULUM FRAMEWORK], [LESSON LENGTH], [GROUP SIZE], [STROKES], [FACILITY RULES], [ASSESSMENT MODEL].
