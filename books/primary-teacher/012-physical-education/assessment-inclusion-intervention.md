# Assessment, Inclusion & Intervention

This workflow family provides copy/paste-ready prompts for assessing primary PE learning, identifying barriers, adapting tasks and planning evidence-led intervention. Assessment should focus on the intended construct, not body size, speed, strength, prior extracurricular experience or competitive status unless these are explicitly part of the stated objective. Inclusive adaptations should preserve the core learning wherever possible and clearly identify when an adaptation changes the construct.

## AI-01 Assess Movement Competence

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary PE teacher and assessment designer specialising in movement competence.

### CONTEXT
I need to assess pupils against a specific movement-learning objective.

Use:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Learning objective: [OBJECTIVE]
- Skill or movement: [SKILL]
- Evidence available: [OBSERVATION OR PERFORMANCE EVIDENCE]
- Previous learning: [PREVIOUS LEARNING]

### TASK
Create a fair assessment framework based on observable evidence of the intended movement competence.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Define observable criteria linked directly to the learning objective.
- Separate technique, control, coordination and application where relevant.
- Use progression descriptors rather than vague judgements.
- Distinguish evidence from inference.
- Do not treat speed, strength, body size or prior extracurricular experience as automatic evidence of higher attainment.
- Include opportunities for pupils to demonstrate learning in an appropriate context.
- Provide clear next steps.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Assessment purpose
2. Observable criteria
3. Four-point progression descriptors
4. Evidence examples
5. Teacher recording template
6. Pupil feedback prompts
7. Next-step guidance

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check construct validity, fairness, developmental suitability and alignment with the objective. Ensure the assessment does not reward irrelevant physical characteristics.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add: [CURRICULUM], [ASSESSMENT SCALE], [ACTIVITY], [GROUP SIZE].

## AI-02 Assess Decision-Making in PE

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary PE teacher and assessment specialist in tactical and movement decision-making.

### CONTEXT
I need to assess pupils' decision-making during a PE activity.

Use:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Activity: [ACTIVITY]
- Tactical or decision-making objective: [OBJECTIVE]
- Evidence available: [EVIDENCE]

### TASK
Create an assessment approach that measures the quality of pupils' decisions rather than simply the outcome of each attempt.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Define the decisions pupils are expected to make.
- Identify observable evidence of appropriate decision-making.
- Consider whether a decision was suitable given the situation, even if execution did not succeed.
- Separate tactical understanding from physical execution where appropriate.
- Avoid equating winning with attainment.
- Include questions that reveal pupils' reasoning.
- Provide progression descriptors and next steps.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Intended decision-making construct
2. Observation criteria
3. Situational examples
4. Progression descriptors
5. Pupil questioning prompts
6. Recording method
7. Feedback and intervention guidance

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that the assessment measures decisions and reasoning rather than only athletic performance or game results.

## AI-05 Identify PE Misconceptions

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary PE teacher and diagnostic assessment specialist.

### CONTEXT
I have pupil responses, observations or explanations that may reveal misconceptions about PE learning.

Use:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Topic: [TOPIC]
- Pupil evidence: [EVIDENCE]
- Intended understanding: [INTENDED UNDERSTANDING]

### TASK
Identify likely misconceptions and create targeted teaching responses.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Quote or describe the relevant evidence briefly.
- Distinguish a genuine misconception from an incomplete response or lack of evidence.
- Explain the likely conceptual issue in teacher-friendly language.
- Create a short corrective teaching activity.
- Include a check to determine whether the misconception has changed.
- Do not diagnose cognitive, medical or developmental conditions.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide a table with:
1. Evidence
2. Likely misconception
3. Confidence level
4. Why it matters
5. Corrective teaching activity
6. Checking question or task
7. Follow-up action

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that conclusions are evidence-led and proportionate. Do not infer a diagnosis from limited classroom evidence.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add: [TOPIC], [YEAR GROUP], [PUPIL RESPONSES], [CURRICULUM OBJECTIVE].

## AI-06 Plan Targeted PE Intervention

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary PE teacher specialising in evidence-led intervention and skill development.

### CONTEXT
A pupil or small group has not yet secured an identified PE learning objective.

Use:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Objective: [OBJECTIVE]
- Current evidence: [EVIDENCE]
- Likely barrier: [BARRIER OR UNKNOWN]
- Space and equipment: [SPACE AND EQUIPMENT]

### TASK
Create a short intervention that targets the smallest useful learning component before rebuilding the full skill.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Base the intervention on supplied evidence.
- Separate observed difficulty from hypotheses about its cause.
- Use manageable practice steps and high-quality repetition.
- Include clear success criteria.
- State how the teacher will check whether the intervention worked.
- Include a progression route back into the main lesson.
- Avoid medical or psychological diagnosis.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Evidence summary
2. Intervention objective
3. Target skill component
4. Activity sequence
5. Teacher cues
6. Success criteria
7. Monitoring method
8. Progression back to main learning
9. Review decision

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that intervention is focused, feasible, evidence-led, inclusive and technically accurate.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add: [SKILL], [YEAR GROUP], [EQUIPMENT], [NUMBER OF SESSIONS].

## AI-07 Adapt a PE Task Without Changing the Core Objective

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary PE teacher specialising in inclusive curriculum design.

### CONTEXT
I need to adapt a PE task because a pupil or group is encountering a participation barrier.

Use:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Core objective: [OBJECTIVE]
- Original task: [TASK]
- Barrier: [BARRIER]
- Available resources: [RESOURCES]
- Relevant school guidance: [GUIDANCE]

### TASK
Design one or more adaptations that improve access while preserving the core learning objective.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Consider space, equipment, task complexity, instructions, demonstration, communication, grouping, pace, repetition and response route.
- Preserve the core objective wherever reasonably possible.
- Explain exactly what changes and why.
- State whether each adaptation changes the assessment construct.
- Do not invent specialist equipment, medical requirements or support arrangements.
- Avoid lowering expectations when a change in access is sufficient.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Core objective
2. Original barrier
3. Adaptation options
4. Teacher implementation notes
5. Success criteria
6. Construct impact
7. Assessment implications
8. Review point

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check accessibility, dignity, feasibility and preservation of the intended learning. Clearly flag any construct change.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add: [KNOWN ACCESS REQUIREMENTS], [EQUIPMENT], [SPACE], [PUPIL VOICE].

## AI-08 Design Inclusive PE Grouping

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary PE teacher and inclusive lesson designer.

### CONTEXT
I need to organise pupils into groups for a PE lesson.

Use:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Class size: [CLASS SIZE]
- Activity: [ACTIVITY]
- Learning objective: [OBJECTIVE]
- Pupil considerations: [CONSIDERATIONS]
- Space and equipment: [SPACE AND EQUIPMENT]

### TASK
Create a grouping strategy that supports learning, participation, safety and pupil dignity.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Explain the purpose of the grouping approach.
- Consider mixed attainment, compatible working relationships, equitable opportunities and meaningful practice time.
- Avoid permanently labelling pupils as high, middle or low ability.
- Avoid grouping pupils solely by body size or perceived athleticism.
- Include mechanisms for regrouping based on learning needs.
- Provide roles that remain meaningful to the physical learning where appropriate.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Grouping strategy
2. Rationale
3. Initial grouping method
4. Teacher instructions
5. Regrouping triggers
6. Inclusion safeguards
7. Reflection and review

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check fairness, inclusion, safety and learning opportunity. Ensure grouping does not create fixed ability labels or unnecessary stigma.

## AI-10 Plan PE Assessment, Inclusion and Intervention Across a Unit

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary PE curriculum leader designing assessment and inclusion across a complete unit.

### CONTEXT
I need a coherent system for assessing progress, adapting teaching and intervening across a PE unit.

Use:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Unit topic: [TOPIC]
- Number of lessons: [NUMBER]
- Learning objectives: [OBJECTIVES]
- Starting-point evidence: [STARTING POINT]
- Space and equipment: [SPACE AND EQUIPMENT]
- Inclusion considerations: [INCLUSION]

### TASK
Create a unit-level assessment, inclusion and intervention plan that tracks progress without reducing PE attainment to physical performance alone.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Map assessment opportunities to each learning objective.
- Use observable evidence and clear progression descriptors.
- Distinguish movement execution, control, decision-making, knowledge, safe participation, collaboration and other constructs where relevant.
- Include diagnostic checkpoints and intervention triggers.
- Build inclusion into planning rather than treating it as an afterthought.
- Identify adaptations that preserve the core objective and flag those that alter the construct.
- Include pupil self-assessment and meaningful pupil voice.
- Avoid body-size, appearance or prior extracurricular experience as proxies for attainment.
- Avoid medical or psychological diagnosis.
- Include local-setting checks where school-specific procedures are required.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Unit assessment framework
2. Objective-to-evidence map
3. Lesson-by-lesson assessment checkpoints
4. Progression descriptors
5. Inclusion and adaptation framework
6. Intervention decision rules
7. Pupil self-assessment approach
8. Teacher recording template
9. End-of-unit judgement process
10. Moderation and quality-assurance checklist

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check construct validity, fairness, developmental suitability, inclusion, feasibility and evidence quality. Ensure assessment does not confuse athletic performance with broader PE attainment and does not introduce unsupported medical or diagnostic claims.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add: [CURRICULUM FRAMEWORK], [ASSESSMENT MODEL], [UNIT TOPIC], [CLASS PROFILE], [SCHOOL PROCEDURES].
