# Primary Physical Education Prompt Framework

## Purpose

This framework defines the architecture for the Primary Physical Education prompt pack. The prompts are designed to help teachers plan, teach, assess and adapt high-quality PE while preserving physical activity, skill development, pupil participation, safety and inclusion.

**Total workflows: 100.**

## Core Principles

1. **Physical participation matters**: prompts should produce practical PE that gets pupils moving rather than replacing activity with excessive explanation or writing.
2. **Movement competence before performance comparison**: pupils should develop control, coordination, confidence and understanding without unnecessary ranking against peers.
3. **Progression is deliberate**: activities should develop from simple movement patterns and decisions towards greater complexity, control, fluency and independence.
4. **Technical accuracy matters**: movement, tactics, rules, fitness concepts and safety guidance must be accurate for the stated age and activity.
5. **Safety is activity-specific**: prompts must consider space, equipment, supervision, contact, environment, warm-up where appropriate, hydration, clothing and emergency procedures without inventing school-specific policies.
6. **Inclusion preserves meaningful PE**: adaptations should remove access barriers while retaining the intended physical, cognitive or social learning wherever reasonably possible.
7. **Pupil agency is important**: pupils should have appropriate opportunities to make movement, tactical, compositional and evaluative decisions.
8. **Assessment is evidence-led**: judgements should use observable movement, decision-making, knowledge and participation evidence rather than vague impressions.
9. **Avoid unsupported health claims**: distinguish general physical-activity education from individual medical or clinical advice.
10. **Respect pupil dignity**: avoid body-shaming, unnecessary public comparison and assumptions about fitness, ability, weight or health.

## Standard Workflow Architecture

Every workflow follows the approved structure:

**ROLE → CONTEXT → TASK → REQUIREMENTS → OUTPUT FORMAT → QUALITY CHECKS → OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION**

Prompts should be copy/paste-ready and sufficiently detailed that a teacher can obtain a useful result without unnecessary iteration.

## PE Quality Gates

Every workflow should be checked for:

- Curriculum alignment
- Appropriate physical activity and movement time
- Age and developmental suitability
- Technical accuracy
- Safe and realistic activity design
- Appropriate equipment and space assumptions
- Clear progression
- Observable success criteria
- Inclusive access
- Pupil agency
- Respectful language
- Feasible teacher workload
- Evidence-based assessment
- Appropriate handling of uncertainty

## Planning Model

Where relevant, PE planning should connect:

**Learning intention → Demonstration/model → Guided practice → Independent or game application → Feedback → Reflection/evaluation → Progression**

## Skill Development Model

Where relevant, prompts should consider:

**Explore → Practise → Stabilise → Apply → Adapt → Combine → Perform → Evaluate**

The model should not be applied mechanically. The appropriate progression depends on the activity, pupils' prior learning and the intended outcome.

## Assessment Model

Assessment should distinguish, where relevant:

- Movement execution
- Control and coordination
- Decision-making
- Tactical understanding
- Composition and creativity
- Knowledge and vocabulary
- Safe participation
- Collaboration and communication
- Progress over time

Do not equate sporting performance, speed, strength or prior extracurricular experience with overall PE attainment unless these are explicitly part of the stated construct.

## Inclusion Model

Adaptations may address:

- Space
- Equipment
- Task complexity
- Demonstration method
- Instructions and communication
- Grouping
- Pace and repetition
- Physical access
- Sensory access
- Recording and reflection
- Choice of role or response route

Prompts must state when an adaptation changes the skill or construct being assessed.

## Safety Model

Safety guidance should be proportionate and specific to the activity. Prompts must use supplied school procedures where these exist and must not invent emergency arrangements, medical protocols or supervision ratios.

For activities involving water, heights, outdoor environments, specialist equipment or physical contact, safety requirements must be treated as integral to lesson design rather than as an afterthought.

## Verification Model

Verification will separate:

### 1. Structural QA

Confirm all 100 workflows exist, are correctly numbered and follow the approved architecture.

### 2. Representative Execution Testing

Test 20 workflows, with exactly two workflows from each family, using realistic teacher inputs.

### 3. Targeted Risk Spot Checks

Complete 16 additional checks covering higher-risk PE behaviours, including:

1. Safe practical lesson design
2. Movement terminology accuracy
3. Appropriate progression
4. Avoidance of unsafe physical tasks
5. Inclusive adaptation without unnecessary loss of physical learning
6. Fair assessment of movement competence
7. Tactical reasoning accuracy
8. Dance and composition terminology
9. Gymnastics safety and body-management guidance
10. Athletics technique and measurement claims
11. Outdoor and orienteering safety
12. Swimming and water-safety boundaries
13. Fitness and health claims
14. Respectful handling of body image and fitness differences
15. Evidence-based intervention
16. Safe handling of missing school-specific procedures

## Release Sequence

1. Create framework.
2. Create all ten workflow families.
3. Complete structural QA.
4. Execute representative tests.
5. Complete targeted spot checks.
6. Remediate and re-test any failures.
7. Freeze the subject only when all acceptance criteria pass.
