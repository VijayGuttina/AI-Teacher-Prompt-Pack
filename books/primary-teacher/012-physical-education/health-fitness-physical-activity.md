# Health, Fitness & Physical Activity

This workflow family provides copy/paste-ready prompts for primary PE learning about physical activity, fitness, healthy participation and personal wellbeing. Prompts should use age-appropriate language, avoid medical diagnosis and avoid unsupported claims about health, body composition or exercise outcomes. Where school policies or pupil-specific professional guidance are relevant, use the supplied information and do not invent it.

## HF-01 Plan a Physical Activity Lesson

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary PE teacher and curriculum planner specialising in inclusive physical activity and fitness learning.

### CONTEXT
I am planning a lesson that develops pupils' understanding and experience of physical activity.

Use:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Lesson length: [LENGTH]
- Learning focus: [FOCUS]
- Current learning: [STARTING POINT]
- Space and equipment: [SPACE AND EQUIPMENT]
- Inclusion considerations: [INCLUSION NEEDS]

### TASK
Create a practical, age-appropriate lesson that gives pupils meaningful opportunities to be physically active while developing the stated learning objective.

### REQUIREMENTS
- State one clear learning intention and observable success criteria.
- Include a purposeful warm-up, main learning, application and reflection.
- Prioritise active participation while allowing appropriate recovery, instruction and transitions.
- Explain the purpose of activities in pupil-friendly language.
- Include adaptations for different starting points.
- Avoid unsupported claims that a particular activity guarantees a health outcome.
- Do not prescribe medical exercise programmes.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Lesson overview
2. Learning intention
3. Success criteria
4. Activity sequence with approximate timings
5. Teacher cues
6. Differentiation
7. Assessment opportunities
8. Reflection questions
9. Next learning step

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check developmental suitability, meaningful movement time, feasibility, inclusion and safe participation. Remove unsupported medical or physiological claims.

## HF-02 Teach the Benefits of Physical Activity

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary PE teacher who explains physical activity and wellbeing concepts accurately and appropriately for children.

### CONTEXT
I want pupils to understand why regular physical activity can be valuable.

Use:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Curriculum objective: [OBJECTIVE]
- Prior knowledge: [PRIOR KNOWLEDGE]

### TASK
Create a short teaching sequence that helps pupils explain age-appropriate benefits of physical activity without turning the lesson into medical advice.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Use simple, accurate explanations.
- Distinguish general wellbeing concepts from individual medical advice.
- Cover relevant aspects such as movement, enjoyment, physical capability, social participation and wellbeing where appropriate.
- Avoid claims that exercise prevents, treats or cures specific conditions unless supported by reliable supplied evidence and clearly framed.
- Avoid implying that body size, appearance or athletic performance determines health or worth.
- Include questions that require pupils to explain ideas in their own words.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Learning intention
2. Teacher explanation
3. Child-friendly examples
4. Misconceptions to address
5. Discussion questions
6. Quick formative assessment
7. Reflection task

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check scientific accuracy, age suitability and respectful language. Remove diagnostic, treatment or unsupported causal claims.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add: [YEAR GROUP], [CURRICULUM LANGUAGE], [PRIOR LESSON], [SPECIFIC BENEFIT].

## HF-09 Diagnose Participation Barriers and Plan Intervention

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary PE teacher specialising in inclusive participation and evidence-led intervention.

### CONTEXT
A pupil is participating less than expected in physical activity or is finding a PE task difficult.

Use:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Observed evidence: [EVIDENCE]
- Activity: [ACTIVITY]
- Environment and equipment: [ENVIRONMENT]
- Pupil voice: [PUPIL VOICE]
- Known school guidance: [GUIDANCE]

### TASK
Analyse the evidence and propose practical teaching changes that could improve participation and learning.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Separate observed barriers from assumptions about causes.
- Consider task design, space, equipment, instructions, confidence, grouping, communication and opportunity to practise.
- Use pupil voice where available.
- Propose small, testable adjustments before making broad conclusions.
- Do not diagnose medical, psychological or developmental conditions.
- If the supplied information indicates a concern requiring specialist input, state that the teacher should follow the school's appropriate process.
- Define what evidence would show whether the intervention helped.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Observed evidence
2. Possible participation barriers
3. Uncertainty and information gaps
4. Immediate teaching adjustments
5. Short intervention plan
6. Evidence to monitor
7. Review point
8. Escalation or referral boundary if relevant

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that recommendations are evidence-led, inclusive, practical and proportionate. Do not turn classroom observations into diagnoses.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add: [ACTIVITY], [KNOWN ACCESS NEEDS], [PUPIL VOICE], [SCHOOL PROCEDURES].
