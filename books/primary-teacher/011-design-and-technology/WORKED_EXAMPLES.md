# Primary Design & Technology Worked Examples and Test Fixtures

## Purpose

This file provides representative teacher inputs and expected output characteristics for D&T workflows. These are **test fixtures and worked examples**, not evidence that a model execution has occurred.

## Fixture selection

Twenty workflows are selected: two from each workflow family. Together they exercise planning, technical explanation, diagnosis, practical safety, assessment, inclusion, digital design, sustainability and cross-curricular integration.

---

## 1. Design, Users & Ideas

### DU-01 — Design Brief
**Representative input:** Year 5 pupils need to design a lunchbox insert that stops a banana being squashed. User: a child carrying lunch to school. Constraints: cardboard, paper, tape, limited lesson time.

**Expected characteristics:** Clear user, purpose, success criteria and constraints; multiple valid solutions remain possible; no premature prescription of a final design.

### DU-08 — Diagnose Weak Design Thinking
**Representative input:** Pupil idea: “Make it out of thick cardboard because thick cardboard is strong.” No testing evidence is supplied.

**Expected characteristics:** Separate the statement from assumptions; identify missing evidence; propose a practical comparison before concluding that the material is suitable.

## 2. Materials, Tools, Making & Technical Skills

### MT-03 — Match Tools to Tasks
**Representative input:** Year 4 pupils are cutting card, measuring strips and joining pieces. Available equipment: rulers, pencils, child-safe scissors, tape and glue.

**Expected characteristics:** Appropriate tool-purpose matches, realistic alternatives, and no unnecessary recommendation of hazardous equipment.

### MT-05 — Diagnose Making Difficulties
**Representative input:** A model repeatedly leans to one side after construction. Evidence: one support is visibly shorter and the base is narrow.

**Expected characteristics:** Evidence first, plausible causes, a discriminating check and a proportionate intervention.

## 3. Cooking, Nutrition & Food Skills

### CN-01 — Plan a Cooking Demonstration
**Representative input:** Year 5 fruit salad using supplied school-approved knives and chopping boards. Lesson time: 45 minutes.

**Expected characteristics:** Preparation, hygiene and teacher-approved safety controls are integrated into the sequence; no replacement of local risk procedures.

### CN-05 — Adapt a Recipe Within Constraints
**Representative input:** Adapt a simple wrap filling for a vegetarian requirement, using only ingredients supplied by the teacher.

**Expected characteristics:** Clearly distinguish supplied dietary constraints from assumptions; explain likely functional and sensory effects without unsupported guarantees.

## 4. Mechanisms & Electrical Systems

### ME-03 — Diagnose a Mechanism Failure
**Representative input:** A cardboard lever moves but does not lift the intended flap. The pivot is close to the effort point and the card bends during use.

**Expected characteristics:** Separate observed behaviour from hypotheses and propose tests of pivot position and material stiffness.

### ME-06 — Diagnose a Circuit Fault
**Representative input:** Battery, wires, bulb and switch are connected, but the bulb does not light. Components and observed setup are supplied.

**Expected characteristics:** Systematic fault-finding sequence; safe checks; no unsupported assumption about the exact cause.

## 5. Structures, Textiles & Product Construction

### ST-01 — Explore Structural Stability
**Representative input:** Year 3 pupils must build the tallest free-standing paper tower using ten sheets of paper and 30 cm of tape.

**Expected characteristics:** Prediction, build/test cycle and observable evidence; terminology matched to age.

### ST-08 — Plan Product Construction Sequence
**Representative input:** Simple felt pouch with decoration, joining and fastening. Available time: two lessons.

**Expected characteristics:** Logical dependencies, checkpoints and correction points before irreversible steps.

## 6. Testing, Evaluation & Iteration

### TE-01 — Create a Product Test Plan
**Representative input:** Test whether a cardboard bridge prototype can carry increasing loads while meeting a 25 cm span requirement.

**Expected characteristics:** Explicit criteria, observable evidence, safe and feasible testing, and a clear boundary on what the test can conclude.

### TE-07 — Use User Feedback Properly
**Representative input:** Three users say a prototype is “nice”; one says the handle is uncomfortable after carrying it.

**Expected characteristics:** Do not treat all comments as equivalent; connect actionable feedback to stated user requirements.

## 7. Assessment, Misconceptions & Intervention

### AMI-02 — Create a D&T Hinge Question
**Representative input:** Concept: why a wider base can improve stability. Likely misconception: “more material always means more stable.”

**Expected characteristics:** One diagnostic question, plausible distractors and explicit teacher responses linked to each misconception.

### AMI-04 — Diagnose a Technical Misconception
**Representative input:** Pupil says, “The bridge failed because the glue was bad,” despite visible bending in the main span before the joint failed.

**Expected characteristics:** Distinguish evidence from conclusion and conceptual misunderstanding from execution difficulty.

## 8. Inclusion, Progression & Classroom Implementation

### IP-04 — Preserve Design Agency During Scaffolding
**Representative input:** A pupil can generate ideas verbally but struggles to produce detailed sketches.

**Expected characteristics:** Alternative access route while retaining pupil ownership of design decisions; clear fade-out path.

### IP-07 — Plan Challenge Through Design Depth
**Representative input:** Secure Year 6 pupils have completed a basic container design.

**Expected characteristics:** Increase challenge through constraints, trade-offs, testing and justification rather than extra decorative workload.

## 9. Digital Design, CAD & Computing Integration

### DC-02 — Use CAD to Develop a Design
**Representative input:** Year 6 pupils design a small desk organiser using available 2D CAD software. Constraints: maximum footprint 15 cm × 10 cm.

**Expected characteristics:** CAD supports dimensions and iteration; output does not depend on invented software features.

### DC-08 — Adapt Digital D&T Tasks for Access
**Representative input:** A digital design activity creates an interface barrier for a pupil who finds precise pointer control difficult.

**Expected characteristics:** Practical access adaptations while retaining meaningful design choices and digital design intent.

## 10. Curriculum, Sustainability & Cross-Curricular D&T

### CS-04 — Evaluate Sustainability Claims
**Representative input:** Packaging is labelled “eco-friendly” and “100% sustainable”; no evidence is supplied.

**Expected characteristics:** Do not validate the claims; specify what evidence would be needed and communicate uncertainty clearly.

### CS-06 — Integrate D&T With Mathematics
**Representative input:** Design a Year 5 project in which pupils create a storage tray and apply measurement and area to genuine design decisions.

**Expected characteristics:** Mathematics informs design constraints and decisions; neither subject is reduced to decorative activity.
