# Design & Technology Framework

**Scope:** UK Primary, defaulting to the National Curriculum for England.

## Purpose

Design & Technology (D&T) workflows should help teachers move pupils through a genuine design process: investigate and evaluate, generate and develop ideas, plan, make, test, evaluate and improve. Workflows must preserve practical decision-making rather than turning D&T into worksheet completion.

## Core strands

1. **Design & User Needs** — investigate users, purposes, existing products and relevant design criteria.
2. **Generating & Developing Ideas** — sketch, annotate, model, compare and refine possible solutions.
3. **Materials, Components & Tools** — select and use materials, components and tools appropriately, with proportionate safety guidance.
4. **Making & Technical Skills** — plan sequences, construct accurately, troubleshoot and manage resources.
5. **Cooking & Nutrition** — apply food preparation, nutrition, hygiene and safety knowledge appropriately.
6. **Testing, Evaluation & Iteration** — test against explicit criteria, interpret evidence and improve designs.
7. **Technical Knowledge & Mechanisms** — develop age-appropriate understanding of structures, mechanisms, electrical systems and digital/programmable elements where relevant.
8. **Assessment, Misconceptions & Intervention** — diagnose conceptual, design-process and practical barriers using evidence.
9. **Inclusion & Access** — adapt tools, processes, communication and recording without unnecessarily removing the intended design challenge.
10. **Curriculum Progression & Implementation** — sequence knowledge and skills across year groups and make classroom delivery feasible.

## Workflow design principles

Every workflow should:

- state the intended design or technical construct;
- distinguish design quality from personal taste;
- preserve pupil agency where multiple valid solutions exist;
- require explicit criteria before evaluating a product;
- distinguish observation/evidence from inference about pupil ability or motivation;
- avoid inventing technical properties, manufacturer guidance or safety requirements;
- include safety controls where tools, heat, food, electricity or moving parts are involved;
- treat testing as evidence for iteration rather than a ceremonial final step;
- use age-appropriate technical vocabulary;
- adapt access while preserving substantive design thinking where possible.

## Workflow architecture

Each workflow should use the established library structure:

**ROLE → CONTEXT → TASK → REQUIREMENTS → OUTPUT FORMAT → QUALITY CHECKS → OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION**

Where a workflow involves diagnosis, assessment or intervention, it should additionally make the evidence, uncertainty and discriminating check explicit.

## Commercial scope

The module should be a curated workflow library, not a catalogue of near-duplicate prompts. Prioritise recurring teacher jobs with broad reuse across projects, materials and year groups.

## Verification boundary

Structural QA and authorship are not model verification. Execution status must be recorded separately, with the exact AI tool/model, test date, representative input, observed result, reviewer notes and remediation where applicable.
