# Primary Art & Design Worked Examples

**Purpose:** Representative teacher inputs and the quality characteristics expected from a strong workflow execution. These examples are test fixtures and guidance, not claims that the model has already produced the outputs shown here.

## Example 1 — AE-01 Plan an Art Enquiry

**Input:** Year 4. Theme: local architecture. Three lessons. Focus on line, shape and observation. Pupils should develop an original architectural drawing rather than copy one model.

**Expected output characteristics:**
- enquiry question connects observation to artistic decision-making;
- substantive knowledge and practical skills are distinct;
- lessons include looking, experimentation, making and reflection;
- success criteria assess relevant knowledge/skills without requiring identical drawings;
- resources and timing are realistic.

## Example 2 — DM-06 Diagnose Drawing Misconceptions

**Input:** A pupil repeatedly draws every object using heavy outlines. Their teacher notes that the pupil can identify light and dark areas verbally but does not show them in the drawing.

**Expected output characteristics:**
- observable evidence is separated from interpretation;
- the workflow does not label the pupil as lacking ability or effort;
- a discriminating task checks whether the barrier is tonal observation, technique or transfer;
- intervention targets the diagnosed barrier.

## Example 3 — CP-05 Plan a Printmaking Investigation

**Input:** Year 3. Foam relief printing. Pupils will explore repeated motifs and positive/negative shapes. One lesson, 30 pupils.

**Expected output characteristics:**
- technically plausible print sequence;
- manageable resource distribution and cleanup;
- safety considerations appropriate to the stated materials;
- experimentation precedes a final composition;
- success criteria focus on the intended printmaking concepts.

## Example 4 — AC-05 Explore Artistic Intent

**Input:** A supplied artwork and a short verified contextual note are provided. Pupils are asked why the artist may have chosen a particular composition.

**Expected output characteristics:**
- visible evidence is listed before interpretation;
- possible interpretations are labelled as possibilities;
- the model does not claim private artist intention as fact without evidence;
- questions encourage pupils to justify interpretations from evidence.

## Example 5 — EV-08 Moderate Art Work

**Input:** Three Year 5 works and shared criteria assessing experimentation with colour, purposeful composition and ability to explain choices.

**Expected output characteristics:**
- judgements reference the stated criteria;
- personal taste and neatness are not substituted for the criteria;
- evidence is distinguished from inference;
- uncertainty is recorded where the supplied evidence is insufficient.

## Example 6 — SK-05 Review Creative Process Evidence

**Input:** A pupil sketchbook contains initial observations, three material experiments, two rejected compositions and a final piece. The final piece is visually less polished than another pupil's work.

**Expected output characteristics:**
- process evidence is recognised as relevant evidence of experimentation and decision-making;
- the output does not infer motivation or effort from visual polish;
- development and gaps are identified against the intended learning construct;
- the next teaching step is specific.

## Example 7 — AMI-09 Adapt Assessment for Inclusion

**Input:** A pupil has a motor-access barrier. The assessment asks pupils to demonstrate understanding of colour relationships and explain artistic choices.

**Expected output characteristics:**
- response mode can be adapted where appropriate;
- the intended colour/conceptual construct remains intact;
- the adaptation is clearly distinguished from lowering the learning demand;
- interpretation guidance explains what evidence remains valid.

## Example 8 — IA-08 Plan Challenge

**Input:** Year 6 pupils can confidently use observational drawing techniques and explain their choices. Teacher wants additional challenge without requiring a second drawing.

**Expected output characteristics:**
- challenge increases depth through refinement, experimentation, alternative decisions or justification;
- workload is not simply increased;
- artistic agency remains intact;
- success evidence is observable.

## QA note

These fixtures should be used during representative execution testing. A passing output must satisfy the Art & Design framework quality gates and the execution test plan. A worked example is not itself evidence of model verification.
