# Listening, Speaking & Classroom Interaction

This family contains ten copy/paste-ready workflows for developing primary MFL listening, speaking and classroom interaction. The workflows treat listening as comprehension, speaking as purposeful communication and interaction as a learnable language practice. They are designed for any specified target language and do not assume French, Spanish or another language unless supplied.

## LS-01 Plan a Listening Comprehension Lesson

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 800 to 1,200 words  
**Typical generation time:** 2 to 3 minutes  
**Curriculum tags:** MFL, KS2, listening, comprehension, lesson planning, target language

### Prompt

```text
ROLE
You are an experienced primary MFL listening specialist. Design a classroom-ready listening lesson in which pupils demonstrate understanding of spoken target language. Treat listening as a comprehension construct, not as an exercise in repeating sounds.

CONTEXT
Target language: [TARGET LANGUAGE]
Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
Pupil age: [AGE]
Topic: [TOPIC]
Curriculum objective: [CURRICULUM OBJECTIVE]
Prior language knowledge: [PRIOR LANGUAGE]
New language: [NEW LANGUAGE]
Listening focus: [LISTENING FOCUS]
Lesson duration: [DURATION]
Available audio or resources: [AUDIO / RESOURCES]
SEND/EAL/access requirements: [ACCESS REQUIREMENTS]
Assessment purpose: [ASSESSMENT PURPOSE]

TASK
Create one complete listening comprehension lesson that moves from preparation and prediction through supported listening, comprehension checking, feedback and transfer into meaningful language use.

REQUIREMENTS
1. State a precise learning intention and pupil-friendly success criteria.
2. Identify the language pupils need to recognise and understand.
3. Activate relevant prior vocabulary or sentence structures before listening.
4. Prepare pupils for the listening without giving away every answer.
5. Use at least two listening passes with different purposes, such as gist followed by specific information.
6. Ensure comprehension questions test meaning, not merely whether pupils can repeat the words they hear.
7. Include visual or contextual support where useful, while avoiding support that makes listening unnecessary.
8. Include teacher modelling of how to listen for the required information.
9. Provide a gradual reduction in scaffolding.
10. Include likely listening misconceptions, such as confusing similar sounds, missing a familiar word in connected speech or relying on one known word to guess the whole meaning.
11. Give precise corrective feedback that returns pupils to the spoken evidence.
12. Include an appropriate short assessment of listening comprehension.
13. Include accessibility adaptations for hearing, processing, language load, attention and response mode while preserving the listening construct.
14. Do not require pupils to disclose personal information or perform publicly unless that is genuinely necessary for the stated objective.
15. Do not fabricate an authentic audio source, speaker, quotation or recording. If a script is needed, label it as teacher-generated unless an authentic source is supplied.
16. If target-language accuracy depends on missing information, use [VERIFY] rather than inventing language.

OUTPUT FORMAT
Use these headings:
1. Lesson overview
2. Learning intention and success criteria
3. Prior-language activation
4. Pre-listening preparation
5. Listening pass 1: gist
6. Listening pass 2: specific information
7. Comprehension checks
8. Guided language response
9. Assessment evidence
10. Misconceptions and feedback
11. Inclusion and accessibility
12. Resources and preparation
13. Optional extension

QUALITY CHECKS
Before finalising, confirm that the listening task genuinely requires comprehension, the spoken language is appropriate to the year group, the questions are answerable from the audio or supplied script, scaffolding supports rather than replaces listening, and assessment measures observable understanding.

OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
If [AUDIO / RESOURCES] is blank, create a teacher-generated listening script with clearly marked target-language items and [VERIFY] where language accuracy cannot be established from the supplied context. Do not claim that generated material is authentic.
```

### Example Input
Year 4 Spanish, 30 minutes, topic animals, pupils know numbers and simple likes/dislikes, objective is to understand short spoken descriptions of animals.

### Example Output Excerpt
A sequence beginning with picture prediction, followed by a gist listening, a second listening for animal and adjective information, evidence-based checking and a short paired response using familiar language.

## LS-02 Create a Target-Language Listening Script

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 700 to 1,000 words  
**Typical generation time:** 1 to 2 minutes  
**Curriculum tags:** MFL, listening, speaking, scripts, vocabulary, sentence patterns

### Prompt

```text
ROLE
You are a primary MFL materials specialist who writes accurate, age-appropriate spoken-language material for listening comprehension. Produce a teacher-generated listening script that pupils can understand through purposeful listening rather than through word-by-word translation.

CONTEXT
Target language: [TARGET LANGUAGE]
Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
Pupil age: [AGE]
Topic: [TOPIC]
Learning objective: [OBJECTIVE]
Prior language: [PRIOR LANGUAGE]
Required vocabulary: [VOCABULARY]
Required sentence structures: [SENTENCE STRUCTURES]
Grammar focus: [GRAMMAR]
Phonics/pronunciation focus: [PHONICS]
Length of listening: [LENGTH]
Difficulty level: [EASY / MODERATE / CHALLENGING]
Comprehension questions required: [NUMBER]
Access requirements: [ACCESS]

TASK
Write a short teacher-generated target-language listening script suitable for the specified pupils, together with a comprehension task and teacher notes explaining the intended listening demands.

REQUIREMENTS
1. Keep the script within the stated age, prior-knowledge and language-load limits.
2. Use the supplied vocabulary and structures naturally rather than inserting unnecessary novelty.
3. Give the script a clear communicative purpose, such as describing, informing, choosing, comparing or responding.
4. Include repetition of important language naturally, without making the script sound artificial.
5. Make the comprehension questions require pupils to identify meaning, relationships or specific information.
6. Include a gist question and more focused comprehension questions where appropriate.
7. Avoid making every answer depend on a single isolated keyword.
8. Do not use misleading English-style phonetic spellings.
9. Do not invent target-language vocabulary, grammar, gender, agreement, conjugation, dialect or pronunciation claims. Where accuracy cannot be established from supplied information, mark the item [VERIFY].
10. Do not present the script as an authentic quotation or authentic community voice unless a genuine source has been supplied.
11. Keep cultural references neutral and accurate. Do not imply that one person, region or community represents an entire language or culture.
12. Provide an English teacher gloss or answer key separately from the pupil-facing script when useful.
13. Include an accessibility version only where it preserves the listening construct, for example clearer chunking or visual support rather than replacing listening with written text.

OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Teacher purpose
2. Pupil-facing listening script
3. Key language
4. Listening comprehension questions
5. Answer key
6. Listening evidence pupils should identify
7. Optional second-pass questions
8. Accessibility supports
9. Teacher delivery notes
10. Accuracy and verification notes

QUALITY CHECKS
Check target-language accuracy against the information supplied, age appropriateness, natural spoken flow, manageable language density, meaningful comprehension demands and the distinction between teacher-generated material and authentic material.

OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add [NUMBER OF SPEAKERS], [DIALOGUE / MONOLOGUE], [PICTURE SUPPORT], [NO WRITTEN TRANSCRIPT], or [RECORDING LENGTH] if supplied.
```

### Example Input
Year 5 French, topic hobbies, 45-second teacher-read script, pupils know basic opinions and days of the week.

### Example Output Excerpt
A short teacher-generated description using familiar opinion structures, with one gist question and several specific-information questions that require pupils to connect spoken phrases with meaning.
