# MFL Family 10: Resources, Authentic Materials & Curriculum Implementation

This family provides copy/paste-ready workflows for selecting, creating, adapting and implementing MFL resources in a primary classroom. It focuses on resource quality, authenticity, accessibility, copyright awareness, curriculum alignment and practical classroom use.

The central principle is: **a resource is valuable when it improves language learning, not simply because it looks engaging or appears authentic**.

Where a language, source, quotation, cultural claim or current fact is uncertain, use `[VERIFY]`. Do not present generated material as an authentic source. Do not invent websites, publications, audio recordings, quotations, lyrics, community views, statistics, school procedures or current information.

## Family-wide operating principles

Every workflow in this family should:

- begin with the curriculum objective and intended MFL construct rather than with a resource
- distinguish teacher-created material, AI-generated material, adapted material and genuinely authentic material
- never label generated text, audio, dialogue, image or example as authentic unless its provenance is known
- use authentic materials purposefully rather than treating authenticity as an end in itself
- consider accessibility, age, language load, vocabulary density, cultural context and curriculum relevance
- protect target-language accuracy through verification
- never invent target-language vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation, quotations or source claims
- use `[TARGET LANGUAGE]` when the target language is not specified
- distinguish cultural information from stereotypes and tourist-style generalisations
- provide context for authentic cultural material without implying that one source represents an entire language community
- respect internal linguistic, regional, cultural and social diversity
- distinguish language, nationality, ethnicity, religion, geography and culture
- consider copyright, licensing and permitted classroom use where relevant
- avoid reproducing substantial copyrighted material when a summary, teacher-created alternative or short permitted extract would meet the objective
- distinguish a source's actual provenance from a generated classroom adaptation
- consider privacy when pupil names, images, recordings or identifiable information are involved
- avoid recommending external services or technologies as if they are approved by the school
- use supplied school policies and technology restrictions as controlling information
- flag current platform, copyright, safeguarding or statutory requirements for reliable verification where relevant
- make resource instructions practical for a real primary classroom
- include fallback options when technology, audio, printing or external resources are unavailable
- preserve the intended construct when adapting resources
- include assessment opportunities where appropriate
- support cumulative progression and reuse of resources rather than isolated activities

## RA-01 - Select Resources for a Primary MFL Learning Objective

**Difficulty:** Beginner  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 800-1,200 words  
**Typical generation time:** 30-50 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** Primary MFL, KS1, KS2, Resources, Curriculum Planning, Accessibility, Assessment

### ROLE

Act as an experienced primary MFL curriculum and resource specialist.

### CONTEXT

I need resources for:

- Year group: `[YEAR GROUP]`
- Target language: `[TARGET LANGUAGE]`
- Curriculum objective: `[CURRICULUM OBJECTIVE]`
- Intended MFL construct: `[INTENDED CONSTRUCT]`
- Topic/unit: `[TOPIC OR UNIT]`
- Key vocabulary/language chunks: `[VOCABULARY OR CHUNKS]`
- Grammar: `[GRAMMAR]`
- Main skill(s): `[LISTENING / SPEAKING / READING / WRITING / TRANSLATION / MEDIATION]`
- Prior learning: `[PRIOR LEARNING]`
- Available resources: `[AVAILABLE RESOURCES]`
- School restrictions: `[SCHOOL RESTRICTIONS]`

### TASK

Recommend a focused set of classroom resources that directly support the stated MFL objective.

### REQUIREMENTS

1. Start with the learning objective, not the resource type.
2. Identify the resource function and the construct it supports.
3. Recommend only resources that are proportionate to the objective.
4. Distinguish teacher-created, generated, adapted and authentic materials.
5. Identify accessibility considerations.
6. Identify target-language verification requirements.
7. Include a low-tech alternative where practical.
8. Include how the resource will be reused or embedded in progression.
9. Flag copyright, licensing or external-resource checks where relevant.
10. Do not invent specific external resources or claim that a resource exists unless it has been supplied or verified.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Provide:
1. Objective and construct
2. Resource-selection criteria
3. Recommended resource set
4. Purpose of each resource
5. Authenticity/provenance status
6. Accessibility notes
7. Language-verification points
8. Low-tech fallback
9. Curriculum/progression link
10. Teacher quality-control checklist

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check that every resource has a clear pedagogical purpose and that engagement, decoration or novelty is not treated as evidence of language-learning value.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Add `[NO-PRINT]`, `[LOW-TECH]`, `[MINIMAL-PREP]` or `[RESOURCE-LIGHT]` options.

### Example Input

Year 5 Spanish objective: pupils understand and use a small set of phrases for describing the weather. Available resources include a projector, mini-whiteboards and teacher-created picture cards.

### Example Output excerpt

Prioritise picture-supported listening, retrieval cards and short communicative tasks because they directly support understanding and use. Avoid adding unrelated cultural or decorative materials simply because they are visually attractive.
