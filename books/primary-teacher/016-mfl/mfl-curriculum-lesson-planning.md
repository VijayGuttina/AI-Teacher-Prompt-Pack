# MFL Curriculum & Lesson Planning

This family contains ten copy/paste-ready workflows for planning primary Modern Foreign Languages. They are designed to work with any specified target language rather than assuming French, Spanish or another language.

## MF-01 Plan an MFL Lesson From a Curriculum Objective

**Difficulty:** Beginner  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 700 to 1,000 words  
**Typical generation time:** 1 to 2 minutes  
**Curriculum tags:** MFL, KS2, lesson planning, curriculum objectives, listening, speaking, reading, writing

### Prompt

```text
ROLE
You are an experienced primary MFL curriculum and pedagogy specialist. Design a practical, age-appropriate language lesson that develops genuine language knowledge and communicative competence. Do not assume the target language unless it is supplied.

CONTEXT
Target language: [TARGET LANGUAGE]
Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
Pupil age: [AGE]
Curriculum objective: [CURRICULUM OBJECTIVE]
Prior language knowledge: [PRIOR KNOWLEDGE]
Topic or unit: [TOPIC]
Lesson duration: [DURATION]
Main language skill(s): [LISTENING / SPEAKING / READING / WRITING]
Grammar focus, if any: [GRAMMAR FOCUS]
Phonics/pronunciation focus, if any: [PHONICS OR PRONUNCIATION FOCUS]
Available resources: [RESOURCES]
SEND/EAL/access requirements: [REQUIREMENTS]
Assessment purpose: [ASSESSMENT PURPOSE]

TASK
Create one complete classroom-ready MFL lesson that clearly connects prior language, new input, guided practice, communicative use, retrieval and assessment.

REQUIREMENTS
1. State the learning intention in teacher-friendly language and give a pupil-friendly version.
2. Identify the essential vocabulary, chunks and grammar. Keep the language load realistic for the year group.
3. Include an appropriate target-language input stage and explain the scaffolding required.
4. Include explicit modelling before independent or paired production.
5. Include at least one meaningful communicative task rather than vocabulary repetition alone.
6. Build in retrieval of previously learned language.
7. Include a short assessment opportunity that measures the stated language objective.
8. Give likely misconceptions or language errors and precise corrective responses.
9. Include inclusion adaptations that improve access without unnecessarily lowering the language-learning ambition.
10. Do not invent school-specific curriculum requirements, policies or procedures.
11. If target-language accuracy depends on information not supplied, mark the item [VERIFY] rather than inventing it.
12. Do not use misleading English-style phonetic spellings that could fossilise pronunciation errors.

OUTPUT FORMAT
Use these headings:
1. Lesson overview
2. Learning intention and success criteria
3. Prior-language retrieval
4. New language
5. Teacher modelling
6. Guided practice
7. Communicative task
8. Independent application
9. Assessment
10. Misconceptions and feedback
11. Inclusion and adaptive teaching
12. Resources
13. Teacher preparation
14. Optional extension

QUALITY CHECKS
Before finalising, check that the lesson is age-appropriate, target-language accurate where specified, coherent with the objective, communicative rather than activity-led, manageable within the stated time, inclusive, and assessable through observable language evidence.

OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
If [TARGET LANGUAGE] is blank, keep language-specific examples as placeholders. If the teacher supplies a scheme of work or school curriculum sequence, align to it without inventing missing requirements.
```

### Example Input
Year 4 Spanish, 30 minutes, pupils know greetings and numbers, objective is to ask and answer age, with speaking and listening emphasis.

### Example Output Excerpt
A concise sequence linking retrieval of greetings, teacher modelling of the age question and answer, supported pair exchanges, a short listening discrimination task and an exit check using the target sentence pattern.

## MF-02 Sequence a Primary MFL Unit

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 1,000 to 1,500 words  
**Typical generation time:** 2 to 3 minutes  
**Curriculum tags:** MFL, KS2, curriculum sequencing, progression, unit planning, retrieval

### Prompt

```text
ROLE
You are a primary MFL curriculum designer specialising in cumulative language progression. Build a coherent unit in which pupils repeatedly encounter, retrieve, combine and apply language rather than learning disconnected vocabulary lists.

CONTEXT
Target language: [TARGET LANGUAGE]
Year group(s): [YEAR GROUP]
Unit topic: [TOPIC]
Number of lessons: [NUMBER]
Lesson length: [DURATION]
Starting knowledge: [PRIOR KNOWLEDGE]
Required curriculum objectives: [OBJECTIVES]
Vocabulary available or required: [VOCABULARY]
Grammar focus: [GRAMMAR]
Phonics/pronunciation focus: [PHONICS]
Language skills to prioritise: [SKILLS]
Cultural/intercultural element: [CULTURAL ELEMENT]
Assessment requirements: [ASSESSMENT]
SEND/EAL/access requirements: [ACCESS]

TASK
Create a progressive unit sequence from prior language to independent application.

REQUIREMENTS
1. Define the end-of-unit language outcomes in observable terms.
2. Sequence vocabulary, chunks, grammar and phonics so later learning depends meaningfully on earlier learning.
3. Show retrieval opportunities in every lesson.
4. Balance listening, speaking, reading and writing according to developmental appropriateness and the stated objectives.
5. Include a gradual increase in sentence complexity and independence.
6. Identify where target-language exposure should increase and where English scaffolding may be useful.
7. Include formative assessment and opportunities to revisit misconceptions.
8. Include a clear end-of-unit assessment plan.
9. Include a manageable cultural or intercultural dimension without stereotypes or tokenistic activities.
10. Distinguish language, nationality, ethnicity, region and culture.
11. Do not invent statutory or school-specific curriculum requirements.
12. Flag any language-specific item requiring verification.

OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
- unit rationale
- end-of-unit outcomes
- progression map
- lesson-by-lesson sequence
- vocabulary and chunk progression
- grammar progression
- phonics/pronunciation progression
- four-skills balance
- retrieval plan
- misconception plan
- assessment plan
- inclusion plan
- cultural/intercultural opportunities
- teacher preparation checklist

QUALITY CHECKS
Check cumulative progression, manageable language load, accurate target-language examples, meaningful reuse, balanced skills, observable assessment and realistic lesson timing.

OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add [SCHOOL SCHEME], [LOCAL CURRICULUM], [TEXTBOOK], or [DIGITAL PLATFORM] only if supplied by the teacher.
```

### Example Input
Year 5 French, six lessons, topic food and preferences, pupils know basic opinions and common classroom instructions.

### Example Output Excerpt
A six-lesson progression that reuses familiar opinion structures, introduces food vocabulary through listening and reading, develops sentence combinations, and culminates in a short supported spoken and written task.

## MF-03 Design an MFL Lesson Around a Language Skill

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 800 to 1,200 words  
**Typical generation time:** 2 minutes  
**Curriculum tags:** MFL, listening, speaking, reading, writing, skill development

### Prompt

```text
ROLE
You are a primary MFL pedagogy specialist. Design a lesson where the named language skill is the genuine learning construct, while supporting skills are used purposefully.

CONTEXT
Target language: [TARGET LANGUAGE]
Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
Primary skill: [LISTENING / SPEAKING / READING / WRITING]
Supporting skill(s): [SUPPORTING SKILLS]
Topic: [TOPIC]
Prior language: [PRIOR LANGUAGE]
New language: [NEW LANGUAGE]
Curriculum objective: [OBJECTIVE]
Duration: [DURATION]
Access needs: [ACCESS NEEDS]

TASK
Design a complete lesson that teaches and assesses the selected language skill rather than merely attaching a skill label to a generic activity.

REQUIREMENTS
1. Define precisely what pupils must understand or produce.
2. Select language appropriate to the year group and prior knowledge.
3. Include teacher modelling and guided practice.
4. Make the progression from supported to less-supported performance explicit.
5. Ensure the assessment actually samples the primary skill.
6. Include likely errors and corrective feedback.
7. Provide access adaptations without changing the intended construct unnecessarily.
8. If using listening, distinguish listening comprehension from simply repeating what was heard.
9. If using speaking, give a communicative purpose and avoid assessing personality or willingness to perform.
10. If using reading, ensure the text supports the intended comprehension demand.
11. If using writing, distinguish language knowledge from handwriting or transcription difficulty.
12. Flag uncertain language-specific details for verification.

OUTPUT FORMAT
Use: objective, language focus, modelling, guided practice, independent application, assessment evidence, misconceptions, feedback, inclusion, resources, timing.

QUALITY CHECKS
Confirm that the primary skill is genuinely assessed, the language demand is appropriate and the activities support rather than obscure the objective.

OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Specify [PAIR WORK], [INDIVIDUAL WORK], [NO PRINTING], [LOW-TECH], or [DIGITAL] constraints.
```

### Example Input
Year 3 German listening lesson on classroom objects, 25 minutes, pupils know greetings and numbers.

### Example Output Excerpt
A short sequence using picture-supported listening discrimination, repeated teacher modelling, a matching task and an exit check that tests comprehension rather than performance confidence.


## Family Quality-Control Checklist

Before releasing any MF workflow, confirm:

- The target language is explicit or represented by `[TARGET LANGUAGE]`.
- No French-only assumption has been introduced unless requested.
- Language-specific examples are accurate or clearly marked for verification.
- Vocabulary is meaningful, age-appropriate and reusable.
- Grammar is accurate and connected to communication.
- Phonics and pronunciation guidance does not rely on misleading English phonetic respelling.
- Listening, speaking, reading and writing are assessed only when they are genuinely the intended constructs.
- Target-language exposure is purposeful and scaffolded.
- Retrieval supports durable language learning and later application.
- Cultural content does not stereotype or equate language with nationality or ethnicity.
- Assessment does not reward accent conformity, confidence, personality or willingness to perform.
- Inclusion adaptations preserve the intended language-learning construct.
- School-specific curriculum, policy and procedure are never invented.
- Authentic sources are not fabricated.
- Every prompt remains usable as a direct copy/paste workflow.
