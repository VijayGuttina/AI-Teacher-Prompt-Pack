# 016 MFL Framework

## Purpose

This framework defines the architecture for the Primary Teacher Prompt Pack: Modern Foreign Languages (MFL). It is designed for teachers planning, teaching, assessing and improving language learning in primary settings, particularly KS2, while remaining adaptable to school-selected language(s), local curriculum requirements and different models of language provision.

The framework treats MFL as a subject in which pupils progressively develop usable language knowledge and communicative competence through listening, speaking, reading and writing, supported by phonics, pronunciation, vocabulary, grammar, retrieval, interaction, translation and intercultural understanding.

The prompts must help teachers produce practical classroom-ready material without inventing school-specific curriculum requirements, current language facts, cultural claims, quotations, texts or pronunciation details.

## Core Principles

1. **Language before performance**: prioritise meaningful language learning rather than entertainment or performance for its own sake.
2. **Communicative purpose**: language should be used to understand, express, interact and respond.
3. **Four-language-skills integration**: listening, speaking, reading and writing should develop coherently rather than as isolated activities.
4. **Phonics and pronunciation matter**: establish reliable sound-spelling relationships and intelligible pronunciation early.
5. **Retrieval and reuse**: previously learned language should recur in meaningful contexts.
6. **High-frequency language and chunks**: teach reusable words, phrases and sentence patterns alongside individual vocabulary.
7. **Grammar supports communication**: grammatical knowledge should help pupils understand and construct meaningful language.
8. **Progression is cumulative**: new language should connect to prior learning and increase in complexity and independence.
9. **Target-language exposure with scaffolding**: use the target language purposefully, with support appropriate to pupil age, prior knowledge and task demands.
10. **Meaning before literalism**: distinguish literal translation from contextual meaning where relevant.
11. **Authenticity with accessibility**: authentic language and cultural materials should be accurate, purposeful and age-appropriate.
12. **Culture without stereotyping**: distinguish language, nationality, ethnicity, region and culture, and represent internal diversity.
13. **Intercultural understanding is not cultural conformity**: pupils should explore perspectives and contexts without being expected to adopt or perform a cultural identity.
14. **Evidence over invention**: do not fabricate vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation, quotations, lyrics, cultural practices or community views.
15. **Assessment measures learning**: assess observable language knowledge and communicative competence, not accent preference, personality or willingness to perform.
16. **Inclusion preserves the construct**: adaptations should improve access without unnecessarily reducing the intended language-learning demand.
17. **Safe uncertainty**: where language, syllabus, school policy or current information is unspecified, identify the missing variable and use a clearly stated adaptable assumption rather than inventing facts.
18. **Teacher judgement remains central**: generated material supports professional planning and does not replace teacher knowledge, curriculum requirements or safeguarding procedures.

## Standard Language-Learning Cycle

**Prior language → Input → Notice → Model → Guided practice → Retrieval → Communicative use → Feedback → Reuse/transfer → Assessment**

Prompts should normally make the relationship between input, practice and later independent use explicit.

## MFL Progression Model

**Hear/Notice → Recognise → Recall → Pronounce → Produce → Combine → Understand → Interact → Read/Write → Apply/Adapt → Reflect**

Progression may involve increasing:
- vocabulary breadth and retrieval fluency
- sentence length and flexibility
- grammatical control
- listening discrimination
- pronunciation and intelligibility
- spontaneous or supported interaction
- reading comprehension
- writing independence
- translation and mediation complexity
- ability to adapt known language to new contexts
- intercultural understanding and comparison

Progression must not be represented as simply learning more words. It should reflect increasing control, comprehension, flexibility and independence.

## Quality Gates

Every MFL workflow must pass the following quality gates:

### 1. Curriculum Alignment
- Supports the stated curriculum objective or clearly identifies where local curriculum information is missing.
- Does not invent statutory, school or trust requirements.
- Distinguishes general good practice from local curriculum expectations.

### 2. Target-Language Accuracy
- Vocabulary is checked for meaning, spelling, grammatical gender where relevant, register and context.
- Grammar examples are accurate for the specified language.
- Target-language output is not presented as verified when the model lacks sufficient information.

### 3. Pronunciation and Phonics
- Pronunciation guidance is accurate for the specified language and dialect/context where relevant.
- Phonetic support is used cautiously and does not create misleading English-style pronunciations.
- IPA or other notation is used only when useful and appropriate.
- Intelligibility is prioritised over accent imitation.

### 4. Vocabulary and Chunks
- Vocabulary is age-appropriate, useful and retrievable.
- High-frequency words and reusable chunks are prioritised where suitable.
- Synonyms, register differences and false friends are handled accurately.
- Vocabulary is not selected solely because it appears culturally interesting.

### 5. Grammar and Sentence Construction
- Grammar is sequenced from prior knowledge.
- Examples are accurate and meaningful.
- Error correction identifies the relevant language feature rather than merely marking answers wrong.
- Grammar terminology is appropriate to pupil age and teacher need.

### 6. Listening and Speaking
- Listening tasks assess comprehension rather than simply hearing target language.
- Speaking tasks have a genuine communicative purpose.
- Interaction includes manageable scaffolding and opportunities for reuse.
- Accent or pronunciation is not treated as a measure of cultural identity.

### 7. Reading, Writing, Translation and Mediation
- Reading and writing tasks preserve the intended construct.
- Translation distinguishes source meaning from literal word-for-word substitution.
- Mediation tasks are age-appropriate and explain what pupils are expected to convey.
- Literacy transfer is used deliberately rather than assuming English and target-language structures are identical.

### 8. Culture and Intercultural Understanding
- Cultural claims are accurate, contextualised and appropriately qualified.
- Religion, nationality, ethnicity, language and culture are not conflated.
- Regional and internal diversity is acknowledged where relevant.
- Avoids stereotypes, caricatures, tokenism and treating one person or region as representative of an entire language community.

### 9. Authentic Materials
- Authentic texts, audio, video, images and online resources are age-appropriate and relevant to the learning goal.
- Quotations, lyrics, transcripts and factual claims are not invented.
- Where current or source-specific information matters, the prompt instructs the user to supply or verify the source.
- Copyright, attribution and school resource rules are respected.

### 10. Assessment Validity
- Assessment targets observable learning such as vocabulary recall, comprehension, pronunciation intelligibility, grammatical control, sentence formation, interaction, reading, writing, translation or mediation.
- Does not assess accent conformity, personality, confidence, willingness to perform or cultural conformity.
- Feedback is specific, actionable and linked to the intended language construct.

### 11. Inclusion
- Considers reading age, language load, visual and aural access, speech and hearing needs, writing access, processing time, memory demands and response mode.
- Provides scaffolds without automatically lowering linguistic ambition.
- Allows alternative participation routes where appropriate.
- Does not assume all pupils can or should pronounce, hear, write or perform in the same way.

### 12. Safe Digital and Classroom Practice
- Digital activities do not require pupils to disclose unnecessary personal information.
- Online or authentic resources are framed for safe classroom use.
- Prompts do not invent school-specific device, filtering, safeguarding or acceptable-use arrangements.
- Use supplied school procedures where they exist.

## Language Accuracy Controls

MFL prompts must explicitly protect against common generative errors:

- Do not invent vocabulary because a word appears plausible.
- Do not assume English pronunciation rules apply to the target language.
- Do not create misleading phonetic respellings that may fossilise errors.
- Do not fabricate etymologies, regional usage or dialect claims.
- Do not invent gender, plural, conjugation or agreement rules.
- Do not invent quotations, song lyrics or authentic conversations and label them as authentic.
- Do not present model-generated language as a verified source when accuracy is critical.
- Where a precise language fact is uncertain, flag it for verification or ask the teacher to supply the authoritative source.

## Target-Language Use Model

Target-language exposure should be purposeful. Prompts may specify:
- teacher input
- classroom instructions
- model dialogues
- retrieval questions
- listening scripts
- sentence stems
- pair interaction
- role-play where appropriate
- reading texts
- writing frames
- translation or mediation tasks

Scaffolding may include images, gestures, familiar language, sentence stems, glossaries, models, repetition, dual coding or carefully chosen English support. Scaffolding should be reduced where pupils demonstrate sufficient independence.

## Assessment Model

Assessment should distinguish among:
- vocabulary knowledge
- listening comprehension
- speaking and interaction
- pronunciation intelligibility
- phonics knowledge
- grammatical knowledge
- sentence construction
- reading comprehension
- writing control
- translation
- mediation
- language adaptation and transfer
- retrieval fluency
- progression over time

Assessment should use observable evidence. It must not require pupils to reveal personal identity, family language background, cultural affiliation or private experiences.

## Inclusion and Adaptive Teaching Model

Adaptations may address:
- reading age
- vocabulary density
- visual accessibility
- hearing and listening access
- speech and communication needs
- writing or motor demands
- processing time
- memory and retrieval load
- task complexity
- response mode
- grouping and participation
- prior language knowledge
- misconceptions
- sensory load

The intended construct should remain visible. For example, a pupil may demonstrate vocabulary knowledge through supported oral, written, visual or alternative response routes where appropriate, without changing the underlying vocabulary objective.

For pupils with SEND or EAL needs, prompts should avoid assuming that difficulty with English automatically means difficulty with the target language, and should distinguish language-learning barriers from access barriers.

## Culture and Intercultural Model

Cultural learning should answer questions such as:
- What is being observed?
- What evidence supports this understanding?
- What context matters?
- Whose perspective is represented?
- Is the example regional, generational, historical or contemporary?
- What diversity exists within the relevant community?
- How might this compare with pupils' own contexts without implying superiority or deficiency?

Avoid activities that ask pupils to impersonate a nationality or treat stereotypical behaviours as authentic cultural identity.

## Resource and Authentic-Material Controls

When creating or recommending resources, prompts should specify:
- target language
- age suitability
- learning purpose
- authenticity status
- source or provenance where supplied
- copyright/attribution considerations
- accessibility requirements
- whether the material is teacher-created, adapted or authentic

If a requested authentic source is not supplied, the prompt should produce a clearly labelled teacher-created alternative rather than falsely presenting generated content as authentic.

## Missing Information Protocol

When critical information is missing, prompts should not silently fabricate it.

Use this order:
1. Identify the missing variable.
2. State why it matters.
3. Use an adaptable placeholder or clearly labelled assumption where possible.
4. Separate generic guidance from language-specific or school-specific claims.
5. Ask the teacher to verify authoritative local information where necessary.

Typical missing variables include target language, school curriculum sequence, pronunciation convention, local assessment framework, available technology and school-specific resource or safeguarding arrangements.

## Verification Model

MFL will use the same release discipline as the other 100-workflow subject modules:

- **100/100 structural QA**: every workflow must contain the required architecture, metadata, examples and MFL-specific quality controls.
- **Exactly 20 representative in-model executions**: 2 workflows from each of the 10 families.
- **16 targeted risk checks** focused on MFL-specific failure modes.
- **Verification and freeze record** only after structural, representative and targeted checks pass.

Representative execution testing verifies that prompts actually generate useful, accurate, classroom-ready outputs. Structural completeness alone does not constitute execution verification.
