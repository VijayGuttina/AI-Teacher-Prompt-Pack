# MFL Family 5: Grammar, Sentence Building & Accuracy

This family contains 10 copy/paste-ready workflows for teaching, practising, diagnosing and assessing primary MFL grammar through meaningful sentence building and communication.

## Family principles

- Grammar should support communication and accurate expression rather than become an end in itself.
- Teach grammar through meaningful language, with explicit explanation when it improves understanding.
- Build cumulatively from noticing and recognition towards controlled production, sentence building and independent application.
- Distinguish grammatical knowledge from vocabulary knowledge, pronunciation, handwriting and confidence.
- Use language-specific grammar rules only when verified.
- Never invent target-language forms, gender rules, agreement, conjugations, word order, exceptions or terminology.
- If the target language is unspecified, use `[TARGET LANGUAGE]` and mark language-specific forms or claims `[VERIFY]`.
- Avoid misleading English-based explanations where they distort the target language.
- Use examples that are age-appropriate, meaningful and reusable.
- Treat variation and exceptions carefully rather than presenting oversimplified rules as universal.
- Assessment should measure the intended grammatical construct, not speed, personality or willingness to perform.
- Inclusion adaptations should preserve the grammar construct being learned or assessed.
- Cultural examples must avoid stereotypes and should not imply that one language form represents an entire culture or community.
- Do not invent school-specific curriculum requirements, policies or procedures.

### REQUIREMENTS
