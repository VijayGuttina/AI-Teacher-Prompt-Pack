# Phonics, Pronunciation & Spelling

This family contains ten copy/paste-ready workflows for teaching primary MFL phonics, pronunciation and spelling. The workflows connect sound, spelling and meaning while protecting pupils from inaccurate modelling and pronunciation fossilisation. They are designed for any specified target language and do not assume one language system unless supplied.

## PP-01 Plan an MFL Phonics Lesson

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 800 to 1,200 words  
**Typical generation time:** 2 to 3 minutes  
**Curriculum tags:** MFL, phonics, pronunciation, spelling, KS2, lesson planning

### Prompt

```text
ROLE
You are a primary MFL phonics specialist. Design a classroom-ready phonics lesson that helps pupils notice and use reliable sound-spelling relationships in the target language. Prioritise accurate modelling, listening discrimination, intelligibility and meaningful language use.

CONTEXT
Target language: [TARGET LANGUAGE]
Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
Pupil age: [AGE]
Phonics focus: [SOUND / SOUND-SPELLING RELATIONSHIP]
Target graphemes: [GRAPHEMES]
Target words or phrases: [WORDS / PHRASES]
Prior phonics knowledge: [PRIOR KNOWLEDGE]
Vocabulary meaning: [MEANINGS]
Curriculum objective: [OBJECTIVE]
Lesson duration: [DURATION]
Resources: [RESOURCES]
Access requirements: [ACCESS]

TASK
Create one complete MFL phonics lesson moving from listening and noticing to guided decoding or encoding and then meaningful spoken language use.

REQUIREMENTS
1. State the phonics learning intention precisely.
2. Explain the sound-spelling relationship in teacher-friendly language.
3. Use only language forms supplied or reliably established. Mark uncertain details [VERIFY].
4. Begin with listening and noticing before expecting extended production.
5. Include accurate teacher modelling.
6. Include discrimination between the target sound and at least one relevant contrast where appropriate.
7. Link sound and spelling to meaning rather than treating phonics as isolated sound drills.
8. Include controlled practice followed by meaningful application.
9. Avoid misleading English-style phonetic respellings.
10. Do not invent pronunciation rules, dialect claims or exceptions.
11. Include common pronunciation and spelling errors with precise corrective feedback.
12. Include access adaptations that preserve the intended phonics construct.
13. Do not treat accent conformity as the learning objective. Focus on accurate, intelligible target-language production.

OUTPUT FORMAT
Use:
1. Lesson overview
2. Learning intention
3. Prior phonics retrieval
4. Sound and spelling focus
5. Teacher modelling
6. Listening discrimination
7. Guided decoding or encoding
8. Meaningful language application
9. Assessment
10. Misconceptions and feedback
11. Inclusion
12. Resources and preparation
13. Optional extension

QUALITY CHECKS
Check phonics accuracy, target-language accuracy, intelligibility, sound-spelling consistency, age appropriateness, manageable cognitive load and genuine connection between phonics and language meaning.

OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add [WORD SET], [MINIMAL PAIRS], [PICTURE SUPPORT], [NO PRINTING], [LOW-TECH], or [DIGITAL].
```

### Example Input
Year 5 Spanish, focus on a supplied sound-spelling pattern in familiar classroom vocabulary.

### Example Output Excerpt
A short lesson beginning with sound discrimination, followed by teacher modelling, word sorting, supported pronunciation and a final sentence-level task using the target words meaningfully.
