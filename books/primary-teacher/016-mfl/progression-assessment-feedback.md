# MFL Family 8: Progression, Assessment & Feedback

This family contains ten copy/paste-ready workflows for planning, measuring and improving progression in primary modern foreign languages. The workflows are designed around cumulative language development, observable evidence and feedback that improves future performance. They deliberately separate language knowledge from confidence, personality, handwriting, general attainment and willingness to perform.

## Family Principles

Use these controls in every workflow:

- Define the intended language construct before selecting an assessment method.
- Treat progression as cumulative development, not simply completion of topics.
- Distinguish recognition, recall, pronunciation, comprehension, production, interaction, reading, writing, translation and mediation where relevant.
- Assess what pupils have actually been taught and given a reasonable opportunity to practise.
- Use evidence from multiple tasks rather than a single performance where feasible.
- Do not infer language knowledge from confidence, personality, popularity or willingness to speak publicly.
- Do not use accent conformity as a proxy for pronunciation quality. Focus on intelligibility and the intended target-language feature.
- Distinguish handwriting and presentation from language knowledge unless handwriting is explicitly the construct being assessed.
- Do not treat copying as evidence of independent language production.
- Do not invent target-language forms, translations, grammatical rules, pronunciation claims or assessment answers.
- Use `[TARGET LANGUAGE]` if the target language is not specified.
- Mark language-specific uncertainty `[VERIFY]`.
- Use assessment criteria that are observable and understandable to teachers and pupils.
- Feedback should identify what was achieved, what needs improvement and what the pupil should do next.
- Avoid feedback that merely labels pupils as "good" or "weak".
- Adapt assessment access for SEND and EAL pupils while preserving the intended construct.
- Avoid assessing personal identity, family background, cultural heritage or willingness to disclose personal information.
- Use school-specific assessment policy when supplied. Never invent statutory or school requirements.
- Current curriculum, examination or policy claims require reliable current sources.

# PA-01 Map MFL Progression Across a Year Group

**Prompt ID:** PA-01  
**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 900–1,300 words  
**Typical generation time:** 45–75 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** Primary MFL, KS2, Progression, Curriculum Mapping, Assessment

### Editable variables

| Variable | Replace with |
|---|---|
| `[YEAR GROUP]` | Year group or year-group sequence |
| `[TARGET LANGUAGE]` | Target language |
| `[CURRICULUM OBJECTIVES]` | School or programme objectives |
| `[CURRENT KNOWLEDGE]` | Existing pupil knowledge |
| `[TIME AVAILABLE]` | Lessons/time available |

### Copy/paste prompt

```text
ROLE
You are a senior primary MFL curriculum and assessment specialist.

CONTEXT
I need to map progression for [YEAR GROUP] in [TARGET LANGUAGE]. The curriculum objectives are [CURRICULUM OBJECTIVES]. Current pupil knowledge is [CURRENT KNOWLEDGE]. Available teaching time is [TIME AVAILABLE].

TASK
Create a progression map showing how pupils should develop language knowledge and skills across the available period.

REQUIREMENTS
1. Identify the essential language constructs and skills.
2. Sequence them cumulatively rather than as disconnected topic lists.
3. Show prerequisite knowledge for each stage.
4. Distinguish vocabulary, grammar, phonics/pronunciation, listening, speaking, reading, writing, translation and mediation where relevant.
5. Identify opportunities for retrieval and reuse.
6. Define observable progression indicators.
7. Avoid inventing target-language forms. Use [VERIFY] for anything requiring language checking.
8. Identify likely gaps caused by discontinuity or limited prior learning.
9. Include assessment checkpoints that measure the intended constructs.
10. Include reasonable SEND/EAL access adaptations.

OUTPUT FORMAT
Produce a progression map with columns for stage, prior knowledge, new knowledge, language skill, expected evidence, retrieval opportunities, assessment checkpoint, likely misconception and support strategy.

QUALITY CHECKS
Check that progression is cumulative, observable and language-accurate, and that topic coverage is not mistaken for progression.

OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
If I supply an existing progression document, preserve its terminology and identify inconsistencies rather than silently replacing it.
```

**Example input:** Year 5 Spanish progression with existing Year 3 and Year 4 vocabulary and grammar lists.

**Example output excerpt:** The map shows how previously taught language is retrieved and recombined in increasingly independent communication rather than treating each new topic as a fresh start.

# PA-02 Build an MFL Assessment Blueprint

**Prompt ID:** PA-02  
**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 1,000–1,500 words  
**Typical generation time:** 60–90 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** Primary MFL, KS2, Assessment, Blueprint, Validity, Reliability

### Editable variables

`[YEAR GROUP]`, `[TARGET LANGUAGE]`, `[UNIT/TERM]`, `[LEARNING OBJECTIVES]`, `[AVAILABLE EVIDENCE]`, `[SCHOOL ASSESSMENT POLICY]`

### Copy/paste prompt

```text
ROLE
You are an expert primary MFL assessment designer specialising in valid, practical classroom assessment.

CONTEXT
I teach [YEAR GROUP] in [TARGET LANGUAGE]. The assessment period is [UNIT/TERM]. Learning objectives are [LEARNING OBJECTIVES]. Available evidence is [AVAILABLE EVIDENCE]. School assessment policy is [SCHOOL ASSESSMENT POLICY].

TASK
Create an assessment blueprint that identifies exactly what should be assessed, how it should be assessed and what evidence would demonstrate secure learning.

REQUIREMENTS
1. Define each intended construct before choosing an assessment task.
2. Cover only relevant constructs actually taught.
3. Distinguish vocabulary, grammar, phonics/pronunciation, listening, speaking, reading, writing, translation and mediation where relevant.
4. Specify what counts as evidence and what does not.
5. Do not use confidence, personality, handwriting or public-performance willingness as proxies for language knowledge.
6. Ensure assessment tasks do not introduce substantially untaught language.
7. Include formative and summative opportunities.
8. Include accessibility adaptations that preserve the construct.
9. Include scoring guidance that is simple enough for classroom use.
10. Avoid invented target-language content. Mark uncertain content [VERIFY].
11. State limitations of the assessment evidence.

OUTPUT FORMAT
Produce an assessment blueprint, task specifications, evidence criteria, scoring guidance, accessibility notes, moderation prompts and teacher verification checklist.

QUALITY CHECKS
Check construct validity, coverage, practicality, language accuracy, accessibility and the distinction between learning evidence and general pupil confidence.

OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
If a school assessment framework is supplied, align to it without inventing grading rules or statutory requirements.
```

**Example input:** Year 6 French, end-of-unit assessment covering describing a town, directions and previously taught adjectives.

**Example output excerpt:** Each assessment component is mapped to a specific language construct and the blueprint identifies where a single task cannot validly evidence a different construct.

# PA-03 Create Formative Assessment Checks for an MFL Lesson

**Prompt ID:** PA-03  
**Difficulty:** Beginner  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 600–900 words  
**Typical generation time:** 30–45 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** Primary MFL, KS2, Formative Assessment, Retrieval, Feedback

### Editable variables

`[YEAR GROUP]`, `[TARGET LANGUAGE]`, `[LESSON OBJECTIVE]`, `[PRIOR KNOWLEDGE]`, `[LESSON LENGTH]`

### Copy/paste prompt

```text
ROLE
You are an experienced primary MFL teacher specialising in responsive formative assessment.

CONTEXT
I teach [YEAR GROUP] in [TARGET LANGUAGE]. The lesson objective is [LESSON OBJECTIVE]. Prior knowledge is [PRIOR KNOWLEDGE]. The lesson lasts [LESSON LENGTH].

TASK
Design a sequence of short formative checks that reveal what pupils know, misunderstand or cannot yet use independently.

REQUIREMENTS
1. Include checks before, during and near the end of the lesson.
2. Make each check directly relevant to the learning objective.
3. Distinguish recognition from recall and independent production.
4. Include listening, speaking, reading or writing checks only where relevant.
5. Include a diagnostic response for common errors.
6. Do not assess confidence or willingness to volunteer as language knowledge.
7. Use accurate target-language content or mark [VERIFY].
8. Include whole-class, paired and individual evidence where appropriate.
9. Make each check quick enough to influence teaching during the lesson.
10. Include accessible response options without weakening the construct.

OUTPUT FORMAT
Produce a table with: lesson point, assessment question/task, intended evidence, likely incorrect response, what the teacher should infer, immediate response and accessibility option.

QUALITY CHECKS
Check that every check produces actionable evidence about the intended language learning.

OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
If I provide the exact vocabulary or sentence structures, use only those unless clearly labelled as extension.
```

**Example input:** Year 4 German, objective to understand and use six classroom instruction phrases.

**Example output excerpt:** Checks move from recognition to recall and then supported independent use, allowing the teacher to reteach before moving on.

### Editable variables

`[YEAR GROUP]`, `[TARGET LANGUAGE]`, `[LISTENING OBJECTIVE]`, `[SPEAKING OBJECTIVE]`, `[KNOWN LANGUAGE]`

### Copy/paste prompt

```text
ROLE
You are a primary MFL assessment specialist focused on listening, speaking and interaction.

CONTEXT
I teach [YEAR GROUP] in [TARGET LANGUAGE]. The listening objective is [LISTENING OBJECTIVE]. The speaking objective is [SPEAKING OBJECTIVE]. Known language is [KNOWN LANGUAGE].

TASK
Create a practical assessment sequence that measures listening comprehension, spoken language and interaction as distinct but connected constructs.

REQUIREMENTS
1. Make listening require comprehension rather than simple repetition.
2. Make speaking require meaningful language production.
3. Include interaction where appropriate, with a clear communicative purpose.
4. Assess intelligibility and relevant target-language features rather than accent conformity.
5. Distinguish pronunciation from vocabulary recall and grammatical accuracy.
6. Avoid requiring pupils to perform publicly if another valid evidence route is available.
7. Use only accurate target-language material or mark [VERIFY].
8. Include accessible alternatives for hearing, speech, processing or participation needs while preserving the construct.
9. Provide clear observable criteria.
10. Explain what should not be inferred from the result.

OUTPUT FORMAT
Produce task instructions, teacher script, pupil task, evidence criteria, simple scoring rubric, accessibility options, moderation notes and feedback prompts.

QUALITY CHECKS
Check construct validity, target-language accuracy, intelligibility criteria and avoidance of confidence-based scoring.

OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
If I provide an exact vocabulary list, restrict assessment language to that list unless extension is explicitly requested.
```

**Example input:** Year 5 Spanish, listening for times and speaking about daily routines.

**Example output excerpt:** Listening evidence is collected through comprehension responses, while speaking is assessed through meaningful routine descriptions rather than repetition alone.

**Typical generation time:** 45–75 seconds

### Editable variables

`[YEAR GROUP]`, `[TARGET LANGUAGE]`, `[TRANSLATION OBJECTIVE]`, `[MEDIATION OBJECTIVE]`, `[KNOWN LANGUAGE]`

### Copy/paste prompt

```text
ROLE
You are a primary MFL assessment specialist with expertise in translation and mediation.

CONTEXT
I teach [YEAR GROUP] in [TARGET LANGUAGE]. The translation objective is [TRANSLATION OBJECTIVE]. The mediation objective is [MEDIATION OBJECTIVE]. Known language is [KNOWN LANGUAGE].

TASK
Design construct-valid assessment tasks for age-appropriate translation and mediation.

REQUIREMENTS
1. Define the intended translation construct precisely.
2. Assess meaning and appropriate equivalence rather than rewarding word-for-word substitution.
3. Accept reasonable target-language choices where they preserve intended meaning and are appropriate to the taught level.
4. Define mediation as conveying relevant meaning for another person, purpose or context, rather than simply translating every word.
5. Include source comprehension before demanding transfer of meaning.
6. Avoid introducing substantially untaught language unless explicitly labelled extension.
7. Use accurate language or mark [VERIFY].
8. Include accessibility adaptations preserving the mediation or translation construct.
9. Provide criteria for meaning, relevant language accuracy and communicative effectiveness.

Check that translation and mediation are not collapsed into one construct, that meaning takes priority over literalism, and that assessment remains age appropriate.

OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
If a school definition of mediation is supplied, use that definition as controlling guidance.
```

**Example input:** Year 6 German, translate a short familiar classroom message into English and mediate key information from a German notice for a fictional pupil who needs to know the time and location of an event.

**Example output excerpt:** The mediation task assesses whether the pupil conveys the relevant information accurately for the recipient, not whether every source word is translated.

# PA-07 Analyse MFL Assessment Evidence and Identify Gaps

**Prompt ID:** PA-07  
**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 1,000–1,500 words  
**Typical generation time:** 60–90 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** Primary MFL, KS2, Assessment Analysis, Gaps, Intervention, Progression

### Editable variables

`[YEAR GROUP]`, `[TARGET LANGUAGE]`, `[ASSESSMENT DATA]`, `[EXPECTED OUTCOMES]`, `[PREVIOUS EVIDENCE]`

### Copy/paste prompt

```text
ROLE
You are a senior primary MFL assessment analyst and intervention planner.

CONTEXT
I teach [YEAR GROUP] in [TARGET LANGUAGE]. Assessment evidence is [ASSESSMENT DATA]. Expected outcomes are [EXPECTED OUTCOMES]. Previous evidence is [PREVIOUS EVIDENCE].

TASK
Analyse the evidence to identify secure learning, partial learning, likely gaps and priorities for teaching.

REQUIREMENTS
1. Analyse each language construct separately.
2. Distinguish absence of evidence from evidence of non-mastery.
3. Look for patterns across vocabulary, grammar, phonics/pronunciation and the four skills where relevant.
4. Do not infer causes beyond what the evidence supports.
5. Identify likely misconceptions and propose a short evidence-based intervention for each priority.
6. Include retrieval and reassessment.
7. Avoid labelling pupils using vague global terms such as "weak at languages".
8. Do not infer confidence, motivation or personality from language performance.
9. Include SEND/EAL considerations only where supported by supplied information.
10. Flag data-quality limitations.

OUTPUT FORMAT
Produce a construct-by-construct analysis, strengths, gaps, confidence level of each inference, intervention priorities, teaching response, reassessment plan and data limitations.

QUALITY CHECKS
Check that conclusions are evidence-based and distinguish demonstrated gaps from missing evidence.

OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
If I provide pupil-level information, anonymise it where possible and use only the information needed for the analysis.
```

**Example input:** A class spreadsheet showing vocabulary recall, listening comprehension and sentence-writing results.

**Example output excerpt:** The analysis identifies a common verb-form gap while noting that speaking evidence is insufficient to conclude whether the same pupils have an interaction difficulty.

# PA-08 Generate High-Quality MFL Feedback

**Prompt ID:** PA-08  
**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 700–1,000 words  
**Typical generation time:** 30–60 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** Primary MFL, KS2, Feedback, Improvement, Assessment

### Editable variables

`[YEAR GROUP]`, `[TARGET LANGUAGE]`, `[PUPIL WORK/EVIDENCE]`, `[SUCCESS CRITERIA]`, `[NEXT LEARNING]`

### Copy/paste prompt

```text
ROLE
You are an expert primary MFL teacher specialising in actionable feedback.

CONTEXT
I teach [YEAR GROUP] in [TARGET LANGUAGE]. Pupil evidence is [PUPIL WORK/EVIDENCE]. Success criteria are [SUCCESS CRITERIA]. The next learning is [NEXT LEARNING].

TASK
Generate concise, specific feedback that identifies achievement, the most important improvement and an actionable next step.

REQUIREMENTS
1. Base every comment on supplied evidence.
2. Identify the specific language construct involved.
3. Explain the error or gap in pupil-friendly language.
4. Give one manageable correction or improvement action.
5. Include a short opportunity to apply the feedback.
6. Do not praise or criticise personality, confidence or willingness to speak.
7. Do not use vague labels such as "excellent language skills" without evidence.
8. Do not invent target-language explanations. Mark [VERIFY] where needed.
9. Prioritise the highest-value improvement rather than correcting every minor feature.
10. Keep feedback appropriate to [YEAR GROUP].

OUTPUT FORMAT
For each pupil or work sample provide: achieved, improve, why it matters, next action and a short follow-up task.

QUALITY CHECKS
Check evidence alignment, construct specificity, accuracy, clarity and actionability.

OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
If the school uses a particular feedback policy, follow it where supplied.
```

**Example input:** Year 5 French writing with errors in adjective agreement and otherwise secure vocabulary.

**Example output excerpt:** Feedback focuses on the relevant agreement pattern and gives a short retrieval-and-rewrite task instead of overwhelming the pupil with every possible correction.

# Family-Level Quality Control Checklist

Before releasing this family, verify every workflow against the following controls:

- [ ] Exactly 10 workflows are present: PA-01 to PA-10.

- [ ] Every workflow contains required metadata.
- [ ] Progression is cumulative and not simply topic coverage.

- [ ] Vocabulary, grammar, phonics/pronunciation and language skills are distinguished where relevant.

- [ ] Pronunciation assessment focuses on intelligibility and relevant target-language features, not accent conformity.
- [ ] Reading assessment distinguishes decoding/recognition from comprehension.
- [ ] Writing assessment distinguishes independent language production from copying and handwriting.
- [ ] Translation prioritises meaning and appropriate equivalence rather than word-for-word substitution.

- [ ] No invented target-language vocabulary, grammar, pronunciation or assessment answers.

- [ ] Confidence, personality, popularity and willingness to perform are never used as language-attainment proxies.
- [ ] Personal identity, family circumstances and cultural heritage are never assessment constructs.
- [ ] Handwriting/presentation are not allowed to distort language judgements unless explicitly intended.

- [ ] Intervention is linked to identified constructs and followed by reassessment.
