# MFL Family 9: Inclusion, SEND, EAL & Adaptive Teaching

This family provides copy/paste-ready workflows for adapting primary MFL teaching so that pupils can access the intended language learning without unnecessarily reducing linguistic, cognitive or communicative challenge.

The prompts are designed around a central principle: **adapt access, not the learning ambition, unless there is clear evidence that the intended construct itself needs changing**.

Use supplied pupil information, school guidance and professional judgement. The prompts do not diagnose SEND, EAL needs, speech or hearing conditions, developmental conditions, medical conditions or learning difficulties.

Where the target language is not specified, use `[TARGET LANGUAGE]`. Where a language-specific fact, pronunciation feature, grammatical form, vocabulary item or cultural claim needs checking, use `[VERIFY]` rather than inventing information.

## Family-wide operating principles

Every workflow in this family should:

- preserve the intended MFL learning construct wherever possible
- adapt access rather than automatically reducing language or cognitive challenge
- use supplied pupil information rather than diagnosing needs
- distinguish language-learning barriers from disability, EAL status, confidence, behaviour or motivation unless evidence supports the inference
- distinguish listening, speaking, pronunciation, vocabulary, grammar, reading, writing, translation and mediation constructs
- consider visual, aural, tactile and multimodal access where appropriate
- use chunking, modelling, repetition, processing time, retrieval support and structured response modes where useful
- support gradual movement towards independence and avoid permanent over-scaffolding
- consider assistive technology only where supplied, available and appropriate
- provide alternative evidence routes where an access need prevents a pupil from demonstrating the intended construct in the default mode
- avoid treating English proficiency as evidence of MFL aptitude or lack of aptitude
- recognise that multilingual pupils may have useful transferable language knowledge without assuming that a particular language will transfer directly
- protect pronunciation and phonics accuracy and avoid misleading English phonetic respelling
- avoid treating accent conformity as the goal of pronunciation teaching
- avoid stereotypes about disability, neurodivergence, EAL, multilingualism or language aptitude
- avoid forced personal disclosure
- avoid medical, diagnostic, therapeutic or counselling advice
- never invent school SEND policy, EHCP processes, reasonable adjustments, safeguarding procedures or specialist recommendations
- use supplied school policy as the controlling source where it is provided
- flag current statutory guidance or local requirements for reliable verification where relevant
- keep adaptations age-appropriate and realistic for a primary classroom
- ensure assessment adaptations preserve the intended construct rather than making the task merely easier
- use evidence-led intervention and reassessment rather than generic labels

## IA-01 - Plan an Inclusive MFL Lesson for a Mixed-Needs Class

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 900-1,400 words  
**Typical generation time:** 30-60 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** Primary MFL, KS2, Inclusion, SEND, EAL, Adaptive Teaching, Accessibility, Lesson Planning

### ROLE

Act as an experienced primary MFL teacher and adaptive teaching specialist. Design inclusive MFL lessons that maintain ambitious language learning while removing unnecessary barriers to access.

### CONTEXT

I am planning an MFL lesson for:

- Year group: `[YEAR GROUP]`
- Target language: `[TARGET LANGUAGE]`
- Topic/unit: `[TOPIC OR UNIT]`
- Lesson length: `[LESSON LENGTH]`
- Intended learning objective: `[LEARNING OBJECTIVE]`
- Key vocabulary/language chunks: `[VOCABULARY OR CHUNKS]`
- Grammar or sentence pattern: `[GRAMMAR OR SENTENCE PATTERN]`
- Main language skill(s): `[LISTENING / SPEAKING / READING / WRITING / TRANSLATION / MEDIATION]`
- Prior learning: `[PRIOR LEARNING]`
- Known access needs or supplied pupil information: `[PUPIL INFORMATION]`
- Available resources: `[AVAILABLE RESOURCES]`
- School guidance or required adjustments: `[SCHOOL GUIDANCE]`

Do not infer or diagnose needs that I have not supplied.

### TASK

Create a complete inclusive lesson plan that enables pupils with different access needs, prior knowledge and levels of independence to work towards the same core MFL learning intention wherever possible.

### REQUIREMENTS

1. State the intended language construct clearly.
2. Identify likely access barriers from the information supplied, without diagnosing pupils.
3. Design adaptations for input, modelling, guided practice, independent application and assessment.
4. Include at least one scaffold and a planned route for reducing that scaffold.
5. Include appropriate visual, aural, processing-time, response-mode and grouping adaptations where relevant.
6. Preserve linguistic and cognitive challenge rather than automatically simplifying the language.
7. Distinguish adaptations for SEND, EAL and mixed prior knowledge where this is useful, without treating any label as a diagnosis of learning ability.
8. Include teacher language and pupil response expectations.
9. Include an accessible formative assessment check.
10. Identify what should remain unchanged so that the learning construct is preserved.
11. Flag any language-specific content requiring verification with `[VERIFY]`.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Provide:
1. Lesson overview
2. Intended MFL construct
3. Success criteria
4. Likely access barriers
5. Adaptive teaching plan
6. Teacher modelling
7. Guided practice
8. Independent or paired application
9. Scaffold-to-independence plan
10. Formative assessment
11. Adaptation notes by pupil need
12. Resources
13. Teacher quality-control checklist

### QUALITY CHECKS

Before finalising, check that the lesson does not lower challenge merely because pupils have additional needs, does not diagnose pupils, does not confuse EAL with MFL aptitude, does not require personal disclosure, and does not invent school-specific procedures. Check target-language accuracy and mark uncertain language `[VERIFY]`.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Also include `[NO-PRINT OPTION]`, `[LOW-TECH OPTION]`, or `[HIGH-SCAFFOLD OPTION]` if requested.

### Example Input

Year 5 Spanish; topic: classroom objects; objective: pupils can understand and say six classroom-object phrases in short sentences; mixed prior knowledge; two pupils need additional processing time; one pupil has supplied information indicating difficulty retaining spoken sequences; several pupils are multilingual.

### Example Output excerpt

The core construct is understanding and producing the target-language vocabulary in meaningful short sentences. Keep the target phrases unchanged, but provide visual word cards, controlled repetition, additional processing time and a structured response frame. Fade the visual prompt after successful retrieval rather than removing the language challenge.

## IA-02 - Adapt MFL Input Without Reducing Language Challenge

**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 800-1,200 words  
**Typical generation time:** 30-50 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** Primary MFL, KS2, SEND, EAL, Adaptive Teaching, Input, Scaffolding

### ROLE

Act as a primary MFL specialist and adaptive teaching expert.

### CONTEXT

I need to adapt an MFL teaching input for `[YEAR GROUP]` learning `[TARGET LANGUAGE]` through `[TOPIC]`.

The original input is:

`[ORIGINAL INPUT OR SCRIPT]`

Learning intention: `[LEARNING INTENTION]`  
Known pupil information: `[PUPIL INFORMATION]`  
Available supports: `[AVAILABLE SUPPORTS]`

### TASK

Adapt the input so that pupils can access it more successfully without unnecessarily reducing the linguistic or cognitive demand.

### REQUIREMENTS

- Identify the actual language construct first.
- Separate essential language from unnecessary processing load.
- Recommend changes to pace, chunking, modelling, repetition, visual support, sequencing, gesture or response mode where appropriate.
- Keep core target-language content intact unless there is a clear pedagogical reason to alter it.
- Explain which supports should be temporary and how they can be faded.
- Distinguish support for vocabulary, listening comprehension, pronunciation, grammar and working-memory load.
- Do not diagnose a pupil or assume a disability from a performance pattern.
- Do not treat EAL status as a reason to reduce MFL ambition.
- Do not invent target-language forms. Mark uncertain language `[VERIFY]`.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Produce:
1. Construct being taught
2. Barrier analysis
3. Adapted input
4. Scaffolds
5. Teacher delivery notes
6. Scaffold-fading sequence
7. Independent check
8. What has deliberately not been simplified
9. Verification points

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check that the adaptation removes access barriers rather than removing the intended language learning, and that target-language accuracy is protected.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Add a version for `[VISUAL SUPPORT]`, `[AURAL SUPPORT]`, `[PROCESSING TIME]` or `[LOW-TECH CLASSROOM]`.

### Example Input

A Year 4 French listening sequence contains eight short sentences. Several pupils lose track after the first three sentences.

### Example Output excerpt

Keep the same eight sentences but present them in smaller meaningful chunks, preview only essential vocabulary, use a visual sequence and allow a short processing pause after each chunk. Do not replace the listening task with a vocabulary copying task because that would change the construct.

### ROLE

Act as an experienced primary MFL teacher with expertise in inclusive listening, speaking and pronunciation teaching.

### CONTEXT

I am teaching `[YEAR GROUP]` `[TARGET LANGUAGE]`.

### TASK

### REQUIREMENTS

1. Identify whether the task primarily assesses listening comprehension, speaking, pronunciation, interaction or a combination.
2. Provide access adaptations such as controlled repetition, visual cues, chunking, processing time, predictable routines and structured response frames where appropriate.
3. For speaking, distinguish intelligibility from accent conformity.
4. Do not require public performance where a private or paired evidence route can validly demonstrate the construct.
5. Where hearing or speech-related access needs affect the default evidence route, provide an alternative way to demonstrate the intended learning where valid.
6. Do not provide medical or therapeutic advice.
7. Do not diagnose the pupil.
8. Do not invent specialist recommendations or school procedures.
9. Do not use misleading English phonetic respelling.
10. Mark language requiring verification `[VERIFY]`.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

6. Pronunciation support
7. Interaction structures
8. Alternative evidence routes where valid
9. Scaffold fading
10. Assessment criteria
11. Teacher checklist

### QUALITY CHECKS

Ensure that support does not turn listening into visual reading or speaking into copying unless that is explicitly intended. Preserve the construct and use evidence rather than assumptions about SEND.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Add options for `[PAIR WORK]`, `[ONE-TO-ONE CHECK]`, `[HEARING ACCESS NEED]` or `[SPEECH ACCESS NEED]` using only supplied information.

### Example Input

### Example Output excerpt

Use the same target-language sentence pattern, allow additional preparation time and assess the intended language production through a valid response route where needed. Do not equate reduced spontaneous speed with reduced language knowledge without evidence.

### Example Input

## IA-08 - Diagnose Barriers to MFL Learning and Select Interventions

**Difficulty:** Advanced

**Typical generation time:** 40-70 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** Primary MFL, KS2, SEND, EAL, Assessment, Intervention, Misconceptions, Adaptive Teaching

### ROLE

Act as a primary MFL assessment and intervention specialist. Analyse observable learning evidence without diagnosing pupils.

### CONTEXT

Year group: `[YEAR GROUP]`  
Target language: `[TARGET LANGUAGE]`  
Learning objective: `[OBJECTIVE]`  
Observed pupil evidence: `[EVIDENCE]`  
Previous teaching/support: `[PREVIOUS SUPPORT]`  
Known pupil information: `[SUPPLIED INFORMATION]`

### TASK

Analyse the evidence to identify the most plausible barrier or learning gap and propose proportionate interventions followed by reassessment.

### REQUIREMENTS

- distinguish vocabulary, phonics, pronunciation, grammar, listening, speaking, reading and writing barriers
- distinguish lack of knowledge from retrieval difficulty, processing load or access difficulty where evidence permits
- do not infer SEND, EAL status, motivation or confidence as the cause without evidence
- identify alternative hypotheses where evidence is ambiguous
- propose a short intervention matched to the construct
- specify what evidence should be collected after intervention
- include when to stop, continue or change the intervention
- do not provide medical or diagnostic advice
- do not invent school procedures
- use `[VERIFY]` for target-language uncertainty

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Provide:
1. Evidence summary
2. Construct being assessed
3. Plausible barriers ranked by evidence
4. What cannot be concluded
5. Intervention
6. Duration/frequency
7. Reassessment measure
8. Decision rule
9. Escalation or professional-discussion flag if appropriate, without prescribing a diagnosis
10. Teacher checklist

### QUALITY CHECKS

Ensure conclusions are evidence-led and proportionate. Do not convert a classroom performance pattern into a SEND or medical diagnosis.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Add `[VOCABULARY]`, `[PHONICS]`, `[LISTENING]`, `[SPEAKING]`, `[READING]`, `[WRITING]` or `[MIXED PROFILE]` versions.

### Example Input

A Year 5 pupil can recognise target-language vocabulary on cards but consistently fails to retrieve the same words during oral interaction.

### Example Output excerpt

The evidence suggests a difference between recognition and active retrieval. First test retrieval with reduced cues and varied contexts before concluding that the pupil lacks vocabulary knowledge.
