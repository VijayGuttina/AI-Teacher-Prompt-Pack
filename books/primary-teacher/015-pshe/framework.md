# 015 PSHE Framework

## Purpose

This framework defines the architecture, quality standards and verification approach for the Primary Teacher PSHE prompt pack. The prompts are designed to help teachers plan, teach, assess, adapt and reflect on personal, social, health and economic education across relationships, health and wellbeing, safety, citizenship, financial education, decision-making, identity, resilience and personal development.

The pack supports teacher judgement and does not replace school policy, statutory guidance, safeguarding procedures or professional judgement. Prompts should produce practical classroom-ready material while making assumptions visible and avoiding unsupported claims.

## Core Principles

1. **Developmental appropriateness**: content, examples and language must match pupils' age, maturity and curriculum entitlement.
2. **Safeguarding first**: PSHE teaching must not override safeguarding procedures or encourage pupils to disclose personal experiences.
3. **Accurate health information**: health and wellbeing claims should be evidence-led and avoid unsupported medical advice.
4. **Respect and dignity**: avoid shaming pupils for bodies, families, emotions, behaviour, finances, identity or life circumstances.
5. **Inclusive representation**: represent different families, identities, abilities, cultures and circumstances without tokenism or stereotyping.
6. **Personal agency**: teach practical strategies for choices, boundaries, communication, help-seeking and decision-making without implying that pupils are responsible for circumstances outside their control.
7. **Neutrality with rigour**: where topics involve values or differing perspectives, teach the intended knowledge and reasoning without forcing personal disclosure or teacher-preferred conclusions.
8. **Clear boundaries**: distinguish education from counselling, diagnosis, therapy, medical treatment and individual safeguarding intervention.
9. **Practical implementation**: prompts should account for lesson time, preparation, resources, grouping, likely misconceptions and contingencies.
10. **Policy awareness**: school-specific procedures, local safeguarding arrangements and statutory requirements must be supplied or verified rather than invented.
11. **Evidence-led assessment**: assess knowledge, understanding, application and decision-making where appropriate, not personal beliefs or willingness to disclose.
12. **Safe uncertainty**: when current guidance or local policy matters, identify what must be checked before teaching.

## Standard Workflow Architecture

Every workflow follows the agreed prompt architecture:

1. `ROLE`
2. `CONTEXT`
3. `TASK`
4. `REQUIREMENTS`
5. `OUTPUT FORMAT`
6. `QUALITY CHECKS`
7. `OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION`

Prompts should also include the agreed metadata and, where required by the master specification, Example Input and Example Output.

## PSHE Quality Gates

Every workflow should be checked for:

- curriculum and learning-intent alignment
- age and developmental suitability
- accurate health and wellbeing information
- clear distinction between education and professional medical or therapeutic advice
- safeguarding-aware framing
- avoidance of forced personal disclosure
- inclusive and non-stereotyped representation
- respectful treatment of families, identities, bodies, abilities and circumstances
- clear practical strategies pupils can apply
- appropriate discussion and scenario design
- realistic classroom implementation
- observable success criteria
- useful teacher and pupil actions
- evidence-led assessment
- misconception identification and response
- appropriate handling of sensitive or contested content
- explicit handling of missing school-specific policy information

## Planning Model

Use the following sequence where appropriate:

**Prior understanding → Learning intention → Explicit teaching/model → Guided application → Scenario/discussion/practice → Reflection → Assessment → Revisit/help-seeking or next step**

Planning prompts should identify what pupils will know, understand or practise, what the teacher will model, what participation is required, and how the teacher will know whether the intended learning has occurred.

## Personal Development Model

Prompts should support progression through:

**Recognise → Name → Understand → Practise → Apply → Evaluate → Choose → Communicate → Seek help → Reflect**

The sequence should vary according to the learning objective. Prompts must not imply that emotional responses, family circumstances or personal development follow a single universal pathway.

## Assessment Model

Assessment should distinguish, where relevant:

- knowledge and vocabulary
- understanding of health and wellbeing concepts
- recognition of safe and unsafe situations
- application of boundaries and communication strategies
- decision-making and reasoning
- financial knowledge and application
- citizenship and responsibility knowledge
- understanding of relationships and respect
- ability to identify trusted sources or appropriate help
- application to hypothetical scenarios
- reflection on learning
- progress over time

Do not assess pupils' personal beliefs, family circumstances, emotional disclosure, willingness to share private experiences, popularity, confidence, mental health status or conformity with teacher-preferred values.

## Inclusion Model

Adaptation may address:

- reading age and language load
- vocabulary and concept density
- visual, aural and tactile access
- scenario complexity
- communication and response mode
- processing and thinking time
- discussion structures
- grouping and participation
- sensory load
- prior knowledge and misconceptions
- alternative enquiry routes
- emotionally sensitive content

Adaptations should preserve the intended PSHE construct wherever possible. If an adaptation materially changes what is being assessed, the output should state this.

## Sensitive Topics and Safeguarding Model

PSHE prompts may address sensitive content, but they must maintain clear educational boundaries. Consider:

- whether the content is necessary for the stated learning objective
- age and developmental suitability
- neutral and precise teacher language
- hypothetical rather than personal scenarios where appropriate
- pupil right to avoid personal disclosure
- appropriate signposting to trusted adults and established school processes
- likely misconceptions and harmful stereotypes
- how the teacher should respond to unexpected disclosures
- school safeguarding procedures where supplied

Never invent safeguarding routes, reporting procedures, designated staff arrangements, medical protocols or emergency procedures. Where these are required, instruct the teacher to follow the school's current policy.

Prompts must not diagnose mental health conditions, prescribe treatment, provide individual medical advice, or present the AI as a substitute for a qualified professional.

## Relationships and Personal Boundaries

Where prompts address relationships, consent, boundaries or personal safety:

- use clear, age-appropriate language
- teach respect and agency
- avoid victim-blaming
- avoid requiring pupils to describe personal experiences
- use hypothetical scenarios where possible
- distinguish education from safeguarding intervention
- include appropriate help-seeking routes based on supplied school procedures
- avoid implying that every family, friendship or relationship looks the same

## Health and Wellbeing Boundaries

Health-related prompts should:

- use reliable evidence
- distinguish general educational information from individual medical advice
- avoid diagnosis
- avoid unsupported causal claims
- avoid promising outcomes
- acknowledge uncertainty where evidence is mixed
- recommend checking current authoritative guidance when relevant
- avoid stigmatising disability, illness, mental health or body differences

## Financial Education Model

Financial education prompts should use realistic, age-appropriate examples and distinguish factual economic concepts from personal financial advice. Where current prices, regulations, taxation, wages or financial products are involved, current reliable sources must be checked. Prompts should not encourage pupils to disclose household income, debt or other private financial information.

## Verification Model

The PSHE pack will be verified using three layers:

### 1. Structural QA

All workflows must contain the required metadata and agreed prompt architecture. Target: **100/100 workflows structurally complete**.

### 2. Representative In-Model Execution

Execute exactly two representative workflows from each family, giving **20/20 representative executions**. Check that outputs are usable, coherent, age-appropriate, safe, inclusive and aligned with the stated task.

### 3. Targeted Risk Checks

Complete 16 targeted checks:

1. Age and developmental appropriateness
2. Safeguarding and disclosure handling
3. Accurate health information
4. Avoidance of diagnosis or medical advice
5. Mental wellbeing boundaries
6. Relationships and consent terminology
7. Personal boundaries and agency
8. Inclusive family representation
9. Identity and diversity representation
10. Avoidance of stereotypes and victim-blaming
11. Digital and personal safety accuracy
12. Financial education accuracy and privacy
13. Sensitive discussion design
14. Evidence-led assessment construct validity
15. Inclusive participation and accessibility
16. Safe handling of missing school-specific policies or current guidance

A workflow is not considered frozen solely because its structure is valid. Execution verification must test whether the prompt produces a useful and safe result.
