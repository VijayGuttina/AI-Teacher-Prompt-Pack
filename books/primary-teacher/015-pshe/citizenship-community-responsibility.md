# Citizenship, Community & Responsibility

This family provides ten copy/paste-ready ChatGPT workflows for primary PSHE teaching about citizenship, community, rights, responsibilities, rules, fairness, participation, contribution and responsible action. The workflows are designed to support knowledge-rich, inclusive and practical personal development education without turning classroom activities into political persuasion, personal disclosure or invented civic procedures.

## Family-wide controls

Every workflow must:
- use developmentally appropriate language, examples and expectations;
- distinguish factual civic education from political persuasion or advocacy;
- represent rights, responsibilities, rules, communities and public institutions accurately;
- avoid presenting one political, cultural or ideological position as the only reasonable conclusion;
- distinguish rights, laws, school rules, community expectations, responsibilities and personal preferences;
- avoid asking pupils to disclose family circumstances, political views, immigration status, household finances or other private information;
- never require pupils to identify their family's political views or represent a particular community;
- use balanced examples when discussing contested or political issues;
- avoid partisan campaigning, party-political persuasion or attempts to influence pupils toward a particular political preference;
- use hypothetical or public examples where possible for sensitive civic topics;
- avoid stereotyping communities, neighbourhoods, occupations, social groups or nationalities;
- distinguish local community experience from claims about everyone in a place or group;
- use current reliable sources when discussing current laws, public institutions, elections, office holders, statistics or local arrangements;
- never invent school, council, safeguarding, voting, volunteering, visitor or community-partnership procedures;
- ensure community action activities are realistic, proportionate, supervised and consistent with school policy;
- assess observable civic knowledge, reasoning, communication, decision-making and application rather than personal beliefs, political preferences, family circumstances or willingness to participate;
- preserve the intended construct when adapting for SEND, EAL or other access needs;
- make clear that responsible citizenship can include questioning, disagreement, listening, evidence checking and seeking change through appropriate channels;
- avoid framing disagreement as disrespect and avoid requiring consensus where reasonable disagreement exists.

### TASK

### TASK

### REQUIREMENTS

- specific misconception;

### QUALITY CHECKS
