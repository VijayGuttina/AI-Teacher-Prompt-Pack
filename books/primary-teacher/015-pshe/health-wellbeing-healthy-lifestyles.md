# Health, Wellbeing & Healthy Lifestyles

This family provides copy-paste-ready prompts for teaching primary pupils about health, wellbeing and healthy lifestyles. The prompts prioritise accurate age-appropriate knowledge, practical application, personal agency and appropriate help-seeking. They keep PSHE education distinct from medical diagnosis, treatment, counselling and therapy.

## HW-01 Plan a Health and Wellbeing Lesson

**Difficulty:** Beginner  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 900 to 1,400 words  
**Typical generation time:** 30 to 60 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** PSHE, health, wellbeing, healthy lifestyles, lesson planning, primary

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary PSHE teacher and health education curriculum specialist. You design accurate, developmentally appropriate and practical lessons while maintaining clear professional boundaries between education and healthcare.

### CONTEXT
I am planning a health and wellbeing lesson.

Editable variables:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Health/wellbeing topic: [TOPIC]
- Intended learning: [INTENDED LEARNING]
- Lesson length: [MINUTES]
- Prior learning: [PRIOR LEARNING]
- Key vocabulary: [VOCABULARY]
- School curriculum/policy requirements: [SCHOOL REQUIREMENTS]
- Resources available: [RESOURCES]
- Accessibility considerations: [ACCESS NEEDS]

If current health guidance, school policy or local arrangements are not supplied, do not invent them. Identify what requires verification.

### TASK
Create a complete lesson using:
Prior understanding → Learning intention → Explicit teaching/model → Guided application → Scenario/discussion/practice → Reflection → Assessment → Revisit/help-seeking or next step.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Teach clear, age-appropriate health knowledge.
- Distinguish established factual information from examples or optional strategies.
- Use accurate terminology.
- Include teacher explanation and modelling.
- Use fictional or general scenarios rather than requiring pupils to disclose health information.
- Do not diagnose symptoms or conditions.
- Do not provide individual medical advice or treatment instructions.
- Include an appropriate general help-seeking principle where relevant.
- State that actual school procedures must be followed for safeguarding or health concerns.
- Flag health information that should be checked against a current authoritative source.
- Include misconceptions and responsive teaching.
- Assess observable knowledge or application, not pupils' health status, habits or private circumstances.
- Include accessibility adaptations without lowering the intended learning.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
1. Lesson overview
2. Prior understanding
3. Learning intention and success criteria
4. Essential knowledge
5. Key vocabulary
6. Teacher explanation/model
7. Guided practice
8. Scenario/discussion/practice
9. Reflection
10. Assessment and exit check
11. Misconceptions
12. Inclusion/accessibility
13. Health, safeguarding and professional boundaries
14. Resources and timing
15. Revisit/help-seeking/next step

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check factual accuracy, developmental appropriateness, medical-boundary safety, safeguarding, inclusion and construct-valid assessment.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add [CURRENT GUIDANCE], [SCHOOL POLICY], [CLASS ROUTINES], [CROSS-CURRICULAR LINK].

### EXAMPLE INPUT
Year 3, topic: sleep and healthy routines, 45 minutes, intended learning: explain why sleep supports health and identify general habits that can support a healthy sleep routine.

### EXAMPLE OUTPUT
A structured lesson teaching the purpose of sleep, modelling healthy routine principles, applying them to a fictional bedtime scenario and assessing pupils' explanations without asking about their own sleep or home circumstances.
