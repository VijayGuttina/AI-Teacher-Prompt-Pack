# Assessment, Inclusion & Intervention

This family provides copy-paste-ready workflows for assessing PSHE learning, identifying barriers and misconceptions, designing proportionate intervention, and monitoring progress. The workflows protect pupil privacy and preserve the intended educational construct while making access more inclusive.

## Family principles

Use these controls throughout every workflow:

- Assess observable PSHE knowledge, vocabulary, reasoning, decision-making, communication and application where these are part of the intended learning.
- Do not assess personal beliefs, family circumstances, emotional disclosure, popularity, confidence, mental-health status, willingness to share private experiences or conformity with teacher-preferred values.
- Never use assessment tasks to elicit trauma, abuse, self-harm, family difficulties, household finances, political views or other private information.
- Distinguish lack of knowledge from language, literacy, processing, communication, accessibility or prior-knowledge barriers.
- Diagnose specific misconceptions rather than relying on right/wrong scores alone.
- Do not diagnose medical, mental-health, developmental or safeguarding conditions from assessment responses.
- Preserve the intended construct when adapting tasks for SEND, EAL, literacy or communication needs.
- Use alternative response modes where appropriate, including oral, written, visual, selected-response or supported formats, while keeping the learning target clear.
- Distinguish legitimate differences of opinion from factual misunderstanding.
- Do not reward teacher-preferred ethical, political or personal conclusions where genuine disagreement is compatible with the learning objective.
- Use fictional, hypothetical or generic scenarios when application tasks could otherwise invite personal disclosure.
- Where safeguarding concerns arise, follow supplied school procedures. Never invent reporting or referral arrangements.
- Interventions should be proportionate, targeted and educational. Do not turn intervention into counselling or therapy.
- Reassess after intervention and use evidence of change to determine next steps.
- Current health, legal, digital, financial or policy information should be verified using reliable current sources where material.
- Make inclusion practical through resources, grouping, timing, access, response mode and teacher preparation.

## AI-01 Plan an Evidence-Led PSHE Assessment Lesson

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 1,000 to 1,500 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 30 to 60 seconds  
**Curriculum Tags:** PSHE, assessment, formative assessment, retrieval, application, KS1, KS2

### ROLE

Act as an experienced primary PSHE assessment designer who understands construct validity, developmental appropriateness and inclusive assessment.

### CONTEXT

I am teaching **[YEAR GROUP]** about **[TOPIC]**. The intended learning is **[LEARNING INTENTION]**. Pupils already know **[PRIOR KNOWLEDGE]**. I have **[LESSON LENGTH]** minutes and need an assessment-focused lesson.

### TASK

Design a lesson that gathers reliable evidence of what pupils know, understand and can apply, then uses that evidence to determine next teaching steps.

### REQUIREMENTS

1. Define the assessed construct and explicitly state what is not being assessed.
2. Include a short retrieval activity.
3. Provide at least three assessment opportunities with increasing cognitive demand.
4. Include a fictional or generic application scenario where appropriate.
5. Provide model answers or indicative evidence, while recognising legitimate variation where relevant.
6. Identify common misconceptions and what evidence would reveal them.
7. Include accessibility adaptations that preserve the construct.
8. Provide an alternative response mode where speaking or writing is not the intended construct.
9. Avoid questions that require personal disclosure.
10. Include a final exit assessment and a next-step decision rule.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Use:

1. Assessment purpose
2. Assessed construct
3. Non-assessed personal information
4. Prior knowledge
5. Lesson sequence
6. Assessment opportunities
7. Indicative responses
8. Misconceptions
9. Inclusion and accessibility
10. Exit assessment
11. Teacher decision rules
12. Quality check

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check that the assessment measures the stated learning rather than confidence, disclosure or personal circumstances, and that accessibility adaptations do not change the construct.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Adapt for **[YEAR GROUP]**, **[TOPIC]**, **[SEND/EAL]**, **[READING AGE]**, **[ASSESSMENT MODEL]** and **[LESSON LENGTH]**.

### Example Input

Year 4; topic: digital safety; objective: identify warning signs in fictional online interactions and explain appropriate safe responses.

### Example Output Excerpt

Pupils analyse fictional examples, identify observable warning signs and justify a safe response. The assessment does not ask whether pupils have received similar messages themselves.

## AI-02 Diagnose Barriers to PSHE Learning From Pupil Evidence

**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 1,100 to 1,700 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 45 to 75 seconds  
**Curriculum Tags:** PSHE, assessment, inclusion, barriers, SEND, EAL, misconceptions, KS1, KS2

### ROLE

Act as a primary PSHE assessment and inclusion specialist.

### CONTEXT

I have pupil work or responses from **[YEAR GROUP]** on **[TOPIC]**. The intended learning is **[LEARNING INTENTION]**. Evidence is **[PASTE OR SUMMARISE RESPONSES]**.

### TASK

Analyse the evidence to distinguish knowledge gaps, misconceptions and possible access barriers without making clinical, developmental or safeguarding diagnoses.

### REQUIREMENTS

For each response identify evidence of secure understanding, missing knowledge, misconception, vocabulary/language barrier, literacy demand, processing or communication barrier where supported by the evidence, and any uncertainty. Do not infer hidden conditions. Explain what additional low-risk evidence could distinguish competing explanations. Recommend an appropriate classroom adjustment or teaching response. Never infer family circumstances, mental-health status, trauma or safeguarding status from educational work.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Use a table with: Response ID, Evidence, Secure learning, Knowledge gap, Misconception, Possible access barrier, Confidence in interpretation, Recommended next step.

Then provide a class-level pattern analysis and priority actions.

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check that every conclusion is grounded in observable evidence, uncertainty is acknowledged, no diagnosis is made and inclusion recommendations preserve the intended learning.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Adapt for **[YEAR GROUP]**, **[TOPIC]**, **[SEND/EAL]**, **[ASSESSMENT TYPE]** and **[CURRICULUM EXPECTATION]**.

### Example Input

Year 5 written responses where several pupils use vague language to explain safe online choices.

### Example Output Excerpt

The evidence may indicate vocabulary or explanation difficulty rather than a conceptual misunderstanding. The teacher is advised to probe with a structured oral or visual task before concluding that pupils lack the underlying concept.

## AI-03 Create a PSHE Assessment Rubric That Measures the Intended Construct

**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 1,000 to 1,600 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 30 to 60 seconds  
**Curriculum Tags:** PSHE, rubric, assessment, progression, knowledge, reasoning, application, KS1, KS2

### ROLE

Act as a primary PSHE assessment lead with expertise in valid, observable success criteria.

### CONTEXT

Create a rubric for **[YEAR GROUP]**, **[TOPIC]** and **[TASK OR LEARNING OBJECTIVE]**. The intended construct is **[CONSTRUCT]**.

### TASK

Build a practical rubric that differentiates levels of performance without assessing private experiences, emotions or personal beliefs.

### REQUIREMENTS

Define 3 to 5 assessment dimensions appropriate to the construct. Use observable descriptors rather than vague terms such as “good attitude” or “confidence”. Where relevant assess knowledge accuracy, vocabulary, explanation, reasoning, application, decision-making or respectful communication. Include examples of evidence at each level. Do not assess willingness to speak, personal disclosure, emotional state, family context or conformity with teacher-preferred values. Include accessibility notes so that alternative response modes can still demonstrate the construct.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Provide a rubric table with dimensions and four performance levels: Beginning, Developing, Secure and Advanced. Follow with teacher moderation notes and examples of valid evidence.

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check construct validity, observable descriptors, developmental appropriateness, fairness and independence from personal circumstances.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Adapt for **[TOPIC]**, **[YEAR GROUP]**, **[TASK]**, **[CURRICULUM]** and **[RESPONSE MODE]**.

### Example Input

Year 6; topic: decision-making; task: analyse a fictional scenario and justify an appropriate response.

### Example Output Excerpt

Higher performance is demonstrated by identifying relevant information, comparing plausible options, explaining consequences and justifying a proportionate response. It is not demonstrated by revealing a personal experience or agreeing with the teacher.

## AI-04 Plan Targeted PSHE Intervention After Assessment

**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 1,100 to 1,700 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 45 to 75 seconds  
**Curriculum Tags:** PSHE, intervention, misconceptions, reteaching, assessment, KS1, KS2

### ROLE

Act as a primary PSHE intervention specialist focused on precise reteaching and measurable improvement.

### CONTEXT

Assessment evidence shows that pupils in **[YEAR GROUP]** are struggling with **[MISCONCEPTION OR LEARNING GAP]**. Evidence is **[EVIDENCE]**. The intended learning is **[LEARNING INTENTION]**.

### TASK

Create a short, targeted intervention that addresses the specific barrier or misconception and ends with reassessment.

### REQUIREMENTS

Identify the exact misunderstanding before planning activities. Explain the likely cause only where supported by evidence. Use explicit teaching, worked examples, guided practice and a new application task. Keep examples fictional where sensitive topics are involved. Do not use intervention as counselling. Do not ask pupils to disclose personal experiences. Include adaptations for literacy, language, communication and processing where appropriate. Define a measurable success criterion and a short reassessment. State what the teacher should do if the misconception remains.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Use:

1. Evidence and diagnosis of the learning issue
2. Intervention objective
3. Misconception explanation
4. Explicit reteaching
5. Guided practice
6. Independent application
7. Accessibility
8. Assessment and reassessment
9. Escalation or next teaching step
10. Teacher quality check

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check that the intervention is educational, targeted, proportionate, non-stigmatising and based on observable evidence.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Adapt for **[YEAR GROUP]**, **[TOPIC]**, **[MISCONCEPTION]**, **[GROUP SIZE]**, **[TIME]** and **[SEND/EAL NEEDS]**.

### Example Input

Year 3 pupils confuse “being responsible for your own choice” with “being responsible for someone else's harmful behaviour”.

### Example Output Excerpt

The intervention uses two fictional scenarios to contrast personal responsibility with responsibility for another person's actions, followed by a new scenario to test whether pupils can transfer the distinction.

## AI-05 Design Inclusive PSHE Assessment Without Lowering Ambition

**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 1,200 to 1,800 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 45 to 75 seconds  
**Curriculum Tags:** PSHE, inclusion, accessibility, SEND, EAL, assessment, KS1, KS2

### ROLE

Act as a senior primary PSHE inclusion and assessment specialist.

### CONTEXT

I need to assess **[LEARNING OBJECTIVE]** with **[YEAR GROUP]**. Pupils have the following access needs: **[SEND/EAL/LITERACY/COMMUNICATION NEEDS]**. The current assessment is **[ASSESSMENT DESCRIPTION]**.

### TASK

Redesign the assessment to improve access while preserving the intended knowledge or skill being assessed.

### REQUIREMENTS

First define the intended construct. Identify which parts of the current task create irrelevant barriers. Provide specific adaptations to language load, vocabulary, reading demand, visual layout, processing time, communication mode, response mode, scenario complexity, sensory demands and adult support where relevant. Clearly distinguish legitimate access support from changing the learning target. Do not make assumptions about individual pupils. Do not make emotional disclosure a condition of assessment. Include a moderation note explaining how teachers can judge equivalent evidence across response modes.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Use:

1. Intended construct
2. Current assessment barriers
3. Risk of construct distortion
4. Revised assessment
5. Accessibility adaptations
6. Equivalent evidence across response modes
7. Teacher support boundaries
8. Moderation guidance
9. Quality assurance checklist

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check that adaptations improve access without reducing cognitive demand or changing the intended PSHE learning.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Adapt for **[YEAR GROUP]**, **[ASSESSMENT]**, **[SEND NEEDS]**, **[EAL NEEDS]**, **[READING AGE]** and **[RESPONSE MODE]**.

### Example Input

Year 5; objective: explain how to respond safely to peer pressure; current task is a long written scenario; pupils include EAL learners and pupils with writing difficulties.

### Example Output Excerpt

The scenario is shortened and key vocabulary is pre-taught, while pupils may provide an oral explanation or structured response. The reasoning demand remains unchanged.

### CONTEXT

Create a progress tracker that records substantive learning and disciplinary application over time.

### REQUIREMENTS

Define measurable indicators for knowledge, vocabulary, reasoning, application and decision-making where relevant. Include baseline, formative and end-of-unit evidence. Provide simple status categories and space for misconception/intervention notes. Do not include personal belief, emotional state, family circumstances or disclosure as progress measures. Progress is recorded against specific indicators such as “identifies a boundary in a fictional scenario” and “explains a proportionate help-seeking response”, rather than “is confident discussing relationships”.

## AI-07 Create a Diagnostic PSHE Exit Ticket

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 700 to 1,100 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 20 to 45 seconds  
**Curriculum Tags:** PSHE, exit ticket, formative assessment, misconceptions, KS1, KS2

### ROLE

Act as a primary PSHE formative assessment designer.

### CONTEXT

The lesson is **[LESSON TOPIC]** for **[YEAR GROUP]**. The learning intention is **[LEARNING INTENTION]**. I need a **[5 OR 10]** minute exit ticket.

### TASK

Create an exit ticket with enough diagnostic value to reveal what pupils know, misunderstand and can apply.

### REQUIREMENTS

Include a retrieval item, vocabulary item, explanation item and application item where developmentally appropriate. Use a fictional or generic scenario if the topic is sensitive. Include one misconception probe. Provide answers or indicative responses and teacher interpretation rules. Avoid questions that ask pupils to report personal experiences or feelings about private matters. Include accessible versions without changing the construct.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Provide the pupil-facing exit ticket first, followed by answer guidance, misconception interpretation and next-step actions.

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check brevity, diagnostic value, developmental appropriateness, privacy and construct validity.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Adapt for **[YEAR GROUP]**, **[TOPIC]**, **[LESSON LENGTH]**, **[SEND/EAL]** and **[RESPONSE FORMAT]**.

### Example Input

Year 2; topic: fairness; objective: explain why treating everyone fairly does not always mean giving everyone exactly the same thing.

### Example Output Excerpt

A simple fictional classroom scenario asks pupils to choose and explain a fair response. The teacher can distinguish pupils who understand fairness from pupils who equate fairness with identical treatment.

## AI-08 Diagnose PSHE Reasoning Errors and Plan Corrective Teaching

**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 1,100 to 1,700 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 45 to 75 seconds  
**Curriculum Tags:** PSHE, reasoning, misconceptions, scenarios, decision-making, assessment, KS1, KS2

### ROLE

Act as an expert primary PSHE assessment specialist focused on reasoning and decision-making.

### CONTEXT

Pupils have responded to **[SCENARIO OR TASK]** about **[TOPIC]**. Their responses are **[PASTE RESPONSES]**. The intended reasoning is **[INTENDED REASONING]**.

### TASK

Analyse reasoning errors and design corrective teaching that improves the underlying thinking rather than merely correcting the final answer.

### REQUIREMENTS

Identify errors such as missing relevant information, false assumptions, overgeneralisation, victim-blaming, failure to consider consequences, unsafe risk assessment, confusion between disagreement and harm, or failure to identify appropriate help-seeking where these are supported by the evidence. Distinguish factual errors from reasonable alternative judgements. Do not infer personal experience or emotional state. Provide a contrasting example, teacher explanation, guided practice and transfer task.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Use: Reasoning error, Evidence, Corrective explanation, Guided practice, Transfer task, Success criterion.

Then provide a short intervention sequence and reassessment method.

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check evidence-based interpretation, neutral framing, no personal disclosure, no diagnosis and observable success criteria.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Adapt for **[YEAR GROUP]**, **[TOPIC]**, **[SCENARIO]**, **[CURRICULUM]** and **[REASONING SKILL]**.

### Example Input

Year 5 responses to a fictional peer-pressure scenario where pupils focus only on whether the first choice was sensible and ignore subsequent pressure.

### Example Output Excerpt

The correction teaches pupils to consider the full sequence of events and changing information rather than simply labelling the first decision as “good” or “bad”.

## AI-09 Plan Reassessment and Retrieval After PSHE Intervention

**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 900 to 1,400 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 30 to 60 seconds  
**Curriculum Tags:** PSHE, reassessment, retrieval, intervention, progress, KS1, KS2

### ROLE

Act as a primary PSHE assessment specialist responsible for closing assessment and intervention loops.

### CONTEXT

An intervention has been delivered for **[MISCONCEPTION OR LEARNING GAP]** to **[YEAR GROUP]**. Original evidence was **[ORIGINAL EVIDENCE]**. The intervention was **[INTERVENTION SUMMARY]**.

### TASK

Design a reassessment and retrieval sequence that tests whether the intended learning has been secured and can transfer to a new context.

### REQUIREMENTS

Use a new but equivalent task rather than simply repeating the original question. Include immediate reassessment and later retrieval where practical. Distinguish improvement from memorising a model answer. Include an application or transfer item. Provide decision rules for secure, developing and not-yet-secure performance. If the topic is sensitive, use fictional or generic scenarios. Do not assess personal experience or disclosure.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Provide:

1. Original learning issue
2. Reassessment construct
3. Immediate reassessment
4. Delayed retrieval
5. Transfer task
6. Indicative evidence
7. Decision rules
8. Next steps
9. Teacher quality check

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check equivalence of difficulty, construct validity, transfer value and absence of personal disclosure.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Adapt for **[YEAR GROUP]**, **[TOPIC]**, **[MISCONCEPTION]**, **[INTERVENTION LENGTH]** and **[RETRIEVAL INTERVAL]**.

### Example Input

Year 4; misconception: confusing a boundary with an instruction imposed by someone else; intervention completed; reassessment needed the following week.

### Example Output Excerpt

The new task uses a different fictional context so the teacher can test whether pupils understand the concept rather than remember the original scenario.

## Family-Level Quality Gate

Before using any AI workflow in this family, confirm:

- The intended PSHE construct is explicit.
- Non-assessed personal information is explicitly excluded.
- Assessment is based on observable evidence.
- Personal beliefs, emotions, family circumstances and disclosure are not assessed.
- Sensitive topics use safe fictional, hypothetical or generic contexts where appropriate.
- No assessment task pressures pupils to reveal private experiences.
- No diagnosis is made from pupil responses.
- Legitimate differences of opinion are not misclassified as misconceptions.
- Misconceptions are identified precisely and supported by evidence.
- Accessibility adaptations preserve the intended construct.
- Alternative response modes are available where appropriate.
- Intervention is educational and proportionate, not therapeutic.
- Safeguarding procedures are taken from supplied school policy and never invented.
- Reassessment tests the same construct through equivalent but, where possible, new contexts.
- Delayed retrieval and transfer are used where appropriate.
- Progress indicators are observable and meaningful.
- Current health, legal, digital, financial or policy claims are verified when necessary.
- The assessment system is realistic for teacher workload and classroom implementation.
