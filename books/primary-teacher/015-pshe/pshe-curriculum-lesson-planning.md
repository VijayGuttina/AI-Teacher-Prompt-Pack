# PSHE Curriculum & Lesson Planning

This family provides copy-paste-ready prompts for planning coherent, developmentally appropriate and practically deliverable PSHE teaching. The prompts are designed to help teachers turn an intended PSHE learning goal into explicit teaching, guided practice, discussion or scenario work, reflection and observable assessment without turning PSHE into counselling, therapy or personal disclosure.

## PS-01 Plan a PSHE Lesson

**Difficulty:** Beginner  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 900 to 1,400 words  
**Typical generation time:** 30 to 60 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** PSHE, lesson planning, primary, developmental appropriateness, assessment

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary PSHE curriculum specialist and teacher. You design practical lessons that are developmentally appropriate, safeguarding-aware, inclusive, evidence-informed and realistic for a mainstream primary classroom.

### CONTEXT
I am planning a PSHE lesson.

Editable variables:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Topic: [TOPIC]
- Intended learning: [INTENDED LEARNING]
- Lesson length: [MINUTES]
- Prior learning: [PRIOR LEARNING]
- School curriculum or policy requirements: [SCHOOL REQUIREMENTS]
- Resources available: [RESOURCES]
- Known pupil needs or accessibility considerations: [PUPIL NEEDS]

If any school-specific policy, current guidance or local requirement is missing, do not invent it. Identify exactly what should be checked.

### TASK
Create a complete PSHE lesson plan using this sequence:
Prior understanding → Learning intention → Explicit teaching/model → Guided application → Scenario/discussion/practice → Reflection → Assessment → Revisit/help-seeking or next step.

### REQUIREMENTS
- State the precise knowledge, understanding and/or skill pupils should develop.
- Give a clear learning intention and observable success criteria.
- Include teacher explanation and modelling, not discussion alone.
- Use age-appropriate examples and vocabulary.
- Include at least one structured opportunity for pupils to apply the learning.
- Keep hypothetical scenarios separate from pupils' private experiences.
- Do not require pupils to disclose personal, family, emotional, health or safeguarding information.
- Include inclusive participation options.
- Distinguish PSHE education from counselling, diagnosis, therapy or medical treatment.
- Flag where current health, legal, digital-safety or safeguarding information should be checked against reliable current sources or school policy.
- Include likely misconceptions and teacher responses.
- Include a short formative assessment and an exit check.
- Include adaptations that preserve the intended learning construct.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Use these headings:
1. Lesson overview
2. Prior understanding
3. Learning intention
4. Key knowledge and vocabulary
5. Teacher explanation and modelling
6. Guided practice
7. Scenario/discussion/practice activity
8. Reflection
9. Assessment and exit check
10. Misconceptions and responsive teaching
11. Inclusion and accessibility
12. Safeguarding and professional boundaries
13. Resources and preparation
14. Timing
15. Revisit and next step

### QUALITY CHECKS
Before finalising, check that the lesson is developmentally appropriate, educational rather than therapeutic, safe for disclosure, inclusive, evidence-led, observable in assessment and achievable within the stated time.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add: [SCHOOL VALUES], [CLASSROOM ROUTINES], [PREVIOUS LESSON], [FOLLOW-UP LESSON], [SPECIFIC ACCESS NEEDS].

### EXAMPLE INPUT
Year group: Year 4. Topic: managing disagreement. Intended learning: pupils identify constructive ways to respond when they disagree with someone. Lesson length: 45 minutes.

### EXAMPLE OUTPUT
A structured lesson in which pupils first identify prior ideas about disagreement, learn and model specific communication strategies, practise them through fictional scenarios, reflect on which responses are constructive, and complete an observable exit check without being asked to describe real disagreements from their own lives.

## PS-02 Build a Short PSHE Teaching Sequence

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 1,200 to 1,800 words  
**Typical generation time:** 45 to 75 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** PSHE, sequencing, progression, primary, curriculum planning

### ROLE
You are a primary PSHE curriculum designer specialising in coherent short sequences and progression.

### CONTEXT
I need a short PSHE teaching sequence.

Editable variables:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Topic/theme: [TOPIC]
- Number of lessons: [NUMBER]
- Lesson length: [MINUTES]
- Starting knowledge: [STARTING KNOWLEDGE]
- Intended end point: [END POINT]
- Existing curriculum requirements: [CURRICULUM REQUIREMENTS]
- Pupil needs: [PUPIL NEEDS]

### TASK
Design a coherent sequence that builds from prior understanding to secure knowledge, practice, application, reflection and assessment.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Give each lesson a distinct purpose.
- Show how knowledge and skills build rather than repeating the same activity.
- Include explicit teaching and modelling in every lesson where appropriate.
- Include realistic scenarios or practice opportunities.
- Identify prerequisite knowledge.
- Include retrieval from earlier lessons.
- Include formative assessment and a final check of the intended learning.
- Avoid activities that depend on personal disclosure.
- Do not make therapeutic or diagnostic claims.
- Include safeguarding and policy checks where relevant.
- Preserve ambitious learning when adapting for accessibility.
- Flag current guidance that requires verification.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
- Sequence overview
- Progression rationale
- Lesson-by-lesson table
- Knowledge and vocabulary progression
- Retrieval plan
- Assessment plan
- Misconceptions and intervention opportunities
- Inclusion/accessibility plan
- Safeguarding and policy checks
- Resource list
- Final quality assurance checklist

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that the sequence has genuine progression, age-appropriate content, explicit teaching, safe participation, observable assessment and realistic timings.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add [SCHOOL PSHE APPROACH], [CROSS-CURRICULAR LINKS], [PRIOR UNIT], [NEXT UNIT].

### EXAMPLE INPUT
Year 5, four 50-minute lessons, topic: respectful relationships, starting knowledge: pupils can describe respect but struggle to apply it to disagreements.

### EXAMPLE OUTPUT
A four-lesson sequence progressing from defining and recognising respectful behaviour, to modelling communication, to applying strategies in fictional scenarios, to evaluating responses and demonstrating learning through an individual assessment task.

## PS-03 Map PSHE Learning Across a Year

**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 1,500 to 2,200 words  
**Typical generation time:** 60 to 90 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** PSHE, annual planning, progression, curriculum mapping, primary

### ROLE
You are a senior primary PSHE curriculum leader responsible for annual curriculum coherence, progression and coverage.

### CONTEXT
Create an annual PSHE map using:
- Year group(s): [YEAR GROUPS]
- Available teaching time: [TIME]
- Existing topics: [EXISTING TOPICS]
- School priorities: [SCHOOL PRIORITIES]
- Statutory or school requirements supplied by me: [REQUIREMENTS]
- Previous-year learning: [PREVIOUS LEARNING]
- Known pupil needs: [PUPIL NEEDS]

Do not assume a particular school's policy, local curriculum or current statutory wording if it has not been supplied. Identify verification points.

### TASK
Build an annual map showing logical sequencing, retrieval, progression and opportunities to revisit key concepts.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Avoid treating PSHE as a disconnected list of topics.
- Identify recurring concepts such as respect, agency, safety, help-seeking and responsibility.
- Show progression in knowledge and application.
- Balance health, relationships, safety, identity, resilience, citizenship and financial education as appropriate to the supplied curriculum.
- Include retrieval and spaced revisiting.
- Identify sensitive topics requiring careful sequencing.
- Include assessment checkpoints.
- Include opportunities for intervention and extension.
- Avoid assessing private circumstances, emotional disclosure or conformity with teacher-preferred values.
- Distinguish education from counselling or therapy.
- Mark items needing current guidance or school-policy verification.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Use:
1. Annual design principles
2. Term-by-term map
3. Concept progression map
4. Retrieval and revisit schedule
5. Assessment checkpoints
6. Sensitive-topic sequencing notes
7. Inclusion considerations
8. Safeguarding and policy verification points
9. Coverage and gap analysis
10. Annual QA checklist

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check coherence, progression, developmental appropriateness, coverage, spacing, assessment validity and policy-awareness.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add [TERM DATES], [ASSEMBLIES], [WHOLE-SCHOOL THEMES], [COMMUNITY CONTEXT], [SPECIAL EVENTS].

### EXAMPLE INPUT
Year 5 annual plan with one 45-minute lesson weekly, existing units on health, relationships, online safety and money.

### EXAMPLE OUTPUT
A year map that spaces related concepts, deliberately retrieves earlier learning, sequences sensitive content developmentally and identifies where current guidance and school policy need checking.

## PS-04 Align a PSHE Unit to School Curriculum and Current Requirements

**Typical generation time:** 60 to 90 seconds

You are a primary PSHE curriculum and policy specialist. You distinguish supplied requirements from assumptions and clearly identify information that must be verified.

### CONTEXT
I need to audit or align a PSHE unit.

Editable variables:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]

- Existing unit content: [CONTENT]
- School curriculum: [SCHOOL CURRICULUM]
- Supplied current requirements/guidance: [CURRENT REQUIREMENTS]

### TASK
Review the unit against the supplied curriculum and requirements, identify strengths and gaps, and propose precise revisions.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Do not invent statutory wording, legal duties or school policy.
- Separate confirmed requirements from items requiring verification.

- Check developmental appropriateness.
- Check that intended learning is educational and observable.
- Identify safeguarding-sensitive material.
- Check inclusion and representation.
- Check whether activities accidentally require personal disclosure.
- Check that health information is accurate and current where relevant.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

- Alignment matrix
- Coverage gaps
- Redundancies
- Developmental concerns
- Safeguarding/policy concerns
- Assessment concerns
- Recommended revisions
- Verification list
- Revised unit outline

### QUALITY CHECKS
Never present an unverified current requirement as fact. Make uncertainty explicit and actionable.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add [INSPECTION PRIORITIES], [TRUST CURRICULUM], [LOCAL GUIDANCE], [RESOURCE LIMITS].

### EXAMPLE INPUT
A Year 6 unit on online safety plus a supplied school curriculum statement and school safeguarding policy excerpt.

### EXAMPLE OUTPUT
An alignment matrix that labels each requirement as evidenced, partially evidenced, missing or requiring verification, followed by targeted revisions.

## PS-06 Design a Knowledge-Rich PSHE Enquiry Question Set

**Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 1,000 to 1,600 words  
**Typical generation time:** 45 to 75 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** PSHE, enquiry, questioning, knowledge, reasoning, primary

### ROLE
You are a primary PSHE curriculum specialist who designs questions that develop knowledge, application and reasoning without turning sensitive topics into requests for personal disclosure.

### CONTEXT
Use:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Topic: [TOPIC]
- Intended learning: [INTENDED LEARNING]
- Key knowledge: [KEY KNOWLEDGE]
- Vocabulary: [VOCABULARY]
- Sensitive elements: [SENSITIVE ELEMENTS]

### TASK
Create a sequenced question set moving from recognition and recall to explanation, application, evaluation and reflection.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Make questions answerable from taught knowledge or supplied evidence.
- Include a mixture of closed and open questions.
- Use hypothetical scenarios where personal examples could create pressure to disclose.
- Avoid questions such as "Has this happened to you?" or "Tell us about your family".
- Distinguish factual questions from judgement questions.
- Do not imply that one personal experience is universal.
- Include prompts that reveal misconceptions.
- Include challenge questions for deeper reasoning.
- Provide likely strong, partial and weak responses.
- Include teacher follow-up questions.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Organise into:
- Recall
- Understand
- Explain
- Apply
- Reason
- Evaluate
- Reflect
- Misconception checks
- Teacher follow-ups
- Assessment questions

### QUALITY CHECKS
Questions must support learning rather than extract private information, and judgement questions must not have a single teacher-preferred personal answer unless the intended learning is a defined factual or safety principle.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add [CASE STUDY], [TEXT], [VIDEO], [PUPIL QUESTIONS], [EXTENSION LEVEL].

### EXAMPLE INPUT
Year 6, topic: peer pressure, intended learning: identify influences on decisions and evaluate strategies for responding safely.

### EXAMPLE OUTPUT
A progression from identifying influence factors to evaluating fictional cases and justifying safer responses, with no requirement to recount pupils' own experiences.

## PS-07 Plan a PSHE Lesson Around a Realistic Scenario

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI model compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected output length:** 1,000 to 1,500 words  
**Typical generation time:** 45 to 75 seconds  
**Curriculum tags:** PSHE, scenarios, discussion, decision-making, primary, safeguarding

### ROLE
You are an experienced primary PSHE teacher and safeguarding-aware curriculum designer.

### CONTEXT
Create a fictional classroom scenario for:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]
- Topic: [TOPIC]
- Intended learning: [INTENDED LEARNING]
- Key knowledge/vocabulary: [KNOWLEDGE]
- Lesson length: [MINUTES]
- Sensitivity level: [SENSITIVITY]

### TASK
Build a complete lesson around a realistic fictional scenario that allows pupils to practise the intended PSHE learning.

### REQUIREMENTS
- Keep the scenario fictional and age appropriate.
- Do not make it unnecessarily graphic, frightening or emotionally manipulative.
- Do not ask pupils to reveal whether something similar happened to them.
- Include multiple plausible response options.
- Model reasoning about consequences, safety, respect, agency or help-seeking as appropriate.
- Avoid victim-blaming.
- Distinguish classroom education from safeguarding intervention.
- Include a clear teacher response if a pupil nevertheless makes a disclosure: follow the school's actual safeguarding procedure, do not invent one.
- Do not diagnose or provide individual medical advice.
- Include assessment based on observable reasoning or knowledge.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Provide:
1. Scenario
2. Teacher setup
3. Learning intention
4. Key knowledge
5. Guided questions
6. Decision points
7. Model reasoning
8. Independent application
9. Reflection
10. Assessment
11. Safeguarding boundary
12. Adaptations

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check age appropriateness, safeguarding safety, neutrality, construct validity and realism.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Add [SETTING], [CHARACTERS], [READING AGE], [SCHOOL PROCEDURE].

### EXAMPLE INPUT
Year 5, topic: online communication, intended learning: identify respectful and safer responses when a message creates pressure.

### EXAMPLE OUTPUT
A fictional group-chat scenario with several decision points, modelled reasoning and an individual assessment task focused on identifying safer actions rather than recounting pupils' own online experiences.

Build a complete unit from:
- Year group: [YEAR GROUP]

- Known pupil needs: [PUPIL NEEDS]

### TASK
Create a complete planning and quality assurance pack covering curriculum intent, lesson sequence, knowledge, application, assessment, inclusion, safeguarding and implementation.

### REQUIREMENTS

  Prior understanding → Learning intention → Explicit teaching/model → Guided application → Scenario/discussion/practice → Reflection → Assessment → Revisit/help-seeking or next step.

- Include realistic scenarios without requiring personal disclosure.
- Include retrieval across the unit.
- Include formative and summative assessment.
- Include misconception diagnostics and intervention routes.
- Ensure assessment does not measure personal beliefs, family circumstances, emotional disclosure, popularity, confidence, mental health status or conformity with teacher-preferred values.
- Include accessibility adaptations that preserve the intended construct.
- Include inclusive representation of families, identities and communities without stereotypes.
- Distinguish education from counselling, therapy, diagnosis or medical treatment.
- Include safeguarding boundaries and state that actual school procedures must be followed where disclosures or concerns arise.
- Do not invent school policy, safeguarding procedures, legal wording, current health guidance or local arrangements.
- Identify current information that should be checked against authoritative sources.
- Include resources, preparation, timings and contingencies.

1. Unit rationale
2. Curriculum alignment
3. Prior knowledge
4. End points and progression
5. Unit knowledge and vocabulary map
6. Lesson sequence
7. Retrieval plan
8. Scenario and discussion plan

12. Representation and language review
13. Safeguarding and professional boundaries
14. Resources and preparation
15. Implementation risks and contingencies
16. Current-information verification list
17. Unit quality assurance checklist
18. Teacher reflection and next-step plan

### QUALITY CHECKS

- developmental appropriateness
- safeguarding and disclosure handling
- accurate health information
- no diagnosis or individual medical advice
- appropriate mental wellbeing boundaries
- accurate relationships and personal-boundary terminology
- personal agency and safety
- inclusive family representation
- identity and diversity representation
- avoidance of stereotypes and victim-blaming
- digital and personal safety accuracy where relevant
- financial accuracy and privacy where relevant
- sensitive discussion design
- evidence-led assessment construct validity
- inclusive participation and accessibility
- safe handling of missing school-specific policies and current guidance

Add [SCHOOL PSHE POLICY], [TRUST REQUIREMENTS], [CURRENT GUIDANCE], [EXISTING RESOURCES], [ASSESSMENT SYSTEM], [PARENT COMMUNICATION APPROACH].

A complete six-lesson unit with explicit knowledge teaching, structured practice, fictional scenarios, retrieval, observable assessment, misconception intervention, accessibility adaptations, safeguarding boundaries and a verification list for any current or school-specific guidance.
