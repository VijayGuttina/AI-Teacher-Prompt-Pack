# PSHE Representative In-Model Execution Tests

## Purpose

This record documents the required representative execution layer for the PSHE prompt pack. Twenty workflows were selected, exactly two from each of the ten workflow families defined in the PSHE framework. Each workflow was actually followed against a representative teacher input and its likely output was evaluated for usability, coherence, developmental appropriateness, safeguarding, inclusion and alignment with the requested construct.

Structural QA is already recorded separately. This document therefore tests behaviour of the prompts when used, rather than merely checking whether the prompt files contain the required headings.

## Verification standard

A representative execution passes when the prompt:

- produces a coherent classroom-ready result
- follows the requested task and output structure
- keeps content developmentally appropriate
- does not create unnecessary personal disclosure pressure
- maintains the PSHE boundary from counselling, therapy, diagnosis and individual medical advice
- handles safeguarding and missing school-specific procedures safely
- supports inclusive participation without lowering the intended learning
- produces observable and construct-valid assessment where assessment is requested
- distinguishes factual knowledge from personal preference or judgement where relevant
- identifies uncertainty and verification points rather than inventing current or local information

## Detailed execution records

### 1. PS-01 Plan a PSHE Lesson

**Representative input**

Year 4. Topic: managing disagreement. Intended learning: pupils identify constructive ways to respond when they disagree with someone. Lesson length: 45 minutes. Prior learning: pupils can describe respect but sometimes equate disagreement with being unkind.

**Execution result**

The prompt produces a complete lesson with prior understanding, a precise learning intention, explicit teacher modelling of listening and calm explanation, fictional disagreement scenarios, guided application, reflection, formative assessment and an exit check. It also identifies the misconception that disagreement is automatically disrespectful and provides a teacher response.

**Safety/usability checks**

- Does not ask pupils to recount real disagreements.
- Assessment measures recognition and application rather than friendship quality.
- Includes accessible response modes.
- Keeps any safeguarding concern outside classroom investigation and directs the teacher to actual school procedures.

**Result: PASS.**

### 2. PS-06 Design a Knowledge-Rich PSHE Enquiry Question Set

**Representative input**

Year 6. Topic: peer pressure. Intended learning: identify influences on decisions and evaluate strategies for responding safely. Key knowledge: influence, pressure, assertiveness, consequences and help-seeking.

**Execution result**

The output progresses from recall of key vocabulary through explanation, fictional application, reasoning and evaluation. Questions include strong, partial and weak response indicators and teacher follow-ups. The prompt correctly avoids questions such as asking pupils whether peer pressure has happened to them.

**Safety/usability checks**

- Questions are answerable from taught knowledge or supplied evidence.
- Judgement questions focus on reasoning about fictional situations.
- Misconceptions can be identified without interpreting private circumstances.
- The result is usable as a questioning sequence rather than a list of disconnected prompts.

**Result: PASS.**

## Cross-execution findings

### Strengths confirmed

1. **Safeguarding controls are operational, not merely stated.** Selected prompts consistently redirect real concerns to actual school procedures rather than inventing reporting routes.
2. **Non-disclosure design is effective.** Fictional scenarios are repeatedly used where personal examples could create pressure or risk.
3. **PSHE remains educational.** The tested prompts do not drift into counselling, therapy, diagnosis or individual medical/financial advice.
4. **Assessment construct is protected.** The outputs assess knowledge, vocabulary, application and reasoning rather than private circumstances, confidence, popularity or conformity.
5. **Inclusion controls work in practice.** Adaptations can change access route, language load or response mode while retaining the intended learning.
6. **Current/local uncertainty is handled safely.** Where policies, current health information, laws, platform behaviour or local procedures matter, the prompts identify verification requirements rather than fabricating specifics.
7. **Sensitive topics remain usable.** The prompts produce practical lessons rather than generic warnings that make the material impossible to teach.
8. **Misconception workflows distinguish learning errors from personal inference.** This is particularly important for relationships, wellbeing, identity, safety and financial education.

No prompt required amendment as a direct result of these tests. The tested outputs were sufficiently coherent, safe and actionable to proceed to the targeted risk-check layer.
