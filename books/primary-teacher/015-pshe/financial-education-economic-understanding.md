# Financial Education & Economic Understanding

This family provides ten copy/paste-ready ChatGPT workflows for primary PSHE teaching about money, saving, spending, needs and wants, budgeting, value, borrowing, risk, work, income, economic choices and financial decision-making. The workflows are designed to build practical financial capability without requiring pupils to disclose household income, debt, family circumstances or access to money.

## Family-wide controls

Every workflow must:
- use developmentally appropriate financial concepts, examples and expectations;
- distinguish financial education from financial advice, regulated financial services advice or personalised recommendations;
- avoid asking pupils to disclose household income, savings, debt, benefits, financial hardship, spending habits, family employment or other private circumstances;
- use fictional households, generic examples or school-provided information rather than real pupil finances;
- never imply that financial circumstances are a simple result of individual choices or effort;
- avoid shaming pupils or families because of income, spending, housing, employment or access to resources;
- distinguish needs, wants, preferences, priorities and constraints;
- teach budgeting as a planning tool rather than a moral judgement;
- explain that prices, wages, taxes, interest rates, regulations, products and economic conditions can change over time;
- use current reliable sources when discussing current prices, tax rules, benefits, financial products, consumer protections or economic statistics;
- never invent current prices, interest rates, legal requirements, benefits, financial products, bank policies or consumer rights;
- avoid recommending specific financial products or providers to children or families;
- distinguish saving, spending, borrowing, giving and investing where these concepts are taught;
- introduce borrowing and financial risk carefully and avoid encouraging pupils to take on debt;
- use age-appropriate examples of earning, work and enterprise without suggesting every child must become an entrepreneur or that paid work is the measure of personal worth;
- avoid stereotypes about occupations, income, gender, family structure, geography or social class;
- make clear that responsible financial decisions depend on circumstances, information, constraints and risk;
- assess observable financial knowledge, vocabulary, calculation, reasoning and decision-making rather than household circumstances or personal financial behaviour;
- preserve the intended construct when adapting for SEND, EAL or other access needs;
- use fictional scenarios for sensitive decisions and allow pupils to reason without revealing personal financial experiences;
- ensure classroom activities involving money, purchases or fundraising use school-approved arrangements and never invent permissions or handling procedures.
