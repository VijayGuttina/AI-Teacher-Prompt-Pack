# Discussion, Scenarios & Sensitive Topics

This family provides copy-paste-ready workflows for planning PSHE discussion, scenarios and sensitive-topic teaching. The workflows are designed to support safe, structured classroom learning without turning PSHE into counselling, therapy, diagnosis or personal disclosure.

## Family principles

Use these controls throughout every workflow:

- Keep discussion educational, purposeful and developmentally appropriate.
- Never require pupils to disclose personal experiences, family circumstances, mental-health history, trauma, abuse, self-harm, sexuality or identity details, political views, household finances or other private information.
- Prefer fictional, hypothetical, anonymised, public or generic scenarios when sensitive material is involved.
- Do not use disclosure circles, peer interrogation, forced sharing or activities that pressure pupils to reveal private experiences.
- Do not ask pupils to role-play abuse, trauma, serious distress or other harmful experiences.
- Distinguish ordinary disagreement, uncertainty, poor judgement, risk, unsafe behaviour and safeguarding concerns.
- Do not invent safeguarding, reporting, pastoral, medical or school-specific procedures. Use supplied school policy and procedures where relevant.
- Do not diagnose pupils from comments, behaviour or scenario responses.
- Make clear how a pupil can pause, opt for an alternative response mode, or seek appropriate adult help where the topic requires it.
- Avoid victim-blaming and avoid implying that a person is responsible for another person's harmful behaviour.
- Handle genuinely contested issues neutrally and evidence-led. Do not force a teacher-preferred conclusion where legitimate disagreement is part of the learning.
- Verify current health, legal, digital, safeguarding or policy claims when they are material to the lesson.
- Provide inclusive examples without stereotyping, tokenism or assuming family structures, cultures, identities or experiences.
- Assess observable knowledge, vocabulary, reasoning, decision-making and application, not confidence, emotional state or willingness to disclose.
- Preserve the intended PSHE construct when making accessibility adaptations.

## DS-01 Plan a PSHE Discussion Lesson

**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 900 to 1,400 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 30 to 60 seconds  
**Curriculum Tags:** PSHE, discussion, questioning, communication, reflection, KS1, KS2, Year 1, Year 2, Year 3, Year 4, Year 5, Year 6

### ROLE

Act as an experienced primary PSHE teacher and curriculum designer who specialises in structured classroom discussion, developmental appropriateness and safe participation.

### CONTEXT

I am planning a PSHE discussion lesson for **[YEAR GROUP]** on **[TOPIC]**. The intended learning is **[LEARNING INTENTION]**. The lesson will last **[LESSON LENGTH]** minutes. Relevant school or curriculum guidance is **[SUPPLIED GUIDANCE OR NONE]**.

### TASK

Design a complete discussion-based PSHE lesson that develops pupils' understanding through explicit teaching, structured questioning, guided discussion, application and reflection.

### REQUIREMENTS

1. Start with prior knowledge and a clear learning intention.
2. Identify the substantive knowledge pupils need before discussion.
3. Provide a carefully sequenced set of teacher questions from accessible to more demanding.
4. Include at least one structured pair or small-group discussion routine and one whole-class routine.
5. Give sentence stems that support explanation, disagreement and respectful listening.
6. Use hypothetical or generic examples where personal disclosure could otherwise be invited.
7. Include teacher guidance for correcting misconceptions without shaming pupils.
8. State explicitly what pupils should not be required to disclose.
9. Include an alternative participation route for pupils who do not wish to speak publicly.
10. Include a short formative assessment and an exit question.
11. Distinguish ordinary disagreement from statements that may indicate a safeguarding concern, without asking pupils to disclose personal circumstances.
12. If a safeguarding or school-specific procedure is needed, identify the point at which the teacher should follow the supplied policy rather than inventing a process.
13. Keep all examples age-appropriate and avoid unnecessary emotionally distressing detail.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Use these headings:

1. Lesson overview
2. Prior understanding
3. Learning intention and success criteria
4. Key knowledge and vocabulary
5. Teacher explanation
6. Discussion sequence
7. Questions and sentence stems
8. Misconceptions and teacher responses
9. Safe participation and boundaries
10. Assessment and exit ticket
11. Resources and preparation
12. Teacher quality check

### QUALITY CHECKS

Before finalising, check that discussion is learning-focused, no personal disclosure is required, examples are developmentally appropriate, safeguarding procedures are not invented, assessment measures observable PSHE learning, and alternative participation is available.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Adjust for **[READING AGE]**, **[SEND ACCESS NEEDS]**, **[EAL NEEDS]**, **[CLASS SIZE]**, **[DISCUSSION ROUTINE]** and **[SCHOOL POLICY]**.

### Example Input

Year 5; topic: responding to peer pressure; learning intention: identify factors that can influence decisions and practise safe ways to respond; 50 minutes.

### Example Output Excerpt

The discussion begins with a fictional scenario about a pupil being encouraged to make a choice they are uncomfortable with. Pupils first identify observable influences, then compare possible responses. The teacher explicitly states that pupils do not need to describe anything that has happened to them.

### CONTEXT

Create scenario cards for **[YEAR GROUP]** on **[TOPIC]**. The learning goal is **[LEARNING GOAL]**. I need **[NUMBER]** cards for a lesson or sequence lasting **[TIME]** minutes.

### TASK

Create fictional scenarios that allow pupils to identify information, consider options, explain consequences and practise safe decision-making without requiring personal disclosure.

### REQUIREMENTS

Do not require pupils to role-play harmful behaviour. ### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

### Example Input

Year 3; topic: online communication; six cards; learning goal: identify safe and respectful responses when communication becomes uncomfortable.

### Example Output Excerpt

A fictional pupil receives a message asking for information they should not share. Pupils identify the warning signs, compare safe responses and explain why seeking help is appropriate. No pupil is asked whether anything similar has happened to them.

### ROLE

Act as a senior primary PSHE curriculum and safeguarding-aware education specialist. You are not acting as a counsellor, therapist, clinician or safeguarding officer.

### CONTEXT

I need to teach **[SENSITIVE TOPIC]** to **[YEAR GROUP]**. The learning intention is **[LEARNING INTENTION]**. Relevant school policy or safeguarding guidance supplied to me is **[POLICY OR NONE]**.

### TASK

Design a safe teaching approach that addresses the educational content directly while preventing pressure to disclose personal experiences.

### REQUIREMENTS

1. Separate educational objectives from pastoral or therapeutic needs.

3. Identify questions that could unintentionally invite disclosure and replace them with safer alternatives.
4. State what pupils should never be required to reveal.

9. Do not diagnose, counsel or clinically interpret pupil responses.
10. Include a pause or alternative route if discussion becomes emotionally difficult.
11. Avoid victim-blaming and avoid implying that harmful experiences result from poor choices alone.
12. Use current reliable guidance where health, law or safeguarding claims require it.
13. Include an assessment method that does not depend on personal disclosure.

Use these headings:

1. Educational purpose
2. Sensitive-content risk assessment
3. Safe learning boundaries
4. Teacher introduction script
5. Safer question replacements
6. Teaching and discussion sequence
7. Alternative participation routes
8. Handling volunteered disclosures
9. Safeguarding and policy boundary
10. Assessment
11. Resources and preparation
12. Final safety and quality check

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check that the lesson remains educational, does not become counselling, does not request personal stories, does not invent procedures, and provides an explicit route to appropriate adult help where required by supplied policy.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Adapt for **[TOPIC]**, **[YEAR GROUP]**, **[SCHOOL POLICY]**, **[CLASS SIZE]**, **[SEND/EAL NEEDS]** and **[LESSON LENGTH]**.

### Example Input

Year 6; topic: recognising unsafe online interactions; learning intention: identify warning signs and explain appropriate help-seeking; school safeguarding procedure supplied separately.

### Example Output Excerpt

The lesson uses fictional messages rather than asking pupils about their own online experiences. If a pupil volunteers a personal disclosure, the teacher acknowledges it, avoids investigative questioning, follows the supplied school procedure and does not use the disclosure as classroom discussion material.

### TASK

### Example Input

Year 5; topic: making safe choices when unsure whether an online request is appropriate.

### Example Output Excerpt

Pupils classify fictional situations as something they can handle independently, something to check with a trusted adult, or something requiring immediate adult help under school procedures. The categories are taught as decision-making tools, not as diagnostic labels.

**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 1,000 to 1,500 words  
**Typical Generation Time:** 30 to 60 seconds  
**Curriculum Tags:** PSHE, questioning, discussion, reasoning, reflection, vocabulary, KS1, KS2

### ROLE

Act as an expert primary PSHE questioning designer.

### CONTEXT

I teach **[YEAR GROUP]** and need a questioning toolkit for **[TOPIC]**. The main learning objective is **[OBJECTIVE]**.

### TASK

Generate a bank of high-quality questions that move pupils from recognition and recall to explanation, application, evaluation and reflection without inviting inappropriate personal disclosure.

### REQUIREMENTS

Organise questions by cognitive purpose. Include knowledge checks, vocabulary questions, scenario questions, consequence questions, evidence questions, perspective questions, decision questions, respectful disagreement questions, reflection questions and metacognitive questions. Mark questions that are unsuitable for sensitive contexts and provide safer alternatives. Avoid questions such as “Has this happened to you?” or “How would you feel if this happened to you?” when they could pressure disclosure. Use fictional or generic framing instead. ### OUTPUT FORMAT

Create a structured question bank with columns: Purpose, Question, Follow-up, Expected thinking, Misconception to probe, Safe-framing note.

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check age suitability, progression in thinking, neutral framing, no forced disclosure and alignment to the stated learning objective.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Adapt for **[TOPIC]**, **[YEAR GROUP]**, **[COGNITIVE DEMAND]**, **[READING AGE]** and **[DISCUSSION FORMAT]**.

### Example Input

Year 2; topic: friendship; objective: identify behaviours that help friendships work well.

### Example Output Excerpt

Instead of asking pupils to describe their own friendship problems, ask: “In this fictional situation, which behaviour is likely to help the friendship and why?” Follow with: “What evidence from the scenario supports your answer?”

### REQUIREMENTS

Do not infer mental-health status, family circumstances, abuse, trauma, personal beliefs or safeguarding status from responses. Do not treat an unusual personal statement as proof of a wider problem. Then provide a short group intervention plan and a misconception priority ranking.

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check that conclusions are evidence-led, that no clinical or safeguarding diagnosis is made from educational responses, and that assessment remains focused on observable PSHE learning.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Adapt for **[TOPIC]**, **[YEAR GROUP]**, **[ASSESSMENT FORMAT]**, **[CURRICULUM]** and **[MISCONCEPTION LIST]**.

### Example Input

Five Year 6 responses to a fictional peer-pressure scenario, including one response that says the person is “to blame because they agreed at first”.

### Example Output Excerpt

The response is diagnosed as a misunderstanding of responsibility and pressure, not as evidence about the pupil's personal experiences. The intervention uses a new fictional scenario to distinguish responsibility for one's own choices from responsibility for another person's harmful behaviour.

## Family-Level Quality Gate

Before using any DS workflow, confirm:

- The intended PSHE learning is explicit.
- Discussion is structured rather than coercive.
- Sensitive content is framed educationally.
- No pupil is required to disclose personal experiences or private information.
- Fictional, hypothetical, generic or public scenarios are used where appropriate.
- No harmful role-play, trauma simulation or distressing reenactment is used.
- Safeguarding procedures are taken from supplied school policy and are never invented.
- Teacher responses do not diagnose or clinically interpret pupils.
- Victim-blaming and unsafe assumptions are challenged.
- Genuine disagreement is handled neutrally and respectfully.
- Current health, legal, digital and policy claims are verified when necessary.
- Inclusion and accessibility adaptations preserve the intended learning construct.
- Assessment measures observable PSHE knowledge, reasoning, application and reflection.
- Confidence, emotional state and willingness to disclose are not used as assessment criteria.
- The lesson or unit is realistic to deliver, including resources, preparation, timing and contingencies.
