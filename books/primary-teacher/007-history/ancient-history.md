# Ancient History Workflows

## AH-01 — Ancient Civilisation Overview

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 700–1,000 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, History, Ancient History, Civilisations, Chronology

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[CIVILISATION]` | `[PERIOD]` | `[ENQUIRY_FOCUS]` | `[PRIOR_KNOWLEDGE]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History teacher and historian with strong knowledge of the specified ancient civilisation and evidence-based historical teaching.

### CONTEXT
Teach `[CIVILISATION]` during `[PERIOD]` to `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils, focusing on `[ENQUIRY_FOCUS]` and building from `[PRIOR_KNOWLEDGE]`.

### TASK
Create a concise, enquiry-led overview that establishes chronology, key features and relevant evidence without becoming an encyclopaedic list.

### REQUIREMENTS
Distinguish established evidence from interpretation. Use precise dates or periods where appropriate. Avoid presenting an ancient civilisation as culturally uniform or static, and identify important differences in social status, geography or period when they affect the enquiry.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Enquiry question; chronology; context; key features; evidence; vocabulary; teaching sequence; questions; answers; caveats.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check dates, names, terminology and geographical relationships. Avoid anachronistic labels and unsupported claims about what all people believed or did.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for Ancient Egypt, Greece, Rome, the Maya, Shang China or another specified civilisation.

### Example Input
Year 4; civilisation: Ancient Egypt; period: c.3000–30 BCE; focus: how historians know about Egyptian life; prior knowledge: chronology and primary/secondary sources.

### Example Output
Build the overview around evidence such as buildings, artefacts, writing and human remains, while explaining that surviving evidence is uneven and does not represent every Egyptian equally.

## AH-02 — Ancient Society Comparison

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, History, Ancient History, Comparison, Historical Reasoning

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[SOCIETY_A]` | `[SOCIETY_B]` | `[THEME]` | `[EVIDENCE]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History specialist designing evidence-led comparative history.

### CONTEXT
Compare `[SOCIETY_A]` and `[SOCIETY_B]` for `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils through `[THEME]`, using `[EVIDENCE]` where supplied.

### TASK
Create a comparison using equivalent criteria and require pupils to support similarities and differences with historical evidence.

### REQUIREMENTS
Define each comparison category clearly before comparing. Avoid judging one society against an unexamined modern standard. Distinguish chronological overlap from actual interaction and avoid assuming that similarity proves influence.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Enquiry question; comparison criteria; evidence table; similarities; differences; interpretation; questions; answers; conclusion.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure the categories are genuinely comparable and that every conclusion is supported by the evidence. Flag where evidence is incomplete.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for government, religion, daily life, warfare, technology, trade or social structure.

### Example Input
Year 6; societies: Ancient Athens and Ancient Sparta; theme: education and citizenship; evidence: supplied extracts and artefacts.

### Example Output
Compare equivalent features, distinguish citizen groups from the whole population and require pupils to qualify conclusions where the surviving evidence is uneven.

## AH-03 — Ancient Source Enquiry

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 700–1,000 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, History, Historical Sources, Provenance, Evidence

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[SOURCE]` | `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]` | `[TOPIC]` | `[SOURCE_CONTEXT]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History teacher and source-analysis specialist.

### CONTEXT
Use `[SOURCE]` to investigate `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]` about `[TOPIC]` with `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils. Known source context is `[SOURCE_CONTEXT]`.

### TASK
Design a source enquiry that separates what the source says or shows from what historians infer about its usefulness and limitations.

### REQUIREMENTS
Analyse content, provenance, purpose and context where evidence permits. Never fabricate a translation, inscription, quotation, date, author or provenance. If source information is missing, state that it is missing rather than filling the gap.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Source presentation; observation; inference; provenance; purpose; usefulness; limitation; questions; model answers; caveats.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Verify that claims about the source are supported by the supplied material. Distinguish contemporary evidence from later interpretation.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for artefacts, inscriptions, written accounts, images, architecture or archaeological evidence.

### Example Input
Year 5; source: supplied image of an Egyptian tomb painting; question: what might this source tell us about work and society?; source context: tomb painting, date supplied by teacher.

### Example Output
Ask pupils first to identify visible features, then infer cautiously what they may indicate, while explaining why a tomb painting cannot automatically represent every person's working life.

## AH-04 — Ancient Daily Life

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 700–1,000 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, History, Ancient History, Daily Life, Social History

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[CIVILISATION]` | `[SOCIAL_GROUP]` | `[TOPIC]` | `[EVIDENCE]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary ancient-history specialist designing evidence-led reconstruction of everyday life.

### CONTEXT
Investigate `[TOPIC]` for `[SOCIAL_GROUP]` within `[CIVILISATION]` for `[YEAR_GROUP]`, using `[EVIDENCE]`.

### TASK
Create a historically cautious reconstruction showing what the evidence supports, what is inferred and what remains uncertain.

### REQUIREMENTS
Make social group, period and location explicit. Avoid projecting modern categories directly onto ancient societies. Do not use evidence from elites, cities or one gender as if it represented everyone.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Enquiry question; context; evidence; supported reconstruction; inference; uncertainty; vocabulary; questions; answers.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that each reconstruction claim is traceable to evidence and that uncertainty is visible rather than hidden behind fluent prose.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for homes, food, work, childhood, education, clothing, trade or leisure.

### Example Input
Year 4; civilisation: Roman Britain; social group: children in a town; topic: education; evidence: archaeological finds and supplied written evidence.

### Example Output
Construct a cautious account of possible schooling while clearly separating evidence for particular children or locations from claims about Roman Britain as a whole.

## AH-05 — Archaeology Enquiry

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, History, Archaeology, Evidence, Inference

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[SITE]` | `[ARTEFACTS]` | `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]` | `[SITE_CONTEXT]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History teacher with expertise in archaeological reasoning.

### CONTEXT
Pupils investigate `[SITE]` using `[ARTEFACTS]` to answer `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]`. Available context is `[SITE_CONTEXT]`.

### TASK
Create an inference activity in which pupils move systematically from observations about archaeological evidence to cautious historical interpretations.

### REQUIREMENTS
Require pupils to identify what an artefact directly demonstrates, what it may suggest and what alternative explanations remain possible. Explain that archaeological evidence is affected by preservation, excavation and survival.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Site context; artefact set; observations; inferences; alternative explanations; questions; answers; limitations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Do not invent archaeological findings. Ensure each inference is proportionate to the evidence and that absence of an artefact is not treated automatically as proof of absence in the past.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for settlements, burials, tools, pottery, buildings or trade evidence.

### Example Input
Year 5; site: Roman settlement; artefacts: pottery fragments, coins and tools; question: what might these finds suggest about the settlement?

### Example Output
Have pupils classify observations first, then construct several possible interpretations and identify which additional evidence would help distinguish them.

Explore `[BELIEF_OR_PRACTICE]` in `[CIVILISATION]` during `[PERIOD]` for `[YEAR_GROUP]`, using `[EVIDENCE]`.

### TASK
Explain what the evidence can reasonably tell pupils about beliefs and practices, while distinguishing ancient testimony, archaeological evidence and later interpretation.

### Example Input
Year 4; civilisation: Ancient Egypt; practice: burial; evidence: tombs, objects and supplied inscriptions; period: New Kingdom.

### Example Output
Use the evidence to explore burial practices while explaining why surviving elite tomb evidence cannot automatically establish identical practices for every Egyptian.

## AH-07 — Ancient Technology

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, History, Ancient Technology, Innovation, Significance

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[TECHNOLOGY]` | `[CIVILISATION]` | `[EVIDENCE]` | `[ENQUIRY_FOCUS]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History teacher specialising in technological change and historical significance.

### CONTEXT
Investigate `[TECHNOLOGY]` in `[CIVILISATION]` for `[YEAR_GROUP]`, using `[EVIDENCE]` and focusing on `[ENQUIRY_FOCUS]`.

### TASK
Trace the development, use and historical significance of the technology without assuming that innovation produced one automatic social outcome.

### REQUIREMENTS
Distinguish invention, adaptation, diffusion and later improvement where evidence allows. Consider who used the technology, for what purpose and with what limitations. Avoid attributing inventions to a civilisation when the evidence supports a more complex development history.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Development; context; evidence; uses; significance; limitations; questions; answers; uncertainty.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check chronology and causal claims. Do not equate “oldest known evidence” with definitive proof of the absolute first invention unless justified.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for writing, irrigation, roads, concrete, metallurgy, transport or agricultural technology.

### Example Input
Year 5; technology: Roman roads; civilisation: Roman Britain; evidence: road remains and maps; focus: why roads mattered to the Roman settlement and administration.

### Example Output
Link road construction to movement and administration while avoiding the claim that every road was built for exactly the same purpose or had identical effects.

## AH-08 — Ancient Legacy

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 700–1,000 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, History, Legacy, Continuity, Historical Interpretation

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[ANCIENT_SOCIETY]` | `[LATER_PERIOD]` | `[CLAIM]` | `[EVIDENCE]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History specialist assessing claims about continuity, influence and legacy.

### CONTEXT
Test `[CLAIM]` about the relationship between `[ANCIENT_SOCIETY]` and `[LATER_PERIOD]` using `[EVIDENCE]`.

### TASK
Determine whether the evidence supports continuity, reuse, influence, adaptation, indirect transmission or simple similarity.

### REQUIREMENTS
Require pupils to identify the proposed link and evidence for it. Distinguish direct influence from later revival or independent development. Include evidence that could weaken the claim.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Claim; chronology; evidence; proposed link; counter-evidence; alternative explanation; limitations; judgement.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Do not infer influence from similarity alone. Check the chronological pathway and whether there is evidence of transmission, knowledge or deliberate reuse.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for Roman architecture, Greek ideas, ancient writing systems, engineering or political concepts.

### Example Input
Year 6; ancient society: Rome; later period: Georgian Britain; claim: Georgian architecture directly copied Roman architecture; evidence: supplied buildings and historical context.

### Example Output
Ask pupils to test whether similarity resulted from direct Roman influence, later classical revival or other architectural traditions, and to justify the strongest conclusion supported by the evidence.

## AH-09 — Ancient History Misconception Diagnosis

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, History, Assessment, Misconceptions, Historical Reasoning

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[TOPIC]` | `[PUPIL_RESPONSES]` | `[TARGET_OBJECTIVE]` | `[RECENT_TEACHING]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History diagnostic specialist.

### CONTEXT
Analyse `[PUPIL_RESPONSES]` from `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils studying `[TOPIC]` against `[TARGET_OBJECTIVE]`, considering `[RECENT_TEACHING]`.

### TASK
Identify observable historical errors and generate discriminating questions that distinguish factual recall problems, source interpretation problems, chronology errors and broader conceptual misunderstandings.

### REQUIREMENTS
Separate the pupil's stated evidence from the teacher's interpretation. Do not infer misunderstanding solely from vocabulary or spelling. Include at least one diagnostic task requiring transfer to a new source or context.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Observed error; possible explanations; evidence; diagnostic question or source task; expected response patterns; interpretation; targeted response; follow-up check.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure diagnostic tasks distinguish plausible causes and do not simply repeat the original assessment question.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for individual conferencing, small-group intervention or whole-class hinge questions.

### Example Input
Year 5; topic: Roman Britain; response: “The Romans invaded Britain because everyone in Britain wanted to become Roman”; objective: explain possible motives for Roman expansion; recent teaching: map and source enquiry.

### Example Output
Separate the unsupported assumption about British people from the pupil's understanding of Roman motives and test whether they can distinguish different groups' perspectives using evidence.

## AH-10 — Adapt Ancient-History Enquiry

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, History, Adaptive Teaching, Sources, Historical Reasoning

### Editable Variables
`[ORIGINAL_TASK]` | `[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[HISTORICAL_OBJECTIVE]` | `[ACCESS_BARRIER]` | `[EVIDENCE]` | `[SUPPORT_AVAILABLE]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History teacher specialising in adaptive teaching and evidence-based historical enquiry.

### CONTEXT
The original task is `[ORIGINAL_TASK]` for `[YEAR_GROUP]`. The historical objective is `[HISTORICAL_OBJECTIVE]`; the access barrier is `[ACCESS_BARRIER]`; evidence and support available are `[EVIDENCE]` and `[SUPPORT_AVAILABLE]`.

### TASK
Adapt source presentation, language, sequencing, recording or participation so pupils can access the enquiry while preserving the central historical evidence and judgement demand.

### REQUIREMENTS
Analyse the barrier before choosing support. Consider reading, language, working memory, visual access, motor demands and recording separately. Do not remove the need to select evidence, distinguish observation from inference or reach a historical judgement. Include scaffold fading.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Original objective; barrier analysis; adapted task; source/evidence access; support; preserved historical demand; recording alternatives; independence route; review point.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Confirm that the pupil still has to reason from historical evidence and that the adaptation has not replaced the key judgement with a supplied answer.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for SEND, EAL, dyslexia, sensory access, motor access, working-memory or language barriers.

### Example Input
Year 5; original task: analyse a Roman source and judge what it reveals about Roman Britain; objective: use evidence to make a supported inference; barrier: difficulty reading dense text; evidence: original source plus teacher-provided vocabulary support.

### Example Output
Chunk the source, preserve key wording, add targeted vocabulary glosses and offer oral recording while retaining the pupil's responsibility for selecting evidence and making the inference.
