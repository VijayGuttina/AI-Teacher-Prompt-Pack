# British History Workflows

## BH-01 — British History Overview

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 700–1,000 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, British History, Chronology

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[PERIOD]` | `[FOCUS]` | `[PRIOR_KNOWLEDGE]` | `[DURATION]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History teacher and historian with strong knowledge of British chronology and historical enquiry.

### CONTEXT
Teach `[FOCUS]` within `[PERIOD]` to `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils, building from `[PRIOR_KNOWLEDGE]` in `[DURATION]`.

### TASK
Create a concise contextual overview that establishes chronology, significant developments, relevant people and groups, and the evidence pupils need for the stated enquiry.

### REQUIREMENTS
Keep the chronological and geographical scope explicit. Distinguish securely established evidence from interpretation or contested claims. Avoid reducing a period to famous individuals alone. Select details that serve the enquiry rather than producing an encyclopaedic list.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Enquiry question; context; timeline; key developments; people and groups; evidence; vocabulary; teaching sequence; questions; answers; misconception check.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Verify dates, terminology, sequence and geographical scope. Check that significance claims are justified rather than presented as self-evident.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for Romans, Anglo-Saxons, Vikings, Normans, Tudor Britain, Victorian Britain, World War periods or local history.

### Example Input
Year 4; period: Roman Britain; focus: how Roman rule changed life in Britain; prior knowledge: Iron Age Britain; duration: 60 minutes.

### Example Output
Establish a before-and-after chronology, then use evidence from settlements, roads, artefacts and written sources to examine different types of change while identifying important continuities.

## BH-02 — Monarchy and Power

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 700–1,000 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, History, British History, Monarchy, Power, Historical Interpretation

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[PERIOD]` | `[MONARCH_OR_RULERS]` | `[EVIDENCE]` | `[ENQUIRY]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History specialist with expertise in political and social history.

### CONTEXT
Investigate `[ENQUIRY]` during `[PERIOD]` for `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils, considering `[MONARCH_OR_RULERS]` and `[EVIDENCE]`.

### TASK
Examine how power was acquired, exercised, challenged and limited, using evidence from institutions, social groups and individuals rather than biography alone.

### REQUIREMENTS
Identify different centres of power and explain relationships between rulers and other groups. Distinguish formal authority from practical influence. Where sources are partisan, explain what they reveal and what they may conceal.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Context; power structure; key actors and groups; evidence set; source analysis; change over time; questions; model answers; judgement; limitations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Avoid implying that monarchs alone caused historical change. Check chronology and distinguish later interpretations from evidence produced at the time.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for Norman rule, Tudor monarchy, Magna Carta, the Civil War, the Restoration or changing constitutional power.

### Example Input
Year 6; period: Tudor Britain; monarchs: Henry VIII and Elizabeth I; evidence: proclamations, portraits and accounts; enquiry: how did monarchs maintain authority?

### Example Output
Compare royal messaging with evidence about institutions and social groups, then ask pupils to judge how far royal power depended on factors beyond the monarch personally.

## BH-03 — Conflict and Conquest

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 750–1,050 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, British History, Conflict, Causation, Consequences

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[EVENT]` | `[PERIOD]` | `[EVIDENCE]` | `[ENQUIRY]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History teacher and historian specialising in evidence-led explanations of conflict.

### CONTEXT
Teach `[ENQUIRY]` about `[EVENT]` in `[PERIOD]` to `[YEAR_GROUP]`, using `[EVIDENCE]`.

### TASK
Build a causal and chronological explanation of the conflict or conquest, distinguishing long-term conditions, triggering factors, actions during the event and consequences.

### REQUIREMENTS
Avoid single-cause narratives. Separate cause from trigger and consequence from later significance. Include evidence from more than one perspective where available and identify contested interpretations.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Chronology; background conditions; causes; trigger; key developments; evidence; perspectives; consequences; questions; answers; judgement.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Verify dates and sequence. Do not present a later outcome as inevitable, and flag claims whose evidence is disputed or incomplete.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for Roman invasion, Norman Conquest, Viking conflict, Wars of the Roses, Civil War or twentieth-century conflicts.

### Example Input
Year 6; event: Norman Conquest; evidence: Bayeux Tapestry, Domesday Book and selected chronicles; enquiry: why did William succeed?

### Example Output
Compare military, political and logistical factors rather than attributing success to one battle or one individual's personality.

Act as a primary History subject specialist in social and cultural history.

### CONTEXT
Explore `[THEME]` in `[PERIOD]` with `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils, comparing `[SOCIAL_GROUPS]` using `[EVIDENCE]`.

### TASK
Create an evidence-led comparison of experiences, opportunities, responsibilities and constraints across relevant groups.

### Example Input
Year 5; period: Victorian Britain; social groups: factory owner, skilled worker and child labourer; theme: working life; evidence: photographs, census extracts and testimony.

### Example Output
Compare working conditions and opportunities while explicitly acknowledging that experiences varied by occupation, location, age and household circumstances.

## BH-06 — Migration and Movement

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 700–1,000 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, History, British History, Migration, Social History, Evidence

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[PERIOD]` | `[GROUP]` | `[PLACE]` | `[EVIDENCE]` | `[ENQUIRY]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History specialist with expertise in migration and population history.

### CONTEXT
Investigate movement involving `[GROUP]` in `[PERIOD]` and `[PLACE]`, addressing `[ENQUIRY]` through `[EVIDENCE]`.

### TASK
Explore why people moved, the constraints and opportunities they encountered, their experiences and the effects of movement on receiving and sending communities.

### REQUIREMENTS
Avoid simplistic push/pull lists when motives were multiple or changing. Distinguish voluntary, constrained and forced movement where relevant. Avoid treating a group as homogeneous and include the limitations of surviving evidence.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Context; chronology; reasons for movement; evidence; experiences; responses; impacts; perspectives; limitations; questions; answers.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check terminology and historical context. Do not project modern migration categories or motives backwards without explanation.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for Huguenots, Irish migration, Jewish migration, Windrush, wartime evacuation or local migration histories.

### Example Input
Year 6; period: post-war Britain; group: Caribbean migrants; place: Britain; evidence: oral histories, photographs and newspaper extracts; enquiry: why did people move and what did they experience?

### Example Output
Use multiple sources to distinguish reasons for migration from later experiences and require pupils to compare perspectives without treating one testimony as universal.

## BH-08 — British Local and National Comparison

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, British History, Local History, Comparison

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[LOCATION_A]` | `[LOCATION_B]` | `[PERIOD]` | `[THEME]` | `[EVIDENCE]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History specialist with expertise in comparative and local history.

### CONTEXT
Compare `[LOCATION_A]` and `[LOCATION_B]` during `[PERIOD]` through `[THEME]`, using `[EVIDENCE]`.

### TASK
Create a fair historical comparison using the same criteria for both locations and requiring pupils to explain both similarities and differences.

### REQUIREMENTS

### QUALITY CHECKS

### Example Input
Year 5; locations: Manchester and a rural village; period: Victorian Britain; theme: industrial change; evidence: maps, census extracts and photographs.

### Example Output
Compare population, employment, transport and housing using consistent criteria, then ask which differences can be explained confidently from the evidence.

## BH-09 — British History Misconception Diagnosis

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, British History, Diagnosis, Assessment

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[TOPIC]` | `[PUPIL_RESPONSE]` | `[RECENT_TEACHING]` | `[TARGET_OBJECTIVE]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History diagnostic specialist with expertise in disciplinary thinking and historical knowledge.

### CONTEXT
Analyse `[PUPIL_RESPONSE]` from `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils studying `[TOPIC]` against `[TARGET_OBJECTIVE]`, considering `[RECENT_TEACHING]`.

### TASK
Identify the observable error, distinguish factual recall problems from disciplinary reasoning problems, and design a discriminating follow-up that tests competing explanations.

### REQUIREMENTS
Consider chronology, vocabulary, source interpretation, causation, significance, similarity/difference and change/continuity as distinct possibilities. Do not infer a fixed misconception from one response without further evidence.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Observable error; possible explanations; evidence for and against; diagnostic question or source task; expected response patterns; interpretation; targeted next step; follow-up check.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure the diagnostic task tests the relevant historical concept rather than merely rephrasing the original question.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for individual conferencing, small-group intervention or whole-class hinge questions.

### Example Input
Year 6; topic: Norman Conquest; response: “The Normans won because the English were weak”; recent teaching: Battle of Hastings; objective: explain multiple factors affecting William's success.

### Example Output
Test whether the pupil lacks factual knowledge, is overgeneralising from the battle, or cannot yet distinguish a factor from a complete explanation.

## BH-10 — Adapt British History Task

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, British History, Adaptive Teaching, Inclusion

### Editable Variables
`[ORIGINAL_TASK]` | `[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[HISTORICAL_OBJECTIVE]` | `[ACCESS_BARRIER]` | `[EVIDENCE]` | `[SUPPORT_AVAILABLE]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History teacher specialising in adaptive teaching and historical enquiry.

### CONTEXT
The original task is `[ORIGINAL_TASK]` for `[YEAR_GROUP]`. The historical objective is `[HISTORICAL_OBJECTIVE]`; the access barrier is `[ACCESS_BARRIER]`; relevant `[EVIDENCE]` and `[SUPPORT_AVAILABLE]` must be considered.

### TASK
Adapt language, source presentation, chronology support, representation or recording while preserving the core historical evidence and reasoning demand.

### REQUIREMENTS
Analyse the barrier before choosing support. Consider vocabulary, reading load, chronology, visual access, working memory, motor demands and recording separately. Do not remove source evaluation, comparison, causation, significance or judgement merely to make the task easier. Include scaffold fading.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Original objective; barrier analysis; adapted source/task; support; preserved evidence; preserved historical demand; recording alternatives; independence route; review point.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Confirm that the adapted task still requires pupils to interpret or reason from historical evidence and that support does not provide the intended judgement for them.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for SEND, EAL, dyslexia, visual access, working-memory needs, motor access or language barriers.

### Example Input
Year 5; original task: compare two Victorian sources about factory work; objective: infer similarities and differences in experiences; barrier: high reading load; evidence: two authentic short extracts; support: text-to-speech and vocabulary cards.

### Example Output
Reduce unnecessary linguistic complexity, retain the source claims and provenance information that matter, allow oral or structured recording, and preserve the pupil's responsibility for making the historical comparison.
