# Historical Enquiry and Questions Workflows

## HE-01 — Big Enquiry Question

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Historical Enquiry, Disciplinary Thinking

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[TOPIC]` | `[PERIOD]` | `[DISCIPLINARY_FOCUS]` | `[AVAILABLE_EVIDENCE]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History teacher and curriculum specialist with expertise in historical enquiry design.

### CONTEXT
Create a substantive enquiry for `[YEAR_GROUP]` about `[TOPIC]` in `[PERIOD]`, focused on `[DISCIPLINARY_FOCUS]` and grounded in `[AVAILABLE_EVIDENCE]`.

### TASK
Develop one overarching historical enquiry question that requires pupils to construct an answer through evidence, reasoning and explicit historical concepts rather than simple factual recall.

### REQUIREMENTS
Ensure the question is answerable at the intended age and evidence level. Define the disciplinary thinking required. Avoid questions with predetermined answers, purely moral judgements or claims that cannot be investigated with the available evidence. Include meaningful sub-questions that build towards, rather than merely repeat, the final judgement.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Enquiry question; rationale; disciplinary focus; sub-questions; evidence requirements; lesson implications; likely misconceptions; success criteria.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Test whether the final question genuinely requires historical reasoning and whether the proposed evidence could support more than one defensible conclusion where appropriate.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for causation, change and continuity, similarity and difference, significance, interpretation or source use.

### Example Input
Year 5; topic: Viking settlement; period: early medieval Britain; disciplinary focus: change and continuity; available evidence: place names, archaeological finds and written accounts.

### Example Output
Frame a question such as how far Viking settlement changed life in a particular area, supported by sub-questions examining what changed, what continued and how confidently the evidence allows conclusions.

## HE-02 — Enquiry Sequence

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 800–1,200 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Enquiry Planning, Curriculum Sequencing

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]` | `[TOPIC]` | `[EVIDENCE]` | `[LESSON_COUNT]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History curriculum designer specialising in coherent evidence-led enquiry sequences.

### CONTEXT
Plan a `[LESSON_COUNT]`-lesson enquiry for `[YEAR_GROUP]` answering `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]` about `[TOPIC]`, using `[EVIDENCE]`.

### TASK
Design a sequence in which each lesson develops necessary knowledge or disciplinary understanding and contributes evidence towards the final historical conclusion.

### REQUIREMENTS
Map each lesson to a clear contribution to the final answer. Sequence contextual knowledge before interpretations that depend on it. Include opportunities to revisit and revise interim conclusions. Avoid filler activities that do not strengthen the final enquiry.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Enquiry map; lesson sequence; knowledge focus; evidence; disciplinary thinking; interim conclusion; assessment evidence; final task; misconception checks.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Verify that every lesson has an evidential, conceptual or disciplinary purpose and that the final task can be completed from knowledge and evidence developed across the sequence.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for three to eight lessons, single-source enquiries, local history or comparative studies.

### Example Input
Year 6; enquiry question: Why did the Normans win control of England?; topic: Norman Conquest; evidence: Bayeux Tapestry, maps, accounts and Domesday evidence; lesson count: 5.

### Example Output
Sequence context, succession, competing claims, military evidence and consequences so pupils progressively build and revise a multi-factor explanation before reaching a final judgement.

## HE-03 — Prior Knowledge Diagnostic

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Assessment, Prior Knowledge, Historical Enquiry

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[TOPIC]` | `[PREREQUISITES]` | `[UPCOMING_ENQUIRY]` | `[COMMON_CONFUSIONS]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History assessment specialist designing concise, diagnostic prior-knowledge checks.

### CONTEXT
Before `[UPCOMING_ENQUIRY]`, assess whether `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils have the necessary `[PREREQUISITES]` and identify likely `[COMMON_CONFUSIONS]` relating to `[TOPIC]`.

### TASK
Create a short diagnostic that distinguishes missing knowledge, chronology confusion and disciplinary misunderstanding sufficiently to guide immediate teaching decisions.

### REQUIREMENTS
Assess only knowledge genuinely needed for the enquiry. Include plausible distractors where useful and explain what each response pattern may indicate. Do not use the diagnostic as a proxy for general ability or as a full assessment of everything previously taught.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Prerequisite map; diagnostic questions; answers; response interpretation; immediate teaching actions; transition into enquiry.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that each item is necessary, unambiguous and diagnostically useful. Avoid conclusions about pupil ability, effort or motivation.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for oral checks, mini-whiteboards, retrieval quizzes, source sorting or chronology activities.

### Example Input
Year 4; topic: Roman Britain; prerequisites: Iron Age chronology, invasion and settlement vocabulary; upcoming enquiry: how did Roman rule change Britain?; common confusion: Romans and Anglo-Saxons placed in the wrong order.

### Example Output
Include a short sequencing task and vocabulary check that identifies whether pupils need immediate chronology repair before beginning the enquiry.

## HE-04 — Historical Mystery

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 700–1,000 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Historical Enquiry, Sources, Inference

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[TOPIC]` | `[MYSTERY]` | `[SUPPLIED_EVIDENCE]` | `[TARGET_REASONING]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History teacher designing rigorous evidence-led historical mysteries.

### CONTEXT
Create a historical mystery for `[YEAR_GROUP]` about `[TOPIC]`, centred on `[MYSTERY]`, using only `[SUPPLIED_EVIDENCE]` and developing `[TARGET_REASONING]`.

### TASK
Structure a sequence in which pupils examine evidence, generate hypotheses, test them against further evidence and revise or qualify their explanations.

### REQUIREMENTS
Do not invent quotations, artefacts, translations, provenance or eyewitness accounts. If evidence is incomplete, preserve that uncertainty. Distinguish observation, inference and speculation. Include evidence that challenges an initially plausible explanation where possible.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Mystery; context; evidence cards; provenance notes; observation prompts; hypothesis prompts; reasoning route; model conclusion; alternative interpretation; limitations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure every clue originates from supplied evidence and that the final conclusion does not claim more certainty than the evidence supports.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for archaeology, crime and punishment, exploration, settlement, conflict or local history.

### Example Input
Year 5; topic: Roman Britain; mystery: why was a settlement abandoned?; supplied evidence: excavation plan, coin dates, pottery evidence and flood deposits; target reasoning: evidence and causation.

### Example Output
Ask pupils to develop competing explanations, identify which evidence supports or challenges each one and finish with a qualified conclusion rather than a fictional definitive answer.

## HE-05 — Historical Question Ladder

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 600–900 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Questioning, Sources, Inference

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[SOURCE_SET]` | `[TOPIC]` | `[OBJECTIVE]` | `[TARGET_DISCIPLINARY_THINKING]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History pedagogy specialist with expertise in progressive questioning and source-based reasoning.

### CONTEXT
Create a question sequence for `[YEAR_GROUP]` using `[SOURCE_SET]` to achieve `[OBJECTIVE]` through `[TARGET_DISCIPLINARY_THINKING]`.

### TASK
Build a progression from orientation and observation to contextualisation, inference, evaluation and historical explanation, using only stages justified by the objective and evidence.

### REQUIREMENTS
Make the function of each question explicit. Label inference as inference, not fact. Do not force pupils into evaluative judgements before they possess enough contextual knowledge. Include prompts that require evidence to be cited or described precisely.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Question ladder; intended thinking; teacher prompts; expected evidence; guidance or answers; likely misconceptions; extension question.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that questions genuinely increase or shift the disciplinary demand and do not merely ask the same idea in different wording.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for images, artefacts, written sources, maps, oral histories or mixed evidence sets.

### Example Input
Year 6; source set: two contrasting accounts of the same protest; topic: suffrage; objective: compare interpretations; target thinking: inference and interpretation.

### Example Output
Move from identifying claims and provenance to comparing what each account suggests, explaining differences and identifying limits without assuming either source is automatically true or false.

## HE-06 — Enquiry Evidence Planner

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Evidence, Historical Enquiry, Source Evaluation

### Editable Variables
`[QUESTION]` | `[PERIOD]` | `[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[AVAILABLE_SOURCES]` | `[KNOWN_LIMITATIONS]`

### ROLE
Act as a historian and primary History teacher specialising in evidence planning and source evaluation.

### CONTEXT
Determine what evidence `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils need to investigate `[QUESTION]` in `[PERIOD]`, considering `[AVAILABLE_SOURCES]` and `[KNOWN_LIMITATIONS]`.

### TASK
Map possible claims or sub-claims to evidence types, specify what each source could legitimately establish and identify important limitations or unanswered questions.

### REQUIREMENTS
Distinguish source content from provenance and historical context. Avoid assuming that one source can settle a complex historical question. Identify where corroboration, contextual knowledge or additional evidence would strengthen an interpretation.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Claim map; evidence needed; possible sources; what each can establish; limitations; corroboration needs; follow-up questions; pupil-friendly version.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure proposed evidence is relevant to the claim and that limitations reflect the actual kind of source rather than generic statements about “bias.”

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for a source pack, archive visit, museum collection, local history enquiry or limited classroom evidence.

### Example Input
Question: How far did industrialisation change family life?; period: nineteenth-century Britain; year group: Year 5; available sources: census extracts, photographs and worker testimony; known limitations: uneven geographical coverage.

### Example Output
Map claims about work, household structure and childhood to appropriate evidence, specifying where each source is informative and where broader conclusions would require caution.

## HE-08 — Pupil-Generated Historical Questions

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 600–900 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Questioning, Historical Enquiry

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[STIMULUS]` | `[TOPIC]` | `[AVAILABLE_EVIDENCE]` | `[DISCIPLINARY_FOCUS]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History teacher specialising in developing pupils' enquiry and questioning skills.

### CONTEXT
Use `[STIMULUS]` to help `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils generate useful questions about `[TOPIC]`, considering `[AVAILABLE_EVIDENCE]` and `[DISCIPLINARY_FOCUS]`.

### TASK
Create scaffolds that move pupils from noticing a historical stimulus to generating questions that are historically meaningful, investigable and appropriate to the available evidence.

### REQUIREMENTS
Distinguish factual lookup questions from questions requiring historical enquiry without dismissing useful factual questions. Include sorting or refinement criteria and examples of how a weak question can be improved. Do not pretend every interesting question can be answered with the available evidence.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Stimulus orientation; noticing prompts; question stems; examples; question-sorting criteria; refinement process; challenge; teacher guidance.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure generated examples are historically meaningful and that refinement criteria are appropriate to the age group.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for artefacts, photographs, maps, buildings, oral histories or unfamiliar objects.

### Example Input
Year 3; stimulus: photograph of a Victorian classroom; topic: Victorian schooling; available evidence: photograph, timetable and school log extract; disciplinary focus: similarity and difference.

### Example Output
Guide pupils from observations towards questions about who attended, what learning was like and how confidently each question could be investigated from the available evidence.

## HE-09 — Enquiry Conclusion Scaffold

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Historical Writing, Evidence, Judgement

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]` | `[EVIDENCE]` | `[TARGET_DISCIPLINARY_CONCEPT]` | `[WRITING_LENGTH]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History teacher specialising in evidence-based historical writing and disciplinary literacy.

### CONTEXT
Support `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils to answer `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]` using `[EVIDENCE]`, demonstrating `[TARGET_DISCIPLINARY_CONCEPT]` in approximately `[WRITING_LENGTH]`.

### TASK
Create a scaffold that requires a clear claim, carefully selected evidence, explanation of how the evidence supports the claim and appropriate qualification or acknowledgement of limits.

### REQUIREMENTS
Do not provide a conclusion unsupported by the evidence. Distinguish quotation or source detail from explanation. Include scaffold fading so pupils can move from structured prompts towards increasingly independent historical writing.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Writing goal; claim prompts; evidence selection; explanation prompts; qualification prompts; model structure; model paragraph; success criteria; self-check; faded scaffold.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Verify that the model uses evidence accurately and that qualifications reflect genuine uncertainty rather than empty phrases such as “maybe” without explanation.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for causation, significance, change and continuity, comparison or source interpretation.

### Example Input
Year 6; enquiry question: Was the Industrial Revolution a turning point for working people?; evidence: factory records, reform legislation and testimony; target concept: change and continuity; writing length: 250 words.

### Example Output
Provide a structure that compares evidence of significant change with continuities and ends in a qualified judgement based on explicit criteria.

## HE-10 — Adapt Historical Enquiry

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Adaptive Teaching, Inclusion, Historical Enquiry

### Editable Variables
`[ORIGINAL_TASK]` | `[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[HISTORICAL_OBJECTIVE]` | `[ACCESS_BARRIER]` | `[EVIDENCE]` | `[SUPPORT_AVAILABLE]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History teacher specialising in adaptive teaching and inclusive historical enquiry.

### CONTEXT
The original task is `[ORIGINAL_TASK]` for `[YEAR_GROUP]`. The historical objective is `[HISTORICAL_OBJECTIVE]`; the access barrier is `[ACCESS_BARRIER]`; relevant `[EVIDENCE]` and `[SUPPORT_AVAILABLE]` must be considered.

### TASK
Adapt language, source presentation, task sequencing, sensory access or recording while preserving the pupil's responsibility to use evidence to make the intended inference, explanation or judgement.

### REQUIREMENTS
Analyse vocabulary, reading load, chronology, visual access, working memory, motor demands and prior knowledge separately before selecting support. Do not simplify away the historical question or provide the intended conclusion. Include scaffold fading and an independence route.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Original objective; barrier analysis; adapted task; source access adjustments; support; preserved historical demand; recording alternatives; independence route; review point.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Confirm that the adapted task still requires evidence-based historical reasoning and that support changes access rather than replacing the disciplinary thinking.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for SEND, EAL, dyslexia, sensory access, working-memory needs, motor access or language barriers.

### Example Input
Year 5; original task: evaluate two written sources about factory conditions; objective: compare evidence and reach a judgement; barrier: high reading load; evidence: two authentic extracts; support: text-to-speech and vocabulary glossary.

### Example Output
Preserve the contrasting claims and source information while reducing unnecessary decoding demands, allowing alternative recording and retaining the pupil's responsibility for comparing evidence and reaching the judgement.
