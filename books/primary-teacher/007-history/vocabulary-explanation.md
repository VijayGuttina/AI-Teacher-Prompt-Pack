# Historical Vocabulary and Explanation Workflows

## Chapter Overview

This chapter provides ten copy/paste-ready prompts for developing precise historical vocabulary, explanation, causal language, historical writing, discussion and diagnostic assessment. The prompts preserve the distinction between knowing historical information and using disciplinary reasoning to explain it.

## Audience

Primary teachers, History subject leaders, tutors and trainee teachers who want pupils to use historical vocabulary accurately and construct evidence-based explanations.

## How to Use This Chapter

1. Replace the editable variables with the topic, year group and evidence for your lesson.
2. Paste the actual pupil response or source material when the prompt requires diagnosis or improvement.
3. Ask ChatGPT to distinguish factual knowledge, vocabulary and historical reasoning.
4. Check all generated historical claims against your trusted curriculum and source material.
5. Use the resulting model, scaffold or criteria as teaching material, then adjust it for your pupils and school context.

## VE-01 — Historical Vocabulary Builder

**Purpose:** Build precise topic vocabulary.

**Prompt Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 700–1,000 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Historical Vocabulary, Subject Knowledge, Explanation

### Editable Variables

| Variable | Replace with |
|---|---|
| Year group | [year group] |
| Topic | [history topic] |
| Terms | [essential terms] |
| Curriculum objective | [objective] |

### ROLE

Act as a primary History subject specialist who understands progression in historical vocabulary and the difference between everyday and disciplinary meanings.

### CONTEXT

I am teaching **[Topic]** to **[Year group]**. The essential terms are **[Terms]** and the relevant curriculum objective is **[Curriculum objective]**.

### TASK

Build a precise vocabulary resource that gives pupils usable definitions and shows how each term functions within the historical topic. Include examples and non-examples so pupils can distinguish correct historical use from superficially similar language.

### REQUIREMENTS

- Use historically accurate definitions.
- Make definitions accessible without removing important distinctions.
- Explain topic-specific meaning where a word has a different everyday meaning.
- Include a concise example in historical context.
- Include a non-example and explain why it is incorrect where useful.
- Identify closely related terms that pupils may confuse.
- Avoid introducing unnecessary terminology.
- Flag definitions that depend on a particular historical context.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

| Term | Pupil-friendly definition | Historical context | Correct example | Non-example | Common confusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then provide:
1. Essential versus optional vocabulary
2. Teacher explanation notes
3. Retrieval activity
4. Application activity
5. Quick assessment

### QUALITY CHECKS

Definitions must be historically accurate and must not erase distinctions simply to make wording easier. Examples must use the terms in their correct historical sense.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Adapt for **[KS1/KS2]**, **[topic]**, **[display]**, **[knowledge organiser]**, or **[intervention group]**.

### Example Input

Year group: Year 5  
Topic: the Industrial Revolution  
Terms: factory, urbanisation, industry, reform, child labour.

### Example Output

A compact vocabulary table distinguishing each term, including the difference between industrial activity and urbanisation, followed by retrieval and application questions that require pupils to use the terms meaningfully.

### CONTEXT

### Example Input

Year group: Year 6  
Event: a major local change in employment  
Factors: new transport link, technological change, investment  
Evidence: supplied records and photographs.

### Example Output

**Curriculum Tags:** KS2, Assessment, Feedback, Historical Explanation, Evidence, Adaptive Teaching

| Variable | Replace with |
|---|---|
| Pupil response | [pupil work] |
| Question | [historical question] |
| Evidence | [available evidence] |
| Year group | [year group] |

### ROLE

Act as a primary History teacher providing precise, actionable feedback on historical explanation.

### CONTEXT

A **[Year group]** pupil has answered **[Question]** with the following response: **[Pupil response]**. The relevant evidence is **[Evidence]**.

### TASK

Identify the smallest changes needed to improve historical accuracy, evidence use and explanation. Preserve the pupil's intended meaning where possible and make the improvement teachable.

### REQUIREMENTS

- Identify genuine strengths first.
- Separate factual inaccuracies from incomplete explanation and weak evidence use.
- Quote or refer precisely to the pupil's response.
- Do not invent evidence to repair the answer.
- Preserve the pupil's voice where possible.
- Explain why each proposed change improves the historical reasoning.
- Provide prompts before providing a fully improved version.
- Include a next-attempt task.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

1. Strengths
2. Precise issues
3. Diagnostic explanation
4. Teaching prompts
5. Improved version
6. What changed and why
7. Self-check questions
8. Next independent attempt

### QUALITY CHECKS

Preserve the pupil's intended meaning where possible. Do not infer general ability or literacy from one response and do not introduce unsupported historical detail.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Adapt for **[individual feedback]**, **[whole-class feedback]**, **[peer assessment]**, or **[intervention]**.

### Example Input

Pupil response: “The town got bigger because the railway came and this made everything better.”  
Question: Why did the town grow?  
Evidence: supplied map and employment data  
Year group: Year 5.

### Example Output

The response's broad causal claim is retained but refined by requiring the pupil to identify what changed, cite the supplied evidence and explain the specific mechanism linking the railway to growth.
