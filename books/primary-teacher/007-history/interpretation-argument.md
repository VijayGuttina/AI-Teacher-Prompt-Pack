# Interpretation and Historical Argument Workflows

## IA-01 — Historical Interpretation Comparison

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 700–1,000 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Interpretation, Comparison, Evidence

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[TOPIC]` | `[INTERPRETATION_A]` | `[INTERPRETATION_B]` | `[SUPPORTING_EVIDENCE]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History teacher and historian specialising in historical interpretations and evidence-based comparison.

### CONTEXT
Support `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils to compare `[INTERPRETATION_A]` and `[INTERPRETATION_B]` about `[TOPIC]`, using `[SUPPORTING_EVIDENCE]` where supplied.

### TASK
Create a structured comparison that identifies differences in claims, emphasis, selection of evidence and explanation, before reaching a proportionate judgement about the interpretations.

### REQUIREMENTS
Do not invent historians' views, quotations, evidence or motives. Distinguish disagreement in factual claims from differences in emphasis or interpretation. Avoid assuming that different interpretations mean one must be entirely correct and the other entirely wrong.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Interpretation overview; claims; agreements; differences; evidence used; possible explanations; limitations; comparison questions; model judgement.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure every comparison is traceable to the supplied interpretations and evidence. Preserve uncertainty where the material does not establish why an interpretation differs.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for written interpretations, museum panels, textbooks, documentaries or contrasting classroom accounts.

### Example Input
Year 6; topic: causes of the Norman Conquest; Interpretation A: emphasises succession; Interpretation B: emphasises military circumstances; supporting evidence: supplied accounts and chronology.

### Example Output
Compare the weight each interpretation gives to political and military factors without claiming motives for the authors that are not supported by provenance.

## IA-02 — Why Historical Interpretations Differ

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Interpretation, Evidence, Historical Thinking

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[TOPIC]` | `[INTERPRETATIONS]` | `[AVAILABLE_EVIDENCE]` | `[PROVENANCE_INFORMATION]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History specialist with expertise in historical interpretation and disciplinary reasoning.

### CONTEXT
Explore why `[INTERPRETATIONS]` of `[TOPIC]` may differ with `[YEAR_GROUP]`, using `[AVAILABLE_EVIDENCE]` and `[PROVENANCE_INFORMATION]`.

### TASK
Develop an evidence-led explanation of possible reasons for differences, considering evidence selection, questions asked, perspective, purpose, timing, available sources and historical context.

### REQUIREMENTS
Do not reduce all disagreement to personal bias. Distinguish established provenance facts from hypotheses about influence. Make clear that different interpretations can sometimes be compatible or can address different aspects of the same historical problem.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Interpretation differences; possible factors; evidence for each factor; confidence level; pupil questions; model explanation; alternative possibilities; limitations.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure every proposed explanation has an evidential basis or is clearly labelled as a possibility requiring further evidence.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for one pair of interpretations, several accounts, historian extracts or pupil-generated interpretations.

### Example Input
Year 5; topic: why Roman rule ended in Britain; interpretations: political and military accounts; available evidence: supplied extracts; provenance information: publication dates and authors supplied.

### Example Output
Explore whether differences arise from emphasis, available evidence or the questions being addressed without automatically attributing disagreement to bias.

## IA-03 — Historical Significance Judgement

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 700–1,000 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Significance, Evidence, Judgement

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[SUBJECT]` | `[PERIOD]` | `[SIGNIFICANCE_CRITERIA]` | `[EVIDENCE]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History teacher specialising in teaching historical significance.

### CONTEXT
Support `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils to judge the significance of `[SUBJECT]` in `[PERIOD]` using `[SIGNIFICANCE_CRITERIA]` and `[EVIDENCE]`.

### TASK
Create a transparent process for applying explicit criteria to evidence and reaching a justified significance judgement that considers alternative rankings or perspectives.

### REQUIREMENTS
Define significance in historical rather than purely personal terms. Use criteria such as consequence, duration, reach, depth of change or later recognition only where appropriate. Avoid treating fame, modern popularity or moral approval as automatic evidence of significance.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Subject context; criteria; evidence map; criterion-by-criterion analysis; possible counterarguments; ranking or judgement; model response; self-check.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure the criteria are explicit before the conclusion and that every major judgement is supported by relevant evidence.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for a person, event, invention, movement or local historical development.

### Example Input
Year 4; subject: Roman roads; period: Roman Britain; criteria: reach, duration and impact; evidence: maps, archaeological evidence and later road patterns.

### Example Output
Evaluate significance through explicit criteria and distinguish immediate impact from long-term influence.

## IA-04 — Historical Causation Explanation

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 750–1,050 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Causation, Evidence, Explanation

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[EVENT]` | `[PERIOD]` | `[EVIDENCE]` | `[KNOWN_FACTORS]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History subject specialist with expertise in causal explanation.

### CONTEXT
Explain why `[EVENT]` occurred during `[PERIOD]` with `[YEAR_GROUP]`, using `[EVIDENCE]` and considering `[KNOWN_FACTORS]`.

### TASK
Construct a multi-factor causal explanation that distinguishes background conditions, contributing causes, triggers, relationships between factors and consequences where relevant.

### REQUIREMENTS
Avoid single-cause or inevitable-outcome narratives. Do not confuse consequences with causes. Explain relationships between factors rather than producing an unconnected list. Preserve uncertainty where evidence cannot establish relative importance confidently.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Event context; causal factors; factor categories; evidence; relationships; trigger; model explanation; alternative weighting; limitations; pupil task.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Verify chronology and ensure each claimed cause precedes and plausibly contributes to the event. Do not present correlation alone as causation.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for invasions, rebellions, political change, industrial developments, migration or local events.

### Example Input
Year 6; event: Norman Conquest; period: 1066; evidence: succession claims, Stamford Bridge, Bayeux Tapestry and contemporary accounts; known factors: political and military.

### Example Output
Connect succession, military circumstances and leadership decisions while distinguishing longer-term conditions from immediate triggers.

## IA-05 — Historical Consequence Analysis

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 700–1,000 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Consequences, Change, Evidence

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[EVENT_OR_DEVELOPMENT]` | `[PERIOD]` | `[EVIDENCE]` | `[TIMESCALES]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History teacher specialising in consequences, change and continuity.

### CONTEXT
Analyse the consequences of `[EVENT_OR_DEVELOPMENT]` during `[PERIOD]` with `[YEAR_GROUP]`, using `[EVIDENCE]` across `[TIMESCALES]`.

### TASK
Trace defensible immediate, medium-term and longer-term consequences while distinguishing direct effects, contributing effects, continuities and later developments that cannot confidently be attributed to the event alone.

### REQUIREMENTS
Do not construct deterministic chains unsupported by evidence. Distinguish temporal sequence from causation. Consider uneven consequences across groups or places where evidence supports this.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Event context; timescale map; consequences; evidence; affected groups; continuities; causal confidence; limitations; questions; model analysis.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check chronology and ensure later developments are not automatically treated as consequences simply because they followed the event.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for political events, wars, inventions, reforms, migrations or local developments.

### Example Input
Year 5; event: introduction of railways; period: nineteenth-century Britain; evidence: maps, passenger figures and local accounts; timescales: immediate, 20 years, longer term.

### Example Output
Trace changes in travel and trade while qualifying claims about wider social change that require additional evidence.

## IA-06 — Historical Argument Planner

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 700–1,000 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Argument, Evidence, Historical Writing

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]` | `[AVAILABLE_EVIDENCE]` | `[TARGET_LENGTH]` | `[DISCIPLINARY_FOCUS]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History teacher specialising in evidence-based argument and disciplinary writing.

### CONTEXT
Help `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils answer `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]` using `[AVAILABLE_EVIDENCE]`, with `[DISCIPLINARY_FOCUS]` in approximately `[TARGET_LENGTH]`.

### TASK
Create a planning structure that develops a defensible thesis, selects relevant evidence, explains how evidence supports claims, addresses a meaningful counterpoint and reaches a qualified conclusion.

### REQUIREMENTS
Do not provide claims unsupported by the supplied evidence. Distinguish evidence selection from explanation. Include qualification where the evidence is incomplete or competing interpretations remain plausible. Avoid artificial counterarguments that no reasonable reading of the evidence supports.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Question unpacking; thesis options; claim map; evidence selection; explanation prompts; counterpoint; qualification; conclusion plan; success criteria; self-check.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Every major claim must be traceable to available evidence, and the counterpoint must strengthen rather than mechanically weaken the argument.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for oral argument, paragraph writing, extended enquiry conclusion or structured debate preparation.

### Example Input
Year 6; enquiry question: Was the Industrial Revolution a turning point for working people?; evidence: factory records, legislation and testimony; target length: 300 words; disciplinary focus: change and continuity.

### Example Output
Plan a judgement using explicit criteria, evidence of major changes and evidence of continuities before reaching a qualified conclusion.

## IA-07 — Alternative Historical Explanation Task

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Reasoning, Interpretation, Evidence

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]` | `[INITIAL_EXPLANATION]` | `[EVIDENCE]` | `[CONTEXT]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History specialist with expertise in comparative reasoning and evidence-based explanation.

### CONTEXT
Challenge premature closure by examining `[INITIAL_EXPLANATION]` for `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]` with `[YEAR_GROUP]`, using `[EVIDENCE]` and `[CONTEXT]`.

### TASK
Generate and test plausible alternative explanations that are compatible with the supplied evidence, then guide pupils to compare explanatory strength and limitations.

### REQUIREMENTS
Do not manufacture alternatives that contradict known evidence without flagging the contradiction. Distinguish genuinely competing explanations from explanations that can coexist. Require evidence for and against each explanation and preserve uncertainty where the evidence cannot discriminate.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Initial explanation; alternatives; supporting evidence; challenging evidence; compatibility; explanatory strength; limitations; comparison task; model judgement.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure alternatives arise from the supplied evidence or clearly identified historical possibilities rather than arbitrary speculation.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for causation, source interpretation, significance or change and continuity.

### Example Input
Year 5; enquiry question: Why did a Roman settlement decline?; initial explanation: invasion caused abandonment; evidence: coin chronology, building repairs and environmental deposits; context: evidence is incomplete.

### Example Output
Compare environmental, economic and political explanations while identifying which evidence genuinely supports each and what remains uncertain.

## IA-08 — Evidence-Based Historical Debate

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 750–1,100 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Debate, Argument, Evidence

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[MOTION]` | `[TOPIC]` | `[EVIDENCE]` | `[DEBATE_FORMAT]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History teacher designing structured evidence-based classroom debate.

### CONTEXT
Plan a `[DEBATE_FORMAT]` debate for `[YEAR_GROUP]` around `[MOTION]` on `[TOPIC]`, using `[EVIDENCE]`.

### TASK
Create positions, evidence requirements, preparation tasks, rebuttal prompts and a debrief that evaluates historical reasoning rather than confidence or speaking volume.

### REQUIREMENTS
Anchor every substantive claim to supplied evidence. Do not turn complex historical questions into false binaries where the evidence supports qualified positions. Distinguish historical argument from present-day personal opinion and ensure pupils can revise their position when evidence warrants it.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Motion; historical context; evidence rules; roles; preparation; opening prompts; rebuttal prompts; evidence tracker; success criteria; debrief; assessment notes.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure both sides have defensible evidence-based routes and that the debate does not require pupils to endorse historically harmful positions as personal beliefs.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for formal debate, structured academic controversy, small groups, fishbowl discussion or written debate.

### Example Input
Year 6; motion: The railways changed Britain more than factories did; topic: Industrial Revolution; evidence: transport, employment and urbanisation sources; debate format: structured academic controversy.

### Example Output
Require pupils to argue both evidence-based positions before forming a final individual judgement that may be qualified rather than binary.

## IA-09 — Historical Argument Misconception Diagnosis

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Assessment, Argument, Diagnosis

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[PUPIL_RESPONSE]` | `[QUESTION]` | `[AVAILABLE_EVIDENCE]` | `[RECENT_TEACHING]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History assessment specialist diagnosing weaknesses in historical explanation and argument.

### CONTEXT
Analyse `[PUPIL_RESPONSE]` from `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils answering `[QUESTION]` using `[AVAILABLE_EVIDENCE]`, in light of `[RECENT_TEACHING]`.

### TASK
Identify observable weaknesses such as unsupported assertion, chronology confusion, source misuse, weak causal reasoning, overgeneralisation or difficulty qualifying a judgement, then design a discriminating follow-up.

### REQUIREMENTS
Separate historical reasoning from transcription, handwriting, spelling or general writing fluency unless these directly affect interpretation of the historical response. Do not infer a stable misconception from one answer. Generate competing explanations and specify expected response patterns.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Observable issue; evidence; competing explanations; diagnostic follow-up; expected responses; interpretation matrix; targeted next step; verification check.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure the follow-up tests the suspected reasoning problem rather than simply asking the same question again in different words.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for individual conferencing, small-group intervention, whole-class hinge questions or extended writing analysis.

### Example Input
Year 6; pupil response: “William was the most important because he won”; question: Why did William succeed?; available evidence: succession and battle evidence; recent teaching: multiple causation.

### Example Output
Distinguish a factual gap from a weak causal model and test whether the pupil can explain relationships between factors using a new but comparable event.

## IA-10 — Adapt Historical Argument

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Argument, Adaptive Teaching, Inclusion

### Editable Variables
`[ORIGINAL_TASK]` | `[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[HISTORICAL_OBJECTIVE]` | `[ACCESS_BARRIER]` | `[EVIDENCE]` | `[SUPPORT_AVAILABLE]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History teacher specialising in adaptive teaching and evidence-based argument.

### CONTEXT
Adapt `[ORIGINAL_TASK]` for `[YEAR_GROUP]` while preserving `[HISTORICAL_OBJECTIVE]`. The access barrier is `[ACCESS_BARRIER]`; relevant `[EVIDENCE]` and `[SUPPORT_AVAILABLE]` must be considered.

### TASK
Adapt language, planning structure, evidence presentation, recording or response mode while preserving the pupil's responsibility to select evidence, explain its relevance and construct a historical judgement.

### REQUIREMENTS
Analyse vocabulary, reading load, working memory, chronology, visual access, motor demands and recording separately before selecting support. Do not provide the thesis, evidence selection or final judgement when these are part of the objective. Include scaffold fading and an independence route.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Original objective; barrier analysis; adapted task; access support; preserved reasoning; evidence responsibilities; recording alternatives; scaffold fading; independence route; review point.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Confirm that the adaptation changes access rather than reducing the central reasoning demand and that pupils remain responsible for constructing and supporting the historical judgement.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for SEND, EAL, dyslexia, working-memory needs, motor access, visual access or language barriers.

### Example Input
Year 5; original task: write a two-paragraph judgement using three sources; objective: reach and justify a historical conclusion; barrier: extended writing difficulty; evidence: three supplied sources; support: speech-to-text and claim-evidence organiser.

### Example Output
Permit alternative recording and structured planning while retaining the pupil's responsibility to choose relevant evidence, explain it and reach the historical judgement.
