# Local History and Historical Place Workflows

## Chapter Overview

This chapter provides ten copy/paste-ready prompts for using ChatGPT to plan and teach local history and historical place work in primary classrooms. The prompts keep local evidence at the centre of enquiry and require clear separation between observation, evidence, inference and interpretation.

## Audience

Primary teachers, subject leaders, tutors and trainee teachers planning local-history enquiries, source work, fieldwork, oral history and adaptive teaching.

## How to Use This Chapter

1. Replace the editable variables with your own local context.
2. Supply the actual sources, maps, photographs, interview material or site information where available.
3. Ask ChatGPT to identify uncertainty rather than fill gaps with plausible-sounding local facts.
4. Review the output against the quality checks before using it with pupils.
5. Keep pupils working with evidence rather than treating familiarity with a place as historical knowledge.

## LH-01 — Local History Enquiry

**Purpose:** Create an evidence-led enquiry about a local place or community.

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 800–1,200 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, Local History, Historical Enquiry, Sources, Chronology, Change and Continuity

### Editable Variables

| Variable | Replace with |
|---|---|
| Place | [local place/community/site] |
| Period | [historical period or date range] |
| Year group | [year group] |
| Enquiry question | [main question] |
| Available evidence | [sources, photographs, maps, objects, records] |

### ROLE

Act as an experienced primary History teacher and local-history enquiry designer. You understand progression in historical enquiry, the distinction between evidence and interpretation, and the need to avoid inventing local information.

### CONTEXT

The class is studying **[Place]** during **[Period]**. The pupils are in **[Year group]**. The intended enquiry question is **[Enquiry question]**. Available evidence includes **[Available evidence]**.

### TASK

Build a complete, evidence-led local-history enquiry that allows pupils to answer the enquiry question by examining the supplied evidence. Structure the enquiry so that pupils move from orientation and observation to questioning, evidence analysis, interpretation and a supported conclusion.

### REQUIREMENTS

- Use only local facts explicitly supplied by me or clearly identified as requiring verification.
- Do not invent buildings, people, dates, events, industries, occupations, traditions or archive material.
- Explain what each source can and cannot establish.
- Include opportunities to distinguish observation, inference and interpretation.
- Include chronology where it genuinely helps answer the question.
- Include change and continuity where relevant.
- Use age-appropriate historical vocabulary and explain specialist terms.
- Make the enquiry manageable within normal primary lesson time.
- Include at least one point where pupils must compare evidence before reaching a judgement.
- Flag any factual claim that needs checking against a reliable local source.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

1. Enquiry question and historical focus
2. Prior knowledge and likely misconceptions
3. Evidence required
4. Lesson-by-lesson enquiry sequence
5. Pupil questions
6. Evidence-analysis activities
7. Supported conclusion task
8. Teacher notes on uncertainty and verification
9. Differentiation and challenge
10. Final assessment opportunity

### QUALITY CHECKS

Before finalising, check that the enquiry is answerable from the available evidence, that unsupported local claims are not presented as facts, and that pupils are required to use evidence rather than guess from present-day familiarity.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Adapt the sequence for **[number of lessons]**, **[KS1/KS2]**, **[individual/group work]**, **[museum/archive visit]**, or **[specific local source set]**.

### Example Input

Place: a local high street  
Period: 1850–1950  
Year group: Year 5  
Enquiry question: How and why did our high street change?  
Available evidence: two historic photographs, one modern photograph and three historic maps.

### Example Output

A source-led sequence in which pupils first identify visible features, then compare photographs and maps, establish a defensible chronology, investigate possible explanations using only supplied evidence, and finish with a judgement explaining which changes are best supported and which remain uncertain.

### ROLE

Act as a primary History subject specialist with expertise in historical sources and local-history teaching.

### CONTEXT

I am planning a local-history investigation about **[Place]** during **[Period]** for **[Year group]**. The sources available are **[Available sources]**. The intended enquiry question is **[Enquiry question]**.

### TASK

Organise the supplied sources into a coherent pupil-friendly source pack. Explain the evidential purpose of each source and design questions that encourage pupils to consider provenance, content, context, usefulness, limitations and corroboration without reducing sources to simplistic reliable/unreliable labels.

### REQUIREMENTS

- Use supplied source information accurately.
- Never fabricate archive references, quotations, dates, creators or provenance.
- Distinguish what is directly visible or stated from what may be inferred.
- Identify missing context where it affects interpretation.
- Include corroboration between sources where appropriate.
- Avoid treating a single source as definitive proof when it cannot support that conclusion.
- Keep questions suitable for **[Year group]**.
- Include extension questions for pupils ready for greater evidential challenge.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

| Source | Provenance | What it directly shows/states | What it may help establish | Limitation | Pupil question |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then provide:
1. Recommended source order
2. Teacher explanation
3. Corroboration activity
4. Final enquiry question
5. Expected evidence-based conclusions
6. Uncertainties requiring verification

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check every provenance statement against the supplied information. Do not create missing source details. Make uncertainty explicit and ensure questions require evidence rather than speculation.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Create versions for **[mixed attainment]**, **[SEND/access needs]**, **[independent source investigation]**, or **[exam-style written response]**.

### Example Input

Period: 1900–1950  
Sources: a 1911 census extract, a 1930 photograph and a 1945 newspaper extract.  
Year group: Year 6.

### Example Output

A three-source pack with provenance prompts, direct-observation questions, corroboration questions and a final judgement task. Any information not contained in the supplied extracts is clearly marked for verification rather than invented.

### ROLE

Act as a primary History and geography specialist who can use maps as historical evidence without over-interpreting them.

### CONTEXT

Pupils will compare maps of **[Place]** from **[Maps]**, covering **[Period]**. They are in **[Year group]** and are investigating **[Enquiry question]**.

### TASK

Create a map-comparison activity that identifies defensible changes and continuities, builds chronology and asks pupils to consider possible explanations only when supported by additional evidence.

### REQUIREMENTS

- Distinguish absence from evidence of non-existence where the map scale or purpose makes this important.
- Identify changes in roads, buildings, land use, boundaries, transport or settlement only where visible.

### Example Input

Place: local town centre  
Maps: 1880 and 2020  
Year group: Year 6  
Enquiry question: What changed in the way the town was organised?

### Example Output

A comparison sequence identifying roads, buildings and land-use changes, followed by questions asking what the maps can establish and what other sources would be needed to explain the reasons for those changes.

### ROLE

Act as an experienced primary History teacher specialising in oral history and ethical source use.

### CONTEXT

Pupils in **[Year group]** will prepare an interview about **[Topic]** concerning **[Period]**. The interviewee is **[Interviewee]**. The purpose is **[Interview purpose]**.

### TASK

Create neutral, open-ended interview questions that encourage useful historical testimony while avoiding leading questions. Include appropriate follow-ups and explain how pupils should treat memory as valuable evidence that may be selective, incomplete or unrepresentative.

### REQUIREMENTS

- Use neutral wording.
- Avoid questions that suggest the expected answer.
- Include prompts for dates, places, routines, experiences and change over time where appropriate.
- Include follow-ups asking for clarification rather than confrontation.
- Include consent, privacy and safeguarding reminders appropriate to a school activity.
- Do not request unnecessary sensitive personal information.
- Explain the difference between testimony and independently corroborated historical evidence.
- Do not assume that a remembered account represents everyone in the community.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

1. Interview purpose
2. Opening questions
3. Main questions by theme
4. Follow-up prompts
5. Questions to avoid and why
6. Consent/privacy/safeguarding checklist
7. Recording and note-taking guidance
8. Post-interview source evaluation
9. Corroboration activity

### QUALITY CHECKS

Check that questions are neutral, age-appropriate and historically useful. Make clear that memory is evidence but not automatically complete, precise or representative.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION

Adapt for **[live interview]**, **[recorded interview]**, **[written testimony]**, **[museum oral-history project]**, or **[specific historical topic]**.

### Example Input

Topic: childhood in the local area  
Interviewee: community member who lived locally during the 1960s  
Period: 1960–1970  
Year group: Year 5.

### Example Output

A neutral interview schedule beginning with broad questions, followed by prompts about school, transport, shops and leisure, and ending with questions about how the account could be compared with photographs, maps or written records.

### REQUIREMENTS

### Example Input

Criteria: remembered, affected people, reveals change over time  
Evidence: supplied photographs, inscription and verified local account  
Year group: Year 6.

### Example Output

A criteria matrix showing how each piece of evidence supports or limits a significance claim, followed by a balanced final judgement rather than a simple significant/not significant label.

## Chapter Summary

Local history is strongest when pupils investigate the places they know without assuming that familiarity tells them what happened in the past. These prompts keep local evidence, chronology, provenance, corroboration, uncertainty and historical reasoning at the centre of teaching.
