# Assessment, Misconception and Intervention Workflows

## ADI-01 — History Exit Ticket

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
**Expected Output Length:** 500–800 words
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Assessment, Formative Assessment

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[OBJECTIVE]` | `[TOPIC]` | `[RECENT_LEARNING]` | `[NUMBER_OF_QUESTIONS]` | `[LIKELY_ERRORS]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History assessment specialist with expertise in substantive knowledge and disciplinary thinking.

### CONTEXT
Create an exit ticket for `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils after learning about `[TOPIC]` against `[OBJECTIVE]`. Take account of `[RECENT_LEARNING]`, `[NUMBER_OF_QUESTIONS]` and `[LIKELY_ERRORS]`.

### TASK
Create a concise assessment that samples the intended historical knowledge and, where relevant, chronology, evidence use, causation, change and continuity, significance or interpretation needed to determine the next teaching step.

### REQUIREMENTS
Map every item to the objective. Avoid generic factual trivia and avoid assessing reading complexity when historical understanding is the target. Include at least one item capable of revealing a plausible historical error where appropriate. Ensure the assessment can realistically be completed at the end of the lesson.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Assessment objective; item map; pupil questions; answer guidance; what each item assesses; likely interpretations; immediate next-step options.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Verify historical accuracy, chronology and terminology. Check that success or failure on each item supports a meaningful interpretation rather than a vague attainment label.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for mini-whiteboards, paper exit tickets, oral responses, digital forms or source-based lessons.

### Example Input
Year 4; objective: explain one significant change following the Roman invasion; topic: Roman Britain; recent learning: roads, towns and military control; questions: 4; likely error: assuming everything in Britain changed immediately.

### Example Output
Include direct knowledge, evidence-based explanation and a diagnostic item distinguishing significant change from total change.

## ADI-02 — History Hinge Question

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
**Expected Output Length:** 450–700 words
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Formative Assessment, Misconceptions

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[OBJECTIVE]` | `[TOPIC]` | `[MISCONCEPTION]` | `[RESPONSE_FORMAT]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History assessment specialist designing high-discrimination hinge questions for live teaching.

### CONTEXT
Create a hinge question for `[YEAR_GROUP]` on `[TOPIC]` against `[OBJECTIVE]`. The suspected error is `[MISCONCEPTION]`, using `[RESPONSE_FORMAT]`.

### TASK
Design one decisive question that can distinguish secure understanding from plausible alternative explanations and guide an immediate teaching decision.

### REQUIREMENTS
Use one unambiguous best answer unless the task explicitly requires open reasoning. Make distractors represent meaningful errors such as chronology confusion, presentism, unsupported causation, overclaiming from evidence or misunderstanding historical significance. Explain what each response pattern may indicate and the appropriate teacher response.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Objective; hinge question; response options; correct answer; distractor logic; interpretation grid; immediate teacher response; follow-up check.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check historical accuracy and ensure distractors are plausible without becoming ambiguous or dependent on irrelevant reading difficulty.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for multiple choice, source selection, timeline ordering, mini-whiteboards or paired discussion.

### Example Input
Year 6; objective: explain that historical change may be uneven; topic: industrialisation; misconception: factories immediately changed the lives of everyone in Britain; response format: four-option multiple choice.

### Example Output
Use distractors representing total, immediate, selective and evidence-supported change, then specify whether to proceed or reteach based on the response pattern.

## ADI-03 — Historical Knowledge and Disciplinary Assessment

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
**Expected Output Length:** 800–1,200 words
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Assessment, Curriculum

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[OBJECTIVES]` | `[TOPIC]` | `[ITEM_COUNT]` | `[DURATION]` | `[KNOWN_CONFUSIONS]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History assessment designer with expertise in curriculum alignment and diagnostic interpretation.

### CONTEXT
Build an assessment for `[YEAR_GROUP]` covering `[OBJECTIVES]` within `[TOPIC]`, using `[ITEM_COUNT]`, `[DURATION]` and `[KNOWN_CONFUSIONS]`.

### TASK
Create a balanced assessment blueprint and complete question set sampling relevant substantive knowledge and disciplinary thinking.

### REQUIREMENTS
Map every item to a specific objective and demand. Include chronology, evidence use or explanation only where they are genuinely part of the objective. Assessment blueprint; objective coverage; questions; answer key; marking guidance; item interpretation; cohort analysis guidance; next teaching actions.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Verify historical accuracy, chronological sequence, source claims and objective alignment. Check that each item measures its claimed construct.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for end-of-lesson, end-of-enquiry, end-of-unit, oral assessment or mixed source and written assessment.

### Example Input
Year 5; objectives: Viking raids, settlement, evidence and change; topic: Vikings in Britain; item count: 12; duration: 25 minutes.

### Example Output
Provide a blueprint before the questions and ensure factual recall does not crowd out the disciplinary objectives being assessed.

## ADI-04 — Analyse Pupil History Work

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
**Expected Output Length:** 700–1,000 words
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Diagnosis, Assessment, Feedback

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[OBJECTIVE]` | `[PUPIL_WORK]` | `[QUESTION_OR_TASK]` | `[RECENT_TEACHING]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History assessment and diagnostic specialist.

### CONTEXT
Analyse `[PUPIL_WORK]` from `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils against `[OBJECTIVE]`, considering `[QUESTION_OR_TASK]` and `[RECENT_TEACHING]`.

### TASK
Identify strengths, observable errors and the smallest useful next teaching step. Generate possible explanations only where the evidence does not establish the cause directly.

### REQUIREMENTS
Separate observation from interpretation. Consider factual knowledge, chronology, vocabulary, source comprehension, inference, causation, significance, change and continuity as distinct possible causes. Identify a discriminating follow-up that could distinguish competing explanations.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Strengths; observable evidence; errors or gaps; response pattern; possible explanations; evidence for and against; diagnostic follow-up; recommended next step; feedback language; review check.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Do not infer effort, ability, motivation, literacy level or a fixed misconception from insufficient evidence. Keep recommendations proportionate to the work supplied.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for an individual pupil, small group, whole class, source analysis or extended historical writing.

### Example Input
Year 6; objective: explain multiple causes of the Norman Conquest; pupil work: “William won because Harold was tired”; recent teaching: succession crisis, Stamford Bridge and Battle of Hastings.

### Example Output
Identify the observable single-factor explanation, distinguish missing knowledge from weak causal reasoning and create a short follow-up that tests both possibilities.

## ADI-05 — Source-Use Misconception Diagnosis

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Sources, Diagnosis, Historical Enquiry

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[PUPIL_RESPONSE]` | `[SOURCE]` | `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]` | `[SUSPECTED_ISSUE]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History diagnostic specialist with expertise in historical evidence and source-based reasoning.

### CONTEXT
Investigate `[SUSPECTED_ISSUE]` in a `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupil response to `[SOURCE]` while addressing `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]`.

### TASK
Determine whether the observable issue is more likely to involve observation, inference, provenance, relevance, corroboration, contextual knowledge or overclaiming, and create a diagnostic follow-up that distinguishes these possibilities.

### REQUIREMENTS
Do not invent source information. Base analysis only on the supplied source and response. Change the context or representation where useful rather than simply repeating the original question. State expected response patterns under competing explanations.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Observed issue; source evidence; competing explanations; diagnostic task; expected responses; interpretation matrix; confidence limits; targeted response; verification check.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Do not treat a single weak answer as proof of a stable misunderstanding. Ensure the follow-up generates genuinely new evidence.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for images, artefacts, written accounts, maps, oral histories or mixed source sets.

### Example Input
Year 5; pupil response: “This poster proves all children worked in factories”; source: one campaign poster; enquiry question: what was childhood like during industrialisation?; suspected issue: overgeneralising from one source.

### Example Output
Test whether the pupil understands source limitation, representativeness or the distinction between evidence supporting a claim and proving a universal conclusion.

## ADI-06 — Targeted History Intervention

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
**Expected Output Length:** 800–1,200 words
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Intervention, Adaptive Teaching

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[IDENTIFIED_GAP]` | `[EVIDENCE]` | `[PREREQUISITES]` | `[NUMBER_OF_SESSIONS]` | `[TIME_PER_SESSION]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History intervention teacher specialising in precise, evidence-led re-teaching.

### CONTEXT
Plan an intervention for `[YEAR_GROUP]` addressing `[IDENTIFIED_GAP]`, supported by `[EVIDENCE]`. Consider `[PREREQUISITES]`. The intervention has `[NUMBER_OF_SESSIONS]` sessions of `[TIME_PER_SESSION]`.

### TASK
Design a focused sequence that checks prerequisites, explicitly models the target historical thinking or knowledge, provides guided and independent practice, and verifies whether understanding has changed.

### REQUIREMENTS
Do not build the intervention around an unverified diagnosis. Use the smallest intervention likely to address the evidence. Include retrieval and transfer so success is not limited to repeating a familiar example. Define an exit criterion based on evidence rather than time completed.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Need and evidence; prerequisite check; session sequence; teacher modelling; representations or sources; guided practice; independent application; diagnostic checks; exit criterion; escalation route.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure each activity addresses the identified gap and that the final check tests transfer through a sufficiently different historical context or evidence set.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for individual tutoring, small groups, short daily sessions, chronology, causation, source use or historical writing.

### Example Input
Year 5; identified gap: distinguishing evidence from inference; evidence: pupils repeatedly state inferences as facts; prerequisites: observation and contextual vocabulary; sessions: 3; time: 20 minutes.

### Example Output
Begin with observation, explicitly model the language of inference, then assess transfer using a new source rather than repeating the original image.

## ADI-07 — History Retrieval Planner

**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
**Expected Output Length:** 600–900 words
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Retrieval Practice, Curriculum Sequencing

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History teacher with expertise in curriculum sequencing and purposeful retrieval practice.

### CONTEXT
Plan retrieval for `[YEAR_GROUP]` before or during `[CURRENT_ENQUIRY]` on `[CURRENT_TOPIC]`. Reactivate `[PREREQUISITE_KNOWLEDGE]` through `[NUMBER_OF_ITEMS]` within `[TIME_AVAILABLE]`.

### TASK
Create a retrieval set that reactivates knowledge genuinely needed for the current historical learning and reveals whether a prerequisite requires immediate re-teaching.

### REQUIREMENTS

### Example Input

## ADI-08 — Adapt History Assessment

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Assessment, Adaptive Teaching, Inclusion

### Editable Variables
`[ORIGINAL_ASSESSMENT]` | `[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[HISTORICAL_OBJECTIVE]` | `[ACCESS_BARRIER]` | `[SUPPORT_AVAILABLE]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History teacher specialising in adaptive assessment and inclusive historical learning.

### CONTEXT
Adapt `[ORIGINAL_ASSESSMENT]` for `[YEAR_GROUP]` while preserving `[HISTORICAL_OBJECTIVE]`. The access barrier is `[ACCESS_BARRIER]`; available support is `[SUPPORT_AVAILABLE]`.

### TASK
Adapt language, layout, source presentation, response mode or task sequencing so the pupil can access the assessment without changing the historical knowledge or reasoning being assessed.

### REQUIREMENTS
Analyse vocabulary, reading load, chronology support, visual access, working memory, motor demands and recording separately. Do not provide the historical answer through the adaptation or remove the central disciplinary construct. Include scaffold fading or an independence route where appropriate.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Assessment construct; barrier analysis; adapted assessment; access support; preserved historical demand; alternative response modes; independence route; review point.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Confirm that the adaptation changes access rather than silently changing what the assessment measures.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for SEND, EAL, dyslexia, visual access, motor access, working-memory or communication barriers.

### Example Input
Year 4; original assessment: compare two short sources about Roman Britain; objective: infer similarities and differences; barrier: extended writing difficulty; support: speech-to-text and structured comparison grid.

### Example Output
Allow oral or speech-to-text responses while preserving responsibility for interpreting both sources and supporting the comparison with evidence.

## ADI-09 — Evidence Summary for Teaching

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
**Expected Output Length:** 600–900 words
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Assessment Analysis, Subject Leadership

### Editable Variables
`[ASSESSMENT_DATA]` | `[PUPIL_WORK]` | `[OBJECTIVES]` | `[PUPIL_OR_GROUP_CONTEXT]` | `[NEXT_LESSON_CONSTRAINTS]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History subject leader analysing assessment and pupil-work evidence for proportionate teaching decisions.

### CONTEXT
Analyse `[ASSESSMENT_DATA]` and `[PUPIL_WORK]` against `[OBJECTIVES]` for `[PUPIL_OR_GROUP_CONTEXT]`, considering `[NEXT_LESSON_CONSTRAINTS]`.

### TASK
Summarise what the evidence establishes, what it suggests but does not prove, and what cannot yet be concluded. Prioritise next actions by educational value and confidence in the evidence.

### REQUIREMENTS
Separate secure evidence, developing evidence, observable errors and interpretation hypotheses. Distinguish cohort patterns from isolated anomalies. Recommend diagnostic follow-up where uncertainty remains rather than inventing certainty.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Evidence overview; secure learning; developing learning; errors and patterns; evidence limits; priority actions; diagnostic follow-up; next-lesson plan.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Every recommendation must be traceable to supplied evidence. Do not overgeneralise from small samples or attach unsupported attainment labels.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for a class teacher, History subject leader, intervention team or concise leadership summary.

### Example Input
Assessment data: 28 pupil responses on causes of the Norman Conquest; pupil work: short explanations; objectives: identify and explain multiple factors; context: whole class; next lesson: 45 minutes.

### Example Output
Separate factual gaps from causal reasoning issues, identify the strongest class-level pattern and recommend whether the next lesson should proceed, diagnose further or include targeted re-teaching.

## ADI-10 — Unit Review and Reteach Planner

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot
**Expected Output Length:** 800–1,200 words
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Unit Review, Reteaching, Curriculum Planning

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[UNIT_OBJECTIVES]` | `[ASSESSMENT_RESULTS]` | `[OBSERVED_ERRORS]` | `[NEXT_UNIT]` | `[AVAILABLE_TIME]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History subject leader and curriculum planner.

### CONTEXT
Review a completed History unit for `[YEAR_GROUP]` using `[UNIT_OBJECTIVES]`, `[ASSESSMENT_RESULTS]` and `[OBSERVED_ERRORS]`. Plan preparation for `[NEXT_UNIT]` within `[AVAILABLE_TIME]`.

### TASK
Identify secure learning, unresolved gaps, prerequisite risks and high-value reteaching priorities. Produce a proportionate plan for retrieval, targeted reteaching, reassessment and future sequencing.

### REQUIREMENTS
Map evidence to individual objectives. Distinguish widespread patterns from isolated errors and temporary performance variation. Prioritise knowledge or disciplinary concepts that are foundational, persistently insecure or likely to obstruct future learning. Do not reteach the entire unit by default.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Evidence summary; objective-by-objective status; secure learning; unresolved gaps; priority misconceptions; reteach plan; retrieval schedule; reassessment checks; links to next unit; future sequencing notes.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Keep claims proportionate to the evidence. Ensure reteaching addresses the underlying knowledge or disciplinary concept rather than simply repeating the original activity, and define how improvement will be verified.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for whole-class review, intervention planning, subject-leader analysis or short pre-next-unit preparation.

### Example Input
Year 6; unit objectives: Norman Conquest; assessment results: objective-level class summary; observed errors: single-cause explanations and treating the Bayeux Tapestry as neutral fact; next unit: medieval Britain; available time: two 45-minute sessions.

### Example Output
Prioritise the foundational reasoning gaps, avoid reteaching objectives already secure for most pupils and include a delayed check using a new evidence set.
