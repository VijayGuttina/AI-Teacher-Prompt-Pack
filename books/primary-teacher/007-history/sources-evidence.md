# Sources and Historical Evidence Workflows

## SE-01 — Historical Source Analysis

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 700–1,000 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Sources, Evidence, Historical Enquiry

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[SOURCE]` | `[SOURCE_INFORMATION]` | `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]` | `[PRIOR_KNOWLEDGE]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History teacher and historian specialising in evidence-led source analysis.

### CONTEXT
Support `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils to analyse `[SOURCE]` using only `[SOURCE_INFORMATION]` and relevant `[PRIOR_KNOWLEDGE]` to investigate `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]`.

### TASK
Create a structured analysis that separates direct source content, provenance, contextual knowledge, justified inference, evidential value and limitation.

### REQUIREMENTS
Do not invent missing provenance, context or source content. Distinguish what the source directly states or shows from what may reasonably be inferred. Explain what the source can contribute to the enquiry without treating one source as a complete answer.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Source overview; direct observations; provenance; contextual links; justified inferences; evidence for each inference; evidential value; limitations; pupil questions; model responses.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Verify that every inference is traceable to source content and relevant context. Preserve uncertainty where the evidence does not justify certainty.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for photographs, artefacts, written accounts, maps, propaganda, oral histories or archaeological evidence.

### Example Input
Year 5; source: nineteenth-century factory photograph; source information: date and location supplied, photographer unknown; enquiry question: what was factory work like?; prior knowledge: industrialisation and child labour legislation.

### Example Output
Separate visible details from inferences about working conditions and explain why the photograph can contribute evidence without proving that every factory was the same.

## SE-02 — Source Provenance Questions

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 600–900 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Sources, Provenance, Interpretation

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[SOURCE]` | `[KNOWN_PROVENANCE]` | `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]` | `[VOCABULARY_LEVEL]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History specialist with expertise in provenance and historical interpretation.

### CONTEXT
Design provenance questions for `[YEAR_GROUP]` using `[SOURCE]` and `[KNOWN_PROVENANCE]` in relation to `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]`, at `[VOCABULARY_LEVEL]`.

### TASK
Create a progressive question sequence exploring who created the source, when, where, for whom and for what purpose, then explain how each answer may affect interpretation.

### REQUIREMENTS
Use only known provenance. Distinguish an evidenced purpose from a hypothesis about purpose. Do not imply that personal interest, persuasion or partial perspective automatically makes a source false or useless.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Known provenance; unanswered provenance questions; question ladder; possible implications; evidence needed; pupil-friendly explanations; misconceptions; model reasoning.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure provenance questions lead to a meaningful interpretation of the source rather than becoming a checklist detached from the enquiry.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for KS1 discussion, KS2 written analysis, paired talk or source annotation.

### Example Input
Year 6; source: wartime government poster; known provenance: government department, 1942, Britain; enquiry question: how did the government encourage civilian behaviour?; vocabulary level: Year 6.

### Example Output
Guide pupils from identifying creator and audience to explaining how persuasive purpose affects what the poster can reveal about official messaging.

## SE-03 — Source Usefulness Judgement

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Sources, Evidence, Judgement

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[SOURCE]` | `[SOURCE_INFORMATION]` | `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]` | `[COMPARISON_EVIDENCE]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History teacher specialising in question-specific evaluation of historical evidence.

### CONTEXT
Evaluate the usefulness of `[SOURCE]` with `[SOURCE_INFORMATION]` for `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils investigating `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]`, alongside `[COMPARISON_EVIDENCE]` where supplied.

### TASK
Build a balanced judgement about usefulness based on relevant content, provenance, scope, representativeness and the limits of what the source can establish.

### REQUIREMENTS
Define usefulness in relation to the specific enquiry. Avoid generic statements that a source is useful simply because it is old or primary, or useless simply because it is biased. Include evidence that could strengthen, qualify or challenge the judgement.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Enquiry focus; relevant evidence; useful because; limitations; corroboration needs; overall judgement; pupil scaffold; model answer; self-check.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Check that every evaluation point explains usefulness for this question, not source quality in the abstract.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for short oral judgements, paragraph writing, evidence sorting or extended enquiry conclusions.

### Example Input
Year 6; source: soldier's letter; source information: written during the First World War to family; enquiry question: what were conditions like for soldiers?; comparison evidence: trench photographs and official reports.

### Example Output
Explain what the letter may reveal about one individual's experience while considering audience, selection and corroboration without dismissing it as simply biased.

## SE-04 — Historical Source Comparison

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 700–1,000 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Sources, Comparison, Interpretation

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[SOURCE_A]` | `[SOURCE_B]` | `[PROVENANCE_INFORMATION]` | `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History subject specialist with expertise in comparing historical evidence and interpretations.

### CONTEXT
Compare `[SOURCE_A]` and `[SOURCE_B]` for `[YEAR_GROUP]`, using `[PROVENANCE_INFORMATION]` to investigate `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]`.

### TASK
Identify meaningful agreements, differences, omissions and differences in perspective, then explore plausible explanations supported by provenance and context.

### REQUIREMENTS
Do not assume that different sources contradict each other. Separate differences in content from differences in interpretation or perspective. Avoid inventing motives or contexts not supplied. Identify what further evidence could help explain unresolved differences.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Comparison criteria; Source A; Source B; agreements; differences; possible explanations; evidence gaps; limitations; pupil questions; model comparison.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Use equivalent criteria for both sources and preserve the meaning of supplied material when quoting or paraphrasing.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for written sources, images, maps, artefacts, oral histories or primary-versus-secondary evidence.

### Example Input
Year 5; Source A: newspaper report; Source B: worker testimony; provenance: supplied publication and speaker details; enquiry question: how did people experience industrial change?

### Example Output
Compare claims about experience, identify where different perspectives may arise, and distinguish genuine disagreement from sources describing different aspects of the same period.

## SE-05 — Historical Source Inference

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Sources, Inference, Evidence

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[SOURCE]` | `[SOURCE_INFORMATION]` | `[QUESTION]` | `[TARGET_VOCABULARY]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History teacher specialising in teaching evidence-based historical inference.

### CONTEXT
Use `[SOURCE]` and `[SOURCE_INFORMATION]` with `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils to answer `[QUESTION]`, using `[TARGET_VOCABULARY]`.

### TASK
Create a progression from precise observation to possible inference, evidence selection, alternative inference and confidence judgement.

### REQUIREMENTS
Label observation and inference explicitly. Require pupils to identify which source detail supports each inference. Include at least one alternative inference where the evidence is genuinely compatible with more than one explanation. Do not convert inference into fact through model answers.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Observation prompts; evidence extraction; inference stems; alternative inference; confidence scale; model reasoning; guided practice; independent task; misconception check.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure each inference is proportionate to the evidence and that uncertainty is meaningful rather than an automatic disclaimer.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for KS1 oral reasoning, KS2 writing, image analysis, artefact evidence or short written sources.

### Example Input
Year 4; source: image of a Roman road; source information: location and approximate date supplied; question: what can this source suggest about Roman priorities?; target vocabulary: evidence, infer, suggests.

### Example Output
Model the distinction between “I can see...” and “This suggests...” while requiring pupils to connect each inference to a visible feature.

## SE-06 — Source Reliability Discussion

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Sources, Reliability, Interpretation

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[SOURCE]` | `[KNOWN_PROVENANCE]` | `[CLAIM_TO_EVALUATE]` | `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History specialist with expertise in nuanced discussion of reliability and evidential claims.

### CONTEXT
Support `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils to evaluate `[SOURCE]`, using `[KNOWN_PROVENANCE]`, in relation to `[CLAIM_TO_EVALUATE]` and `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]`.

### TASK
Develop a discussion that evaluates how confidently the source can support a specific claim, considering creator position, purpose, timing, access to information and corroboration where available.

### REQUIREMENTS
Do not reduce reliability to a permanent binary label. Separate whether a source may be dependable for one type of claim from whether it can establish another. Avoid assuming official sources are automatically accurate or personal sources automatically unreliable.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Claim; relevant source evidence; reliability factors; supporting considerations; limitations; corroboration; confidence judgement; pupil discussion prompts; model conclusion.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Keep every reliability judgement tied to the claim being evaluated and distinguish missing evidence from evidence of inaccuracy.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for debate, structured discussion, evidence continuum or written evaluation.

### Example Input
Year 6; source: official government report; known provenance: authoring body and date supplied; claim: factory conditions were improving; enquiry question: how did reform affect working conditions?

### Example Output
Evaluate whether the report is well placed to establish official policy, observed conditions or nationwide experience, rather than applying a single “reliable/unreliable” label.

## SE-07 — Primary Source Classroom Preparation

**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 750–1,100 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Primary Sources, Vocabulary, Source Analysis

### Editable Variables
`[SOURCE_TEXT_OR_DESCRIPTION]` | `[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[OBJECTIVE]` | `[KNOWN_PROVENANCE]` | `[ACCESS_BARRIERS]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History teacher and curriculum designer specialising in accessible use of primary evidence.

### CONTEXT
Prepare `[SOURCE_TEXT_OR_DESCRIPTION]` for `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils to meet `[OBJECTIVE]`, using `[KNOWN_PROVENANCE]` and addressing `[ACCESS_BARRIERS]`.

### TASK
Produce teacher notes, access support and pupil questions that preserve the source's historical meaning and evidential features.

### REQUIREMENTS
Do not silently modernise, rewrite or sanitise the source as though the adapted wording were original. Clearly separate original content, explanatory support and modern glossing. Preserve relevant ambiguity, omission and historical language where these are part of the evidential task.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

### QUALITY CHECKS

### Example Input

### Example Output
Provide a glossary and chunked presentation while preserving the original wording and explicitly labelling any teacher-created paraphrase.

## SE-08 — Historical Source Pack Curator

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 750–1,100 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Evidence, Source Selection, Enquiry Planning

### Editable Variables
`[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]` | `[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[AVAILABLE_SOURCES]` | `[TOPIC_CONTEXT]` | `[LESSON_COUNT]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History curriculum specialist and historian curating balanced evidence sets for classroom enquiries.

### CONTEXT
Select and sequence evidence from `[AVAILABLE_SOURCES]` for `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils investigating `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]` within `[TOPIC_CONTEXT]` over `[LESSON_COUNT]` lessons.

### TASK
Recommend a coherent source pack, explain the evidential contribution and limitation of each source, and sequence sources to build contextual understanding and increasingly sophisticated reasoning.

### REQUIREMENTS
Do not fabricate archive references, quotations, translations or source content. Identify evidence gaps honestly. Avoid selecting sources merely because they are varied; each source must serve a clear enquiry purpose. Include perspectives and evidence types only where relevant and available.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Enquiry evidence map; selected sources; rationale; what each can establish; limitations; sequencing; pupil task; evidence gaps; teacher notes.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure the combined pack can support the intended enquiry without forcing a predetermined conclusion and that no invented evidence fills gaps.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for one lesson, a full unit, museum resources, local archives or a supplied classroom source bank.

### Example Input
Enquiry question: How far did the Romans change life in Britain?; Year 4; available sources: roads, villas, inscriptions, settlement evidence and written accounts; topic context: Roman Britain; lesson count: 5.

### Example Output
Sequence contextual evidence before comparative evidence and make explicit where surviving evidence is stronger for some regions or groups than others.

## SE-09 — Source Misconception Diagnosis

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Sources, Assessment, Misconceptions

### Editable Variables
`[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[PUPIL_RESPONSE]` | `[SOURCE]` | `[SOURCE_INFORMATION]` | `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]`

### ROLE
Act as a primary History assessment specialist diagnosing errors in pupils' use of historical evidence.

### CONTEXT
Analyse `[PUPIL_RESPONSE]` from `[YEAR_GROUP]` pupils using `[SOURCE]` and `[SOURCE_INFORMATION]` to address `[ENQUIRY_QUESTION]`.

### TASK
Identify the observable error, generate competing explanations involving observation, inference, provenance, relevance, corroboration or overclaiming, and design a follow-up that discriminates between them.

### REQUIREMENTS
Separate what the response demonstrates from hypotheses about why it occurred. Do not infer a fixed misconception from one answer. Use a new representation, source feature or context where useful so the follow-up produces genuinely new diagnostic evidence.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Observable response pattern; possible explanations; evidence for and against; diagnostic task; expected responses; interpretation matrix; targeted teaching response; verification check.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Ensure the diagnostic task tests the suspected source-thinking issue rather than simply repeating the original wording.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for individual conferencing, small-group intervention, whole-class hinge questions or source annotation.

### Example Input
Year 5; pupil response: “The poster proves all children worked in factories”; source: campaign poster; source information: date and creator supplied; enquiry question: what was childhood like during industrialisation?

### Example Output
Test whether the pupil is overgeneralising from one source, confusing a persuasive claim with direct evidence, or lacks the language to qualify a conclusion.

## SE-10 — Adapt Historical Source Analysis

**Prompt Difficulty:** Advanced  
**AI Model Compatibility:** ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot  
**Expected Output Length:** 650–950 words  
**Curriculum Tags:** KS1, KS2, History, Sources, Adaptive Teaching, Inclusion

### Editable Variables
`[SOURCE]` | `[YEAR_GROUP]` | `[HISTORICAL_OBJECTIVE]` | `[ACCESS_BARRIER]` | `[KNOWN_PROVENANCE]` | `[SUPPORT_AVAILABLE]`

### ROLE
Act as an experienced primary History teacher specialising in adaptive teaching and accessible source analysis.

### CONTEXT
Adapt `[SOURCE]` for `[YEAR_GROUP]` while preserving `[HISTORICAL_OBJECTIVE]`. The access barrier is `[ACCESS_BARRIER]`; relevant provenance is `[KNOWN_PROVENANCE]`; available support is `[SUPPORT_AVAILABLE]`.

### TASK
Adapt presentation, vocabulary support, sensory access, task sequencing or recording while preserving the source evidence and the historical reasoning pupils must perform.

### REQUIREMENTS
Analyse vocabulary, reading load, visual complexity, chronology, working memory, motor demands and prior knowledge separately. Do not remove relevant provenance, simplify away ambiguity, highlight the intended inference or provide the judgement pupils are meant to construct. Include scaffold fading and an independence route.

### OUTPUT FORMAT
Original objective; barrier analysis; adapted source presentation; support; preserved evidence; preserved disciplinary demand; alternative recording; scaffold fading; independence route; review point.

### QUALITY CHECKS
Confirm that the adaptation changes access rather than the historical construct being assessed and that pupils remain responsible for interpreting evidence.

### OPTIONAL CUSTOMISATION
Adapt for SEND, EAL, dyslexia, visual access, sensory needs, working-memory needs, motor access or communication barriers.

### Example Input
Source: two contrasting written accounts of factory work; Year 5; objective: compare perspectives and support a conclusion with evidence; barrier: high reading load; provenance: supplied for both sources; support: text-to-speech and vocabulary cards.

### Example Output
Chunk the texts, provide access support and flexible recording while retaining both accounts, provenance information and the pupil's responsibility to compare evidence and construct the conclusion.
