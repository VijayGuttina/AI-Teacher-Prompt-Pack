# Primary History Worked Examples

These examples demonstrate the intended workflow behaviour. They are examples of use, not execution-verification evidence.

## Example 1 — Source inference

**Workflow:** SE-05  
**Input:** A supplied photograph of a Victorian classroom and the question: “What can this source suggest about classroom life?”

**Expected approach:** Separate visible observations from inferences, then identify what additional evidence would be needed. Do not invent photographer, date, school or location if they are not supplied.

**Teacher value:** Models the distinction between evidence and inference.

## Example 2 — Change and continuity

**Workflow:** CC-03  
**Input:** Compare children’s work and school routines in two supplied historical periods.

**Expected approach:** Use the same criteria for both periods, identify evidence of change and continuity, and qualify claims where evidence is limited.

**Teacher value:** Prevents a superficial “then versus now” activity from replacing historical comparison.

## Example 3 — Significance

**Workflow:** SP-01  
**Input:** “How significant was this event to people at the time?” with a supplied evidence set.

**Expected approach:** Establish significance criteria, select relevant evidence, make a judgement and acknowledge an alternative view.

**Teacher value:** Makes significance an evidence-based judgement rather than a popularity exercise.

## Example 4 — Local history

**Workflow:** LH-02  
**Input:** A teacher supplies three local archive extracts and asks for a source pack.

**Expected approach:** Organise the supplied sources by provenance, evidential value and limitation. Do not create missing archive details.

**Teacher value:** Protects against a major AI risk in local history: plausible but invented local information.

## Example 5 — Assessment diagnosis

**Workflow:** ADI-04  
**Input:** A pupil writes an answer that correctly recalls an event but gives an unsupported claim about its cause.

**Expected approach:** Distinguish secure factual knowledge from the observable causal-reasoning issue, then propose a discriminating diagnostic question and targeted next step.

**Teacher value:** Avoids turning one response into an unsupported judgement about general ability.

## Example 6 — Adaptive teaching

**Workflow:** VE-09  
**Input:** A historical explanation task is difficult for a pupil because of language/recording demands.

**Expected approach:** Reduce unnecessary language or recording barriers while preserving the requirement to select evidence and explain its relevance.

**Teacher value:** Keeps the historical construct intact rather than making the task merely easier.
