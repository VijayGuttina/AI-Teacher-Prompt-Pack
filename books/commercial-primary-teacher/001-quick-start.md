# Part 1: Quick Start

## What this toolkit is

This is a practical collection of ready-to-use AI workflows for primary teachers.

The prompts are designed around real teacher tasks rather than around what AI happens to be capable of generating. You can use them to plan, teach, assess, adapt and repurpose material you already have.

## The fastest way to use the book

1. Find the task you need to complete.
2. Read the short guidance above the prompt.
3. Replace the editable fields.
4. Copy the prompt.
5. Paste it into your approved AI assistant.
6. Review the output before using it.
7. Keep the useful result and refine your own process over time.

## Give the AI the context that matters

The quality of the result depends heavily on the information you provide. Useful context usually includes:

- Year group
- Subject
- Topic
- Learning objective
- Lesson duration
- Prior learning
- Attainment range
- Resources available
- Accessibility requirements
- Desired output

Do not add pupil names or other personally identifiable information unless your school's approved systems and policies explicitly allow it.

## Use variables instead of rewriting prompts

Most prompts contain editable fields such as `[YEAR_GROUP]`, `[TOPIC]`, `[OBJECTIVE]` or `[DURATION]`.

Change the values, not the underlying instructions.

That makes one workflow useful across different classes, subjects and lessons.

## Review before use

AI can produce confident but incorrect material. Before using an output with pupils, check:

- factual accuracy;
- curriculum alignment;
- age appropriateness;
- calculations and answer keys;
- examples and quotations;
- accessibility;
- safeguarding implications;
- whether the activity actually serves the learning objective.

## Keep teacher judgement at the centre

Use AI to reduce preparation work, generate options and help you inspect material. Do not outsource professional judgement.

This matters particularly for assessment, behaviour, SEND, safeguarding, parent communication and any decision about an individual pupil.

## When information is missing

A good prompt should not force you into unnecessary back-and-forth.

Where missing information does not materially change the task, use a sensible assumption and label it clearly.

Where the missing information would materially change the result, provide it before using the prompt.

## A useful working habit

Save strong outputs in your own planning system. Over time, you will build a bank of examples that can be reused with the prompts in this toolkit.

The goal is not to ask AI to do everything. The goal is to remove repetitive preparation so that more of your time can go into teaching, feedback and pupils.
